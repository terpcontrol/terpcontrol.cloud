# C05 — Thumb Journal

**Design philosophy:** capture speed is the product.
**Author's stance:** this concept is deliberately lopsided. It optimises one thing — the cost of recording
what just happened — and subordinates everything else to it. Section 15 says where that hurts.

---

## 1. Thesis

**The journal is the app: a single chronological thread that the machine writes into by itself, that a human
adds to with one thumb in under two seconds, and that every other screen is merely a lens onto.**

Terp Control already stores the three things nobody else fuses — environment (Influx), machine state
(`out_*` series), and human events (`DeviceLog`) — but it stores them in three places, shows them on three
screens, and makes the human half cost six taps and a modal. Thumb Journal collapses them into one thread and
then makes the human half cost **one tap**. The bet is that a grow diary is not a document you sit down and
write; it is a stream of two-second interruptions in a dark room, one hand busy, the other holding a phone,
and the app that wins is the one that survives being used badly. Everything that cannot be done in one or two
taps is moved *after* the write, not before it: the entry is posted first and refined later, or never. The
owner's ask — *"it does the diary keeping FOR YOU"* — is taken literally: the server writes a dated entry into
the same thread every morning with yesterday's verdict, every excursion, and a photo from the moment it went
wrong. The human's job is reduced to the four things the machine genuinely cannot know: **I watered, I fed, I
looked at it, I did something to it.**

## 2. Information architecture

### 2.1 Navigation model

Two rules decide the whole layout:

1. **The thumb zone belongs to capture, not to navigation.** The bottom 56 px + safe area is the *Thumb Bar*
   on every device screen. It is never a tab bar. Navigation happens at the top, because you navigate ten
   times a week and you capture ten times a day.
2. **One device = one shell.** All device screens are children of a `DeviceShellModule` with a scrollable
   `ion-segment` in the header. Non-device screens (account, shares, admin) keep today's `ion-menu`.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ☰   Zelt 1                        ●live   ⋮  │  40px header
│ ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐ │
│ │ Journal │ Jetzt │ Kurve │ Pflanzen       │ │  40px ion-segment (scrollable)
│ └──────────────────────────────────────────┘ │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                              │
│                 <router-outlet>              │
│                                              │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ [📷]  [💧]  [🧪]  [🎙]  [⋯]                  │  56px Thumb Bar + safe-area
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The `ion-split-pane` + `ion-menu` at app level stays exactly as it is (it is load-bearing for
`/account`, `/shares`, `/classes`, dark mode, logout). On ≥ 992 px the shell becomes three columns and the
Thumb Bar becomes a left rail — see §12.4.

**Rejected:** `ion-tab-bar`. It would either sit under the Thumb Bar (112 px of chrome on a 667 px phone) or
compete with it for the same pixels. Navigation moving to the top is a real cost, stated in §15.

### 2.3 The Thumb Bar (the one component that matters)

`app-thumb-bar`, declared and exported in `components.module.ts`. Present on `journal`, `now`, `charts`,
`plants`, `plants/:id`, `week/:iso`. Absent on `settings`, `feed`, `members`, `tidy` (screens where you are
already in a form).

Five slots. **Fixed order. Never auto-rearranged.** I deliberately rejected a "learned" thumb bar that floats
frequently-used actions leftward: a moving target destroys the muscle memory that makes one-tap logging fast,
and the whole point is that you can hit 💧 without looking. Order is user-editable once, in ⋯ → *Leiste
anpassen*, and stored in `DeviceJournalPrefs.thumbBarOrder`.

| Slot | Icon | Tap | Long-press (400 ms) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 📷 `camera-outline` | opens the OS camera (`<input type=file capture=environment>` — **already in the repo** at `diary-entry-modal.component.html:126`) | photo library picker |
| 2 | 💧 `water-outline` | posts a watering, repeating last volume + last plant set | **Mengenring** — radial of the 4 last-used volumes + "…" |
| 3 | 🧪 `flask-outline` | opens the Feed sheet, prefilled from the regime | jumps to `/feed` |
| 4 | 🎙 `mic-outline` | opens a text note field with the keyboard | **hold to dictate**, release to post |
| 5 | ⋯ `ellipsis-horizontal` | the More sheet (12 chips) | — |

Defaults per device type: controller/fridge → as above. fan/light/plug → `📷 🎙 ⋯` (3 slots, 💧/🧪 available
via ⋯ → *anheften*). See §9.

State: the 🧪 slot carries a small green dot when a feed is due per the regime, and reads *"fällig"* under the
icon. The 💧 slot carries a grey dot if nothing has been watered for longer than the user's own median
interval × 1.5 — a hint, never a nag, never a push notification.

**Reading rules.** Human entries are right-aligned bubbles with a tinted surface
(`--tc-surface-subtle`, 12 px radius = existing `ion-card` override). Machine entries are left-aligned,
transparent, with a 3 px accent bar in the severity colour. Trivial machine lines (light on/off, boot,
config echo) are single 24 px rows with no bubble, collapsed into `▸ 6 Geräte-Meldungen` when three or more
occur inside 30 minutes. Photos render as a 2-up grid inside the bubble, max 160 px tall.

**Day dividers** carry the day counter. Which counter depends on scope: with no plants it is *"Tag N"* since
the device's first sample; with plants it is *"Tag N · Blüte Tag M"* for the selected plant, and in the
all-plants view it is just the date plus a stage pill per plant present.

**Scroll behaviour.** The thread opens at the bottom (newest) like a chat. Pull-down loads the previous 50
entries (`?before=<ts>&limit=50`, server-side — see §5.2). A right-edge date rail (a thin scrubber) lets you
fling to a week. Sticky day dividers.

**Filter.** ⋮ → *Filter* opens chips: `Alles · Meine Einträge · Gießen · Düngen · Fotos · Messungen · Gerät`.
Reuses `collectLogCategories` / `matchesLogCategory` semantics from `log-entry-viewer.component.ts`, extended
to `JournalEntry.kind`. Filter state goes in query params (`?kinds=water,feed`) so it is shareable.

### 3.2 The Now strip

72 px, sticky under the segment. Three or four values chosen by capability, not by device type:
temperature, humidity, VPD, and CO₂ **only if `deviceHasCo2()`**. Second line is a status sentence. Tapping
anywhere goes to `/now`.

Its whole job is to answer *"is it fine?"* in one glance so the user can go back to the thread.

### 3.3 Live / stale / offline / absent — the four states, spelled out

This is P0 #5 in the dossier ("nothing distinguishes live from stale from offline", the #1 complaint across
five consumer brands). Thumb Journal fixes it in the one place everybody looks.

| State | Condition | Now strip renders | Dot | Thumb Bar |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **live** | newest sample < 60 s | full-colour values, `vor 12 s` | ● green | normal |
| **stale** | 60 s – 10 min | values at 55 % opacity, `vor 4 min` | ● amber | normal |
| **offline** | > 10 min (matches the existing rule) | **values struck through**, last value + time | ● grey | normal |
| **absent** | device has never reported this measure | `—` (existing `value-display` already renders `—`) | — | normal |

Copy, EN / DE:

| Key | English | German |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `now.live` | `{{age}} ago` | `vor {{age}}` |
| `now.stale` | `Last reading {{age}} ago` | `Letzter Wert vor {{age}}` |
| `now.offline` | `Offline since {{time}}` | `Offline seit {{time}}` |
| `now.offline.hint` | `Values below are the last known ones.` | `Die Werte unten sind die zuletzt bekannten.` |
| `now.noData` | `No readings yet` | `Noch keine Messwerte` |
| `now.allOnTarget` | `Everything on target` | `Alles im Ziel` |
| `now.outOfTarget` | `{{measure}} out of target` | `{{measure}} außerhalb des Ziels` |
| `now.monitorOnly` | `Measuring only — nothing connected to control` | `Nur Messen — nichts zum Regeln angeschlossen` |

**The critical behaviour:** *offline does not disable capture.* The device being offline has nothing to do
with whether you can log that you watered. The Thumb Bar stays live and the thread gains a grey band
`Gerät offline — deine Einträge werden trotzdem gespeichert.` Every competitor conflates these; this concept
must not, because the whole product proposition is that the journal keeps working.

When the *network* is gone (phone, not device), a capture goes into `PendingCaptureQueue` in `localStorage`
and the bubble renders with a clock icon and 60 % opacity, retried on `window.online`. Each queued entry
carries a `clientId` UUID used as an idempotency key, so a retry can never double-post. **Honest limit:**
there is no service worker, so a cold start with no network shows the browser's offline page — the queue
survives, the app shell does not. Adding `@angular/service-worker` is listed as post-v1 in §13.

## 4. The chart overhaul

### 4.1 Library and licence

Per the owner's decision: **off Highcharts, onto Apache ECharts 6**, tree-shaken, with a hand-rolled
~50-line Angular directive (`[tcEchart]`) rather than `ngx-echarts`. Delete `chart.js`, `ng2-charts`,
`chartjs-adapter-luxon` from `charts.page.ts`, `charts.module.ts`, `diary.module.ts`, `diagnostics.*`.

Honest accounting: tree-shaken ECharts ≈ **195.6 KB gz** vs Highstock's **132.7 KB gz**; deleting Chart.js
repays **68.8 KB gz**, so the net is roughly a wash. The migration is justified by **licence**
(proprietary Highstock in a repo with no LICENSE file, marketed as *quelloffen*, serving a public
`/demo`) and by **capability** (`markArea`, `markLine`, `grid[]` + `axisPointer.link`, `sampling`,
`darkMode:'auto'`), never by size.

Two things that must ship with the migration or the chart regresses:
- **Explicit downsampling.** Highstock's implicit `dataGrouping` was the only thing keeping a 4,320-point
  render cheap and nobody configured it. Replacement: `sampling: 'minmax'` on value series (LTTB smooths away
  exactly the bang-bang oscillation a grower is looking for), LTTB on the navigator.
- **The Flux fixes first.** `measure/from/to/interval` are interpolated raw into Flux and the
  `limit(n:50000)` guard is piped *after* `yield()`. This concept widens the measure vocabulary
  (EC target series, per-plant lanes), so §8 of the gap list is a hard prerequisite, not a nice-to-have.

### 4.2 Panels

Stacked small multiples, shared x-axis, one linked crosshair (`echarts.connect()` + `axisPointer.link`).
Dossier §7.2 #1 adopted without reservation — Few's dual-scale argument is unanswerable and today's page runs
*fifteen* independent hidden scales as translucent fills.

Mobile default: **3 panels**, ~110 px each. Desktop default: 5. Panels are added/removed from a chip row and
the set is stored in the query string (`&panels=temp,rh,vpd`).

| # | Panel | Series | Goal drawn as |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Temperatur °C** | actual (solid, `#D55E00`), min/max envelope (12 % fill of the same hue), leaf temp (thin dotted) if MLX90632 present | **dashed stepped setpoint** from `day.temperature`/`night.temperature`, stepping at `daynight.day`/`night`; soft band = alarm thresholds if the user set any, else setpoint ± 1.5 °C |
| 2 | **Luftfeuchte %** | actual, envelope | dashed stepped setpoint from `day.humidity`/`night.humidity`; band ± 7 % |
| 3 | **VPD kPa** | derived actual | **a band that changes along the x-axis** as the plant moves through stages (`GROW_STAGE_PRESETS.vpdRange`, driven by `PlantStageEvent`) |
| 4 | **CO₂ ppm** | actual | `co2.target` as a flat dashed line — only if `deviceHasCo2()` |
| 5 | **Licht %** | `out_light` PWM 0–100 | `lights.limit` ceiling; a small **DLI bar per day** under it when a PPFD figure exists |
| 6 | **EC / pH** | logged `feed.ecMs` / `measure.phValue` as points, runoff as hollow points | the **regime's target EC per step** as a dashed step line — goal-vs-actual for feeding |

Panel 6 appears only once the user has logged one EC or pH value. Panel 4 appears only with the sensor.
Capability drives panels; `device_type` does not.

**Capability honesty in the drawing itself.** Humidity can only ever go down — there is no humidifier role in
the firmware. When measured RH sits *below* the setpoint and no humidifier exists, the lower half of the
target band renders greyed with a one-line label `nicht regelbar` / `not controllable`. Drawing a target the
hardware cannot reach, silently, is the current sin (P0 #3); drawing it *and saying so* is the fix. The same
applies to the `dehumidifier`-doubling-as-cooler ambiguity: the outputs lane labels that region by the active
workmode (`Entfeuchter` in `small`/`full`, `Kühlung` in `temp`/`breed`), because the socket means two things.

### 4.3 Lanes below the panels

```
 ── panels ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
 Ausgänge  ▐████▌   ▐██▌      ▐███████▌            (Heizung)
           ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░           (Licht)
 Wasser    ╷        ╷              ╷2L                       ← lollipops, height ∝ litres
 Journal   ● ●●2   ●        ●   ●▲                           ← 2 rows, clustered
 Film      ▤ ▤ ▤ ▤ ▤ ▤ ▤ ▤ ▤ ▤ ▤ ▤ ▤ ▤                       ← positioned by timestamp
 ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 00:00      06:00      12:00      18:00      00:00
```

- **Ausgänge (outputs).** A state-timeline lane (Grafana's state-regions model), region length = duration,
  null = a gap. Adopted from §7.2 #6. This replaces 0/1 lines fighting for the value axis.
- **Wasser/Dünger.** Lollipops: water in `#0072B2` with height ∝ litres, feed in `#009E73` with the step
  number as a badge. This is the owner's *"it should also be visualized in the graph"*, and it is the one
  lane no competitor has.
- **Journal-Schiene.** Adopted from §7.2 #5: an annotation rail *under* the plot, 14 px, **max two rows**
  (row 1 = user entries, row 2 = device events), pixel-proximity clustering at 10 px bins with count badges,
  severity ranking, and **full-height dashed verticals for stage changes only**. Tap a cluster → the journal
  thread scrolls to that entry (see §4.5).
- **Filmstreifen.** Adopted from §7.2 #9: thumbnails positioned by timestamp on the same x-scale, so uneven
  capture reads honestly as a gap. Delivery is **one sprite + a JSON index per window** (Mux's storyboard
  pattern, 256×160 tiles, 50 tiles under 15 min of span / 100 above), not N image requests. `Image.timestampEnd`
  already marks mp4 timelapses as spanning a range — those render as a bracket, not a point.

### 4.4 Mobile interaction

Adopted from §7.2 #10, verbatim as rules:

- one finger = page scroll; **two fingers = pan/zoom** (`dataZoom: {type:'inside'}`); tap or long-press =
  crosshair. No Ctrl-to-pan, no Shift-to-zoom — those are the current desktop-only bindings.
- **No floating tooltip.** A **pinned scrub header** sits above the panels: a compact table, one row per
  visible panel, showing the value at the cursor and the timestamp. A fingertip cannot occlude it.
- Range presets are the primary control, ≥ 44 px: `24 h · 7 T · Phase · Grow · Eigen`. `Phase` and `Grow`
  are new and are only offered when plants exist. Interval is **constrained by span** server-side (this also
  kills the `3y × 5s` self-DoS).
- **Axis labels are never hidden.** Below 320 px they shorten to 3 ticks and the unit moves into the panel
  title. An axis-less chart is not a chart.
- "Als Tabelle anzeigen" disclosure under the chart (no OSS library matches Highcharts' a11y module and
  Terp does not load that module today anyway).

### 4.5 The fusion: chart ⟷ thread

The differentiator, and the reason this chart belongs to *this* concept:

- **In the chart:** dragging the crosshair moves the film strip *and* scrolls a 3-entry journal peek under
  the lanes. Releasing on a cluster opens the thread at that entry.
- **In the thread:** tapping any entry expands an inline mini-chart of ±6 h around it — one panel, chosen by
  entry kind (water → RH; feed → EC; stage → VPD; anything else → temp). Copy: `Was das Zelt dazu gemacht hat`
  / `What the tent did around this`. This is what turns *"I fed on Tuesday"* into *"I fed on Tuesday and RH
  spiked to 71 % for four hours"* without the user ever opening a chart.

### 4.6 Day/night shading, timezone, colour

- Shading derived from **measured `out_light > 0`** where available, falling back to the `daynight` schedule,
  with `lights.sunrise`/`sunset` ramp gradients at the edges, rendered in an **explicit timezone** carried in
  the query string. A failed contactor then shows as a *missing band* — a diagnostic, not decoration.
  (§7.2 #4 adopted.) The DST note is real: a 12/12 flip near a DST boundary drifts an hour without this.
- **Okabe-Ito** palette (§7.2 #11 adopted): `#D55E00` temp, `#0072B2` humidity, `#009E73` VPD, `#CC79A7` CO₂,
  `#E69F00` light, `#56B4E9` EC, `#999999` grey. Today's `#e05a4e` next to `#b0743c` is a textbook
  deuteranopia collision; `Lights` and `Day` currently share `#c8a23c`; `out_fan` and `out_fan-internal`
  share `#2e9e8f`. All gone.
- Target bands get a **1 px stroke at ≥ 3:1** against the panel background rather than relying on a 12 %
  fill, which would fail WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11. `darkMode:'auto'` replaces the hand-maintained 30-property
  palette and the `MutationObserver` on `body.class`.
- Series names go through `TranslateService`. Today a German user reads `Temperature`, `Humidity`,
  `Critical logs` in English inside a German page.

## 5. The multi-plant diary

### 5.1 Why a new collection, not a new category

A per-plant diary **cannot** be layered onto `DeviceLog`. That collection has: no entry-type discriminator
(the split is a string prefix), a `deleted` flag that means "hide from the overview" (so *"Clear logs" deletes
nothing from the diary* — P0 #6), no actor, no server-side pagination, a `data` field that is
`Schema.Types.Mixed` with zero validation, and a runtime cycle-inference algorithm in the browser that **forks
your history if you rename your plant**. Every one of those is a bug this concept would inherit and then
multiply by one-tap logging.

So: `DeviceLog` stays exactly as it is and keeps doing what it is good at — device events. Human events move
to a new collection.

### 5.2 New entities

All cloud-side in MongoDB, keyed by `device_id`. Nothing goes into `device.configuration` — the firmware
strips unknown keys on echo. All new fields are optional with read-time normalisers; no migration tooling
exists in this repo and none is invented here.

```ts
// shared-types/index.d.ts additions

export interface Plant {
  plant_id: string;              // uuid
  device_id: string;             // index {device_id: 1, archivedAt: 1}
  owner_id: string;
  label: string;                 // "Blaubeere" — user-facing, FREELY RENAMEABLE
  strain?: string;               // "Amnesia Haze" — optional, separate from label
```

### 5.3 Creating a plant, and the "no plants" contract

`plantsEnabled` defaults **false**. Until it is true:

- there is no *Pflanzen* segment tab,
- no plant picker appears anywhere, in any sheet,
- every entry is written with `plant_ids: []`, which means *the tent*,
- the word "Pflanze" appears in exactly one place: a small `+ Pflanzen benennen` link at the bottom of ⋯.

That is the whole tax on a user who does not care: one link in a sheet they may never open.

Turning it on takes one tap on that link and then one field: `Wie heißt sie?` with a keyboard. The first plant
is created with `startedAt` = the device's first sample (or today, whichever is later), `stage` inherited from
the last stage event if the old diary had one, `colorKey: 0`. **Prior entries are not retro-assigned.** They
keep `plant_ids: []` and appear in every plant thread rendered in a muted style with a small `Zelt` pill —
they are context, not claims.

Adding a second plant is the moment sheets start showing a plant row, and even then it is **prefilled with
"alle"**, so the tap count does not change:

```
╭─ Gießen ──────────────────────────╮
│  Menge      1 L  [2 L]  3 L  5 L  │
│  Pflanzen   [alle]  Blaubeere  #2 │   ← "alle" preselected
│                    [ Passt ]      │
╰───────────────────────────────────╯
```

### 5.4 Rename, re-veg, clone, harvest

The current app derives grow cycles at runtime by scanning log rows and **cutting a new cycle whenever the
stage ordinal rolls back or the trimmed `lifecycleName` string differs**. Consequences: renaming forks your
history; a re-veg fabricates a plant; cycle identity in share-link URLs is a *start timestamp*, so editing an
entry's time breaks links in circulation. All four are fixed by having an entity:

- **Rename** = `PATCH /plants/:id {label}`. Nothing forks, no history moves, share links keep resolving
  because they carry `plant_id`, not a name and not a timestamp.
- **Re-veg** = a `PlantStageEvent` back to `vegetative` with `cycleNo + 1`. Same plant, same photos, same
  entries. The day counter since `startedAt` keeps running; the *phase* counter restarts; the chart's
  `Phase` range preset uses the newest `stageSince`. Copy: `Zurück in die Wachstumsphase (2. Zyklus)`.
- **Clone** = ⋯ → `Klon nehmen` on a plant → creates a **new** `Plant` with `parentPlantId`, `source:'clone'`,
  `startedAt: now`, `label` prefilled `"{{mother}} Klon 1"`. The mother's entries stay with the mother. The
  plant header shows `Klon von Blaubeere` with a tap-through.
- **Harvest** = a `stage: 'harvest'` event with a date. **Harvest is a stage** here; today it is not even in
  the enum (`flowering → drying`), which is why *"how did this grow do?"* is unanswerable. Weights are
  optional and off by default (§8.4). After `curing`, ⋯ → `Abschließen` sets `archivedAt`; the plant leaves
  pickers and the *Pflanzen* tab's active list, and moves to `Abgeschlossen`.

### 5.5 Migration off the old diary

A one-time, idempotent, boot-time backfill (the house pattern — service constructor, no migrate-mongo):

1. For each device, read `DeviceLog` rows with `categories[0] === 'diary'` **or** `categories` containing
   `diary-plant-lifecycle` / legacy `plant-lifecycle` (the server writes its own stage rows *without* the
   `'diary'` prefix, which is why they are invisible in today's Entries report — P0 #7).
2. Run `convertEventsToGrowCycles()` **once, server-side**, to mint `Plant` rows and `PlantStageEvent` rows.
   The algorithm becomes a migration instead of a runtime behaviour. Its 5 existing specs in
   `grow-report.component.spec.ts` become the migration's test suite — the only real tests in the repo, reused.
3. Map each diary row onto a `JournalEntry` (`diary-plant-log`→`note`, `diary-measurement`→`measure`,
   `diary-co2-refill`→`co2refill`, `diary-plant-lifecycle`→`stage`, `diary-fridge-log`→`note`), setting
   `migratedFromLogId`. Re-running skips anything already migrated.
4. `DeviceLog` diary rows are **not deleted**. `/diary` redirects; the old grow report is removed from the
   router but the data stays until a later cleanup release.

## 6. Watering & fertilising

There is no irrigation or dosing hardware. This is capture-and-recall, and the design says so out loud
instead of pretending a schedule is an automation.

### 6.1 The everyday flows, with tap counts

Definition of a tap: one discrete touch (a long-press counts as one; typing is counted separately and
avoided). OS-level taps inside the system camera are counted but marked, because they are not ours to remove.

| # | Action | Thumb Journal sequence | **Taps** | Today |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Photo of the tent** | `📷` → *(OS camera: shutter, Use Photo)* → bubble appears | **1** (+2 OS) | 6 (+2 OS): Diary → Add entry → Kategorie → Pflanzen-Log → 📷 → Speichern |
| 2 | **Watering, same as last time** | `💧` → posted with last volume + last plant set + now; toast `💧 Gegossen · 2,0 L  [Rückgängig]` | **1** | **impossible** — no watering category exists anywhere in the repo |
| 3 | **Watering, different amount** | long-press `💧` → Mengenring → tap `3 L` → posted | **2** | impossible |
| 4 | **Feed following the plan** | `🧪` → sheet prefilled from the regime step → `Passt` | **2** | impossible |
| 5 | **Voice note** | hold `🎙`, speak, release → posted with the transcript | **1** | 6 + typing |
| 6 | **Stage change → bloom** | the auto card `12/12 seit 3 Tagen — Blüte gestartet?` → `Ja` | **1** (or 2 manually: `⋯` → `Blüte`) | 6: Diary → Add → Kategorie → Phasenwechsel → Stage-Select → option → Speichern |

Two supporting numbers:

| Action | Sequence | Taps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Photo attributed to one plant (2 plants exist) | `📷` → *(OS)* → tap the `alle` chip on the fresh bubble → tap `Blaubeere` | 3 (+2 OS) |
| Full feed with EC + pH typed | `🧪` → `Mehr` → 2 number fields → `Passt` | 3 + typing |

**Why one tap is possible at all:** the write happens *before* the classification. There is no modal, no
required field, no validation gate, no save button on the fast path. `POST /journal` fires optimistically with
a `clientId`; the bubble renders instantly; a 6-second `ion-toast` with `Rückgängig` / `Undo` is the entire
error-recovery model. If the post fails it goes to `PendingCaptureQueue` and the bubble shows a clock.

**Duplicate protection**, because a stoned user will double-tap: two entries of the same `kind` with the same
payload within 10 minutes collapse into one bubble rendered `💧 Gegossen · 2,0 L  ×2` with a `Doch nur einmal`
chip that deletes the second. They stay two rows in the database; the collapse is presentational and
reversible. I would rather store the truth and render it kindly than debounce data away.

### 6.2 The Mengenring (amount ring)

Long-pressing 💧 opens a radial menu at the thumb, not a modal:

```
              ╭─────╮
       ╭─────╮│ 3 L │╭─────╮
       │ 2 L │╰─────╯│ 5 L │
       ╰─────╯  💧   ╰─────╯
       ╭─────╮       ╭─────╮
       │ 1 L │       │  …  │
       ╰─────╯       ╰─────╯
```

The four values are the user's own four most-used volumes (from `JournalEntry.water.liters`), seeded
`0,5 / 1 / 2 / 5 L`. `…` opens a number pad. Release-over-a-value posts without a second tap on devices where
the long-press gesture supports it; tap-to-select is the fallback for accessibility (and is what the tap count
above assumes). Same pattern is reused for the feed batch size.

### 6.3 The Feed sheet

```
╭─ Düngen ────────────────────── Schritt 6 / 11 ─╮
│  Bio-Grow          4 ml/L        [ − ] [ + ]   │
│  Bio-Heaven        2 ml/L        [ − ] [ + ]   │
│  Alg-A-Mic         1 ml/L        [ − ] [ + ]   │
│  ─────────────────────────────────────────────  │
│  Menge             [5 L]  ← Mengenring          │
│  Ziel-EC           1,4 mS/cm  (Leitungswasser   │
│                    + 0,4 = am Messgerät 1,8)    │
│  ▸ Mehr:  EC gemessen · pH · Ablauf-EC · Notiz  │
│  ─────────────────────────────────────────────  │
│              [ Passt ]      [ Übersprungen ]    │
╰─────────────────────────────────────────────────╯
```

The `[ − ] [ + ]` rows are the existing `value-edit-row` component (tap → expands into `[−] ion-range [+]`,
ARIA-annotated, step-aware, protected by the `rangeGuard` directive). Nothing new is invented for it.

Facts the sheet encodes, because getting them wrong burns crops:

- **EC basis.** CANNA and others publish **EC +**, not EC: *"add the EC of the tap water that is used to the
  recommended EC."* So `FeedRegimeStep.ecBasis` is `absolute | delta_over_source`, the user's source-water EC
  is asked once in `/feed`, and the sheet shows **both** numbers with the meter reading spelled out. An alarm
  band computed against a raw chart value would be actively wrong.
- **Never a bare ppm.** EC is stored canonically in mS/cm. Where the user's meter reads ppm, the scale is
  stored (`500 | 640 | 700`) and rendered as `1260 ppm₇₀₀`. At EC 1.8 a Bluelab reads 1260 and a Hanna reads
  900 for the same liquid; this is repeatedly cited as the #1 cause of accidental over/under-feeding.
- **Every prefilled value stays editable.** The grower who deviates is the normal case, not the error case.
- **`Übersprungen`** records a skip. A schedule you can only obey is a schedule that becomes a lie by week 3.

### 6.4 Refine-later, and the Tidy-up table

An entry posted in one tap may be missing things. It carries `refineNeeded: ['water.liters']` and renders a
small chip on the bubble:

```
╭──────────────────────────────╮
│ 💧 Gegossen                  │
│ [ Menge? ]  [ Welche? ]      │   ← tap = the ring / the plant picker, in place
╰──────────────────────────────╯
```

The chips are never modal, never block, and disappear when filled or after 7 days.

**`/device/:id/tidy` — Aufräumen / Tidy up** is the desktop counterpart: a dense table of every entry with
`refineNeeded` in the last 30 days, keyboard-navigable, one row per entry, tab-through number inputs, batch
plant assignment. This is where a club secretary or a techie reclaims the data quality that one-tap logging
costs, on a big screen, on a Sunday. It is the honest answer to "chips lose data": the loss is *deferred*,
not accepted, and there is a purpose-built place to recover it.

### 6.5 Following a schedule

`/device/:id/feed`:

```
Welches Düngeschema?         [ Biobizz Grow/Bloom ▾ ]     ← FeedRegime

Nächster Schritt   6 / 11 · Blüte Woche 2
```

Three behaviours that make this a *controller* feature rather than a calculator app:

1. **Advance by feed events, not by wall clock.** `Schritt 6 von 11`, incremented when a feed is logged.
   Growers feed Tuesday/Friday and slip; a calendar-driven plan is wrong by week 3.
2. **Auto-detect the 12/12 flip from `out_light` history** and use it to place the grower on the right bloom
   step — proposed in the thread, confirmed with one tap. No bottle brand's calculator can do this because
   none of them can see the room.
3. **Move the EC target band on the chart as the schedule advances**, instead of setting an alarm once.

**The All-Mix rule, which proves the model.** The selectable thing is a **medium + regime pair**, labelled the
way growers say it out loud. With `preCharged: 'heavy'` and `feedStartWeek: 4`, the 🧪 chip reads
`noch nicht nötig` for the first three weeks, and tapping it shows:

> **Noch nicht düngen** — All-Mix ist stark vorgedüngt. Ab Woche 4.  `[ Trotzdem düngen ]`
> *(EN: Don't feed yet — All-Mix is heavily pre-charged. From week 4. [Feed anyway])*

A schedule feature that cannot express "don't feed yet" is not modelling the domain.

### 6.6 Brand posture (invisible to the user, non-negotiable in code)

- Brand names are **plain-text labels only**. No logos, no brand colours, no bottle imagery, and never inside
  a Terp Control feature name (a "CANNA-Modus" would convert referential use into trademark use and forfeit
  the Art. 14(1)(c) EUTMR defence).
- Numbers live in a **MongoDB content collection**, never in the bundle; each row carries `source_url` +
  `retrieved_at` + `chart_version`, so any brand is removable in one DB update.
- **Never copy brand prose.** Every description is written in our words.
- Seeded from the brand's own chart or calculator, never from retailer or blog transcriptions — two sites
  disagree by ~3× on CANNA Terra Vega dosing, which is the difference between a fed plant and a burnt one.
- `Zuletzt geprüft` is shown in the UI. The legal hygiene doubles as a user-facing trust feature.
- Ownerless schedules (Lucas Formula, Jack's 321, generic per-medium EC ladders) ship as `kind: 'ownerless'`
  so the feature is useful on day one with nothing borrowed. They are the floor, not the headline.
- A takedown address and a documented notice-and-action policy exist **before** the first brand row ships.
- Every computed dose carries a `Werte prüfen` affordance and never presents itself as an instruction.

## 7. Camera & timelapse

### 7.1 How images relate to time, plants and stages

**By time, not by foreign key.** `Image` already carries `device_id` + `timestamp` (+ `timestampEnd` for mp4
spans). A plant's frames are *all device stills between `Plant.startedAt` and `archivedAt`*; a stage's frames
are the stills between two `PlantStageEvent`s. This is a deliberate decision: it means the existing 30-second
capture loop needs **no change at all**, it survives a plant being renamed or re-vegged, and it degrades
gracefully when several plants share one tent (they do — one camera, one tent).

The only linkage that *is* explicit is the one the user creates: `JournalEntry.images[]` for handheld photos
(`format: 'user/jpeg'`). Those are pinned to the entry and inherit its `plant_ids`.

Two small server changes, both cheap:
- **Deleting an entry deletes its user images.** `DELETE /image/:image_id` exists today and the webapp never
  calls it; retention filters `format:'jpeg'` only, so `user/jpeg` blobs accumulate forever. One-tap photo
  logging makes that a real cost curve, not a nit.
- **A size limit on `express-fileupload`**, which is mounted with none today, plus downscaling to ≤ 2048 px on
  the client before upload. Mongo `Buffer`s are capped by the 16 MB BSON limit with no graceful failure path,
  and the unique index `{device_id, format, timestamp, duration}` collides on two uploads in the same
  millisecond — the capture queue must offset timestamps by its position.

### 7.2 Where images appear

| Surface | Form |
| --- | --- |
| Journal day divider | a 32 px strip of 5 stills evenly sampled across that day; tap → scrubber |
| Journal bubble | user photos, 2-up, ≤ 160 px |
| Chart | the film-strip lane (sprite + JSON index, §4.3) |
| Plant thread header | a 56 px strip across the plant's whole life, with stage tick marks under it |
| Week card | the week's best 6 (largest file size as a crude sharpness proxy) + all user photos |
| Auto excursion card | **the still captured nearest the peak**, inline |

That last one is the concept's favourite detail: the machine-written entry *"2 h 20 zu warm · max 30,8 °C um
15:40"* carries the picture from 15:40. That is a diary entry no human would have written and no competitor
produces.

### 7.3 Timelapses

Today: 1d / 1w / 1m mp4s built hourly, with **no relation to a cycle, phase or entry**. Added:

- **Phasen-Timelapse** — stills between two stage events, generated on demand (a parameterised reuse of the
  existing ffmpeg job in `image.service.ts`), cached as an `Image` with `format:'mp4'` and
  `timestamp`/`timestampEnd` spanning the phase.
- **Grow-Timelapse** — `startedAt` → `harvest`. The single most shareable artefact the product can make.
- Both are lit by whatever the light output was doing, so they read as a day/night rhythm — which the chart's
  day/night shading then explains.
- Sharing a timelapse produces a link, not an upload to anyone's platform. `⋯ → Teilen` on the video offers
  `Link kopieren · An Telegram · An Discord · Datei speichern`. Never Instagram, Threads, TikTok or Reddit —
  a failed post there costs the *user's* account with no appeal, and Terp Control would be the proximate
  cause. No branded footer is appended to anything (§ 6 KCanG: advertising and sponsoring for cannabis are
  prohibited, fines to €30,000, and an auto-appended "Powered by Terp Control" on thousands of German grow
  posts is the single riskiest string in the feature).

## 8. Multi-user / clubs

### 8.1 Roles

One new collection. `Device.owner_id` stays as the billing/claim owner and is never repurposed.

```ts
export interface Membership {
  device_id: string;             // index {device_id: 1, user_id: 1} unique
  user_id: string;
  role: 'owner' | 'grower' | 'member' | 'viewer';
  displayName: string;           // shown in the thread; NOT a legal name
  createdAt: number;
  invitedBy: string;
  lastSeenAt?: number;

```

| Role | Journal | Charts | Settings | Members | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **owner** | read/write | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | can transfer, delete, wipe |
| **grower** | read/write | ✓ | ✓ | read | the second person in a household |
| **member** | **write own, read all** | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ | the `:self` tier: logs their own work, sees the room, changes nothing |
| **viewer** | read | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ | can be name-blind |

`member` is copied from Cannanas' `charges:create:self` / `member:journals:self-checkin` pattern and is the
tier the club case actually needs. Implementation reality, stated plainly: this rewrites
`auth.middleware.ts:172` and `:207` from `find({owner_id, device_id})` to a membership lookup, and loosens
~10 owner-scoped service queries. **It cannot be a flag.**

**Invitations are codes, not emails.** ⋮ → *Mitglied einladen* mints a 6-character code with a 24 h TTL,
shaped like the existing claim-code flow. This avoids inventing an account-lookup-by-email endpoint (which
does not exist and would have to be designed against enumeration abuse) and avoids storing a second person's
email against a device.

### 8.2 Attribution in the thread

Every `JournalEntry` has `createdBy`. How it renders is a per-device decision, not a global one:

- **`privacyMode: 'private'` (default).** Own entries read `du`; everyone else's read nothing at all — the
  bubble simply has no byline. Two people sharing a tent see one shared thread with no ledger of who did what.
- **`privacyMode: 'club'`.** Bubbles carry a 20 px initial avatar + `displayName`. The plant header gains
  `Zuletzt gegossen von Mira, vor 2 Tagen`. `/members` gains **Meine Einsätze** — a per-member count and a
  date-range list of their own entries — which is the one legally-motivated multi-user feature worth owning
  (§ 17 Abs. 2 KCanG requires members to participate *eigenhändig*).

Switching to club mode is an explicit, two-tap, explained action:

> **Namen im Journal zeigen?** Im Vereinsmodus steht an jedem Eintrag, wer ihn gemacht hat. Das hilft eurem
> Verein zu belegen, wer mitgearbeitet hat — und es ist eine Aufzeichnung darüber, wer was getan hat.
> `[ Abbrechen ] [ Vereinsmodus einschalten ]`

EN: *"Show names in the journal? In club mode every entry says who made it. That helps your club show who
took part — and it is a record of who did what."* The second half of that sentence is not a warning we bury.

### 8.3 Editing history

Club mode switches entry edits from in-place mutation to **append-only corrections**: editing an entry writes
a new entry of `kind: 'note'` with `correctsEntryId`, and the original renders struck-through with
`korrigiert`. Private mode edits in place. Cryptographic immutability is **not** a default — tamper-evidence
protects a regulated club against its own staff and victimises an unregulated grower.

### 8.4 Privacy posture

Defaults are privacy-maximal, because harvest weights and plant counts are the exact facts separating a lawful
hobby from an offence in many jurisdictions, and § 28 Abs. 2 KCanG lets German authorities seize digital data
with **no prior judicial approval**.

- `harvestWeightsEnabled: false`. The harvest sheet asks for a **date** and offers `Gewicht erfassen?` as a
  link. Turning it on shows: *"Erntegewichte sind in vielen Ländern genau die Angabe, die dich angreifbar
  macht. Du kannst sie jederzeit löschen."*
- No plant *count* is ever displayed as a headline number, exported by default, or included in a share link.
- **Nothing is public unless a share link is minted.** No feed, no discovery, no "growers near you", no
  location, no IP geolocation, no analytics or ad SDK.
- **Every export carries `NONE | PSEUDONYMIZED | ANONYMIZED` and defaults to pseudonymised** (initials).
- ⋮ → *Alles löschen* wipes the journal, plants and images for a device in one confirmed action, and the
  confirmation is a typed device name, not a checkbox.
- Retention gets an actual answer: raw telemetry 90 days → 5-minute downsample 18 months (today: **infinite**,
  raw 5-second samples, no Influx retention and zero downsample tasks). Journal text is kept. Camera stills
  3 years, unchanged. All configurable down, never up, per device.
- **Refused permanently:** per-member consumption or dispensing records, member DOB/address/membership
  numbers, per-member grams and THC content, payments, any real-name requirement, and becoming the club's
  § 26 system of record. That market is served by ~20 SaaS products at €0.50–1.00/member/month, and winning
  it would mean owning 23 differing authority export formats. The right relationship with Cannanas / 420cloud
  is as the **device-attested environmental layer they consume** — a value the controller recorded with its
  own clock beats a hand-typed number in an inspection, and it stores zero personal data.
- **Never a `KCanG-konform` badge.** Not a claim this product can make.

## 9. Old devices (type 7) — fan, light, plug, fridge

These users are not the focus group and must not be broken. The design's answer is structural rather than
generous: **the shell is device-agnostic, and their old screens move into it untouched.**

### 9.1 Exactly what a fan owner sees

Header segment: `Journal | Jetzt` — two tabs. No *Kurve* tab? No: *Kurve* is there too (they have temperature
and humidity series, and the new chart is strictly better for them). *Pflanzen* is hidden (`plantsEnabled`
false). So: `Journal | Jetzt | Kurve`.

- **Jetzt** renders `<fan-overview>` — the *existing component, unmodified*, inside the new shell. Same
  gauges, same values, same `value-display` SVG. The `⋮` menu has `Einstellungen`, which routes to the
  existing `/settings` page and its existing `<fan-settings>` (199 lines of template, untouched). Zero
  rewrite, zero regression surface.
- **Journal** works because the thread's machine half is fed by `DeviceLog` + telemetry, which every device
  type has. A fan owner's thread contains: boot messages, alarms, config changes, the daily verdict card
  (time-in-range against *their* limits), excursions, and offline periods. That is a genuine upgrade over
  today's modal log viewer with a "Clear log" button that deletes nothing from the diary.
- **Thumb Bar** = `📷 🎙 ⋯`. Three slots. No 💧, no 🧪, because a fan is not a grow controller. If the fan is
  in fact in a grow tent — which it usually is — `⋯ → Leiste anpassen` pins them in two taps.
- **What is removed:** nothing. **What changes:** the button grid on the old overview card
  (Charts/Settings/Testmode/Maintenance/Diary/Setup) becomes the segment + `⋮`. That is the only thing a type-7
  user has to relearn, and it is the same six destinations.

### 9.2 Per type

| `device_type` | Jetzt renders | Segment | Thumb Bar | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `fan` | `<fan-overview>` unchanged | Journal · Jetzt · Kurve | 📷 🎙 ⋯ | |
| `light` | `<light-overview>` unchanged | Journal · Jetzt · Kurve | 📷 🎙 ⋯ | DLI bar added to the chart's light panel — the one real gain |
| `plug` | `<plug-overview>` unchanged | Journal · Jetzt · Kurve | 📷 🎙 ⋯ | its six broken i18n keys (`settings.limits.*.enabled`) get fixed in the same pass |

| `dryer` | legacy card, no shell | — | — | AGENTS.md says the type is dead; it is left exactly where it is |

### 9.3 The fridge/controller split

`overview.component.html:40` hard-codes `{{'devices.fridge.title'|translate}}` → **"Terp Control FRIDGE
GROW"**, and an unnamed controller is *named* that string. There is no `devices.controller` i18n block in
either language file, and the workmode labels are the fridge's ("Small Plants", "Greenhouse", "Drying",
"Germination", "Big Plants").

Thumb Journal forks `devices/fridge/overview` into `devices/controller/overview` (the two `ngSwitchCase`
lines in `list.page.html` and `settings.page.html` are the entire integration surface), adds
`devices.controller.*` to `en.json` and `de.json` **in the same commit**, and renames the workmodes into
tent language. The fridge keeps its own components and its own name, forever. This is the cheapest
credibility fix in the whole document and it is a prerequisite for shipping a €289 controller in October.

## 10. Onboarding

Three cards. Every one skippable. Nothing is asked that the device can answer for itself.

**Card 0 — claim.** Unchanged (`.tc-hero` + claim code on `/list`).

**Card 1 — name.** `Wie soll das Zelt heißen?` prefilled `Zelt 1`. `[ Passt ]` = 1 tap, or type.
The name is `Device.name`, an existing field with an existing endpoint.

**Card 2 — what is connected.** *Read from `hardwareInfo.sockets`, not asked.* Each paired Tasmota socket is
shown as a chip with its detected role, and the user confirms or re-assigns:

```
Terp hat gefunden:
  [ Steckdose 1 · Heizung ✓ ]  [ Steckdose 2 · Licht ✓ ]
  [ + Ich habe noch mehr angeschlossen ]
  [ Ich messe nur ]
```

Each role chip carries a 4-second silent loop from `assets/wizard/sockettype_*.mp4` — **~30 MB of already-shot
videos ship in every production build today and are referenced nowhere**. Free.

**The capability rule changes here.** `deviceControlCapability()` currently returns `'full'` when *any* of
dehumidifier/heater/co2 is paired, and **fails open to `'full'` when the key is missing** (old firmware).
Thumb Journal replaces the three-bucket model with the per-role truth that is already in the CSV, and **fails
closed to monitor**: an unknown capability shows *"Nur Messen"* with a `Ich regle doch etwas` escape. A
heater-only tent stops being shown humidity targets, a humidity-deviation warning and a dehumidifier alarm
preset it can never act on.

**Card 3 — what grows in what.** One question, five chips, and it configures three subsystems at once:

```
Was wächst da drin?
  [ Erde, leicht vorgedüngt ]   → mediumKey light-mix,  Feed ab Woche 2, 🧪 sichtbar
  [ Erde, stark vorgedüngt ]    → mediumKey all-mix,    Feed ab Woche 4, 🧪 sagt "noch nicht"
  [ Coco ]                      → Feed ab Tag 1
  [ Hydro ]                     → Feed ab Tag 1, EC-Panel sofort sichtbar
  [ Nur messen ]                → 🧪 und 💧 aus der Leiste, keine Pflanzen, keine Erinnerungen
```

**Not asked:** the growth stage. It is *proposed* from the photoperiod the device is already running
(*"Sieht nach Blüte aus — 12/12 seit 3 Tagen. Stimmt das? [Ja] [Nein]"*), which is both fewer questions and a
better answer than a stoned user's guess. Also not asked: plant names, strain, pot size, feed brand, targets.
Targets come from `GROW_STAGE_PRESETS` once the stage is known; the brand regime is offered the first time
🧪 is tapped, with `Kein Schema, ich mach das selbst` as the first option.

Total: **2–4 taps from claim to a working device**, versus today's `name → connections → stage → plan → done`
wizard, which is barely discoverable (there is no "re-run setup" anywhere in the menu — Thumb Journal puts it
in `⋮ → Einrichtung wiederholen`).

Then the thread's first entry is a machine card:

> **Tipp** — Halte 💧 gedrückt, wenn die Menge mal anders ist.  `[ Verstanden ]`

That is the whole tutorial. Everything else is learned by tapping.

## 11. Per-user-type walkthrough — the first week

### Type 1 — The lazy, monitoring-only user

**Day 1.** Claims. Two chips: name accepted, "Ich messe nur". No plants, no feed, no reminders. The Thumb Bar
shows `📷 🎙 ⋯` — three buttons he never has to press.
**Day 2, 07:00.** The thread has written itself: `📈 Gestern · 22 h 10 im Ziel (92 %) · min 19,4 · max 27,1`.
He reads one line and closes the app.
**Day 4.** A push: `Zelt 1 · 3 h zu warm (max 31,4 °C)`. He opens it, the card carries the camera still from
the peak, taps `Kurve ansehen`, sees the temperature panel with the dashed setpoint and a red deviation fill
for three hours in the afternoon. Closes it.
**Day 7.** The Sunday week card appears in the thread with the week's aggregates and six photos. He taps
`Teilen` once and pastes a link into the Telegram group.
**Verdict: best served type.** He never types. The app writes his diary and he consumes it. This is the type
the philosophy was written for even though he does not log anything — because "capture speed" for him means
*the machine's* capture speed.

### Type 2 — The same user, with a camera

Everything above, plus: the day divider strip gives him five frames a day without navigating, and the
excursion card carries the picture. On day 7 the week card offers `Wochen-Timelapse` and `Grow-Timelapse
(seit Tag 1)`. He shares the timelapse to Telegram with two taps.
**The one thing he wants that v1 does not do well:** correlate a *specific* frame with a *specific* value
without opening the chart. Mitigation: the film-strip scrubber shows the four Now-strip values under the frame
as you drag. That is a genuine, small, buildable answer and it lands in v1.
**Verdict: best served type, jointly with type 1.**

### Type 3 — Controls certain aspects (CO₂ or heating)

**Day 1.** Onboarding finds one heater socket. Capability = `{heater}`, not `full`. The humidity panel's
target band renders greyed with `nicht regelbar`; no dehumidifier tile, no dehumidifier alarm preset, no
humidity-deviation nag. This alone is the fix he has been waiting for.
**Day 3.** He taps *Jetzt* → temperature target row (`value-edit-row`, unchanged) → saves. The change appears
in the thread as a machine entry: `⚙ Ziel Tag 25,0 → 26,0 °C`, with a `Kurve` link.
**Day 5.** The daily card reports 96 % in range for temperature and says nothing about humidity, because
nothing about humidity is his to control.
**Verdict: well served on the honesty axis, under-served on depth.** He wants a settings screen with more
precision than a journal offers; he gets today's settings screen, which is unchanged. That is a non-answer,
and §15 owns it.

### Type 4 — The entire tent

**Day 1.** Four sockets found: heater, dehumidifier, light, CO₂. Capability = full. Stage proposed from the
photoperiod: *vegetative*.
**Day 2.** He waters. One tap. Then he realises he wants to record that he gave 4 L, long-presses, taps `4 L`.
Two taps for the corrected habit; from then on one.
**Day 3.** Feeds. `🧪` → the sheet is prefilled from the regime he picked when he first tapped it → `Passt`.
**Day 5.** Tops two plants. `⋯ → Training → Topping`, then taps the `alle` chip and selects two plants. Four
taps, and it lands on the chart's journal rail as a marker he will see against the VPD panel next week.
**Day 6.** The chart on his laptop: five panels, the outputs lane showing the dehumidifier cycling every 40
minutes at night, and the water lollipops. He learns that his RH spikes for four hours after every watering.
**Verdict: the type that gets the most out of the fusion.** But he is also the type most likely to want
control depth the concept subordinates — his settings live behind `⋮`, two levels from home. Real cost.

### Type 5 — Closed-loop DIY (fridge builder)

**Day 1.** Fridge device type, so plants are available and the fridge components are unchanged.
**Day 2.** He wants his own DS18B20 in the water reservoir. He cannot have it: `VALID_SENSORS` is a hard-coded
allowlist, `device.configuration` cannot carry custom keys, and there is no public write API for outputs. What
he *can* do is `POST /journal/:device_id` from his own script with a `measure` payload — his EC probe, his
soil moisture, his reservoir temperature — and those land on the chart as points on the EC/pH panel and as
entries in the thread. Not a series; points on a timeline.
**Day 4.** He wires a webhook: entry-created → his n8n. And an inbound key so his Home Assistant automation
logs "pump ran 40 s" into the thread.
**Verdict: worst served type, and the concept says so.** He gets a write API for *events*, not for *sensors*
or *outputs*. His actual ask — a read/write API with custom measures — is firmware and allowlist work, and it
is not in v1. §15.

### Type 6 — Cannabis grow club, shared controller

**Day 1.** The owner claims, sets club mode, and mints three invite codes. Three members join with a code —
no email of theirs is stored anywhere.
**Day 2.** Mira waters. One tap. The bubble reads `💧 Gegossen · 2,0 L · Mira`.
**Day 3.** Jonas feeds. Two taps. `🧪 Gedüngt · Schritt 6/11 · Bio-Grow 4 ml/L · 5 L · Jonas`. He adds EC
because the club asked for it: three taps + typing.
**Day 4.** Someone waters twice by accident. The duplicate collapses to `×2` with `Doch nur einmal`.
**Day 6.** The owner opens `/tidy` on a laptop, fills in four missing volumes and assigns eight photos to
plants in about ninety seconds.
**Day 7.** `/week/2026-W34` → `Exportieren` → CSV/JSON with `PSEUDONYMIZED` preselected. Who did what, to
which plants, how much, at what EC — reconstructable months later. **Not** exported: any grams-per-person,
any real name, any THC figure. Terp Control is not their § 26 system of record and says so in the export
header.
**Verdict: well served on capture and attribution, thin on reporting.** "Detailed logging and reporting" is
half-delivered: the logging is excellent, the reporting is a week card and a CSV. §15.

### Type 7 — Old device owners (air, light, socket)

**Day 1.** A `light` owner opens the app and the device card is gone; there is a shell with `Journal · Jetzt ·
Kurve`. *Jetzt* is pixel-identical to what he had. He is briefly annoyed that Settings moved into `⋮`.
**Day 2.** The thread has a daily card with time-in-range against his own limits — something he never had.
**Day 4.** His lamp's contactor sticks. The chart's day/night shading, derived from measured `out_light`,
shows a **missing band**, and the thread carries `⚠ Licht sollte um 06:00 an sein — kein Licht gemessen`.
That is a failure his old app made invisible.
**Day 7.** He takes a photo of the plants under the lamp. One tap. He now has a journal he did not ask for and
did not have to learn.
**Verdict: no regression, one relearn (Settings moved), two real gains.** Acceptable for a non-focus group.

## 12. Stoner / normal / techie

Not three modes. **Three depths of the same object**, and the depth you stop at is your business.

```
   CHIP           →           SHEET          →          DETAIL
   1 tap                      2 taps                    3 taps + typing
   💧                         Menge · Pflanzen           + Ablauf-EC · Notiz · Zeit ändern
   🧪                         Produkte · Menge · Ziel-EC + EC gemessen · pH · Charge · Schritt überspringen
   ⋯ → Phase                  Phase wählen               + Datum rückdatieren · Zyklus
```

Every capture starts at chip depth and *offers* the next depth without requiring it. Nothing is gated,
nothing is hidden behind an "expert" toggle that must be discovered.

### 12.1 The stoner

- One tap writes. No form, no validation, no save button, no dialog.
- The undo toast is 6 seconds and 44 px tall.
- The machine writes the boring half. He never needs to remember to journal — he needs to remember to *water*,
  and the app tells him that too, once, as a dot on an icon.
- Nothing he can tap destroys anything: deletes are soft (`deletedAt`), edits in private mode are reversible
  for 24 h, and the only irreversible action in the app (wipe) requires typing the device name.
- He never sees a table of ml/L. The feed sheet's whole content, in his depth, is `[ Passt ]`.
- German copy is short, informal `du`, no jargon: `Gegossen`, `Gedüngt`, `Passt`, `Rückgängig`,
  `Noch nicht nötig`.

### 12.2 The normal user

- Sheets. Two taps for anything, prefilled with either the schedule or last time.
- The Now strip and the daily card answer *"is it fine?"* without a chart.
- The week card is the artefact she shares; the plant thread is the record she scrolls at harvest.
- Charts default to three panels and she adds a fourth when she has a question.

### 12.3 The techie

Depth, not a separate app:

- `journalDepth: 'full'` (an evolution of today's `localStorage['app-settings-expert']`, which is the codebase's
  only progressive-disclosure precedent) changes **defaults only**: sheets open expanded, EC/pH fields are
  always visible, the chart opens with 5 panels and the outputs lane, and bubbles show `⌄ Rohdaten`.
- Long-press any entry → `Rohdaten` → the raw `JournalEntry` JSON, copyable.
- `/tidy` — the dense keyboard table.
- `GET /journal/:device_id?since=` + `POST /journal/:device_id` with an `x-api-key` scoped per device
  (Pulse Grow's shape, which is read-only; Terp owns the hardware and can offer writes — a structural
  advantage no monitoring vendor has). Documented with OpenAPI. **This also makes Terp the first product in
  the category to publish a clean JSON grow-diary schema**, which costs one documentation page and no
  permission from anyone.
- Outbound: a generic webhook on `entry.created` / `stage.changed` / `excursion` — which transitively unlocks
  n8n, Make, Home Assistant, ntfy, Matrix, and moves the whole platform-policy problem to the user's side of
  the trust boundary. Plus Discord webhooks and Telegram bots, both of which work today with the existing
  `webhookTemplate.ts` `{{placeholder}}` machinery (it already escapes for JSON and URL modes). Missing is
  only a non-alarm trigger and image attachment. The existing sharp edges — raw `http.request()` with no
  timeout, no retry, no queue, no SSRF guard — must be fixed when it stops being alarm-only.
- CSV/JSON export of anything he can see.

### 12.4 Desktop — the unexploited advantage

`≥ 992 px`, three columns:

```
┌──────────┬──────────────────────────────┬──────────────────┐
│ Rail     │  Kurve (5 Panels + Lanes)    │  Journal-Thread  │
│ 📷 💧 🧪  │                              │  (scrollt mit    │
│ 🎙 ⋯     │  ← Crosshair                 │   dem Crosshair) │
│          │                              │                  │
│ Zelt 1   ├──────────────────────────────┤   ← Eintrag      │
│ Zelt 2   │  Filmstreifen                │     anklicken =  │
│          │                              │     Chart springt│
└──────────┴──────────────────────────────┴──────────────────┘
```

The crosshair and the thread are bound in both directions. AC Infinity, TrolMaster, Mars Hydro, Spider Farmer
and VIVOSUN are phone-only; Angular/Ionic gives this for free. This is where the techie and the club secretary
live, and it is the single best answer to "a chat thread is not an analysis tool".

## 14. Non-goals

1. **No two-way sync with any grow-diary platform.** GrowDiaries has no public API and is Cloudflare-hardened;
   Grow with Jane's ToS forbids automation and its backend is Firebase; SeedFinder's API has been dead since
   July 2024. The feature is called `Woche exportieren` and `Diary teilen`, never "sync", because "sync"
   generates support tickets it can never satisfy.
2. **No community feed.** ~200 App Store reviews across TrolMaster and VIVOSUN were read and **not one praised
   the feed**. It is permanent moderation cost and, with real identities, a target list. A link you can paste
   anywhere is not a feed you must moderate.
3. **No Instagram, Threads, TikTok or Reddit auto-posting, ever.** The platforms with strict cannabis policies
   are exactly the ones with an app-review gate; the review *is* the enforcement. A failed post there costs
   the user's account with no appeal.
4. **No "Powered by Terp Control" footer** on user posts. § 6 KCanG, €30,000, read broadly.
5. **No watering or dosing automation.** There is no irrigation hardware. Pretending otherwise with a
   "schedule" that only reminds is the kind of dishonesty that kills trust in the rest of the app.
6. **No compliance system of record.** No per-member dispensing, no grams ledger, no authority export formats,
   no `KCanG-konform` badge. That market has ~20 products in it and winning it means owning 23 export formats
   and liability for wrong ones.
7. **No adaptive/learned UI.** The Thumb Bar never rearranges itself. Muscle memory is the feature.
8. **No gamification.** No streaks, no badges, no "you haven't logged in 3 days". One dot on one icon is the
   entire nagging budget.
9. **No forms on the fast path.** If a capture needs a required field, the design is wrong; make the field
   optional and add a refine chip.
10. **No analytics or ad SDKs. No location, ever.** Not GPS, not IP-geolocation, not a persisted
    timezone-from-IP.
11. **No recipe-engine rewrite in v1.** It stays one-per-device, whole-config, hourly-push, and
    `waitForConfirmation` still halts the plan. Fixing it is real work and it is not this concept's work.
12. **No Highcharts.** Even with seats bought, its source cannot be redistributed under an OSS licence, and
    the cloud is marketed as *quelloffen*.

## 15. Honest weaknesses

**1. The chat metaphor fights analysis, and four of the seven types open the app to analyse.**
Types 1, 3, 4 and 5 mostly want to know a number. In Thumb Journal the numbers are a 72 px strip and
everything else is prose bubbles. The Now strip and the *Jetzt* tab are a real answer, but they are a demotion
of what used to be the entire home screen. If the owner's actual belief is that people open this app to check
their tent rather than to record their work, this concept is optimising the wrong verb and a critic is right
to say so.

**2. One-tap logging degrades data quality, by construction.**
An entry with no volume, no plant and no EC is worse than a form's entry. The mitigations — refine chips,
`/tidy`, "same as last time" — recover some of it, but `/tidy` is a desktop screen shipped in v1.1, and the
stoner this concept is designed for will never open it. **Net effect: more entries, each thinner.** Whether
that is a better diary is an empirical question this document cannot settle, and the club case (§8) is where
thin entries hurt most.

**3. "Same as last time" ossifies mistakes.**
Log 5 L once when you meant 0.5 L and 5 L becomes the default forever, silently, on every subsequent one-tap
watering. The Mengenring's four values are drawn from history, so a bad value is self-reinforcing. Partial
mitigation: the ring shows the *median* first and flags a value that deviates > 3× from the median with a
subtle marker. Not solved.

**4. Accidental writes are guaranteed.**
A 56 px bar of five buttons at the bottom of a phone held in a dark tent will be pressed by accident. Undo is
6 seconds; duplicate collapse handles double-taps; nothing handles a pocket tap noticed three days later.
Every competitor's forms prevent this by being slow. That is the trade, made deliberately.

**5. Voice is a privacy contradiction.**
The Web Speech API on Chrome and Safari sends audio to Google's and Apple's servers. This is a cannabis
product whose §8 posture is privacy-maximal and EU-hosted. Shipping dictation means either a plain-language
opt-in that admits the audio leaves the EU, or on-device recognition that does not exist portably in a browser,
or storing an audio clip (a new `Image.format` enum value and Mongo blob growth) with no transcription. That
is why voice is v1.1 and not v1, and why the philosophy's "voice input is first-class" is the one part of the
brief this concept does not deliver on time. Calling that out is better than shipping it thoughtlessly.

**6. The club's reporting need is under-served.**
The owner said big customers "need very detailed logging and reporting". Thumb Journal nails the logging and
delivers reporting as a week card plus a CSV. There is no per-member report, no cross-grow comparison, no
"show me every feed of Bio-Grow this season" query, no printable PDF. A club secretary will find this thin,
and the multi-user machinery it depends on is deferred to v1.1 anyway.

**7. Type 5 gets almost nothing.**
A write API for *events* is not a read/write API for *sensors and outputs*. His custom probe cannot become a
series; his pump cannot be driven; `VALID_SENSORS` and `device.configuration` are both closed. This is a
platform limit, not a design choice, but a concept that ranks types honestly must record that type 5 is the
loser here.

**8. Navigation moved to the top.**
Giving the thumb zone to capture means the four tabs live in a header segment, which is the hardest place to
reach one-handed on a 6.7" phone. I believe navigation frequency is an order of magnitude below capture
frequency, but I have no data and neither does anyone else. If I am wrong, the concept is worse than a
conventional tab bar for everyone.

**9. Row explosion is a real engineering cost.**
One-tap logging plus daily machine cards plus excursions means a 12-week grow can produce 800–1,500
`JournalEntry` rows where today's diary produces 20. Pagination, the annotation rail's clustering, the film
strip's sprite index and the week aggregation all have to work at that scale on a mid-range Android. None of
it is hard; all of it is work, and none of it exists today (there is no server-side log pagination at all).

**10. The migration is the riskiest ship in v1.**
Moving diary rows out of `DeviceLog` into a new collection, minting plants from a browser algorithm that
forks on rename, while old share links keep resolving, in a repo with **38 `it()` blocks, 24 of them a lone
`should create`, a red suite, and CI that never runs `ng test`** — that is a data-integrity project wearing a
UI project's clothes. The backfill's idempotency (`migratedFromLogId`) and reusing the 5 existing
`convertEventsToGrowCycles` specs are mitigations, not insurance.

**11. "Works in the tent with bad wifi" is only half true in v1.**
`localStorage` + `clientId` protects a queued capture and survives a reload. It does not survive a cold start
with no network, because there is no service worker. The concept's own premise — a distracted user in a dark
tent — is exactly the situation where connectivity is worst, and the full answer is deferred.

**12. The daily machine card can become wallpaper.**
A card that appears every morning saying "92 % in range" is read for a week and then scrolled past forever.
The excursion cards carry the real information, and they are rarer. If the daily card is not genuinely
different day to day, it is noise inside the very thread the concept says is the product. The mitigation —
suppressing the card on days that look like the previous day — is easy to describe and easy to get wrong.
