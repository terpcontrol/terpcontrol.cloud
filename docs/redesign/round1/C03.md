# C03 — "Beet" (The Bed)

**Philosophy:** Plant First. Plants are the primary objects; the device is infrastructure.
**Stance:** Section 15 attacks this document before anyone else gets to. Every claim about today's code
carries the dossier's `[CODE]` tag or was re-read from `/home/user/terpcontrol.cloud` while writing.
**Calendar reality:** today is **2026-08-24**. "October 2026" is **six to eight weeks away.** Section 13 is
written against that, not against a comfortable year.

---

## 1. Thesis

**Terp Control opens on what you are growing, not on what you bought — and because a plant exists as a real
object with a stage, the same air in the same tent gets graded differently for a seedling than for a plant in
week 8, which is the one thing no device-first grow app can say.**

Today the product's root object is a `Device` whose dashboard says *"Terp Control FRIDGE GROW"*
[CODE `overview.component.html:40`], and a grow's entire identity is a free-text string
`DiaryEntryData.lifecycleName`, default literal `'My Strain'`, living on one log row — with cycle boundaries
**reconstructed in the browser on every page load** by comparing that string
[CODE `grow-report.component.ts:691-747`]. Rename your plant and your history forks. Re-veg and the app
fabricates a second plant.

C03 makes the plant a persisted entity with a stable id, and then spends the rest of the design making sure
that costs a user who does not care about plants **exactly zero taps**. The device does not disappear; it
becomes the *context strip* — a thin, deliberately recessive band of climate behind the plant cards. Two
plants at different stages in one tent is the native case: two cards, two stage bands on the chart, two
time-in-range verdicts computed from one set of samples. When those two plants want incompatible climates
— which the hardware can never deliver, because there is one setpoint pair and one PWM output — the app says
so in plain German instead of pretending. That honesty is the feature.

### 3.2 What each element is, precisely

**Tent strip.** Device-scoped, identical on every card below it — because it *is* the same air. Reads, in
order: temperature, humidity, VPD, CO₂ (only if `deviceHasCo2()`), light state (from measured `out_light`,
not from the configured schedule), and a fault chip if any alarm is triggered. Tapping it goes to
`/devices/<id>` — i.e. down into infrastructure. With more than one plant-capable device, the strip becomes
a horizontally swipeable segment per device and the cards below filter to it.

**Plant card, top half (the stoner half).** Photo (88×88, the auto-picked stage cover — §7), name, one
status line, one progress bar. Nothing here needs reading comprehension beyond a day number.

**Plant card, bottom half (the normal half).** The verdict line, the last event, the next due thing, four
fat capture buttons.

**The verdict** is time-in-range over the last 24 h, computed **server-side on raw Influx samples**, never on
`aggregateWindow(fn:mean)` output — averaging destroys exactly the excursions the number counts, and the
error is invisible by eye [dossier §7.2.3]. It always names its band source, in priority order and one at a
time:
1. the user's own alarm thresholds → *"gegenüber deinen Alarmgrenzen"*
2. the device setpoint ± tolerance → *"gegenüber deinen Zielwerten"*
3. the plant's stage preset from `GROW_STAGE_PRESETS` → *"gegenüber Richtwert Blüte"*

Naming the source is not decoration. Grading a user against a band they never chose is how you generate
*"why does the app say I'm failing"* tickets, and (3) is advisory data we picked for them.

**Lead plant (`Leitpflanze`).** Exactly one plant per device carries a ★. The device's setpoints, the running
recipe, and the alarm thresholds belong to it. Other cards say *"Klima folgt Gorilla Glue #4"*. This is not
an elegant abstraction, it is an honest one: there is one PWM output and one `day`/`night` setpoint pair in
`device.configuration`, and the recipe engine is **one per device** whose `RecipeStep.settings` is applied as
the *whole* configuration [CODE `device.service.ts:396-550`]. Pretending otherwise would be the concept's
first lie.

### 3.4 Stale / offline / absent — the three states nothing distinguishes today

This is the **#1 complaint across all five consumer brands** in the competitor research and it is nearly free
to fix. Every number in C03 carries an age.

| State | Rule | Tent strip | Plant card | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Live** | newest sample < 2 min | full-opacity values, filled dot `●`, `Live` | normal | normal |
| **Stale** | 2 min ≤ age < 10 min | values at 60 % opacity, half dot `◐`, `Stand: vor 4 Min` | unchanged | unchanged |
| **Offline** | age ≥ 10 min (matches the documented device rule) | values at 40 % opacity struck through with a hairline, hollow dot `○`, `Gerät offline seit 3 h 20` on an amber `--tc-text-warning` band | photo desaturated 40 %, capture buttons **stay enabled** — you can still log a watering to an offline plant | `Zu wenig Daten für ein Urteil` if coverage < 80 % |
| **No samples yet** | device claimed, zero samples | `— °C  — %  — kPa` + `Warte auf die ersten Messwerte` | day counter shows `Tag 1`, progress bar empty | hidden entirely (not "0 %") |
| **No device** | zero devices claimed | — | the existing `.tc-hero` onboarding block is reused verbatim | — |

The "capture buttons stay enabled while offline" row is deliberate: watering happens in the tent, where the
phone signal is worst, and the app has no service worker. §15.7 owns what that costs.

## 4. The chart overhaul

Today: one `<highcharts-chart constructorType="stockChart">` driven by a 1205-line component, **up to 15
measures each on its own independent hidden y-axis**, each drawn `type:'area', fillOpacity:0.1,
threshold:null`, with axis labels suppressed below 320 px. On a phone that is N overlapping translucent
areas **with no axis at all**. There is no setpoint, no band, no day/night shading, no annotations. Events
are `column` series of constant height 1 readable only by **hovering**, on a mobile-first product. Series
titles are hardcoded English while the chrome is translated [CODE, all of it].

one interaction: switch plant, the data stays, the judgement changes.

### 4.2 Adopt / reject against dossier §7.2

| § | Technique | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Stacked small multiples, shared x, one linked crosshair | **Adopt.** Few's *Dual-Scaled Axes* is unqualified and Terp runs *fifteen* independent hidden scales. Max two units per pane, only when physically coupled (air temp + leaf temp). |
| 2 | Draw the setpoint; stepped dashed line; signed deviation fill; band-source priority | **Adopt in full.** This is the highest-leverage change in the dossier and **needs no new API** — `GROW_STAGE_PRESETS` and `device.configuration` are already in the client. |
| 3 | AGP-style time-in-range scorecard, day/night split, longest excursion as a *duration*, coverage % | **Adopt**, computed server-side on raw samples. Lives on the plant card (24 h) and expanded on `/plants/:id`. |
| 4 | Day/night shading from measured `out_light`, fallback to schedule, explicit timezone | **Adopt.** A failed contactor then reads as a *missing band* — diagnostic, not decoration. Timezone explicit, because a 12/12 flip near a DST boundary otherwise drifts an hour. |
| 5 | Annotation rail under the axis, clustered, max two rows | **Adopt.** Row 1 = plant events (water, feed, photo, training). Row 2 = device events (alarm, boot, config). Full-height dashed line for stage changes. Highcharts flags' `stackDistance:12` does not survive 90 days of logs, so the rail is a custom renderer, not `flags`. |
| 6 | Outputs as a state-timeline lane, not 0/1 lines | **Adopt.** Region length = duration; null renders as a gap, which is how you see a socket that stopped reporting. |
| 7 | min/max envelope on the primary measure, `sampling:'minmax'` not LTTB for the plot | **Adopt.** A single `mean` line is a lie about a bang-bang controller. LTTB only for the navigator. |
| 8a | VPD target band that **changes along x** with stage | **Adopt — this is the concept's signature panel.** It is only expressible because `Plant.stage` has a history. |
| 8b | VPD temperature × RH matrix with the last-24 h trajectory plotted on it | **Reject for v1, keep for v2.** It is a second chart grammar (heatmap + scatter), it is device-scoped so it cannot express two plants, and it competes for the same six weeks. |
| 8c | Show the leaf-temperature offset on the chart itself | **Adopt.** One label: `Blatt = Luft − 2,0 °C (Annahme)`, or `(gemessen)` when an MLX90632 exists. VPD is entirely an artefact of that assumption and burying it in settings is dishonest. |
| 9a | Film strip that *is* an axis, thumbnails positioned by timestamp | **Adopt.** Uneven capture intervals then read honestly as gaps. `Image.timestampEnd` marks mp4 timelapses — render those as a bracket, not a point. |
| 9b | Sprite + index delivery (Mux storyboard pattern) | **Reject for v1.** It needs a new server endpoint and a sprite builder. v1 caps the strip at 48 lazy-loaded thumbnails per window. v1.1 does the sprite and I expect to regret not doing it sooner on 3G. |
| 10 | One finger scrolls, two fingers pan/zoom, tap = crosshair, pinned scrub header, ≥44 px presets, **stop hiding axis labels below 320 px** | **Adopt in full.** Today pan needs Ctrl and zoom needs Shift, which do not exist on a phone. Below 320 px: 3 ticks and the unit moves into the pane title. An axis-less chart is not a chart. |
| 11 | Okabe-Ito palette, `darkMode:'auto'`, "view as table" disclosure | **Adopt.** Assignments in §4.3. Today `#e05a4e` sits next to `#b0743c` (classic deuteranopia collision), `Lights` and `Day` share `#c8a23c`, and `out_fan`/`out_fan-internal` share `#2e9e8f` [CODE]. |
| 12 | DLI and dew point | **Adopt.** Pure arithmetic on stored data (`DLI = PPFD × photoperiod / 1e6`) and it closes a named gap against Pulse Pro. |
| — | `echarts.connect()` across multiple instances | **Reject.** One instance with a `grid[]` array gives linked axes for free and half the memory. |

### 4.3 Colour

Okabe-Ito, verified hex. Position is the primary channel because the panes are separated; colour is
secondary, which is itself the accessibility win.

```
Temperatur        #D55E00  vermillion
Blatttemperatur   #56B4E9  sky blue
Feuchte           #0072B2  blue
VPD               #009E73  bluish green
CO₂               #CC79A7  reddish purple
Licht / PPFD      #E69F00  orange
Sollwert (alle)   #999999  dashed 1.5 px
Nacht-Schattierung  #000 @ 6 % (hell) / #fff @ 5 % (dunkel) — no hue
Abweichung zu warm  #D55E00 @ 20 %      zu kalt  #56B4E9 @ 20 %
```

Target bands are 12 % fill **plus a 1 px solid edge at ≥3:1 contrast**. A bare `fillOpacity: 0.1` band is
meaningful non-text content and almost certainly fails WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11 — which is exactly what ships
today, fifteen times over.

**Plants are never encoded by hue.** They get outlined chips with a numbered badge. Six measures already
consume six of the eight Okabe-Ito slots; adding plant colours would collide with the measures, and the plant
selection is already communicated by which bands are drawn.

### 4.4 The library question — and the call I am making

The dossier recommends migrating to Apache ECharts 6, and it is right about **why**: Highcharts Stock is
proprietary ($732/seat/yr + a quote-only SaaS licence whose text explicitly covers a publicly accessible
webpage), the repo has **no LICENSE file** while terpcontrol.com calls the cloud *"quelloffen"*, and
Highcharts 10.3.3 measured **416 ms / 97 MB peak** against ECharts' **148 ms / 17 MB** on 166,650 points.

**C03 still ships v1 in Highcharts, and migrates in v1.1.** The reasons:

1. Everything in §4.1 is expressible in Highstock 10 *today*: multiple `yAxis` with `top`/`height`
   percentages is the canonical Highstock multi-pane pattern, `xAxis.plotBands` gives day/night and the
   stage bands, `yAxis.plotBands` gives target bands, `plotLines` + a stepped series give the setpoint, and
   the rail/lane/strip are custom renderers either way. The **design is library-independent**; only the
   licence and the memory ceiling are not.
2. Rewriting a 1205-line component *and* changing libraries *and* landing the plant model in six weeks is
   how you ship neither.
3. The licence question is the **owner's** decision, not a designer's, and it has a second half nobody can
   design around: *Highcharts source cannot be redistributed under an OSS licence regardless of how many
   seats are bought.* Design should not pre-empt that.
4. Report 11's warning is load-bearing: **Highstock's implicit `dataGrouping` — configured nowhere — is the
   only thing keeping a 4,320-point render cheap.** Switching libraries silently removes a safety net nobody
   knew was there. When the migration happens, `sampling:'minmax'` is not polish, it is the replacement.

Free cleanup that ships in v1 regardless: delete `chart.js` / `ng2-charts` / `chartjs-adapter-luxon` from
`charts.page.ts`, `charts.module.ts`, `diary.module.ts` and `diagnostics.*` (**68.8 KB gz of pure waste**),
fix the duplicate `noData(Highcharts)` call, fix `CO2 … softMax: 1` on a ppm series, and **translate the
hardcoded English series names** — a German user currently sees `Temperature`, `Humidity`, `Critical logs`
inside a translated page.

### 4.5 Query-string compatibility

`ChartPreset.query` and `ShareLink.query` are **persisted user data in the URL format**. C03 adds exactly two
optional params — `plant=<plant_id>` and `range=stage|grow` — and `applyViewParams` ignores what it does not
know (it already does: it reads `measures` and `vpdMode` by presence). An old preset with no `plant`
resolves to the device's lead plant. No existing link breaks. The five duplicated copies of this format
[CODE] get consolidated into one `chart-query-params.ts` alongside the existing `diary-query-params.ts`
convention, because adding a sixth would be malpractice.

## 5. The multi-plant diary

### 5.1 Data model

All cloud-side MongoDB, keyed by `device_id`. **Nothing goes into `device.configuration`** — the firmware
strips unknown keys when it echoes config back [CODE `grow-presets.ts:31-34`]. All new fields are optional
with read-time normalisers, because there is no migration tooling.

**`plants`**
```ts
plant_id: string          // uuid, STABLE — this is the fix for renaming forking history
device_id: string         // indexed { device_id: 1, archivedAt: 1 }
owner_id: string
name: string
isImplicit: boolean       // true = auto-created, user has never named it. See §5.5
strain?: string
seedSource?: 'seed' | 'clone' | 'unknown'
motherPlantId?: string
startedAt: number         // epoch ms — day 1
stage: PlantStage         // DiaryLifecycleStage | 'harvest'   <- harvest finally exists
stageSince: number
stageHistory: { stage: PlantStage; from: number }[]   // drives the moving chart band
archivedAt?: number
coverImageId?: string
medium?: 'soil' | 'coco' | 'hydro' | 'rockwool' | 'other'
potSizeL?: number
position?: string         // free text, "hinten links"
scheduleId?: string
scheduleAnchor?: { stepIndex: number; anchoredAt: number }
equipmentDeviceIds?: string[]    // old fan/light/plug devices attached — see §9
createdAt, updatedAt: number
```

`PlantStage = DiaryLifecycleStage | 'harvest'`. `DiaryLifecycleStage` is used by `RecipeStep.stage` and must
not change; the new union is additive and maps down to `'flowering'` when written to a legacy `DeviceLog`.

**`plant_events`** — the actual diary. **Not** `DeviceLog`.
```ts
event_id: string
plant_ids: string[]       // ARRAY. One row can belong to three plants. Indexed multikey.
device_id: string
type: PlantEventType
time: number
note?: string
images: string[]          // image_id refs
data: PlantEventData      // typed per type, see below
overrides?: Record<string, Partial<PlantEventData>>   // per-plant deltas, keyed by plant_id
actor_id?: string         // null unless attribution is on — §8
actorLabel?: string       // pseudonymous display name, never a real name
correctsEventId?: string  // append-only compensating correction instead of edit-in-place
regulatoryRelevant?: boolean
migratedFromLogId?: string
createdAt, updatedAt, deletedAt?: number    // deletedAt actually means deleted
```

```ts
type PlantEventType =
  'water' | 'feed' | 'flush' | 'measure' | 'photo' | 'note' |
  'stage' | 'training' | 'pest' | 'transplant' | 'harvest' | 'task';
```

`data` per type — the fields the dossier proved are missing everywhere in the repo today:
```
water     { volumeL, runoffPct?, sourceEc?, sourcePh?, runoffEc?, runoffPh?, waterTempC? }
feed      water fields + { batchVolumeL, products: [{ name, amount, unit, productId? }],
                           mixedEc?, mixedPh?, scheduleId?, scheduleStepIndex? }
flush     { volumeL, reason: 'scheduled' | 'corrective' | 'leach' }    // three different things
measure   { site: 'input'|'runoff'|'reservoir'|'substrate',            // <- "what was measured"
            ec?, ph?, ppm?, ppmScale: 500|640|700, tempC?,
            heightCm?, lampDistanceCm?, ppfd? }
stage     { from: PlantStage, to: PlantStage }
training  { method: 'topping'|'lst'|'defoliation'|'supercrop'|'scrog'|'other' }
pest      { kind?, severity: 0|1|2, treatment? }
transplant{ toPotSizeL?, toMedium? }
harvest   { wetG?, dryG? }        // only rendered when grow_settings.harvestWeights === true
task      { title, state: 'planned'|'pending'|'completed'|'rejected', dueAt? }
```

`ppmScale` is not optional pedantry: at EC 1.8 a Bluelab reads **1260** and a Hanna reads **900** for the
same liquid, and this is repeatedly cited as the #1 cause of accidental over/under-feeding. **EC is stored
canonical; a bare "ppm" is never rendered without its scale.**

**`plant_weeks`** — derived and *persisted*, because that table **is** the export.
```ts
plant_id, weekIndex, dayFrom, dayTo, stage,
avgDayTempC, avgNightTempC, minTempC, maxTempC, avgRhPct, avgVpdKpa, avgCo2Ppm,
timeInRangePct, coveragePct, lightHours, dliMolM2,
waterTotalL, feedCount, avgInputEc, avgRunoffEc, avgPh,
heightCm?, lampDistanceCm?, potSizeL?, photos: string[]
```
Eleven of GrowDiaries' ~16 per-week numeric fields land here automatically because the controller measures
them. Store **both** a day counter and week buckets or one export is permanently lossy (GrowDiaries is
week-bucketed; Grow with Jane is day-indexed with named stages).

**`feed_schedules`** — §6. **`memberships`**, **`grow_settings`** — §8.

### 5.2 How plants are created

Four ways, in descending frequency:

1. **Automatically.** The server creates one on `claimDevice` for any plant-capable `device_type`:
   `{ isImplicit: true, name: device.name || t('plants.implicitName'), startedAt: now, stage: 'seedling' }`.
   **Zero taps.** The user is never asked.
2. **`+ Pflanze hinzufügen`** — one row: name (optional), stage (the existing `stage-preset-picker`), photo
   (optional). Everything else deferred.
3. **Splitting.** `⋯ → Aufteilen` on a card: "Zwei Pflanzen daraus machen." Existing events stay on the
   original; the new plant starts from `startedAt` of the original (same seeds, same day) unless changed.
4. **Cloning.** `⋯ → Klon nehmen` writes a `training`-adjacent event on the mother and creates a child with
   `motherPlantId` and `seedSource: 'clone'`, `startedAt: now`.

### 5.3 How entries attach

`plant_events.plant_ids` is the attachment. One row, N plants. That means "watered three plants" is **one
document**, not three — which matters because the API has **no server-side pagination for logs today** and
the Entries report fetches a device's *entire* history on every open [CODE]. C03 ships cursor pagination on
`GET /plants/:id/events` from day one; multiplying rows without it is how the diary becomes the slowest page
in the app.

Photos attach as `type:'photo'` events. Webcam stills stay device-scoped and un-attributed — honest, because
the camera sees the whole tent, not a plant.

### 5.4 Rename, re-veg, clone, harvest

- **Rename** changes `plants.name`. Nothing else. `plant_id` is stable. This directly kills the current
  behaviour where `convertEventsToGrowCycles()` starts a new cycle *whenever the trimmed `lifecycleName`
  string differs* [CODE `grow-report.component.ts:691-747`].
- **Re-veg** is a `stage` event `flowering → vegetative`. `stageHistory` gains a row, `stageSince` resets,
  `startedAt` does not. The card reads `Wachstum · Tag 6 · gesamt Tag 96`. The chart's stage band steps back
  and the second x-ruler shows it. No fabricated second plant.
- **Clone** as above; the child's card shows `Klon von Gorilla Glue #4` linking to the mother.
- **Harvest** is `stage → 'harvest'` — a stage that does not exist in the type today at all
  (`flowering → drying` with nothing between) — then `drying`, `curing`, then `archivedAt`. `Grow
  abschließen` archives every plant on a device and offers a fresh one. Archived plants leave `/plants` and
  live at `/plants?archived=1`, still readable, still exportable, still shareable.
- **Weights** (`harvest.data.wetG` / `dryG`) are **not rendered at all** unless
  `grow_settings.harvestWeights === true`, default **false**. §8 owns why.

### 5.5 Migration off `DeviceLog`

`convertEventsToGrowCycles` stops being a runtime browser algorithm and becomes a **one-time, idempotent,
server-side backfill** in a service constructor — the house style, since there is no migrate-mongo and no
`schemaVersion`. Per device:

1. Scan `DeviceLog` rows with `categories` containing `diary-plant-lifecycle` **or** the legacy
   `plant-lifecycle` (both must be supported forever).
2. Produce **one** plant per device unless the trimmed `lifecycleName` values are genuinely distinct
   *and* overlap in time. A stage rollback alone no longer forks.
3. Convert the five existing entry types into `plant_events` (`diary-plant-log` → `note`,
   `diary-measurement` → `measure` with `site: 'input'` and `ppmScale: 500` **plus a visible
   `Skala unbestätigt` chip**, `diary-co2-refill` stays a device event, `diary-fridge-log` → `note`),
   stamping `migratedFromLogId`.
4. Original `DeviceLog` rows are **left untouched**. Nothing is destroyed by a migration.
5. On first `/plants` visit after backfill, one review screen: *"Wir haben aus deinem Tagebuch 1 Pflanze
   erstellt. Stimmt das?"* `[ Passt ]  [ Ändern ]`. Once, dismissible, never repeated.

Also fixed here, because a plant-first diary cannot ship on top of them:
- **`deleted` does not mean deleted** — manual diary entries are *created* with `deleted: true` and the diary
  always requests hidden rows, so **"Logs löschen" removes nothing from the diary** [CODE]. In
  `plant_events`, `deletedAt` means deleted, and the device-log visibility flag stays where it belongs.
- **The server's own stage-transition entry is written with `categories: ['diary-plant-lifecycle']` without
  the `'diary'` prefix**, making it invisible in the Entries report and uneditable while the identical manual
  entry is both [CODE `device.service.ts:714`]. In C03 the server writes a `plant_events` row of
  `type:'stage'` like everything else.

### 5.6 The user who does not care about plants — five rules

This is the hardest question in the brief and it gets explicit rules, not good intentions.

1. **A plant is never created by the user on first run.** The server does it silently at claim time.
2. **While `plants.length === 1 && isImplicit`, the word "Pflanze" never appears.** The card header shows the
   device name. The card's `⋯` menu has no plant-specific verbs. `+ Pflanze hinzufügen` exists but is
   `fill="clear"`, small, and below the fold. No badge, no dot, no "complete your setup", no dialog.
3. **All the machinery still runs anyway** — day counter, stage, stage bands, time-in-range, week rows,
   export. The user gets every benefit of the entity without ever learning it exists.
4. **Exactly one question, asked exactly once, at the only moment it is necessary:** when they add a second
   plant, a single row appears — *"Wie soll die erste Pflanze heißen?"* with the device name pre-filled and a
   `[ Weiter ]` that accepts the default. `isImplicit` flips to `false` on that, or on any manual rename.
5. **Renaming the device renames the implicit plant** and only the implicit plant. Once `isImplicit` is
   false, the two names are independent forever.

The net cost to a Type 1 user of the entire plant model is **zero taps and one extra card border.**

## 6. Watering & fertilising

There is **no irrigation or dosing hardware.** This is capture-and-recall, not automation, and C03 does not
pretend otherwise anywhere in the UI. The design goal is therefore singular: **capture speed.**

### 6.1 The everyday flow — counted

**One plant, same as last time:**

```
tap 1  →  [ 💧 ] on the plant card
          ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
          │  Gießen                          ✕   │
          │  Gorilla Glue #4          ☑          │
          │                                      │
          │  Menge     [ 2,0 ] L    ⟨ wie zuletzt⟩│
          │  Jetzt · 22.08. 19:04    [ ändern ]  │
          │                                      │
          │  + EC   + pH   + Ablauf   + Notiz    │  ← chips, each opens one field
          │                                      │
          │           [    Speichern    ]        │  56 px, --ion-color-secondary green
          └──────────────────────────────────────┘
tap 2  →  Speichern
          → snackbar: "Für 1 Pflanze gespeichert   [Rückgängig]"   6 s
```

**= 2 taps.**

**Three plants, same water — the multi-plant answer:**

```
tap 1  →  [ 💧 Gießen ] in the capture bar (scope = every plant on this device)
          ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
          │  Gießen                          ✕   │
          │  ☑ Gorilla Glue #4                   │  ALL PRE-SELECTED
          │  ☑ Zkittlez                          │
          │  ☑ Northern Lights                   │
          │                                      │
          │  Menge je Pflanze  [ 2,0 ] L         │
          │  ...                                 │
          │           [    Speichern    ]        │
          └──────────────────────────────────────┘
tap 2  →  Speichern
```

**= 2 taps for three plants.** One `plant_events` row, `plant_ids: [a,b,c]`. Deselecting one plant costs one
extra tap. Giving one plant a different volume costs two: tap its row → an inline `[ 1,5 ] L` field →
that lands in `overrides[plant_id]`.

**The rule that makes this work: values live at the top, plants live at the bottom, and identical is the
default.** No per-plant form. No wizard. No "next plant" stepper.

### 6.2 The schedule-following flow

```
tap 1  →  [ 🧪 Füttern ]
          ┌────────────────────────────────────────────┐
          │  Füttern                               ✕   │
          │  ☑ Gorilla Glue #4   ☑ Zkittlez            │
          │  ─────────────────────────────────────     │
          │  Schema: Lucas Formula (Coco)              │
          │  Schritt 7 von 13 · Blüte Woche 3   [→]    │
          │                                            │
          │  Ansatz  [ 10 ] L                          │
          │  ① MaxiGrow        0 g                     │
          │  ② MaxiBloom       80 g                    │
          │  ③ Epsom           6 g                     │
          │  ④ pH zuletzt einstellen                   │
          │  Ziel-EC 1,6  (+ Leitungswasser 0,4 → 2,0) │
          │                                            │
          │  + gemessener EC   + pH   + Ablauf-EC      │
          │           [    Speichern    ]              │
          └────────────────────────────────────────────┘
tap 2  →  Speichern  →  scheduleAnchor.stepIndex++
```

**= 2 taps**, and the schedule advances **by feed event, not by wall-clock week** — growers feed Tue/Fri and
slip. `[→]` on the step row lets you jump a step manually.

Three things in that sheet are non-negotiable domain correctness:

- **The mixing order is an ordered, numbered checklist with pH last.** Gypsum precipitation and silica
  reacting with phosphate are real failure modes; *"adding pH- can increase EC"* is a real trap.
- **`ec_basis`.** CANNA publishes **`EC +`, not EC**: *"Add the EC of the tap water that is used to the
  recommended EC."* A UI that alarms against the raw chart value is actively wrong. Hence
  `ecBasis: 'absolute' | 'delta_over_source'` on the schedule, a `sourceEc` on `grow_settings`, and the
  literal string `Ziel-EC 1,6 (+ Leitungswasser 0,4 → 2,0)` in the sheet.
- **A dose is not a scalar.** `{ amount, unit, min?, max?, literal? }` — because real charts contain
  ranges (`5–13`), zeros that mean stop, and literal strings (`"water"`, `"Flush only"`, `"Use as pH up"`).

Also from the research, cheap and non-obvious:
- **Batch volume, not reservoir size**, is the input — it serves a 10 L watering can and a 100 L DWC
  reservoir identically.
- **Autoflower multiplier ×0.25–0.5 with no 12/12 flip event.** Essentially no manufacturer chart has an
  auto column; this is a real gap fillable while borrowing nothing.
- **Run-length policy** on the schedule (`repeat_last | reshape | user_adjust`). Silently truncating an
  8-week chart onto a 10-week cultivar is the failure mode that makes the whole feature untrustworthy.
- **Flush is three different things** (`scheduled | corrective | leach`) and the UI never conflates them.
  It is presented as *"dein Schema sieht das vor"*, never as *"das ist nötig"* — the Rx Green Technologies
  2019 trial found no significant difference and the taste panel statistically preferred un-flushed.

### 6.3 What gets drawn on the chart

- Water and feed events are glyphs on **annotation rail row 1** (droplet / flask), clustered with a count
  badge at 8–12 px proximity.
- If the event carries `mixedEc` or `runoffEc`, an **EC lane** appears under the CO₂ pane as points, with the
  schedule's `targetEc` as a stepped dashed line — the same goal-vs-actual grammar as temperature. This is
  the only place in the product where the *feeding plan* is drawn as a target, and it is the strongest
  argument for the feature living in a controller rather than a standalone app: **no bottle-brand calculator
  can see the grow.**
- Cumulative litres per week come from `plant_weeks.waterTotalL` and render as a small bar strip in the
  `Grow` range preset.
- **Runoff-vs-input EC divergence > 20 %** raises a card-level hint, not an alarm.

### 6.4 Brand-schedule posture

**v1 ships zero brand charts.** In order:

1. The engine is **brand-agnostic and user-editable first**. That is the durable asset and it carries zero IP
   risk.
2. Seeded with **ownerless** schedules only: Lucas Formula (0-5-10 / 0-8-16), Jack's 321, and generic
   per-medium EC ladders for soil / coco / hydro. Useful on day one with nothing borrowed.
3. `Schema erstellen` — the user types what is on their own bottle. Shareable as a JSON blob between users.
4. **If** brand charts ever ship, they are a separate, versioned, hot-swappable `feed_schedules` collection in
   MongoDB — **never baked into the bundle** — each carrying `source: { url, retrievedAt, kind:
   official_pdf | official_calculator | manual_transcription }`, so any brand is removable in one DB update
   and the "zuletzt geprüft" date is a *user-facing honesty feature*. Word marks only: **no logos, no brand
   colours, no bottle imagery**, no brand name in a Terp feature name ("CANNA-Modus" forfeits the
   nominative-fair-use defence), and a published takedown address with a documented 48-hour notice-and-action
   policy *before* shipping.
5. Never transcribe from third parties: for the same CANNA Terra veg stage, one aggregator publishes
   30–50 ml/10 L and another publishes 10–30 — a **~3× divergence** that would burn or starve a crop.
6. Never copy brand prose. The numbers are facts; *"Vegetative phase II — up to growth stagnation after
   fructification"* is expressive text.
7. Terp Control is an EU company, so the **sui generis database right** applies (no originality required,
   15 years, substantial-part extraction barred). Shipping one brand's complete chart is extraction of a
   substantial part; shipping twenty is twenty counts of it. **This is a legal-appetite decision for the
   owner, gated on German IP counsel (UWG § 4 Nr. 3, UrhG §§ 87a–87e), not a design decision.**

And a liability line separate from the IP line, in the user's language, on every computed dose:
*"Richtwerte, keine Vorschrift — starte niedrig und miss nach."*

## 7. Camera & timelapse

Today: `Image { image_id, device_id, timestamp, timestampEnd?, format: 'jpeg'|'mp4'|'user/jpeg',
duration: '1d'|'1w'|'1m' }` with **no log, entry or plant back-reference**, and timelapses that have **no
relation to a grow cycle, phase or entry** [CODE]. Diary photos leak forever: `DELETE /image/:image_id`
exists and is never called by the webapp, and retention filters `format:'jpeg'` only, so `user/jpeg` blobs
accumulate.

C03 relates images to time, plants and stages **without adding a field to `Image`**:

- **Webcam stills stay device-scoped.** They are the tent, not a plant. They power the film strip (§4.1) and
  the chart-scrub image.
- **User photos are `plant_events` of `type:'photo'`** carrying `images: [image_id]` and `plant_ids`. That
  gives a plant gallery, a per-week photo set in `plant_weeks.photos`, and a position on the annotation rail
  — all from the event, none from the image.
- **Stage covers are free.** On every stage transition the server picks the webcam still nearest the
  transition timestamp and writes it to `plants.coverImageId`. Zero taps, and it is the literal delivery of
  *"it does the diary keeping FOR YOU."* The user can override with any photo.
- **The "same moment" affordance.** Open any `plant_event` and there is a *"Kamera-Bild von diesem Moment"*
  row: the nearest webcam still within ±15 min. Costs one indexed query. This is the fusion nobody ships —
  a human action and a machine observation on one timestamp.
- **Phasen-Timelapse** (`/plants/:id`) is honest about what it is in v1: it plays the **existing** device
  `1w` mp4s that overlap the stage, in order, as a playlist. It does **not** render new video — that is
  server cost and encoder work that does not fit six weeks. The label says `Phasen-Timelapse (aus
  Wochenvideos zusammengesetzt)`. v1.1 renders a real per-stage mp4.

Prerequisites, not polish, because Plant First multiplies photo volume:
- `express-fileupload` is mounted with **no size limit** and images are Mongo `Buffer`s capped by the 16 MB
  BSON limit **with no graceful failure path**. Add a 10 MB limit and client-side downscale to 1600 px long
  edge before upload.
- The unique index `{device_id, format, timestamp, duration}` means **two uploads in the same millisecond
  collide.** Add a jitter or widen the key.
- Wire up the existing `DELETE /image/:image_id` when a photo event is deleted, and extend retention to
  `user/jpeg`.

## 8. Multi-user, clubs, and the privacy posture

### 8.1 Roles

`Device.owner_id` is **a single unindexed String**, and the entire authorisation model is one equality test
at `auth.middleware.ts:172` [CODE]. That cannot be flagged around. C03 adds:

**`memberships`** `{ membership_id, device_id, user_id, role, createdAt, invitedBy }`

| Role | Can |
| --- | --- |
| `owner` | everything, incl. transfer and delete. Exactly one. |
| `grower` | read + write plants, events, settings, alarms. Cannot delete the device or manage members. |
| `viewer` | read only. Today's `ShareLink` already delivers this, so `viewer` is the *account-backed* version. |

Plus a `:self` tier for club mode, copied from Cannanas' `member:journals:self-checkin` pattern: a `grower`
in `:self` mode logs their own work and sees the room, and sees nobody else's attribution.

**Scope honesty:** v1 ships the `actor_id` / `actorLabel` **fields** and the `memberships` collection with
`owner` + `grower` only. `viewer` and `:self` are v1.1. The reason is in §13: `memberships` requires
rewriting `auth.middleware.ts:172` and `:207`, loosening ~10 owner-scoped service queries, and an
account-lookup-by-email endpoint that does not exist and must be designed against enumeration abuse.
Shipping the *field* in v1 means the data is right before the UI is.

Two more things get fixed on the way, because they break clubs immediately:
`RecipeTemplate.name` is **globally unique across all users** (two growers cannot both have "Autoflower"),
and `PUT /users/:id` is currently broken.

### 8.2 Attribution

`plant_events.actor_id` + `actorLabel`. `actorLabel` is a **user-chosen pseudonym**, never a real name, never
an email. Rendered as `· Max` at the end of a timeline row. **Off by default.**

Corrections are `correctsEventId` — an append-only compensating correction, never an edit-in-place (the
`dispense:storno` pattern). No cryptographic immutability by default.

### 8.3 Privacy posture — `grow_settings.jurisdictionMode`

**Default: `'private'`. This is a design decision, made here, on purpose.**

| | `private` (default) | `club` (opt-in) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attribution | off | on |
| Harvest weights / plant counts | **field not rendered at all** | rendered, opt-in |
| Telemetry retention | 180 days, then downsampled | configurable, longer |
| Participation log (`task` events) | off | on, `planned → pending → completed → rejected` |
| Export | anonymised by default | `NONE / PSEUDONYMIZED / ANONYMIZED`, defaults anonymised |
| Wipe | **one tap**, really deletes (Mongo + Influx range delete + images) | owner-only, logged |

Harvest weights and plant counts are the exact facts separating a lawful hobby from an offence in many
jurisdictions, and **§ 28 Abs. 2 KCanG lets German authorities seize digital data with no prior judicial
approval.** Report 10 is right that a diary without harvest is pointless; report 05 is right that these are
the dangerous facts. The jurisdiction switch is the resolution, and privacy-maximal is the default.

**Hard refusals, in the product, permanently:**
- No per-member consumption or dispensing records. No real names, no DOB, no addresses, no membership
  numbers, no THC content per hand-over.
- Terp Control is **never** the club's system of record for § 26 KCanG. ~20 SaaS products already do that at
  €0.50–1.00/member/month; winning would mean owning 23 differing authority export formats and liability for
  wrong ones.
- **No `KCanG-konform` badge and no compliance warranty**, ever. Even Cannanas' own compliance endpoint says
  *"Not legal advice."*
- No location, no GPS, no IP-geolocation persistence, no "growers near you".
- No analytics or ad SDKs. No hosting outside the EU for EU users, stated plainly.
- The audit log is **never undeletable for the account owner**. Immutability is opt-in and club-scoped;
  globally it victimises an unregulated grower.
- **No community feed.** ~200 App Store reviews across TrolMaster and VIVOSUN, both of which ship one, and
  not a single review praised it. It is permanent moderation cost.

### 8.4 What is shareable

- **`ShareLink`** keeps working exactly as today, including `page:'diary'`.
- **A public plant page** at `/g/{slug}-{5char}` — read-only, opt-in per plant, revocable, with a
  `Bilder einbeziehen` toggle, no identity, `noindex`. A link you can paste into a forum, Telegram or Discord
  is not a feed you must moderate. (v1.1.)
- **A Markdown / BBCode post generator** with hot-linked image URLs. XenForo and Discourse both render
  remote images, which collapses "integrate with RollItUp, THCFarmer, ICMag and Percy's" — four platforms
  with no usable API between them — into **one copy button**. (v1.1.)
- **No 2-way sync with anything.** GrowDiaries has no public API and is Cloudflare-hardened; Grow with Jane's
  ToS §(c)(7) bans it outright and the backend is Firebase; SeedFinder's API has been dead since
  2024-07-01. The word *"Sync"* appears **nowhere** in the UI. The feature is called **`Woche exportieren`**
  and **`Grow teilen`**.
- Outbound: **Discord webhook, Telegram bot, generic webhook.** Telegram is already where the Terp community
  lives. Never Instagram, Threads, TikTok or Reddit auto-post: a failed Discord post means a revoked webhook;
  a failed Instagram post means the **user** loses their account with no appeal and Terp Control was the
  proximate cause.
- **No auto-appended "Powered by Terp Control" footer.** § 6 KCanG bans cannabis advertising and sponsoring
  with fines to €30,000, read broadly. That string on thousands of German users' grow posts is the single
  riskiest thing in the feature and it needs counsel, not a designer.

### 8.5 A security fix this design forces

`POST /device/claimcode` is **unauthenticated** for any device that has not reported
`hardwareInfo.claimcode_auth === 'on'`, and `claimDevice` overwrites `owner_id` unconditionally with no check
on and no notification to the previous owner. **Knowing a `device_id` — which appears in every webapp URL —
is enough to steal a device** [CODE]. Plant First puts share URLs on `plant_id` instead of `device_id`,
which reduces exposure, but that is mitigation, not a fix. **This is a v1 blocker, not a nice-to-have.**

## 9. Old devices — Type 7, spelled out

A `fan`, `light` or `plug` device has no climate to grade and nothing that is meaningfully "a grow". C03
therefore draws a hard line and states it in code:

```ts
const PLANT_CAPABLE = ['controller', 'fridge', 'fridge2'];
```

**What each owner actually sees:**

| Owner has | `''` redirects to | Tab bar | Plants |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| only `fan` / `light` / `plug` / `dryer` | **`/devices`** | **hidden entirely** | never mentioned |
| a `fridge` | `/plants` | shown | yes — a fridge is a grow space |
| a `controller` | `/plants` | shown | yes |
| old devices **+** a controller | `/plants` | shown | plants on the controller; old devices appear on `/devices` **and** as attachable equipment |

**The pure-old-device user's app is today's app, minus its bugs.** Their `fan-overview`, `light-overview` and
`plug-overview` components are **not touched** — zero regression risk on hardware that is out of production.
What they gain, for free, because it is device-scoped:

1. The **chart overhaul** in full (§4). Their measures — `rpm`, `temperature`, `humidity`, `out_*` — get
   panes, axes, day/night shading, an outputs lane, a readable annotation rail and a mobile touch model. A
   fan owner currently sees translucent areas with hidden axes on a phone; they will see a chart.
2. **Live / stale / offline** on every value (§3.4).
3. Their **real i18n bugs fixed**: `settings.limits.overtemperature.enabled`,
   `.undertemperature.enabled` and `.time.enabled` currently render **raw translation keys as visible text on
   the plug settings checkboxes**, and `devices.plug.settings.heater-day` / `-night` are missing [CODE].
   That is a type-7 bug in production right now.
4. **Log pagination** and a working "Logs löschen".
5. `/device/:device_id/log` gives them a real system-event page instead of a diary that was never for them
   (`diary-fridge-log` is a free-text box on a fan).

**The upgrade path is the interesting part.** `plants.equipmentDeviceIds` lets a controller-owning user
attach an old device to a plant:

- attaching a `light` device makes its measured on/off history drive the plant's **day/night shading** and
  add a lane to the outputs timeline;
- attaching a `plug` adds a **named lane** (`Umluftventilator`) — the first time a user's old smart plug has
  ever had a name in a chart;
- attaching a `fan` adds its `rpm` as a pane.

That is a concrete reason for an existing owner of old hardware to buy the October controller, expressed as a
feature rather than a marketing line. It requires no firmware change: it is a cloud-side array of device ids
and a second Influx query.

**What Type 7 explicitly does not get:** a plant card, a feeding schedule, a public grow page, or any nagging
about any of them. They are not the focus group and the design does not pretend they are.

## 10. Onboarding

**Total mandatory interaction: a claim code and one tap.**

```
1.  /devices — the existing .tc-hero block, unchanged. It is genuinely good:
    three numbered steps, a claim-code input, real help copy.
       "Gerät einstecken" → "Code am Display ablesen" → "Code hier eingeben"

2.  Claim succeeds. The server creates the implicit plant. NOTHING IS ASKED.
    → straight to /plants, with the card already showing "Mein Grow · Tag 1".

3.  A single card slides in above it:
    ┌────────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │  In welcher Phase sind deine Pflanzen?      │
    │  [🌱 Sämling] [🌿 Wachstum] [🌸 Blüte]      │   ← existing stage-preset-picker
    │  [🌸 Spätblüte] [🍂 Trocknung]              │      .tc-preset-card grid, with
    │  Sämling · 24/21 °C · 70 % · 18 h           │      the subtitles it already has
    │  VPD 0,4–0,8 kPa                            │
    └────────────────────────────────────────────┘
    ONE TAP. It sets the device setpoints, the workmode, the plant's stage,
    the chart's target bands, and the day counter's origin — all at once.

4.  Then the card is replaced by, and never again by anything else:
    ┌────────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │  Willst du deinem Grow einen Namen geben?   │
    │  [ Später ]              [ Name eingeben ]  │
    └────────────────────────────────────────────┘
    "Später" is the left, default-focused button. It never comes back.
```

### 10.1 Capability without an interview

Capability is **read from the hardware, never asked**. `hardwareInfo.sockets` is a CSV of paired roles that
already carries per-role truth; today it is collapsed into three buckets by `deviceControlCapability()` and
shown per-role **only inside the one-time wizard step** [CODE `grow-presets.ts:143-173`].

C03 changes three things:

1. **Per-role, not three buckets.** `capability(device) → Set<'heater'|'dehumidifier'|'light'|
   'secondary_light'|'co2'>`. A heater-only tent stops being shown a humidity target row, a humidity
   deviation warning and a "Entfeuchter läuft durchgehend" alarm preset it can never act on — all three of
   which it gets today, because *any* of heater/dehumidifier/co2 returns `'full'`.
2. **Fail closed, not open.** A missing `hardwareInfo.sockets` (old firmware) currently returns `'full'`. It
   will return **monitor-only** plus a plain banner: *"Ältere Firmware — ich kann deine Steckdosen nicht
   auslesen. [Manuell festlegen]"*. Showing a control that does nothing is worse than showing none.
3. **The wizard's `connections` step becomes a confirmation.** *"Ich sehe: Lampe, Heizung. Stimmt das?"*
   `[ Ja ]` `[ Ändern ]`. One tap for the common case.

And the **~30 MB of already-shot wizard videos sitting unreferenced in `webapp/src/assets/wizard/`** —
`connect.mp4`, `teachin.mp4`, `sockettype_*.mp4`, `overview.mp4` — get wired into exactly this step. They
are copied into every production build today and referenced nowhere.

### 10.2 Dead controls get removed, not explained

The entire `daynight.floating` / `float_start` / `day_duration` / `light_duration` beta block is read by
**no firmware anywhere** — zero grep hits across `firmware/` — and `lights.maintenanceOn`, `co2.sunsetOff`
and `daynight.linearChange` are offered to controllers and silently ignored [CODE]. They are removed from
the controller UI. `simpleSettings.light.floatingNote` renders a **raw translation key** to users right now;
deleting the control deletes the bug.

## 11. Per-user-type, first week

**Type 1 — monitoring only, lazy.** Plugs in, enters the code, taps `Sämling`. Sees one card: *"Mein Grow ·
Sämling · Tag 1"* with 24.6 °C / 68 % / 0.6 kPa above it and `● Live`. He does nothing for four days. On day
five he opens the app and the card says *"✓ 91 % im Zielbereich (24 h) · gegenüber Richtwert Sämling"* and
*"Tag 5"*. He taps the card once, sees a chart with a shaded night, a dashed target line and his actual
temperature wandering above it in the afternoons, and closes the app. **He was never asked about plants, and
he now has a week export he did not know he was accruing.** Total taps in week one: about six.

**Type 2 — the same, with a camera.** Identical, plus: on day one the card's photo slot fills itself from the
webcam. On day three a film strip appears under the chart and dragging it moves the crosshair — the picture
and the temperature move together. On the stage change to `vegetative`, the app auto-picks the still nearest
the transition as the phase cover with no interaction, and `/plants/:id` shows *"Phasen-Timelapse ·
Sämling (11 Tage)"*. That auto-cover is the moment the product's *"it does the diary keeping FOR YOU"* claim
becomes literally true.

**Type 3 — controls CO₂ or heating only.** Pairs a heater socket. The wizard says *"Ich sehe: Heizung"*, he
taps `Ja`. The humidity target row **does not appear**, the humidity deviation warning **does not fire**, and
the "Entfeuchter läuft durchgehend" alarm preset **is not offered** — all three of which he would get today.
His temperature pane has a solid actual, a dashed stepped setpoint and a deviation fill; his humidity pane
has a series and a *reference* band labelled `gegenüber Richtwert Blüte`, with a one-line note that nothing
is switched for it. He learns exactly what his hardware does in about ninety seconds.

**Type 4 — the whole tent.** Pairs heater, dehumidifier, light and CO₂. Sees the full outputs state-timeline
lane and immediately notices the thing the old chart hid: `out_dehumidifier` runs in long blocks at night. He
also hits the honest wall — **`out_dehumidifier` doubles as the cooler in `temp`/`breed` workmodes, and there
is no humidifier, no exhaust-fan and no AC role anywhere in the stack.** C03 does not invent them. What it
does is label the lane `Entfeuchter / Kühlung (dieselbe Steckdose)` and say in the equipment page that
humidity can only ever go down. He is annoyed, but he is not lied to, and he can plan around it. In week one
he adds a second plant (Zkittlez, veg) and gets the conflict banner: *"Deine Pflanzen wollen unterschiedliches
Klima. Das Zelt folgt Gorilla Glue #4."* — with a `[Wechseln]` that reassigns the lead.

**Type 5 — closed-loop DIY, a fridge.** The worst-served type in this concept, and §15.4 says so. He builds a
fridge, wants his own EC probe and a write API, and Plant First puts an object he does not want at the root.
His mitigations in week one: `⋯ → Pflanzen ausblenden` on the device (`grow_settings.plantsHidden`) hides the
tab and boots him to `/devices`; the implicit plant keeps existing invisibly so his `plant_weeks` export
still works; his EC probe still cannot enter as a series, because `VALID_SENSORS` is a hard-coded allowlist
and widening it requires fixing the raw Flux interpolation in `/data/series` first. He gets a documented API
in v1.1 and nothing before it. He will not be happy in week one.

**Type 6 — a grow club, shared controllers.** Two people, one tent, one account in v1 (memberships UI is
v1.1). What they get in week one that they cannot get anywhere today: **watering and feeding are first-class
events with volume, products, EC, pH and site**, one row per action, batched across plants in two taps, and
drawn on the same timeline as the climate. *"Who did what, and how did the plants react"* becomes a legible
sequence: droplet on the rail at 19:04, EC 2.0 on the EC lane, and the humidity pane rising for six hours
afterwards. Attribution is stored from v1 (`actorLabel`, pseudonymous) even though the second login lands in
v1.1. The privacy posture is `private` and they are never asked for a real name, a birth year or a gram.

**Type 7 — old fan / light / plug.** Opens the app and lands on `/devices`, which is the page they already
know, with their existing overview components untouched. **The Plants tab does not exist for them.** What
changed: their values now say `Stand: vor 4 Min` instead of pretending to be current, their plug settings
stop showing `settings.limits.overtemperature.enabled` as literal text, and their chart has axes on a phone
for the first time. If they later buy a controller, their light and plug become named lanes on a plant's
chart. Nothing was taken away; a few things got better; nobody asked them to name a plant.

## 12. Stoner / normal / techie

**Depth is vertical distance, not a mode toggle.** This is the concept's position and it is deliberate: a
mode switch asks a stoned user to first answer *"which mode am I in"*, which is the exact question that
person cannot answer. Scrolling is not a decision.

| | Stoner | Normal | Techie |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Where they live** | plant card, top half | plant card, bottom half + `/plants/:id` | `/device/.../settings` Experte, `/device/.../equipment`, chart controls |
| **What they read** | a photo, a day number, one coloured line | last event, next due, verdict + its source, the chart | panes, envelopes, deviation fills, the outputs lane, raw tables |
| **What they do** | 4 fat buttons, 2 taps each, undo on every one | schedules, stages, targets, alarms | recipes, alarms with webhooks, per-role sockets, exports, API keys (v1.1) |
| **Copy register** | imperative, ≤5 words, always a word next to every number | explanatory, one sentence | precise, units and assumptions named |

Concretely, per lens:

**Stoner.** The four capture buttons are 56 px and thumb-reachable, `Speichern` is the green
`--ion-color-secondary` CTA in the bottom-right, every save produces a 6-second `[Rückgängig]`, and no
destructive action exists without one. The verdict is *"✓ 94 % im Zielbereich"*, not *"TIR 94 %"*. The stage
picker uses the existing `.tc-preset-card` grid with pictures. **Nothing on the top half of a plant card is
tappable-but-dangerous.** And the existing `rangeGuard` directive — sliders inert until the row is tapped,
disarming 8 s after last interaction — stays, because it is the correct answer to a fat thumb on a scrolling
page.

**Normal.** Gets the whole card, the chart with bands, the week view, the feeding schedule, and the
`Einfach` settings mode which is already the default and already documented in German at
`docs/einfach-modus.md`. Never sees a recipe editor unless they ask.

**Techie.** The existing `Einfach | Experte` `ion-segment` survives untouched on the settings page — it is
the codebase's only progressive-disclosure precedent and it works. On top: a `Rohdaten` disclosure on
`/plants/:id` (the "view as table" affordance, which is also the accessibility answer since no OSS chart
library matches Highcharts' accessibility module and Terp does not load it today anyway), the equipment page
with per-role socket truth and IPs, `Woche exportieren` as CSV/JSON, webhook destinations for Discord and
Telegram, and — v1.1 — a documented `x-api-key` API shaped after Pulse Grow's, with **writes**, which no
monitoring vendor can offer because none of them own the hardware.

The one place all three lenses collide is the chart, and the resolution is the pinned scrub header: a stoner
sees three big numbers that change as they drag, a normal user sees them against a band, a techie reads the
panes.

## 13. Scope

**October 2026 is six to eight weeks from today (2026-08-24). Everything below is written against that.**

### 13.1 v1 — ships with the hardware

**Credibility floor (non-negotiable, roughly half the effort):**
- `devices.controller.*` i18n block, en + de; controller stops being branded a fridge; a `controller/`
  component shell replacing the two `ngSwitchCase` lines that point at `fridge-overview`.
- `POST /device/claimcode` authenticated; claim no longer silently transfers ownership.
- Capability per-role and **fail closed**; dead controls (`daynight.floating` et al.) removed.
- Live / stale / offline on every value.
- `deletedAt` semantics; "Logs löschen" actually clears the diary; server stage entries visible and editable.
- Flux parameterisation on `/data/series` + a real result bound (the `limit(n:50000)` guard is piped **after**
  `yield()` and does not bound anything).
- Cursor pagination on logs and events.
- InfluxDB retention + a downsample task (there is none today; raw 5-second samples are kept forever).
- The six live i18n bugs; translated chart series names; `express-fileupload` size limit.

**The concept:**
- `plants` + `plant_events` + backfill migration + review screen.
- `/plants` home: tent strip, plant card, capture bar, conflict banner, lead plant.
- 2-tap water / feed / note / photo, multi-plant batch, per-plant overrides, undo.
- Stage as a plant field with `stageHistory`; per-plant stage bands; `Phase` / `Grow` range presets.
- Chart in **Highcharts**: small multiples with real axes, stepped setpoint + target band + deviation fill,
  day/night shading from measured light, annotation rail, outputs state-timeline lane, min/max envelope,
  Okabe-Ito, film strip (lazy thumbnails), pinned scrub header, two-finger pan/zoom, view-as-table.
- Server: combined `{mean,min,max}` series endpoint; a **raw-sample** time-in-range endpoint.
- `plant_weeks` computed on demand + `Woche exportieren` as Markdown and CSV.
- Feed schedule engine, user-editable, three ownerless seeds. **Zero brand charts.**
- `memberships` collection + `actor_id` field (owner/grower only, minimal UI).
- Old devices untouched; `equipmentDeviceIds` attachment; tab bar hidden for type-7-only accounts.
- Bottom tab bar; `/devices`; `/device/:id/log`; `/device/:id/equipment`.

### 13.2 v1.1 — the quarter after

ECharts migration (once the licence question is answered) with `sampling:'minmax'`; sprite film strip;
`memberships` UI with `viewer` and `:self`; public `/g/{slug}` page; Markdown/BBCode generator; Discord +
Telegram + generic webhook destinations decoupled from alarms; rendered per-stage timelapses; the documented
`x-api-key` API with writes; `plant_weeks` nightly job.

### 13.3 Later / never-in-this-release

VPD temperature × RH matrix with trajectory; brand feed charts (gated on counsel); Cannanas / 420cloud
integration; Home Assistant; native app via Capacitor; service worker / offline; run-over-run grow
comparison; new socket roles (humidifier, exhaust, AC, pump) — all firmware, all gated on hardware.

### 13.4 The cut order, if six weeks becomes four

Cut from the bottom up, in this order, and nothing above a line before everything below it:
**8.** film strip → **7.** `plant_weeks` + export → **6.** feed schedule engine (leave `feed` as a `water`
event with a free-text product) → **5.** multi-plant batching (leave single-plant capture) → **4.** outputs
lane → **3.** annotation rail → **2.** the second plant (ship implicit-only: one card, no `+ Pflanze`) →
**1.** the plant card itself.

**Never cut:** the credibility floor in 13.1, the setpoint drawn on the chart, or live/stale/offline. If the
plant model has to be cut to item 2, the concept degrades into "a device dashboard with a day counter and an
honest chart" — which is still strictly better than what ships today, and the schema is already right for
when the plants arrive. **That graceful degradation is the reason `plants` ships in v1 even if the second
card does not.**

### 13.5 Testing

There are **38 `it()` blocks** in 29 spec files, 24 of them a lone `it('should create')`, CI never runs
`ng test`, and `app.component.spec.ts` cannot pass. No delivery gates on the suite. What does get tests,
written in the `grow-report.component.spec.ts` style (plain class construction with stubs):
the backfill migration, `plant_events` → chart-annotation mapping, the time-in-range calculation against a
fixture of raw samples, and the unit conversions in the feed engine — **because with nine unit systems in
active use, conversion bugs are likely and their consequences are physical.**
Every UI claim is verified end-to-end against `./simulate-device.sh`, which fully simulates a controller over
real MQTT including camera stills, socket pairing and history backfill. There is no excuse for an unverified
screenshot.

## 14. Non-goals

1. **No per-plant climate control.** One PWM output, one setpoint pair. The lead plant is named, the conflict
   is stated, and nothing is faked.
2. **No new socket roles and no firmware change in v1.** Humidifier, exhaust fan, circulation fan, AC and
   pump do not exist in the stack and this concept does not invent them in the UI.
3. **No irrigation or dosing automation.** Watering and feeding are capture-and-recall. No schedule ever
   *runs* a pump.
4. **No 2-way sync, and the word "Sync" never appears.** Export and share, both one-way, both honest.
5. **No Instagram, Threads, TikTok, Facebook or Reddit auto-post.** Ever.
6. **No "Powered by Terp Control" footer on user posts** without counsel on § 6 KCanG.
7. **No community feed, no follower graph, no real identities, no location.**
8. **No compliance system of record, no `KCanG-konform` badge, no § 26 export tooling.**
9. **No brand feed charts in v1**, and never baked into the bundle.
10. **No AI photo diagnosis.** A blurred phone picture of a leaf under 3000 K light is not a diagnostic
    input, and a wrong deficiency call costs a crop.
11. **No offline mode and no native app in v1.**
12. **No plant owning hardware.** A plant never has "its" socket; that would be a lie about the tent.
13. **No third mode switch.** `Einfach | Experte` is the only one and it stays where it is.

## 15. Honest weaknesses

**15.1 The one-device, one-plant user pays a navigation tax, and today he does not.**
`/list` currently renders the single claimed device's dashboard *directly*. C03 inserts a card between him
and his numbers. The tent strip is sticky and always visible precisely to blunt this, and the implicit plant
costs zero taps — but the frame is still there, and a critic saying *"you put a plant between a man and his
thermometer"* is not wrong. The counter-argument is that the frame buys him the day counter, the stage band,
the verdict and the week export for free. Whether that trade is good is an empirical question this document
cannot answer, and the only way to find out is to ship it behind §13.4's degradation path.

**15.2 The central conceit advertises a problem the hardware cannot solve.**
Two plants, two verdicts, one setpoint. The conflict banner is honest and it is also a permanent reminder
that the €289 controller cannot do what the app just implied it should. A device-first concept never raises
the question. I chose to raise it because the alternative — showing two target bands and letting the user
assume both are being served — is worse. But "Terp Control tells you it is failing your second plant" is a
line a competitor could use.

**15.3 Four new collections, no migration tooling, no test baseline, six weeks.**
`plants`, `plant_events`, `plant_weeks`, `feed_schedules`, plus `memberships` and `grow_settings`, in a
codebase whose house style for schema change is "optional field + read-time normaliser + idempotent
boot-time backfill" and whose CI never runs the tests. This is the single largest execution risk in the
document. §13.4's cut order exists because I expect it to be needed.

**15.4 Type 5 is served badly and Type 1 is served slightly badly.**
Type 5 (closed-loop DIY) does not want a plant; he wants an API, custom sensors and MQTT. `plantsHidden`
mitigates it, but he is the most sceptical, highest-advocacy segment in the market — the people who write
their own Go apps rather than use anyone's — and C03's root object is the wrong noun for him, in v1 and
after. Type 1 pays the §15.1 tax. **Types 2, 4 and 6 are served best**, and 3 and 7 are served
well but mostly by fixes that any concept would ship.

**15.5 The backfill will produce some wrong-looking history.**
Accounts with messy `lifecycleName` values will get plants they do not recognise, or one plant where they
expected three. The review screen catches the worst of it, but *"the app rewrote my diary"* is a support
category that C03 creates and C01/C02 do not. The mitigation — never touching the original `DeviceLog`
rows — makes it recoverable, not invisible.

**15.6 Grading a user against a band they never chose.**
Time-in-range against `GROW_STAGE_PRESETS` is advisory data we picked. Naming the source on every verdict
helps, and the priority order puts the user's own alarm thresholds first — but the default case for a new
user is the advisory band, and *"why does it say I'm at 61 %"* is a real ticket. There is no clean answer;
the honest one is that a verdict with a named source beats a chart with no verdict.

**15.7 The one moment the feature exists for is the one moment the app is worst at.**
Logging a watering happens standing in a tent, phone in one hand, watering can in the other, on the worst
wifi in the house. There is **no service worker** and C03 refuses to add one in v1. The capture sheet keeps
its payload in `localStorage` and retries on reconnect, so it is *loss-resistant*, not *offline-capable* —
and if the user closes the app before it flushes, the retry queue survives but the confirmation never
arrived, so they may log it twice. A 2-tap capture flow with no offline story is a genuine hole and it is
the first thing I would fix in v1.1 if the API work were not also queued there.

**15.8 Photo volume is a bill I am writing for someone else.**
Images live as `Buffer`s **inside MongoDB**, `express-fileupload` has no size limit, `DELETE /image/:image_id`
is never called by the webapp, and retention filters `format:'jpeg'` so `user/jpeg` blobs accumulate forever.
Plant First multiplies photo events and makes the plant gallery a headline feature. The §7 prerequisites
(size limit, downscale, wiring the delete, extending retention) are listed as prerequisites and not polish
for exactly this reason — but they are infrastructure work I am adding to an already-full six weeks.

**15.9 `plant_ids: []` is a modelling bet.**
A multikey array beats three rows for pagination, for the timeline UI and for "watered 3 plants" reading as
one thing. It is worse for per-plant queries (`$elemMatch` on a multikey index), and `overrides` keyed by
plant id is a second-class citizen that will eventually need first-class treatment when someone wants a
different *product* per plant, not just a different volume. I would make the same bet again, but it is a bet.

**15.10 The Highcharts decision could be wrong in both directions.**
Shipping v1 on a proprietary library the repo may not be licensed for, in a product marketed as
*"quelloffen"*, is a risk I am consciously deferring to the owner. And if the answer comes back "we cannot
use it", §4's entire v1 chart slips into a library migration during the launch window. The design is
library-independent, which is the only real mitigation, and it is not much of one.
