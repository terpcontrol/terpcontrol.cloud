C11 · Der Film

Der Film — the timelapse is the product

The grow is a film, the app is its editor: the timelapse is the primary artifact Terp Control produces, sensor data is its subtitle track, and everything the grower does is a mark on the strip.

A watering is 6 Taps — FAB, Gießen, three taps on the can counter, Speichern — and it stores 3 Kannen · 2,0 l · 6,0 l, the actor, the three plants and the timestamp; litres are derived and displayed, never typed, so there is no keyboard in a dark tent. A feed is 4 Taps because the medium+regime pair chosen once on the run prefills the products, and the litres come from the same counter — the stored value is the resolved total (2,0 ml/l × 6,0 l = 12 ml), not a pointer to a plan that may be edited next month. Two clocks are shown separately and honestly on every screen: the device (● Live · vor 8 s) and the picture (Bild 17:12 · vor 6 Std) — a fresh sensor reading behind a six-hour-old frame is exactly the lie this concept must not tell. The cull is stated as a number rather than hidden: 2 380 Bilder · 968 aussortiert: 611 dunkel, 244 doppelt, 98 Besuch. The dark tiles in the Verlauf strip are not decoration — three are night, one is the ±3 Min window around the Gießen mark, dropped because a human told us they were standing in the shot. · 940 ppm would append itself to the subtitle line only on a device reporting the SCD4x; this tent has no CO₂ upgrade, so there is no CO₂ lane, no CO₂ target and no empty tile.

Was anders ist / What's different
  • The picture is the home screen, not a tile. Every other finalist attaches the camera to a chart. Here the film-so-far is full-bleed with a scrub bar under it, and the numbers are one ranked verdict sentence plus three values — the subtitle track.
  • Pause is the disclosure gesture. Tapping the frame stops the film and unfolds everything known about that minute: measured vs. target vs. delta, which sockets were running, which Klappen fell within ±2 h, and the frame's own exposure, white-balance regime and sharpness verdict. Nothing is persisted, it self-cancels on play, and it hides no feature class — so it is not a mode, and there is no Einfach/Experte switch anywhere.
  • The cull is the engineering, and only Terp Control can do it. We own the lamp, so every frame carries out_light at capture; dark, flashed, duplicate, blurry and human-visit frames are dropped by a database join, not a vision model. A frame within ±3 min of a Klappe goes too, because a human told us they were standing there.
  • The deliverable is a file, not a URL. Schnitt → 60 s, 720p, H.264 yuv420p +faststart, subtitles burned into the corner, share sheet → Video sichern. Today's shipping timelapse is HEVC with no pixel format and plays for roughly half the audience.
  • One playhead, two projections. The strip and the chart render from the same cursor; drag either and both move, a thumbnail tap jumps the chart to that instant, and Nächster Moment über 30 °C → jumps the film. Correlation in both directions.
Stärken / Strengths
  • It sells the hardware. The owner's stated biggest selling point is the camera, and this is the only concept with a concrete, checkable success condition: on day 60 a buyer taps once and has a watchable 60-second mp4 in their camera roll.
  • Half a day of it ships on day one. Three ffmpeg flags fix a defect that makes today's timelapse invisible in Firefox and on Chrome/Linux. Even total failure of everything else leaves the product better than it is now.
  • The capture sheet is the best in the set. No keyboard, litres derived from taps, doses prefilled by a scheme that can say All-Mix ist stark vorgedüngt — Woche 2, noch nicht düngen, and a double-feed soft block that costs one deliberate extra tap.
  • Reveal by data gives clubs away for free. One name means no chips, no attribution, no warning; a second name switches all of it on. No club mode, no settings toggle, and no plant taxonomy for the grower who has one plant.
  • Old plugs, lamps and fans come along. Same three tabs, the existing per-type settings components hosted unmodified, and the frame area becomes a Tagesband poster that exports as PNG instead of mp4. Their share links keep resolving.
Schwächen / Weaknesses
  • Without the camera the concept collapses to a consolation prize. The camera is a €30 upgrade; if attach is a third, two thirds of buyers get a coloured bar poster as their home screen. A diary-first or chart-first concept degrades far more gracefully, and no amount of design fixes this. It is the single biggest strategic risk in the set.
  • The middle screen is a v1.1 promise, not a v1 screenshot. The ECharts migration is explicitly out of scope, so at launch this is the existing Highcharts page plus a strip, a shared playhead and day/night shading. The moving VPD band, the small multiples and the time-in-range scorecard drawn here land later — which makes the dossier's highest-leverage fix worse here at launch than in a chart-first concept. A real cost paid to buy the film.
  • Culling will throw away something the user wanted and there is no way to notice. The strip shows that frames went, not which. The human-burst rule will silently eat a legitimate 40-minute defoliation session — arguably correct, definitely surprising.
  • Pause-as-disclosure is unusual and untested. A user who never pauses never sees a setpoint. The subtitle carries three numbers, the panel carries eleven, and the bridge between them is a gesture nobody has validated. "Your techie has to discover a gesture to reach their data" is a fair hit.
  • The diary is subordinate. A written, searchable, week-bucketed record exists, but it is a list under the Verlauf tab, beneath the film. Against a diary-centric concept this one loses the record keeper, and it should.
  • Ops and privacy both get worse on purpose. Renders need a disk volume, a cleanup job and a backup answer — a second place user data lives besides Mongo and Influx. And a portable, shareable 720p film of a grow tent is a worse privacy position than a chart; 30-day expiry, stripped names and no branded footer are mitigations, not a defence. Attribution is only a label, too: anyone with the password can pick anyone's name.
