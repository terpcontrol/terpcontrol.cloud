# C41 — "Das Band"

**Family:** feeding · **Centre of gravity:** a schedule ribbon running along the grow, carrying plan and
reality on one strip.

---

## 1. Thesis

**A grow is a strip of steps you either did, changed, skipped or still owe — so draw that strip once, put it
on the same horizontal axis as the sensor data and the camera frames, and let every screen in the product be
a different height of the same strip.**

Everything in a tent that a human does is discrete and ordered: feed, water, top, flip, defoliate, harvest.
Everything the device does is continuous: temperature, humidity, light. Today's app renders the continuous
thing as a chart nobody can read on a phone and the discrete thing as a scrolling list of German log lines.
"Das Band" makes the discrete thing the primary object — a horizontal ribbon of segments beneath the
timeline — and the continuous thing its background. The ribbon is authored (a feed plan: Medium + Regime,
selected once, twelve steps) and it is *lived* (what actually happened, at what time, by whom, with what
deviation). Both are drawn on the same strip, at the same x-position, so the gap between them is a visible
horizontal offset rather than a paragraph of text.

The ribbon does **not** advance on the calendar. It advances when you log a feed: *Schritt 4 von 12*. The
calendar is a soft projection drawn to the right with a fuzzy edge, because a grower feeds Tuesday and
Friday and then goes away for a weekend, and an app that says "you are 3 days late" to somebody on a
3.5-day rhythm has taught them to ignore it. The ribbon breathes: slip a feed and the future segments slide
right; feed early and they slide left.

## 2. Information architecture

Seven routes. Five exist today; two are rewritten in place; two are new. No page is a "mode" of another page —
each is the same ribbon at a different height and time-span.

| Route | Status | The ribbon here is… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `/list` | rewritten | **Zelt** — home. Ribbon is a 120 px strip near the bottom, ±3 steps around now. |
| `/device/:id/band` | **new** | **Band** — full page. Ribbon is the whole screen, the entire grow, scrollable. |
| `/device/:id/charts` | rewritten (ECharts) | Ribbon is one lane under the value panels, sharing the crosshair. |
| `/device/:id/film` | **new** | **Film** — ribbon is the chapter index of the timelapse. |
| `/device/:id/diary` | kept, re-sourced | Ribbon flattened to a vertical reading list. Print/export lives here. |
| `/device/:id/settings` | kept + controller shell | Where the plan is authored (`/settings` → *Plan bearbeiten*). |
| `/device/:id/testmode`, `/shares`, `/account`, `/classes` | untouched | — |

**Navigation model.** Add an `ion-tab-bar` (Ionic 6 `ion-tabs`) with four items — **Zelt · Band · Film ·
Mehr** — replacing the six outline buttons currently scattered on the device overview. The existing
`ion-split-pane` + `ion-menu` stays for desktop and moves to "Mehr" on phones. `Film` is hidden when
`hardwareInfo.webcam_did` is absent (reveal by capability, not by preference); the tab bar then has three
items and does not reflow later — pairing a camera adds the tab immediately.

**The single shared piece of state across all four tabs is `cursorTime`.** It is a query parameter `t=<epoch
ms>`; it is what the crosshair, the ribbon and the film strip all read and all write. Moving the ribbon
moves the picture. Moving the picture moves the chart. This is the image-correlation mechanism and it is one
number.

**Two-level depth, never more.** Tab → segment sheet. There is no third level anywhere. A segment sheet is
an `ion-modal` with `breakpoints: [0, 0.45, 1]` — it opens at 45 % showing the commit action and the
prefilled numbers, and drags to full for the fields nobody needs most days.

**Segment glyphs.** `▓▓` filled = done as planned. `░▓` half = done but deviated (dose, volume or date
differed by >20 %). `✕` = skipped (explicitly, or auto-marked when the next step is logged past it).
`███` with a heavy border = the current step. `┆` dashed = projected future. Colour: done = `--tc-text-success`
green, deviated = the same green with a diagonal hatch (never amber — deviating is normal, not a warning),
skipped = `--ion-color-step-400` grey, current-and-overdue = `--tc-text-warning`. Colour is never the only
channel: the glyph shape carries it too.

### Stale, offline, absent

Three states, three different strings, never a silent zero. The freshness stamp is computed from
`device.lastseen` and the newest Influx point, not from "the request succeeded".

- **Frisch** (`< 90 s`): `● jetzt · 14:22`, numbers full opacity.
- **Alt** (`90 s – 10 min`): `◐ vor 4 Min`, numbers drop to 70 % opacity, the band-position bars go grey.
  Copy under the numbers: `Letzter Messwert vor 4 Min.`
- **Offline** (`> 10 min`, matching `DEVICE_ONLINE_TIMEOUT_MS` in `smart-sockets.component.ts:7`): `○ offline seit gestern 21:40`. The three numbers are
  replaced by their last values in grey with a strike-through-free `zuletzt` prefix:
  `zuletzt 24,1 °C · 58 % · 1,12 kPa`. The webcam tile keeps rendering the last still — the server already
  burns an `Offline since …` caption into it (`image.controller.ts:buildOfflineCaption`), and we localise
  that string as part of this work; it is currently English-only.
- **Kamera still**: if `webcam_did` is paired but the newest `format:'jpeg'` row is older than 2 h, the tile
  shows `Kamera meldet sich seit 5 Std nicht` on a dark plate, **and a `Kamera stumm` alarm is raised**
  (`Alarm.sensorType: 'camera_silence'`, `actionType:'info'` by default, e-mail if the user set one). This
  is the camera alarm all ten round-one concepts omitted. It is a server-side cron over
  `Image.find({format:'jpeg'}).sort({timestamp:-1}).limit(1)` per device with a paired camera — twenty lines.
- **No data at all** (fresh account, `setup` never run): the `.tc-hero` onboarding stays exactly as today,
  with the Band region replaced by the `band.noplan` card and nothing else. The ribbon does not render as an
  empty strip; an empty ribbon is worse than no ribbon.
- **No plan, but data**: the ribbon region shows the `band.noplan` card. The rest of the home screen is
  complete and useful. A monitoring-only user never sees the ribbon again after dismissing it once
  (`localStorage['tc-band-dismissed-<device_id>']`), and it returns the moment a `FeedEvent` is written by
  any route.

## 4. NO-MODES MECHANISM — die Daumen-/Nagel-Regel

**The mechanism: coarse aim commits, fine aim corrects. Nothing is hidden from anybody; the only filter is
how precisely you can put your finger down.**

Every card in this product — a ribbon segment, a value tile, a plant chip, a film chapter — obeys one rule:

1. **The card body is a single coarse target**, ≥ 88 px tall, edge-to-edge minus 16 px gutters. Touching
   anywhere in it that is not a number performs *the one obvious action*: commit the step as prefilled, open
   the picture, confirm the reading.
2. **Every rendered number, unit and product name inside it is a fine target**: an inline chip, 24 px tall
   (WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 minimum), 4 px inset from its neighbours, visually a dotted underline. Touching one
   opens an inline editor for *that field alone* — a `[−] ion-range [+]` row, i.e. the existing
   `value-edit-row` component, expanded in place above the fold.
3. **The fine path is never the only path.** Long-press (450 ms) anywhere on the card body opens the full
   sheet, where every one of those fields is a 48 px `ion-item` row. So the techie's accelerator is a
   shortcut, and the accessible route is always there.

Why this is not a mode:

- **There is no persisted state.** No `localStorage['expert']`, no segment control, no preference, no
  onboarding question. Two people can use the same phone one minute apart and get different behaviour from
  the same pixels.
- **Nothing is hidden.** The techie's numbers are on screen for the stoner too — they are just small and not
  where his thumb lands. He is not denied them; he is simply not obstructed by them. Reveal-by-depth without
  a "show more".
- **It is forgiving in the safe direction.** Impairment degrades precision, not intent. An imprecise tap
  necessarily lands on the coarse target, which is the prefilled, reversible, correct-by-default action. A
  precise tap is evidence of intention and gets you the editor. The failure mode of tremor is "you logged
  what the plan said", which is exactly what you meant.

**The stated exception, and it matters.** *Coarse = safe*, not *coarse = commit*. Where committing is the
dangerous action — the double-feed guard, deleting a plant, ending a grow — the coarse target is the
**cancel**, and the proceed action becomes the fine one. The rule is stable; what changes is which action is
safe, and that is a property of the situation, not of the user.

```
Normal segment                    Double-feed guard
┌──────────────────────────┐      ┌──────────────────────────┐
│ Bio-Grow 2 ml/L  ← fine  │      │ Anna hat A1–A3 vor       │
│ 5 L · EC 1,4     ← fine  │      │ 1 Std 30 gefüttert.      │
│                          │      │                          │
│   EINTRAGEN     ← coarse │      │   NICHT EINTRAGEN ←coarse│
└──────────────────────────┘      │   Trotzdem eintragen ←fine│
                                  └──────────────────────────┘
```

**Applied to the rest of the product:**

- **Zelt**: `24,1 °C` is a fine target → opens the raw series for temperature at `cursorTime` in the chart
  tab. The three-number block is a coarse target → opens all three panels. Progressive precision, literally.
- **Charts**: tapping a panel body (coarse) toggles it to full height; tapping the axis label (fine) opens
  the measure picker for that panel.
- **Settings**: `value-edit-row` already implements exactly this — tap the row, it expands to a slider. The
  rule is a generalisation of a component the codebase already ships, which is why this is affordable.

**Honest cost.** 24 px inline chips inside a body of text is fiddly CSS, and mis-hits will happen. Two
mitigations are non-negotiable in the build: (a) a fine tap that resolves to a field never *commits*
anything, it only opens an editor, so a mis-hit is always free; (b) the coarse target's hit area is computed
by exclusion — the card handles the tap unless the event target has `[data-fine]`, so there are no dead
zones between chips.

## 5. The chart

`/device/:id/charts`, rewritten on **Apache ECharts 6** (owner decision), tree-shaken, driven by a
hand-rolled ~50-line directive. The existing URL-query view-model is preserved verbatim so presets and
share links keep working; `applyViewParams` gains one new key, `t` (cursor time), which older presets simply
lack and which defaults to "now".

**Layout — four stacked regions, one x-axis, one `axisPointer.link`:**

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 24,1 °C   58 %   1,12 kPa       Do 04.10  14:22   │ ← pinned scrub header (no tooltip)
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Temperatur °C                                     │
│  ░░░░░░ target band ░░░░░░                        │ ← markArea
│  ┄┄┄┄┄┄┄─────┄┄┄┄┄  dashed stepped setpoint       │ ← markLine, steps at daynight.day/night
│  ▁▂▃▅▆▅▄▃▂▁ solid actual + min/max envelope       │
│  ▓▓░░░░░░░░▓▓▓  night shading                     │ ← from out_light, fallback daynight
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Luftfeuchte % / VPD kPa      (same treatment)     │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Ausgänge  ▉▉▉___▉▉▉___  Licht                     │ ← state-timeline lane
│           __▉_____▉___  Heizung                   │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Ereignisse  ● ●●  ▲     ●        │ Stufe: Blüte   │ ← annotation rail, max 2 rows
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ DAS BAND  ▓▓  ▓▓  ░▓  ✕  ███  ┆   ┆   ┆          │ ← the ribbon, as a chart lane
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ FILM      [▪][▪][▪][▪][▪][▪][▪][▪][▪][▪]         │ ← thumbnail strip, positioned by time
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Goal vs actual.** Three band sources, one at a time, always labelled in the panel header so nobody has to
guess which is drawn: `Alarmgrenzen` (hard, the user's own declared limits) > `Ziel ± Toleranz` (soft, from
`day.temperature`/`night.temperature` ± 1.5 °C, ± 7 % RH — the tolerances the grow-assistant card already
uses) > `VPD-Fenster der Phase` (advisory, from `GROW_STAGE_PRESETS[stage].vpdRange`). The setpoint is a
**dashed stepped line** that steps at `daynight.day` and `daynight.night` (seconds-of-day, UTC → rendered in
the browser's zone, with the zone named in the panel header). The fill between actual and setpoint is signed
and tinted; it is never filled to the axis, which is what today's `type:'area', threshold:null` does and
which encodes nothing.

**Day/night** is derived from measured `out_light > 0` where the series exists, falling back to the
configured `daynight` schedule, with `lights.sunrise` / `lights.sunset` rendered as gradient edges. A failed
contactor therefore appears as a *missing night band* — a diagnostic, not decoration.

**Annotations.** An under-axis rail, not columns in the plot. Row 1 = human events (feeds from the ribbon,
diary entries, conditions opened/closed), row 2 = device events (alarms, boots, config changes, firmware).
Pixel-proximity clustering at 10 px with a count badge. Stage changes are full-height dashed verticals with
the stage name at the top. Tapping a mark sets `cursorTime` — which moves the picture and the ribbon.

**Outputs** get their own state-timeline lane: region length = duration, `null` renders as a gap. Five roles
maximum, because five is all the firmware has. A role with no paired socket does not get a lane.

**Correctness.** Time-in-range for the verdict is computed **server-side on raw Influx samples**, never on
`aggregateWindow(mean)` output — averaging destroys the excursions the metric exists to count. New endpoint
`GET /data/scorecard/:device_id?from&to&measure` returning `{inRange, above, below, coverage, longestExcursionMs,
mean, min, max}` split day/night. It greys itself out below 80 % coverage and says so:
`Nur 62 % Messabdeckung — Bewertung ausgeblendet.`

The `measure`/`from`/`to`/`interval` Flux interpolation must be allowlisted before this ships; the ribbon
adds no measures, but the panel picker widens what a user can request and the injection is currently live.

## 6. The diary and multi-plant

**The diary is not a thing you write. It is what the ribbon leaves behind.** Every commit on a segment
writes a `FeedEvent`, and a projection writes the corresponding human-readable `DeviceLog` row so that
today's `LogEntryViewerModule`, the grow report, share links and the existing chart annotation pipeline all
keep working with zero changes. The diary tab reads `FeedEvent` first and falls back to `DeviceLog` for
everything older — no migration, no data loss, no "clear logs" surprise.

### New collections (all cloud-side Mongo, all optional fields, read-time normalisers, no migration tool needed)

```ts
Grow {
  grow_id: string; device_id: string;
  name: string;                       // "Herbst 26"
  startedAt: number; endedAt?: number;
  stage: DiaryLifecycleStage;         // mirrors the existing enum, plus 'harvest' (see below)
  mediumId?: string;                  // -> GrowMedium
  regimeId?: string;                  // -> FeedRegime
  canVolumeL: number;                 // default 5 — the tap-count unit
  archived?: boolean;
}

Plant {
  plant_id: string; grow_id: string; device_id: string;
  label: string;                      // "A1"  (auto: A1..A9, B1..)
  strain?: string;                    // plain text, never required
  startedAt: number; endedAt?: number;
  potL?: number; note?: string;
  archived?: boolean;
}

FeedPlan {
  plan_id: string; grow_id: string; device_id: string;
  regimeId?: string; mediumId?: string;
  steps: FeedStep[];                  // materialised copy — a regime update never rewrites history
  stepCursor: number;                 // 0-based; the ONLY advance mechanism is a FeedEvent
  observedGapDays?: number;           // median of last 5 real gaps, learned
  anchorEventAt?: number;             // last committed step's timestamp
  flipDetectedAt?: number;            // 18/6 -> 12/12 detected from out_light history
}

FeedStep {
  step_no: number;
  name: string;                       // "Blüte Woche 2"
  phase: 'veg' | 'flower' | 'flush' | 'none';
  waterOnly: boolean;                 // All-Mix weeks 1-3 are waterOnly:true steps
  doses: { productLabel: string; amount: number | [number,number]; unit: 'ml/L'|'g/L'|'ml/10L'|'Tab' }[];
  ecTarget?: number; ecBasis?: 'absolute' | 'delta_over_source';
  phTarget?: number;
  plannedGapDays: number;             // default 3.5
  note?: string;
}

FeedEvent {
  event_id: string; device_id: string; grow_id: string;
  plan_id?: string; step_no?: number;      // absent = an off-plan feed, which is legal
  at: number; actor_id: string;
  plantIds: string[];                       // ['*'] = "the whole tent"
  volumeL: number; canCount?: number;       // tap count -> volumeL = canCount * canVolumeL
  doses: { productLabel: string; amount: number; unit: string }[];
  ec?: number; ph?: number; runoffEc?: number; runoffPh?: number; waterTempC?: number;
  status: 'done' | 'skipped' | 'changed';
  images?: string[];                        // image_id, same convention as DeviceLog
  note?: string;
  idempotencyKey: string;                   // unique index — see §7
  correctedBy?: string;                     // event_id of a compensating correction; never edited in place
}

Condition {                                  // the thing pinned to a tent door
  condition_id: string; device_id: string;
  text: string;
  openedBy: string; openedAt: number;
  closedBy?: string; closedAt?: number;
}

Actor {                                      // device-scoped roster. NO accounts, NO auth change.
  actor_id: string; device_id: string;
  name: string; colorIndex: number; createdBy: string; archived?: boolean;
}
```

Content collections, hot-swappable, never in the bundle, each row carrying `source_url` + `retrieved_at` +
`chart_version` + `kind`:

```ts
GrowMedium  { medium_id, label, brandLabel?, preFertilised: 'none'|'light'|'heavy',
              feedStartWeekHint: number, source_url, retrieved_at }   // "Biobizz All-Mix", heavy, 3.5
FeedRegime  { regime_id, label, brandLabel?, mediumIds: string[], steps: FeedStep[],
              source_url, retrieved_at, chart_version, kind }
```

**Harvest.** `DiaryLifecycleStage` gains `'harvest'` between `flowering` and `drying`. It is additive, the
existing six values keep their meaning, and `convertEventsToGrowCycles`'s ordinal-rollback logic keeps
working. Harvest wet/dry weight is a field on `Grow` (`wetG?`, `dryG?`) that is **never prompted** and only
appears if the user types it. Privacy posture, §9.

### How a user who ignores plants is untaxed

- **`Plant` is created lazily.** A tent with no `Plant` rows has `plantIds: ['*']` on every `FeedEvent`,
  rendered as the word `alle` and nothing else. No chips, no picker, no column, no empty state advertising
  a feature.
- **The plant picker appears at the moment a second plant exists** — and the only way a second plant comes
  into existence is that the user typed a label. Reveal by data, exactly as the brief asks.
- The `Kontostand` sheet (§9) collapses to the tent when there is one plant: `Dieser Zyklus: 38 L Wasser,
  Bio-Grow 46 ml`. Same sheet, same code path, no branch in the UI.

## 7. Watering & fertilising — capture with tap counts

This is the interaction the whole concept exists to make good. **Median case: one tap. Worst case:
eleven taps and zero keyboard.** The keyboard never appears unless you ask for it.

### The commit sheet

Tapping the current segment's body opens the sheet at 45 %:

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ═══                                       │
│ Schritt 5 · Blüte Woche 2                 │
│                                           │
│ Bio-Grow   2 ml/L      Bio-Bloom  3 ml/L  │  ← fine targets
│ EC-Ziel 1,4      pH 6,3                   │  ← fine targets
│                                           │
│ Wasser                                    │
│  [ − ]   ▮▮▮ 3 Kannen · 15 L    [ + 1 ]   │  ← TAP COUNT, 88px row
│                                           │
│ Pflanzen                                  │
│  ( alle )  A1  A2  A3  B1  B2             │  ← tap as you go
│                                           │
│ Wer?  [ Anna ] [ Ben ] [ ich ]            │  ← only if roster.length > 1
│                                           │
│ ┌───────────────────────────────────────┐ │
│ │            EINTRAGEN                  │ │  ← coarse, 88px, --ion-color-success
│ └───────────────────────────────────────┘ │
│  Überspringen        Foto                 │  ← fine
└───────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**The tap count is the volume input.** `canVolumeL` is set once per grow (default 5 L; the setup step asks
`Wie groß ist deine Gießkanne?` with 3 / 5 / 10 / andere). Then `[+ 1]` is an 88 px target you hit once per
can, in the dark, with one hand, while the other holds the can. The tally renders as filled bars `▮▮▮` plus
the derived litres, so both the person who thinks in cans and the person who thinks in litres reads their
own unit off the same widget without a setting. `volumeL` is the stored truth; `canCount` is stored too so a
later change of can size does not rewrite history.

**Plant chips are also tap counts.** You walk the row and tap A1, A2, A3 as you pour. Selected chips fill
green. `alle` is preselected and clears the moment you tap an individual plant. The sheet stays open while
you do this — closing the sheet does **not** discard: the draft is written to `localStorage` under
`tc-draft-<device_id>-<step_no>` on every tap and the segment shows `Entwurf: 2 Kannen, A1 A2` until it is
committed or explicitly discarded. This is the "put the phone down mid-water" case, and it is the single
most common way a capture is lost today.

**Schedule following.** The doses shown are `FeedPlan.steps[stepCursor].doses`, prefilled, always editable
(fine targets). Deviating is normal: any field the user changes is recorded in `FeedEvent.doses` as the real
value, and the event's `status` becomes `'changed'`, which draws the half-filled `░▓` glyph. Nothing scolds.

If the step is `waterOnly: true` — All-Mix weeks 1–3, BioTabs between re-tabs — the sheet has no dose rows
at all and the copy reads:
`Nur gießen. All-Mix ist vorgedüngt — Düngen beginnt ab Schritt 4.` That sentence is our own paraphrase,
never a brand's prose.

**EC honesty.** `ecBasis: 'delta_over_source'` (CANNA-style `EC+`) renders as
`EC-Ziel 1,4 + Leitungswasser` and, if the user has ever entered a source-water EC (`Grow.sourceEcMs`), as
`EC-Ziel 1,8 (1,4 + 0,4)`. We never render a bare `ppm` number, because 500/640/700 scales differ by 40 %
for the same liquid. If a user types ppm we ask which scale, once, and store EC.

**Advancing.** Committing writes the `FeedEvent`, sets `FeedPlan.anchorEventAt = at`, increments
`stepCursor`, recomputes `observedGapDays` as the median of the last five real gaps, and re-projects every
future segment as `anchorEventAt + Σ max(plannedGapDays, clamp(observedGapDays, 0.5×planned, 1.5×planned))`.
Skipping increments the cursor and writes `status:'skipped'` with no dose. Logging a feed with no plan (the
`+` in the Band tab) writes a `FeedEvent` with no `step_no` and draws an extra segment between the planned
ones — an off-plan feed is a first-class citizen, not an error.

**The 12/12 flip anchor.** A server task scans `out_light` history for a sustained photoperiod change
(daily lights-on hours dropping by > 3 h and holding for 3 days) and writes `FeedPlan.flipDetectedAt`. The
ribbon then offers, once, as a card and not a dialog: `Lichtwechsel am 12.09. erkannt — Plan auf Blüte
Woche 1 setzen?` This is the closed-loop bit no bottle-brand calculator can do, because they have no device.

**Double feed.** At commit, before writing: query `FeedEvent` for the same `device_id` where
`at > now − doubleFeedWindowH` (default 12 h, editable per grow) and `plantIds` intersects. If non-empty,
the guard card replaces the sheet content:

> `Anna hat A1–A3 vor 1 Std 30 gefüttert.`
> `[ NICHT EINTRAGEN ]` ← coarse
> `Trotzdem eintragen` ← fine

Note the inverted Daumen/Nagel-Regel from §4. One deliberate fine tap proceeds; the resulting event carries
`note: 'zweite Gabe, bestätigt'` so the record shows it was a decision.

**Offline.** Add `@angular/service-worker` (a real new dependency and a build change — flag it). The commit
POST is queued in IndexedDB with an `idempotencyKey = sha1(device_id + actor_id + floor(at/60000) +
plantIds.sort().join() + (step_no ?? 'x'))` and a **unique index** on the collection. A retry after a cellar
reconnect therefore cannot double-log; the second insert returns the first event. Corrections are
compensating events (`correctedBy`), never in-place edits — which also gives the club an honest audit trail
without making anything undeletable.

## 8. Camera, timelapse and image correlation

**Headline feature, and the ribbon is what makes it different: a timelapse chaptered by what you did to the
plant.** Not "1d / 1w / 1m", which are calendar windows nobody cares about — *"Schritt 4 bis Schritt 7"*,
*"die ganze Blüte"*, *"seit dem Umtopfen"*.

### What ships in v1: "Der Film"

A tab. Three things on it.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  FILM                                        │
│  ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐  │
│  │                                        │  │
│  │      [ <video controls playsinline> ]  │  │
│  │                                        │  │
│  └────────────────────────────────────────┘  │
│  ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░  0:14 / 0:38      │
│  Kapitel: ▏S1 ▏S2 ▏S3 ▏S4 ▏S5   Stufe ▏Blüte│  ← chapter marks = ribbon segments
│                                              │
│  24,1 °C · 58 % · Tag 47 · 04.10. 13:04     │  ← the numbers of the frame on screen
│                                              │
│  Zeitraum   [ Ganze Blüte ▾ ]                │
│  [  FILM ERZEUGEN  ]     [ SPEICHERN ]       │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The `Zeitraum` picker is populated from the ribbon: `Letzte 7 Tage`, `Seit Schritt 4`, `Schritt 4 → 7`,
`Ganze Blüte`, `Ganzer Lauf`, `Eigener Bereich`. `FILM ERZEUGEN` posts a `FilmJob` and returns in 20–90 s
for a 12-week film on the existing hardware (measured basis: today's monthly timelapse already runs ffmpeg
over ~1,400 frames within the hourly budget).

**A video that someone can actually hold:**

- **Codec.** `libx264 -preset slow -crf 23 -pix_fmt yuv420p -movflags +faststart -r 24 -vf scale=1280:-2`.
  This is a change from today: `image.service.ts:convertRtspStreamImagesToVideo` currently emits **libx265**,
  which does not play in Firefox at all and needs OS-level HEVC support in Chrome on Windows. The current
  timelapse feature is partly invisible for that reason alone, and fixing it is a four-line diff.
- **Filename.** `terpcontrol_zelt-nord_blaubeere_tag12-58_2026-10-04.mp4` — slugified device name, slugified
  strain if a `Plant.strain` exists, day range, build date. Served by a new
  `GET /image/film/:device_id/:film_id.mp4?token=<imageToken>` with
  `Content-Disposition: attachment; filename="…"; filename*=UTF-8''…` and `Accept-Ranges: bytes` so the
  `<video>` element can seek.
- **How it leaves.** Three routes, because one is not enough:
  1. `SPEICHERN` calls `navigator.share({ files: [new File([blob], name, {type:'video/mp4'})] })` when
     `navigator.canShare({files})` — this reaches the iOS and Android share sheets, i.e. the camera roll,
     WhatsApp, Telegram. This is the primary path and it is the only one that actually works on iOS Safari.
  2. Fallback: `<a download>` with an object URL (Android Chrome, all desktop).
  3. Fallback of the fallback (old iOS): the video is already rendered inline; copy reads
     `Video lange antippen → „Video sichern".` We say it rather than pretending the button works.
- **Size.** 1280 px wide, CRF 23, 24 fps, ~900 frames → 8–20 MB. Under Telegram's 10 MB-by-URL limit only
  sometimes, so the Telegram/Discord destination attaches by multipart, not by URL.

### Content: making it watchable

A tent timelapse is mostly garbage. Frames are scored **once, at ingest**, by a `sharp` pass costing ~8 ms
per frame, and the scores are stored on the existing `Image` document as new optional fields (legal: Mongo,
not device config):

```ts
Image.qc?: {
  lum: number;        // mean luminance 0-255 of a centre crop
  contrast: number;   // stddev of luminance
  sharp: number;      // variance of a 3x3 Laplacian, downsampled to 320px first
  dhash: string;      // 64-bit perceptual hash, hex
  lightsOn: boolean;  // out_light > 0 at this timestamp, joined at scoring time
  keep: boolean;      // the culling verdict
}
```

The culling cascade, in order, each rule with a named failure it fixes:

1. **Lights off** → drop. `lightsOn === false`. Removes ~50 % of frames and all the black ones. *(Fixes: the
   film being half darkness.)* A user can re-include them with `Nachtaufnahmen zeigen` in the range picker;
   they then render as a deliberate short dark passage rather than half the runtime.
2. **Exposure outlier** → drop. `|lum − rollingMedian(lum, 7d lights-on)| > 55`. *(Fixes: tent door open,
   room light on, flash.)*
3. **Blur** → drop. `sharp < 0.35 × rollingMedian(sharp, 7d)`. *(Fixes: condensation on the lens, fogged
   optics after a watering.)*
4. **Human in shot** → drop. `hamming(dhash, previous kept dhash) > 22` **and** the next frame's hash
   returns to within 10 of the previous kept one. A person is a large transient change that reverts; growth
   is a small change that persists. *(Fixes: a hand in shot, an arm across the lens.)* This is the one
   heuristic that will misfire — on the day a plant is topped or defoliated, the change is large *and*
   permanent, so it correctly stays; on a day a fan blows a leaf across the lens for two frames, it
   correctly goes.
5. **Near-duplicate** → drop. `hamming < 3` from the previous kept frame **and** the target frame count is
   already met.
6. **Even spacing** → keep one per bucket. Buckets are `(range / targetFrames)` where
   `targetFrames = clamp(range_days × 24, 300, 1200)`; within a bucket pick the highest `sharp`. Prefer
   frames at the same *time of day* (nearest to the median lights-on midpoint) to reduce flicker.

**Normalisation** across twelve weeks, applied at encode time, per frame, with `sharp`:

- **Luminance**: `.linear(a, b)` where `a = targetLum / lum`, clamped to `[0.7, 1.45]`, targetLum being a
  smoothed spline of the kept frames' luminance (so the film gets brighter as the plant fills the tent, but
  without the frame-to-frame flicker).
- **White balance**: grey-world channel gains computed on a centre crop, then **pulled 60 % of the way**
  toward the film's median gains via `.recomb()`. 100 % correction makes magenta LED grow look like an
  office; 0 % makes the day a door was opened look like a different planet. 60 % is a judgement and should
  be tested against three real grows before launch.
- **Deflicker**: a 5-frame moving average on the final gain values. Cheap, and it removes the strobing that
  makes tent timelapses unwatchable.

**Stabilisation, honestly.** v1 does **not** warp frames. Instead, camera movement is *detected*: if the
dHash distance between consecutive kept frames exceeds 28 and stays shifted, the film inserts a **hard cut**
and a chapter mark labelled `Kamera verstellt · 12.09.` A cut is truthful; a bad warp is a smear. `ffmpeg`
`vidstabdetect`/`vidstabtransform` is a v1.1 item and needs the `libvidstab` build flag, which the current
container image does not carry.

### Image correlation — the actual mechanism

One state variable, `cursorTime`, in the URL as `t`.

- **Chart → picture**: dragging the crosshair on any panel sets `cursorTime`; the film strip lane scrolls to
  and highlights the nearest frame; the Zelt tab's webcam tile, if visible, swaps to that frame.
- **Picture → chart**: scrubbing the `<video>` maps playback position → source frame timestamp (the film job
  stores a `frames: number[]` index of source timestamps alongside the mp4) → `cursorTime` → the numbers
  under the video update, and the chart tab opens at that instant.
- **Ribbon → both**: tapping a done segment sets `cursorTime` to its `FeedEvent.at`. So *"show me what the
  tent looked like when Anna fed on the 12th"* is one tap, and the temperature at that moment is on screen
  next to it.
- **Delivery**: the film strip lane is served as **one sprite sheet plus a JSON index per window**
  (`GET /image/strip/:device_id?from&to&n=60` → a 60-tile 256×160 JPEG grid + `{timestamps:[…]}`), not 60
  image requests. `Image.timestampEnd` already marks mp4 rows as spanning a range; those render as a bracket
  on the strip, not a point.

**The camera alarm** (§3) is part of this feature, not an afterthought: a camera that stopped three days
into flower must say so, because the whole value proposition is a film you only watch at the end.

## 9. Clubs

**Posture: attribution without accounts. Zero changes to `auth.middleware.ts` in v1.**

**Attribution.** `Actor` is a device-scoped roster of names the owner types once in
`Einstellungen → Wer arbeitet am Zelt?`. No e-mail, no invitation, no password. At capture, the sheet shows
`Wer? [Anna] [Ben] [ich]` **only when `roster.length > 1`** — a solo grower never sees the row. The choice
is remembered per browser for 12 h (`localStorage['tc-actor-<device_id>']`) and then asked again, because a
club phone changes hands. `actor_id` is stamped on every `FeedEvent` and `Condition`.

This is deliberately weak: the roster is honour-system, anybody with the share link can pick any name, and
it proves nothing to anybody. It is *operational memory*, and that is what "who fed A3 on Tuesday" actually
needs. Real memberships — a `Membership` collection, `auth.middleware.ts:172`/`:207` rewritten, ~10
owner-scoped queries loosened, an account-lookup endpoint designed against enumeration — is a v1.1 item and
the `actor_id` field upgrades into it by pointing at a `user_id` instead of an `Actor`.

**The double-feed problem** is answered in §7 and is the single most important club feature here: the
capture sheet queries recent events for overlapping plants *before* writing, and the guard names the person
and the elapsed time. Two additional supports:

- **Live presence.** When any client opens the commit sheet, it PUTs a 10-minute lease
  (`FeedLease {device_id, actor_id, plantIds, until}`). Other clients render `Ben ist gerade am Zelt` above
  the ribbon. No websockets: the home screen already polls; this rides the same poll. A lease is advisory —
  it never blocks.
- **Idempotent capture** (§7) so an offline retry cannot masquerade as a double feed.

**The cumulative question.** Tapping a plant chip anywhere opens `Kontostand`:

```
A3 · Blaubeere · Tag 58
Dieser Zyklus
  Wasser        38 L      (11 Gaben)
  Bio-Grow      46 ml
  Bio-Bloom    120 ml
  Top-Max       18 ml
Zuletzt         vor 2 Tagen, Anna
Von             Anna 7 · Ben 3 · du 1
```

It is a single aggregation over `FeedEvent` where `plantIds` contains the plant and `at >= Grow.startedAt`.
The same sheet, unlabelled by plant, answers it for the tent.

**Conditions.** A standing open fact with an owner, pinned above the ribbon on every tab:

```
Offen am Zelt
• CO₂-Flasche fast leer — seit Di, Anna          [ erledigt ]
• A3 nicht gießen, zu nass — seit Mi, Ben        [ erledigt ]
[ + Notiz ans Zelt ]
```

`erledigt` requires an actor and writes `closedBy`/`closedAt`. Conditions are not tasks — no due date, no
assignee, no completion percentage. They are the thing that gets taped to a tent door, and modelling them as
tasks is what makes every shared-grow tool annoying.

**Privacy.** Default is the privacy-maximal posture, and it is a *default*, not a mode: harvest weight and
plant count are never prompted and never appear unless typed; no location of any kind is collected or
inferred; the roster holds free text so `A.` and `Grüner Daumen` are as valid as `Anna Müller`; the diary
export defaults to `Anonymisiert` (actors become `Person A`, `Person B`) with `Pseudonymisiert` and
`Klarnamen` as the other two options; and `Konto → Alles löschen` wipes the grow, its events, its plants and
its images in one action with one confirmation. We do not store per-member dispensing, we do not claim
KCanG conformity anywhere in the UI, and we do not become the club's system of record. What we offer a club
is a defensible answer to *"who did what to which plant"* — which is § 17 Abs. 2 territory — and nothing
that walks toward § 26.

## 10. Old devices (type 7) — fan, light, plug

A fan/light/plug owner has no camera, no plants, no nutrients and no grow. **They still get the ribbon,
because their device already has a plan.** This is not a metaphor stretch: `plug` settings carry
`settings.timer.timeframes[] = {ontime, duration}` (`webapp/src/app/devices/plug/settings/settings.component.html:125`)
and hysteresis pairs `settings.heater.day.on/off`, `settings.cooler.night.on/off`. Those *are* a schedule and
a target band. Nothing today draws them against what the relay actually did.

**What a plug owner sees**, at `/list`, on the same layout as §3:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Steckdose Keller             ● jetzt · 14:22│
├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│   19,4 °C                    Relais: AN      │
│   Heizen ab 18,0 · aus ab 21,0               │  ← the band, as text and as a bar
├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  PLAN & WIRKLICHKEIT              24 Std     │
│  geplant  ░░░▓▓▓▓▓▓░░░░░░░░▓▓▓▓▓░░░░         │  ← from timer.timeframes
│  echt     ░░░▓▓▓▓▓░░░░░░░░░▓▓▓▓▓▓░░░         │  ← from out_relais history
│           06:00        12:00        18:00    │
│  Zwei Abweichungen · 11 Min kürzer            │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Concretely, per type:

- **Plug**: planned lane = `timer.timeframes` expanded over 24 h; actual lane = `out_relais` state timeline
  from Influx. Deviation summary in one German sentence. Hysteresis thresholds are drawn as a target band on
  the temperature panel in the charts tab, using exactly the §5 machinery.
- **Fan**: planned lane = day/night `settings.day.fixed`/`settings.day.max` speed; actual = `rpm`. The
  "plan" for a fan in `mode: fixed` is a flat line, which is fine — the ribbon shows a flat line and the
  actual rpm against it, which is how you notice a stalled fan.
- **Light**: planned lane = the `sunrise`/`sunset`/`limit` envelope over 24 h; actual = `out_light` PWM
  percentage. A failed contactor is a missing block. There is no DLI today; adding it here is
  `PPFD × photoperiod / 1e6` and only possible where a VEML7700 exists, so it appears only for devices that
  report `lux` — reveal by capability.
- **Dryer**: untouched. `AGENTS.md` says the type is dead.

**What they do not see**: no Film tab (no `webcam_did`), no Band tab (no `Grow`), no plants, no nutrients,
no actor roster. The tab bar for a plug is **Zelt · Verlauf · Mehr**. Their existing settings pages are not
rewritten at all — the only edits to `plug/`, `fan/`, `light/` in this concept are (a) the overview card
gains the freshness stamp, (b) the overview card gains the plan-vs-reality strip, (c) six raw i18n keys get
fixed (`settings.limits.overtemperature.enabled`, `.undertemperature.enabled`, `.time.enabled`,
`devices.plug.settings.heater-day`/`-night`). That is roughly two days of work and it is the entire type-7
budget.

## 11. Per-user-type walkthrough

**Type 1 — monitor-only.** Opens the app maybe weekly. Sees three numbers, a freshness stamp and a verdict
sentence: `Seit 6 Std im Zielbereich`. The Band region shows the dismissible `noplan` card once and then
never again. `deviceControlCapability()` returns `'monitor'` so no target rows, no dehumidifier tile, no
humidity alarm preset appears anywhere. His charts tab has two panels and a scorecard. He is never shown a
control for kit he does not own — which today he is.

**Type 2 — monitor + camera.** The one this concept is loudest for. He gets the Film tab, and at the end of
twelve weeks he presses `FILM ERZEUGEN · Ganzer Lauf` and gets an mp4 with his strain in the filename that
lands in his camera roll through the share sheet. Along the way, the picture on the home screen is the
largest single element after the numbers, and scrubbing the chart moves it. The camera-silence alarm means
he finds out on day 3 rather than on day 84 that the lens fogged.

**Type 3 — partial control (CO₂ or heating only).** `parseSocketRoles(hardwareInfo.sockets)` drives the UI
per-role, not through the three-bucket collapse that currently fails open. A heater-only tent gets a
temperature panel with a target band and a heater output lane, and *no* humidity target row, *no*
dehumidifier lane, *no* "Entfeuchter läuft dauerhaft" alarm preset. His Band is the plug-style
plan-vs-reality strip for the heater. When `hardwareInfo.sockets` is absent (old firmware) we fail **closed**
to `monitor` and show one line: `Gerät meldet keine Steckdosen — Firmware aktualisieren.`

**Type 4 — full tent.** Five roles is all the firmware has; we do not pretend otherwise. He gets four value
panels, up to five output lanes, the setpoint/band/deviation encoding on each, the scorecard split day/night,
and the Band as a feed plan. The honest gap stays honest: there is no humidifier role and
`out_dehumidifier` doubles as the cooler, so the output lane for it is labelled by the current workmode
(`Entfeuchter` in `small`, `Kühlung` in `temp`) with a footnote saying it is one socket. Nobody has drawn
that distinction for him before.

**Type 5 — closed-loop DIY.** He does not want our sheet; he wants the numbers. Every `FeedEvent`,
`Condition` and `Plant` is readable and writable over `POST /device/feedevent/:device_id` with the same
`idempotencyKey` semantics, documented in the existing Swagger annotations that already cover every route in
`device.route.ts`. His own EC probe posts a `FeedEvent` with `ec` and no doses; it appears on the ribbon as
an off-plan mark and on the chart as an annotation. He also gets the raw series view behind every fine
target. What he does **not** get in v1 is a write path to outputs — that is unchanged and we say so.

**Type 6 — club.** Roster, actor stamps, the double-feed guard, presence leases, `Kontostand` per plant,
conditions on the tent door, and an anonymised date-range export. Everything works with one login shared
across a WhatsApp group, which is what clubs actually do, and none of it requires the auth rewrite. What he
does not get is per-member dispensing, and we tell him why in one sentence in the export dialog.

**Type 7 — old device owner.** §10. His app gets faster, gains a freshness stamp and a plan-vs-reality strip,
loses six raw translation keys, and otherwise does not change. Nothing is taken away and nothing new is
demanded of him.

  single "raw" panel.
- **Harvest weights UI.** The field exists; nothing prompts for it.

## 13. Honest weaknesses

1. **The ribbon is dead weight for the user who never feeds on a plan.** Types 1, 2 and 7 are a large share
   of the audience and for them the concept's centre of gravity is a card they dismiss. The home screen is
   designed to survive that — numbers, verdict, picture, and the ribbon region collapses — but a concept
   whose headline feature is dismissible by half the users is fragile, and a critic is right to say so.
2. **The Daumen/Nagel-Regel is the whole no-modes answer and it is untested.** It is a plausible mechanism,
   it maps impairment to safety in the right direction, and I believe it — but 24 px inline chips
   interleaved with an 88 px commit target is genuinely hard CSS on a 360 px screen, and one mis-hit that
   commits something wrong destroys trust in the mechanism permanently. It needs a hallway test with three
   people before a line of it is built, and the fallback if it fails is unattractive: the fine targets
   become a long-press-only accelerator, which weakens the "nobody is denied the number" claim.
3. **Two strips is one too many.** The ribbon and the film strip are both horizontal lanes on the same axis
   and they will be confused with each other. I have given them different heights, different content and
   different colours, but on a phone at 2 a.m. that may not be enough.
4. **Frame culling will throw away a frame somebody wanted.** The "human in shot" heuristic drops large
   transient changes; a grower who reaches in, removes a leaf and leaves is a change that is transient in
   the frame but permanent in the plant. The film will occasionally skip the interesting moment. There is no
   undo for a culled frame in the film (the source jpeg survives, so it is recoverable — but only if
   somebody notices).
5. **The 60 % white-balance pull is a made-up number.** So is the 55-luminance outlier threshold, the 0.35
   blur ratio and the 22/28 hamming distances. They are the right *shape* of parameter and every one of them
   needs tuning against three real grows under magenta LED. Shipping them untuned produces a film that is
   subtly worse than no normalisation at all.
6. **`observedGapDays` learning can drift somewhere silly.** Feed twice in one day to correct a mistake and
   the median moves; the projection then pulls the whole future ribbon left. It is clamped to ±50 % of the
   planned gap, which bounds the damage, but "the app moved my schedule and I do not know why" is a real
   failure and the clamp is a patch over it, not a design.
7. **Attribution without accounts is theatre if anyone wants it to be.** Anybody can pick any name. For a
   club that trusts itself this is exactly right and costs two days; for a club that does not, it is worse
   than nothing because it looks like a record. The export dialog says so, but people do not read export
   dialogs.
8. **Off-plan feeds break the clean ribbon.** A user who logs six unplanned waterings between step 4 and
   step 5 gets a ribbon crowded with segments that carry no plan. It stays *correct*, but the "Schritt 4 von
   12" clarity that sells the concept is exactly what dissolves under real use by a hand-waterer who waters
   daily. The mitigation — collapsing water-only off-plan events into a small `💧×6` cluster glyph — is
   already a compromise on the concept's own thesis.
9. **The chart migration is the single biggest schedule risk in the plan and it is not the concept.** Twelve
   days is optimistic for replacing a 1205-line component while preserving a URL format that is persisted
   user data in share links and presets. If it slips, it takes the ribbon-as-a-chart-lane and the film strip
   with it — and then the image-correlation story ships only on the Film tab.
10. **No service worker means the cellar case is unsolved in v1**, and the brief explicitly lists it as one
    of five things every round-one concept missed. I am solving four of the five and deferring this one.
