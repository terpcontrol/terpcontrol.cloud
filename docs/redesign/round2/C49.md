# C49 — "Die Tür"

**Family:** club · **Centre of gravity:** the app is the tent door. Arriving, being there, leaving.

Written against `NEW-BRIEF.md`, `00-DOSSIER.md`, `00-OWNER-DECISIONS.md`, `00-ORCHESTRATOR-NOTES.md`,
and direct reads of `/home/user/terpcontrol.cloud`. Two shipping defects found by reading and used below:
the timelapse encoder and the image index. Both cited with file and line.

---

## 1. Thesis

**Terp Control's home screen is not a state — it is a difference: what changed since you were last at
the tent, what needs doing while you are here, and what the next person needs to know.**

Every competitor's home screen answers "what is the temperature". That question is already answered by the
display on the front of the controller, and nobody opens an app to read a number they could read by walking
into the room. The question a person actually arrives with is **"what did I miss?"** — and its twin, on the
way out, **"what do I owe the next person?"** A club has an obvious next person. A solo grower's next person
is themself in four days, which is why this frame serves them too: the diff between Tuesday and Friday is
exactly the thing a grower squints at their own photos to reconstruct.

Framing the app as a threshold has one consequence that makes it more than a metaphor. **A difference over
time, rendered visually, is a timelapse.** Die Tür does not put a camera tile on a dashboard; the arrival
band's headline artifact *is* a clip — "du warst drei Tage weg, hier sind sie in elf Sekunden" — cut at the
boundary of your own absence rather than at an arbitrary rolling 1d/1w/1m window. The visit boundary gives
the timelapse a start, an end, and a reason to exist. That is why this concept can treat the camera as the
headline the owner says it is, without bolting anything on.

The second consequence: a threshold is where the handover happens. A door is the surface things get pinned
to. The double-feed, the standing condition, the "wer war das?" — all of them are door problems, and all of
them are solved by the same object.

## 2. Information architecture

Four routes. Two are new; two already exist and are kept.

| Route | State | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `/device/:id` | **new** (today `/list` renders the dashboard inline) | **Die Tür.** The home screen. One vertical document, three bands, always in this order. |
| `/device/:id/verlauf` | **new** (replaces `/charts` route content, keeps `/charts` as an alias) | The chart, the film strip, the annotation rail, the time-in-range card. |
| `/device/:id/diary` | kept | The visit ledger. Every visit is one page. Existing reports stay reachable. |
| `/device/:id/settings` | kept, untouched for old types | Existing `ngSwitch` per `device_type`. Die Tür links into it. |

**Navigation model.** No tabs. The existing `ion-menu` stays. Die Tür is the root of a device and the other
three are reached from a single row of three text links pinned under the door's header:
`Verlauf · Tagebuch · Einstellungen`. Back always returns to the door. The device switcher, absent today,
becomes the door's title: tapping `Zelt 1 ▾` lists claimed devices.

**There is no page for a plant, a person or a note.** They are all reached by tapping their chip on the door,
which opens an `ion-modal` sheet. This keeps the route table at four and means a new entity never costs a
route, a module and a lazy chunk.

**Why `/charts` survives as an alias:** `ShareLink.query` and `ChartPreset.query` are persisted user data in
the existing URL-query format (dossier §1.6, constraint 9). `/verlauf` reads the same query grammar and
`/charts` 302s to it. Nothing in circulation breaks.

## 4. NO-MODES MECHANISM — der Dreizeiler

There is no simple mode, no expert mode, no "mehr anzeigen", no accordion, no preference, and no onboarding
question about what kind of user you are. The single mechanism is a **row grammar**, and every informational
row in the entire product — climate, care, stage, alarm, plant, socket — is written in it.

**Three lines, always all three, always rendered, always in this order, in one 64 px row:**

```
 line 1   VERDICT   17px / 600 / --ion-text-color        a German sentence, no numbers
 line 2   NUMBERS   13px / 400 / tabular-nums / step-600 value · comparison · delta
 line 3   TRACE     28px sparkline + target band          the actual series, tappable
```

```
Wärmer geworden.
24,3 °C · vorher 22,5 °C · +1,8
▁▂▃▅▆▇▇▆▇▆▅▄  Ziel 25,0
```

**Why this is not a mode.** A mode is a persistent selection that changes which features exist. Here nothing
is selected, nothing persists, and no feature is ever absent. All three layers are on screen simultaneously,
for every user, at every moment. The filter is **typographic weight and the reader's own eye**, which is a
filter that requires no decision, cannot be set wrong, and cannot be forgotten in the wrong position at 2am.

- The **stoned user** reads line 1. It is the largest, boldest thing in the row and it is a sentence.
  Four rows of line 1 read top to bottom are a paragraph: *"Wärmer geworden. Feuchte unverändert. Basti hat
  A1–A3 gegossen. Ein Alarm, behoben."* That is the whole product for them, and they never tapped anything.
- The **normal user** reads lines 1 and 2. The number is right there, one line down, in the same row, with
  its comparison already computed. They are never denied it and never made to hunt for it.
- The **techie** reads line 3, which is a real series with a real target band, and taps it. The tap opens
  `/verlauf` scrolled and zoomed to exactly that row's measure and window — the sparkline is a *link with
  coordinates*, not a decoration.

**The rule that keeps it honest:** if a piece of information cannot be written as verdict / numbers / trace,
it does not go on the door. This is a design constraint with teeth. "Firmware 2.4.1" has no verdict and no
trace, so it is not on the door; it is in settings, where it belongs.

**Two supporting mechanisms, both from the brief's allowed list:**

- **Reveal by capability.** Panels, lanes, target rows and alarm presets exist iff the device reports the
  kit. `parseSocketRoles(hardwareInfo.sockets)` gives per-role truth
  (`webapp/src/app/util/socket-info.ts:9`) — Die Tür uses the per-role list directly and **never**
  `deviceControlCapability()`'s three buckets, which fail open to `'full'` on old firmware
  (`grow-presets.ts:143-173`) and are the cause of dossier P0 #3. Absent key → render nothing controllable
  and say `Dieses Gerät meldet keine Steckdosen.` Failing closed is the correct direction.
- **Reveal by data.** No plants → no plant chips, and `CareEvent.plantIds = []` means "das Zelt". No second
  person → no `Wer ist da?`. No open notes → the `An der Tür` box is a single `+ Notiz an die Tür` line.
  No camera → no clip block. Nothing is hidden by a setting; things are absent because they are empty.

**Adaptive ordering is deliberately NOT used.** The brief warns it hurts the impaired user. Die Tür's band
order is fixed and its row order inside band 1 is *chronological*, which is the one ordering a person can
predict without thinking. The single exception: in band 2, a value currently outside its alarm threshold
moves to the front of its line and turns `--tc-text-danger`. That is one deterministic, published rule with
a visible cause, not a learned preference.

**Goal vs actual.** The setpoint is a **dashed stepped line**, stepping at `daynight.day` / `daynight.night`
(seconds-of-day, **stored UTC** — converted to the device's local offset explicitly, with the offset printed
in the panel subtitle, because a 12/12 flip near a DST boundary otherwise drifts an hour). Behind it a
`markArea` target band. Between setpoint and actual, a **signed deviation fill** — warm above, cool below.
Never fill to the axis; today's `type:'area', threshold:null` encodes nothing (`charts.page.ts`).

Band source, one at a time, labelled in the panel subtitle, in this priority: **Alarmgrenze** (the user's own
declared limit, hard edge) → **Sollwert ± Toleranz** (soft) → **VPD-Fenster der Phase** (advisory, from
`GROW_STAGE_PRESETS[stage].vpdRange`). The VPD band **changes along the x-axis** as the grow moves through
stages, drawn as a stepped `markArea` — the differentiating chart the dossier says nobody verifiably ships.

**Day/night** is a `markArea` derived from **measured** `out_light > 0` where that series exists, falling
back to the configured schedule and labelled `(geplant)` when it does. A failed contactor then reads as a
**missing dark band** — a diagnostic, not decoration. Ramp edges use `lights.sunrise`/`sunset` as a gradient.

**Annotations.** A 16 px rail *under* the axis, never columns inside the plot (today's height-1 columns are
readable only by hovering — unusable on touch, `charts.page.ts`). Two rows maximum: row 1 human events,
each dot tinted with its `DoorPerson.color`; row 2 device events by severity. Pixel-proximity clustering at
10 px with a count badge. Stage changes are full-height dashed lines with a label.

**The signature.** `Visit` windows render as a translucent full-height band across all panels. That single
element pays for the whole concept on this screen: the 2 °C spike at 21:40 stops being a mystery and becomes
"Basti stand mit offener Tür im Zelt". No competitor can draw this because no competitor records it.

**Outputs** live in a state-timeline lane (region length = duration, null = gap), never as 0/1 lines on a
value axis.

**Mobile rules.** One finger scrolls the page; two fingers pan/zoom (`dataZoom: inside`); tap or long-press
sets the crosshair. The floating tooltip is replaced by the pinned scrub header. Axis labels are **never**
hidden — below 320 px they shorten to three ticks and the unit moves into the panel title.

**Zeit im Rahmen** card under the chart: one stacked bar, `% im Band / darüber / darunter`, split
**Tag / Nacht**, plus longest excursion **as a duration** and mean/min/max. Computed **server-side on raw
Influx samples** — never on `aggregateWindow(mean)` output, which destroys the excursions the metric exists
to count. Data coverage is printed and the card greys out below 80 %.

**Sampling:** `sampling: 'minmax'` for plotted series (LTTB smooths away exactly the bang-bang oscillation a
grower is looking for) and a min/max envelope on temperature via two extra `aggregateWindow` calls.

**Prerequisite, not optional:** `from`, `to`, `interval` and `measure` are interpolated raw into Flux and the
`limit(n:50000)` guard is piped *after* `yield()` (`server/src/services/data.service.ts:80-89`). `3y × 5s` is
one dropdown away. Fix before widening the measure vocabulary.

## 6. The diary and multi-plant

### The diary is the visit ledger

**A visit produces exactly one diary page, and the page is assembled, not typed.** This is the owner's
"it does the diary keeping for you", made structural: the unit of the diary is not an entry a human wrote,
it is a *visit the app observed*.

A visit page is a **view**, not a new collection — assembled from `Visit` + `CareEvent` + `DoorNote` +
`DeviceLog` filtered to `[openedAt, closedAt]`. Zero new persistence for the page itself.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Mittwoch, 12. Juni · Basti · 18:04–18:31   │
│ ▶ Clip 6 Sek.                              │
│                                            │
│ Gegossen  A1 A2 A3 · 2 Kannen · 10 L       │
│           Bio-Grow 2 ml/L · EC 1,2 · pH 6,3│
│           [ frame from 18:12 ]             │
│ Notiz     „A3 hängt etwas"                 │
│ Zur Tür   A3 nicht giessen, zu nass        │
│ Gerät     Alarm Temperatur > 28, 39 Min    │
│                                            │
│ Für den Nächsten: „CO₂-Flasche noch offen" │
└────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The existing `entries` / `growreport` / `co2report` reports stay reachable behind the existing report
switcher. Nothing is deleted.

### New entities

All cloud-side MongoDB, keyed by `device_id`. `device.configuration` carries nothing new — the firmware
strips unknown keys (`grow-presets.ts:31-34`). Every field optional, read-time normalised, no migration tool
needed (dossier §1.8).

```ts
// collection: visits
interface Visit {
  visit_id: string; device_id: string;
  actor_id: string;                 // DoorPerson.person_id — NOT a user account
  user_id?: string;                 // the logged-in account, when there is one
  openedAt: number; lastSeenAt: number; closedAt?: number;
  didWrite: boolean;                // true once this visit produced a care event / note / setting change
  note?: string;                    // "Für den Nächsten"
  clipImageId?: string;             // the arrival clip generated FOR this visit
}

// collection: door_people  — device-scoped names, no accounts, no invitations
interface DoorPerson {
  person_id: string; device_id: string;
  name: string;                     // "Anna", "Basti", "Ich"
  color: string;                    // Okabe-Ito hex, assigned round-robin
  createdBy: string;                // user_id of the owner who typed the name
  archivedAt?: number;
}

// collection: door_notes  — the standing conditions pinned to the door
interface DoorNote {
  note_id: string; device_id: string;
  text: string;
  openedBy: string; openedAt: number;
  closedBy?: string; closedAt?: number;
  plantIds?: string[];
}

// collection: plants
interface Plant {
  plant_id: string; device_id: string;
  label: string;                    // "A1"
  strain?: string;                  // free text
  medium?: string;                  // regime_id ref, e.g. "biobizz-allmix"
  startedAt: number;
  stage: DiaryLifecycleStage;       // reuse the existing 6-value union
  stageChangedAt: number;
  potLiters?: number;
  archivedAt?: number;
}

// collection: care_events
interface CareEvent {
  event_id: string;                 // CLIENT-generated UUID at tap time — idempotency key
  device_id: string; visit_id: string; actor_id: string;
  at: number;
  kind: 'giessen' | 'duengen' | 'spuelen';
  plantIds: string[];               // [] = "das Zelt"
  tapCount?: number;                // raw taps, kept
  canLiters?: number;               // denormalised so a later can-size change never rewrites history
  totalLiters?: number;             // tapCount × canLiters
  ec?: number; ph?: number;
  doses?: { product: string; mlPerLiter: number; source: 'regime' | 'manual' }[];
  regime_id?: string; regimeStep?: string;
  frameImageId?: string;            // nearest camera frame within ±5 min, resolved at write
  note?: string;
  supersededBy?: string;            // corrections are new rows, never edits
}
```

### How a user who ignores plants is untaxed

**They never meet a plant.** With zero `Plant` rows:

- The plant-chip row does not render in the capture sheet. Capture is: open sheet, tap can twice, done.
- `CareEvent.plantIds` is `[]`, rendered everywhere as `das Zelt`.
- The door says `Zuletzt gegossen: vor 3 Tagen` instead of `A1 A2 A3 seit 3 Tagen nicht gegossen`.
- The diary, the chart annotations and the per-visit page all work unchanged.
- The word "Pflanze" appears **once**, after the third care event, as one dismissible line:
  `Pflanzen einzeln führen? [Ja] [Nein]`. `Nein` is stored on the device document and never asked again.

If plants are later created, existing `CareEvent`s keep `plantIds: []` and read as "das Zelt" forever. No
backfill, no broken history.

= one can poured. Long-press = minus one. The line under it recomputes live:
`totalLiters = tapCount × canLiters`, `litersPerPlant = totalLiters / plantIds.length`.
`canLiters` is set once per device (`door_settings.canLiters`, default 5) and denormalised onto every event.

Minimum interaction for the recurring case: **open sheet → tap bucket ×2 → Gespeichert = 4 taps.** Plants
default to the same selection as the last care event on this device, so the chips are usually already right.

**Stored:** `tapCount`, `canLiters`, `totalLiters`, `plantIds` — all four. Never only the derived litres. If
somebody later argues about the can size, the raw taps are still there.

### The double-feed soft block

Fires at **chip-selection time**, before any can taps, so it costs nothing when you were about to do the
right thing. Window default: 12 h for `giessen`, 24 h for `duengen` (`door_settings.doubleFeedWindowHours`).

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ⚠  Anna hat A1 und A3 vor 1 Std 30 Min    │
│    gegossen. 2 Kannen, 10 L.              │
│                                           │
│    [ Nur A2 nehmen ]      [ Trotzdem ]    │
└───────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

`Nur A2 nehmen` is the visually primary option. `Trotzdem` is one deliberate extra tap and is recorded:
the resulting `CareEvent` carries `note: 'trotz Warnung'`, which shows in the diary and on the annotation
rail. Nobody is blocked; everybody is told.

**Deduplication.** `event_id` is a UUID generated **on the client at tap time**, before the request. The
server upserts on `event_id`. A retried request — flaky cellar wifi, a double-tapped save button, an offline
queue flush — is idempotent. This is the answer to the brief's gap 5: in a shared record, a duplicated row
is indistinguishable from a real double feed, so it must be impossible at the protocol level rather than
prevented by UI discipline.

### Schedule following

`FeedRegime` is a **MongoDB content collection**, never bundled (owner decision 3.4). A regime is a
**medium + feed line** pair, labelled the way growers say it out loud:

```ts
interface FeedRegime {
  regime_id: string;                // 'biobizz-allmix'
  label: string;                    // "Biobizz All-Mix"
  mediumNote: string;               // "stark vorgedüngt — erste Gabe ca. Woche 4"
  startFeedingAfterDays: number;    // 24
  steps: {
    fromDay: number;                // day of the plant's life
    stage?: DiaryLifecycleStage;
    doses: { product: string; mlPerLiter: number | [number, number] }[];
    everyNDays?: number;
    note?: string;                  // "nur giessen"
  }[];
  ecBasis?: 'absolute' | 'delta_over_source';
  source_url: string; retrieved_at: number; chart_version?: string;
}
```

Selected once, per plant or per tent, in a two-tap picker. Its **only** job is to prefill. It never blocks,
never alarms against its own numbers, and every prefilled value is editable — the grower who deviates is the
normal case. `startFeedingAfterDays` is what makes All-Mix work: on day 12 the sheet's dose rows read
`Noch nicht düngen — All-Mix ist vorgedüngt. Erste Gabe ca. Tag 24.` and the dose fields are empty but
present. A scheme that cannot say "don't feed yet" is not modelling the domain.

**Both faces, from the same rows.** Band 3's reminder line (`Nach Plan: heute, Bio-Grow 2 ml/L.`) plus a
one-tap confirm is the stoner's whole experience — they never see a table of ml/L. The club's is the same
data aggregated: `Konto` on the plant sheet, `Summe Bio-Grow diesen Durchgang: 148 ml · Anna 90 · Basti 58`.
One `$group` pipeline over `care_events`, and it answers the brief's gap 3.

**Never render a bare "ppm".** Store EC canonical; if a ppm scale is ever shown, print it (`EC 1,8 ·
900 ppm₅₀₀`). Regimes with `ecBasis: 'delta_over_source'` display `EC + 1,4 (auf Leitungswasser)`.

## 8. Camera, timelapse and image correlation

The owner calls this the biggest selling point. It is the headline of this concept, not a tile.

### 8.0 Two shipping defects found by reading the code

**(a) The timelapse is encoded in a codec most browsers cannot play.**
`server/src/services/image.service.ts:564-607` encodes with `-vcodec libx265 -crf 30`, and passes **no
`-pix_fmt yuv420p`, no `-movflags +faststart`, and no even-dimension scale filter**. HEVC in MP4 does not
play in Chrome or Firefox on Android or desktop; without `faststart` the `moov` atom is written last, so
playback cannot begin before the whole Mongo `Buffer` has transferred. If the timelapse is the selling
point, **this is the first thing to fix and it is one day of work.**

**(b) `Image` has a unique index `{device_id, format, timestamp, duration}`**
(`server/src/models/images.model.ts:37`). Visit clips must therefore be written with `duration: undefined`
so they never collide with the `1d`/`1w`/`1m` rolling slots. Add an optional discriminator
`kind?: 'rolling' | 'visit' | 'export'` — optional field, no migration, index untouched.

### 8.1 What ships in v1: `Der Ankunftsclip`

On opening the door, if a camera is paired and the previous visit closed more than 6 hours ago, the top of
band 1 is a clip covering **`[previousVisit.closedAt, now]`**. It is generated on the visit-open request
(async; the block shows `Clip wird gebaut …` and swaps in when ready, typically 3–20 s).

**Frame selection — the content problem the brief says nobody solved.** Culling in v1 uses data the product
already has, so it needs no image analysis:

1. **Lights-off frames are dropped, by data not by luminance.** Query `out_light` from Influx over the
   window; drop every frame whose containing minute had light output 0. A 12/12 flower clip then contains
   12 h/day of usable footage and the night reads as continuity, not as black flicker.
2. **Ramp frames are dropped.** Frames within ±10 min of a light-output transition, and inside the
   `lights.sunrise`/`sunset` windows, have unusable exposure.
3. **Human-in-shot suppression, for free.** Drop every frame whose timestamp falls inside an open `Visit`
   window **with `didWrite === true`** on this device, and inside any `maintenance_mode_until` window. Somebody
   was at the tent: the door was open, a hand was in shot, the light was wrong. One Mongo range query.
   The precedent already exists — `cloudSettings.maintenanceWebcamOff` already suppresses *capture* during
   maintenance (`image.service.ts:212-213`); this generalises it to *selection*.
   The `didWrite` condition is what stops a read-only check from the train wrongly deleting eight minutes of
   good footage.
4. **Frame budget.** Target 24 fps × ≤75 s = **1,800 frames max**. The frame interval is computed from the
   window: `max(120_000, windowMs / 1800)`.

**Encoder, v1:**

```
ffmpeg -framerate 24 -f image2 -i %06d.jpeg \
       -vf "scale=1280:-2:flags=lanczos,format=yuv420p" \
       -c:v libx264 -profile:v high -level 4.0 -preset veryfast -crf 22 \
       -movflags +faststart -f mp4 out.mp4
```

H.264 High / yuv420p / faststart plays in every browser, every phone, and inline in every messenger. Size
for a 3-day visit clip (~700 frames, 29 s) is 4–8 MB.

**BSON ceiling, stated plainly.** Mongo caps a document at 16 MB. If the encoded buffer exceeds 12 MB, re-run
once at `crf 28` / `scale=960:-2`. If it still exceeds, refuse and show
`Zeitraum zu lang für einen Clip. [Letzte Woche zeigen]`. This is a real foundation crack — the honest
long-term fix is GridFS or object storage, and it is not v1.

### 8.2 How the video LEAVES the product

One button, `Video sichern`, with two paths:

1. **Web Share, when available.** `navigator.canShare({files:[f]})` → `navigator.share({files:[f], title})`
   puts the mp4 straight into the native sheet: WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal, Photos. ~20 lines. This is the
   difference between a share and a URL to a login page.
2. **Download, otherwise.** A same-origin `<a download>` pointing at
   `GET /image/:device_id/clip/:image_id/download?token=<imageToken>`, responding
   `Content-Type: video/mp4` and
   `Content-Disposition: attachment; filename="terpcontrol_zelt-1_2026-06-10_bis_2026-06-12.mp4";
   filename*=UTF-8''terpcontrol_zelt-1_…` (ASCII slug plus RFC 5987 form for umlauts).

Helper text under the button, verbatim and honest:
`Auf dem iPhone landet das Video in „Dateien". Von dort „In Fotos sichern".`

### 8.3 Deferred to v1.1, and why

- **Near-identical deduplication.** Downscale each candidate to 32×32 grey, mean-absolute-difference against
  the last kept frame, drop below 2/255. Also catches a frozen camera. Needs `sharp` — a new dependency.
- **Exposure and white-balance normalisation across 12 weeks.** From the same 32×32 thumbnail take mean luma
  and mean R/B ratio, compute a rolling 24-frame median, and pull each frame to it via
  `sharp(buf).linear(gain, offset).tint(...)` while writing the temp JPEG. This is what kills the
  magenta-LED-versus-daylight flicker. One extra decode/encode per frame; ~2,500 frames per weekly rebuild
  is acceptable at the existing hourly cadence — but it is the riskiest CPU item and does not belong in a
  six-week window.
- **Nudge detection instead of stabilisation.** `vidstab` needs a two-pass and a plugin build. Honest
  substitute: if the 32×32 MAD exceeds 25/255 and stays high for three frames, write a `Kameraruck` diary
  entry, mark it on the film strip, and cut the clip into two chapters there. Detect and disclose rather
  than pretend to fix.
- **`Der ganze Durchgang`** — one clip for a whole grow. Wanted for sharing, blocked by the BSON ceiling.

### 8.4 The camera alarm

The brief: nobody gave the camera an alarm. Piggyback the existing hourly `compressRtspStreams` tick: for
each device with `cloudSettings.rtspStream` set, if the newest `format:'jpeg'` timestamp is older than 3 h,
write a `DeviceLog { severity: 1, categories: ['device'], message: 'message-camera-quiet:3' }`. That flows
through the existing alarm/notification machinery and the existing `message-*` i18n convention unchanged.
Four new i18n keys, both languages. Pinned to the door as a red note with `[Testbild]` and `[Ignorieren]`.
**~40 lines. There is no defensible reason this is not in v1.**

### 8.5 Image correlation — `Der Türspalt`

A 56 px film strip under the chart on `/verlauf`, and under the clip on the door. Thumbnails are positioned
by **real timestamp on the chart's own x-scale**, so uneven capture reads honestly as gaps.

- **Delivery: one sprite, not N requests.** New endpoint
  `GET /image/:device_id/strip?from&to&count=60` → a single JPEG of 60 tiles at 160×90 (9600×90 px,
  ~120 KB) plus a JSON sibling listing each tile's timestamp. The Mux storyboard pattern.
- **Chart → picture.** Dragging the strip and dragging the chart crosshair share one `cursorTime$`
  BehaviorSubject. Moving either moves both and updates the pinned scrub header.
- **Picture → numbers.** Tapping a tile opens the full frame with the conditions at that instant **drawn
  into the corner on a canvas, client-side** — so a screenshot carries its own evidence out of the product.
- **Event → picture, at write time.** Every `CareEvent`, `DoorNote` and stage change resolves the nearest
  camera frame within ±5 min and stores its id in `frameImageId`. A feed entry in the diary therefore shows
  the plant as it looked when it was fed, with no extra capture step and no extra tap. One
  nearest-neighbour query per write.
- `Image.timestampEnd` already marks mp4s as spanning a range — those render on the strip as a **bracket**,
  not a point.

## 9. Clubs

### Attribution without an auth rewrite

`DoorPerson` is device-scoped and account-free: the owner types names once in settings. `Visit.actor_id`,
`CareEvent.actor_id`, `DoorNote.openedBy`/`closedBy` all point at it. `auth.middleware.ts:172` is not
touched, no membership collection, no invitation flow, no account-lookup-by-email endpoint. Two days of work
for attribution on every row and a colour on every annotation dot.

**Stated plainly and repeatedly, including in the UI:** this is attribution, not authorisation. Anyone with
the login can tap any name. The settings screen says
`Namen an der Tür sind Zuordnung, keine Anmeldung. Jeder mit dem Zugang kann jeden Namen wählen.`
The upgrade path is a real `Membership { device_id, user_id, role }` collection plus rewriting
`auth.middleware.ts:172` and `:207` and loosening ~10 owner-scoped queries — a separate release, and
`actor_id` survives it because a membership just becomes a stronger source for the same field.

### The double-feed problem

Section 7. The short version: soft block at chip-selection time, one deliberate extra tap to proceed, the
override recorded, and client-generated `event_id` so a retry can never fake one.

### Conditions

`DoorNote` — the brief's gap 4, and the literal thing that gets pinned to a tent door. Open fact, an owner,
an age, one tap to close. `CO₂-Flasche fast leer · Anna · seit 3 Tagen`. Notes optionally reference plants,
in which case the note surfaces **inside the capture sheet** when that plant is selected:
`📌 A3 nicht giessen, zu nass — Basti, vor 14 Std`. That is the note doing work at the moment it matters
rather than decorating a board.

### The cumulative question

`Wie viel Bio-Bloom hat A3 diesen Durchgang bekommen, und von wem?` One `$group` over `care_events` filtered
by `plantIds` and `at >= plant.startedAt`, rendered on the plant sheet as a `Konto`:

```
A3 · Purple Punch · Blüte Tag 15
Gegossen   14× · 61 L      Anna 8 · Basti 6
Bio-Grow   148 ml          Anna 90 · Basti 58
Bio-Bloom   36 ml          Anna 36
Letzte Gabe  vor 3 Tagen
```

### Privacy posture

Directly from dossier §6.3 and owner decision 4, and it is a design decision made, not deferred:

- **Die Tür stores work done in a room. It never stores grams handed to a person.** No per-member dispensing,
  no member identity, no DOB, no address, no membership number, no THC content. Those are § 26 KCanG's
  problem and arguably Art. 9 GDPR health data; a grow controller has no need for them.
- `DoorPerson.name` is free text. `Anna` may be `A`. Nothing enforces a real name and nothing ever asks for
  one.
- **No location, ever.** No GPS, no IP geolocation, no persisted timezone-from-IP. The device's local offset
  comes from its own configuration.
- **Harvest weight is not prompted.** It is an optional field on the plant sheet, empty by default, and
  reachable only if the user types it. Plant counts exist only because the user created plants.
- **The account owner can delete anything.** Nothing in Die Tür is immutable. Tamper-evidence protects a
  regulated club against its own staff and victimises an unregulated grower; if it is ever offered it must be
  an explicit club-scoped opt-in, never a default.
- Corrections are `supersededBy` rows rather than edits — a legibility choice for shared records, not an
  immutability claim, and the whole chain is still deletable.
- No analytics SDK, no ad SDK, EU hosting, and **no `KCanG-konform` badge, ever**.

## 10. Old devices (type 7) — fan, light, plug

A `fan`, `light` or `plug` owner opens the app and gets Die Tür, because **the door needs a device with a
history, not a controller.** Exactly what they see:

- **Band 1 — the diff.** Rendered from whatever measures the device actually reports.
  A plug: `Länger an als sonst.` / `14 Std 10 Min · vorher 12 Std 04 Min · +2 Std 06` / a 28 px on/off state
  trace. A light: photoperiod and dim level. A fan: rpm. The Dreizeiler grammar is measure-agnostic; nothing
  about it assumes a controller.
- **Band 2 — jetzt.** The device's live values with their age, and its paired-role line. No climate targets,
  because no climate targets exist for the type. The `An der Tür` note box works unchanged — a plug owner
  pins `Zeitschaltuhr um eine Stunde verstellt` just as usefully.
- **Band 3 — bevor du gehst.** The care button **is present**, because logging a watering has nothing to do
  with hardware. Someone growing next to a smart plug can log it in four taps.
- **The camera works.** `cloudSettings.rtspStream` sits on `Device`, not on a device type — any device can
  have a camera, so any device gets the arrival clip and the film strip.
- **Settings are not rewritten.** `fan-settings`, `light-settings`, `plug-settings` keep their components,
  their `ngSwitch` line and their route. The door links to `/device/:id/settings` and hands over.
- **Nothing is removed.** The concrete promise: every screen a type-7 owner can reach today is reachable
  after this release, at the same URL, with the same controls. They gain a diff band, a note board, a care
  button and a working timelapse; they lose nothing.
- Free fixes they benefit from anyway: the four i18n keys that render raw today in plug settings
  (`settings.limits.overtemperature.enabled`, `.undertemperature.enabled`, `.time.enabled`,
  `devices.plug.settings.heater-day`/`-night`).

## 11. Per-user-type walkthrough

**1 — Lazy monitoring-only.** The diff band *is* their product. They open the app every few days and read
four sentences: `Wärmer geworden. Feuchte unverändert. Nichts zu tun.` The numbers sit one line below,
unasked-for and unavoidable. `Zeit im Rahmen` on `/verlauf` turns their chart from decoration into a verdict.
Because no sockets are paired, no control card, no target row and no dehumidifier alarm preset renders —
fixing dossier P0 #3 for exactly this person. Band 3 shows only the note and photo buttons.

**2 — Monitoring with a camera.** The best-served type. Every arrival hands them a playable, saveable clip of
precisely the time they were away, culled of night frames and of the minutes when someone was in the tent,
in a codec their phone can actually play. The film strip under the chart means picking a picture shows its
numbers and scrubbing the chart moves the picture. And when the camera dies on day 3 of flower, they are told
within three hours instead of finding out in October.

**3 — Controls certain aspects (CO₂ or heating).** They see lanes and cards for the roles
`hardwareInfo.sockets` actually reports, and nothing else — no humidity target they cannot act on, no
dehumidifier lane. Their diff band shows the heater's duty cycle as a Dreizeiler
(`Heizung lief länger. 4 Std 20 · vorher 2 Std 55 · +1 Std 25`), which is the single most useful thing a
partial-control owner can be told and which no screen in the product says today.

**4 — Full tent.** Their door is dense: six measures plus up to five output lanes, all as Dreizeiler rows.
This is the type Die Tür is heaviest for (see §13). What they get in return is the visit band on the chart,
which explains the anomalies a full-tent owner spends their time explaining, and a handover note that means
the second person in the household stops undoing their work.

**5 — Closed-loop DIY.** Honestly served worst in v1. They get the setpoint-vs-actual chart, `sampling`,
min/max envelopes and raw-sample time-in-range — real wins — but no write API and no custom measures, because
`VALID_SENSORS` is a hard-coded allowlist (`data.service.ts:12`) and the Flux injection must be fixed first.
The *promise* Die Tür makes them is structural: `visits`, `care_events` and `door_notes` are clean,
device-scoped, foreign-keyed collections designed from day one to be exposed under an `x-api-key`, unlike
`DeviceLog`'s untyped `Mixed` bag. That is a v1.2 endpoint, not a rewrite.

**6 — Grow clubs.** The concept exists for them: attribution on every row at two days of cost, a soft block
that prevents the most expensive mistake a shared room makes, standing conditions with an owner and an age,
a cumulative per-plant per-person feed account, and a privacy posture that refuses to become the club's § 26
system of record. The two-person-household case — the far larger one — is served identically, with the
`Wer ist da?` row auto-collapsing to a single remembered line.

**7 — Old devices.** §10. They keep everything, gain a diff band, a note board and a playable timelapse, and
nothing about their settings screens is touched.

**Capability lenses.** *Stoner:* four bold German sentences, a 96 px bucket, a 64 px green button, fixed band
order, no mode to be in the wrong one of. *Normal:* the number is always on line 2, the comparison already
computed, and the clip is something to show someone. *Techie:* line 3 is a real series with a real target
band and it is a link with coordinates; the chart is small multiples with linked crosshairs, min/max
envelopes and a coverage-gated scorecard computed on raw samples.

## 13. Honest weaknesses

1. **The first visit has nothing to differ from.** A brand-new customer unboxing a €289 controller in October
   opens an app whose entire top band reads `Erster Besuch.` For a product whose home screen *is* a
   difference, that is a weak first impression at exactly the moment the impression matters most. Partial
   mitigation: the first visit's band 1 shows `seit Inbetriebnahme` with whatever hours exist. It is still
   the concept's worst moment and it is unavoidable.

2. **Opening the app is not the same as being at the tent.** A check from the train opens a visit, resets
   `lastVisitAt`, and shortens the next real arrival's diff to something the user did not expect. The
   `didWrite` flag stops read-only visits from wrongly culling good camera frames, but it cannot stop them
   from consuming the diff. The residual: **a user who checks the app often sees less interesting diffs**,
   which is precisely backwards. An escape hatch exists (`Diff seit: letztem Besuch ▾ / gestern / 3 Tagen`)
   but adding a picker to the concept's headline object is an admission that the headline object is not
   quite right.

3. **Attribution without authorisation will be attacked, correctly.** In a 20-member club with one login,
   "Anna" is a claim anyone can make. It is dramatically better than the nothing that exists today and it
   costs two days instead of a release — but a club that has a real dispute cannot resolve it with this
   data, and the UI has to say so, which undercuts the feature at the moment it is being sold.

4. **The door is long for a full-tent owner.** Six measures plus five output lanes is eleven Dreizeiler rows
   at 64 px = ~700 px of band 1 before band 3's buttons. On a 667 px phone that is two full screens of scroll
   before the actionable part. The mitigation — collapsing in-band rows' trace line to 12 px — makes row
   height depend on state, which is a mild unpredictability cost for exactly the impaired user the
   Dreizeiler exists to serve. Type 4 is the type this concept is heaviest for, and there is no clean fix
   that is not a mode.

5. **Frame culling degrades for the users who most want the camera.** Rule 1 needs an `out_light` series. A
   monitor-only owner with a camera — user type 2, the camera type — has no light output at all, so their
   clip cannot be culled by data and is full of black frames until v1.1's luma threshold ships. **The
   headline feature is weakest for the type named after it in v1.**

6. **The visit clip is not the clip people want to post.** People want "the whole grow in sixty seconds".
   Die Tür's natural unit is "the three days you missed", which is right for arriving and wrong for sharing.
   The whole-grow clip is blocked by the BSON ceiling and deferred, so in v1 the most shareable artifact of
   a camera product does not exist.

7. **The handover note is a text field, and text fields do not get filled in.** Prefilling it with the visit's
   own summary means an untouched field still hands over something useful — but the human sentence that
   actually carries the room's tacit knowledge ("A3 hängt seit Dienstag") will mostly be blank, and the
   feature will look better in this document than in a real club's data after a month.

8. **Mongo is not a video store.** The entire camera design is shaped by a 16 MB per-document ceiling, and
   every decision above — frame budget, two-pass re-encode, refusing long windows — is working around it.
   That is a foundation crack this concept builds directly on top of, and it will force a storage migration
   before the whole-grow clip can ever ship.

9. **Two new top-level concepts at once.** A reviewer can fairly say Die Tür asks a user to learn both a
   *visit* and a *door note* before the app makes sense, where a dashboard asks them to learn nothing. The
   defence is that both are objects a person already has in their head when they walk into a tent — but it
   is a defence, not a proof, and it is the first thing a usability test would go after.
