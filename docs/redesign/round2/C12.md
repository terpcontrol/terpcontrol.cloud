# C12 — "Zwei Zeiger"

*Family: camera. One time cursor drives the picture, the numbers and the events. There is no second
place where time is chosen.*

---

## 1. Thesis

**The app has exactly one control — a time cursor made out of camera frames — and everything on
screen is a rendering of the tent at the moment that cursor is on.**

Terp Control already stores three things on one clock: sensor samples in Influx, actuator states in
Influx, human and machine events in `DeviceLog`, and camera stills in `Image` — four, then. They are
joined by `timestamp` and the product has never used the join. C12 makes the join the interface. A
horizontally scrolling strip of real camera thumbnails sits fixed at the bottom of the device screen;
its centre line is `t`. Drag it and the photograph above changes, the four numbers in the header
change, the target line they are judged against changes, the output dots change, the chart's cursor
moves, the event list re-centres. Let go and it stays there. Tap `Jetzt` and it snaps back to live.

Two pointers, hence the name: the clock hand in the strip and the cursor line in the chart. They are
the same variable. There is no state anywhere in the product where a picture is displayed next to
numbers from a different moment — that is the one invariant the whole design defends.

The vertical axis is depth: one long scrolling page per device, blocks ordered by how often the
answer is needed. Horizontal = when. Vertical = how deep. Two gestures, both physical, learned in a
second, and neither of them is a mode.

## 2. Information architecture

Angular 15 / Ionic 6, NgModule. Existing `ion-split-pane` + `ion-menu` shell kept.

| Route | Guard | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `/list` | Auth | Device list. One claimed device → straight to `/device/:id`. Unchanged in kind. |
| `/device/:device_id` | Auth (+share) | **The whole product.** Fixed frame + depth stack. `?t=`, `?grain=` |
| `/device/:device_id/film` | Auth (+share) | Fullscreen player + timelapse export |
| `/device/:device_id/export` | Auth | CSV / JSON / diary export, pseudonymisation picker |
| `/device/:device_id/charts` | Auth (+share) | **301 → `/device/:id#verlauf`**, all query params preserved |
| `/device/:device_id/diary` | Auth (+share) | **301 → `/device/:id#ereignisse`** |
| `/device/:device_id/settings` | Auth | **301 → `/device/:id#ziele`** |
| `/device/:device_id/testmode` | Auth | unchanged |
| `/account`, `/shares`, `/classes`, `/demo`, `/login` | as today | unchanged |

The three redirects are the compatibility contract: every share link, every `ChartPreset.query` and
every deep link from the grow assistant keeps working. `applyViewParams` gains two optional
parameters, `t` (ms epoch, absent = live) and `grain` (`2m|1h|6h|1d`); everything existing —
`measures`, `date`, `dateEnd`, `vpdMode`, `autoUpdate`, `timespan`, `interval`, `logs`, `share` — is
read exactly as today.

**The device screen, top to bottom:**

```
├────────────────────────────────┤
│  ABLESUNG       (fixed, 88 px) │  date/verdict line · 1–4 values · outputs
│  ⚠ offene Notiz (0–2 rows)     │  Conditions, only when open ones exist

│                                │  6 Ziele

│                                │
├────────────────────────────────┤

```

Fixed frame contract, written as a rule and enforced in review: **one picture, at most four values,
one date line, at most four output dots, one grain chip, one `Jetzt` pill, one capture button.**
Conditions are the single permitted exception because they are standing facts, and they are capped
at two visible rows.

## 3. The device screen

### 3.1 Live, everything healthy

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║                                                  ║
║            [ camera frame, 38 vh ]               ║
║                                                  ║
║  Blüte Tag 41                          vor 22 s  ║   overlay, 12 px, bottom corners
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  Jetzt · alles im Ziel                           ║   17 px, semibold
║                                                  ║
║   24,1 °C      62 %      1,18 kPa     780 ppm    ║   22 px, tabular figures
║   Ziel 22,0    Ziel 55   1,2–1,5      Ziel 800   ║   12 px, --tc-text-muted
║                                                  ║
║  ● Licht 100 %   ○ Heizung   ● Entfeuchter       ║   13 px
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  Steuerung                                       ║
║  …                                               ║
```

The arrow glyph sits inside the value when out of band: `24,1 °C ↑` in `--tc-text-warning`. Shape
carries the meaning, colour is redundant (deuteranopia).

### 3.2 The verdict line changes meaning with the cursor, not with a setting

| Cursor | Line 1 reads |
| --- | --- |
| live, in band | `Jetzt · alles im Ziel` |
| live, out of band | `Jetzt · zu warm seit 40 Min` |
| live, alarm active | `Jetzt · Alarm: Temperatur über 28 °C` |
| live, device quiet | `Kein Kontakt seit 14 Min` |
| scrubbed | `Di 14. Okt · 03:12 · Tag 41` |

That is not two modes. It is one slot answering the question the cursor position implies: when you
are on *now*, the question is "how is it?"; when you are on a past moment, the question is "when is
this?". Nobody chooses; the thumb decides.

### 3.3 Stale, offline, absent

**Stale (last sample 3–10 min old).** Values render normally with the age moved into the date line:
`Jetzt · Werte von 03:04`. The picture overlay says `vor 6 Min`.

**Offline (no sample for >10 min).** Line 1: `Kein Kontakt seit 14 Min`. The four values keep their
last known content but drop to 50 % opacity and the sub-line changes from `Ziel 22,0` to
`zuletzt 03:12`. **Targets are hidden while offline** — judging a frozen number against a target is
the lie the dossier's #1 complaint is about. Output dots become hollow with a dash: `⊘ Licht`. The
picture pane gets the server's existing `addOfflineOverlay` caption.

**Absent — no sensor.** A `plug` reporting only temperature gets **one** value cell, not one plus
three `—`. The cell count is computed once per page load from `hardwareInfo` + the last sample's
keys and never changes while the page is open (no reflow during a scrub, ever).

**Absent — no camera.** The picture pane is not rendered at all. The strip moves to the top of the
scroll area, the depth stack gains 38 vh, and the `Gerät` block carries one row: `Kamera koppeln`.
No empty box, no upsell banner.

### 3.4 The scrub gesture (no hover, one hand, wet fingers, dark)

The strip is a native horizontal scroller: `overflow-x: auto`, `scroll-snap-type: x mandatory`,
tiles `scroll-snap-align: center`, `overscroll-behavior-x: contain`. That buys momentum, rubber-band
and fling for free from the browser, on every phone, with zero library.

- **Tiles**: 56 × 84 px, centre-cropped from the frame, 4 px gap. ~7 visible on a 390 px phone.
- **Cursor**: a 2 px `--ion-color-primary` line fixed at the horizontal centre of the strip, with a
  6 px triangle above it. It never moves. The film moves under it. (This is the one thing every
  person on earth has already learned from a video trimmer.)
- **During drag**: `t` is read from `scrollLeft` on a passive `scroll` listener, throttled to
  `requestAnimationFrame`. The header and photo update continuously. No snapping, no lag.
- **On rest**: 120 ms debounce (or native `scrollend` where available) → snap to the centred frame,
  `t` = that frame's exact timestamp, write `?t=` to the URL with `replaceUrl: true`, load the full
  resolution photo.
- **Grain**: pinch on the strip zooms time. One-handed alternative: the grain chip at the strip's
  right end cycles `2 Min → 1 Std → 6 Std → 1 Tag → 2 Min` on tap. **Grain never changes by itself**
  — not from scroll velocity, not from range width. Predictability beats cleverness for an impaired
  user, and the label states the current grain in words at all times.
- **`Jetzt` pill**: 44 px, appears at the strip's right end the instant `t ≠ now`, always the same
  pixel. Tap → smooth-scroll to the live end, re-arm the 10 s live refresh, drop `?t=`. This is the
  "put everything back" button and it is the only control in the app that is guaranteed present.
- **Long-press a tile**: plays forward at 8× while held, stops on release. The timelapse, without a
  render, at any grain. It is also the discoverability path to `/film`.
- **Vertical scroll on the strip** is passed to the page — the strip is 96 px tall and a thumb
  rarely lands there by accident, but it must not trap the page scroll.

Tile content by grain — a deliberate content decision, not a downsample:

| Grain | one tile = | window on a phone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `2 Min` | one captured frame | ≈ 15 min |
| `1 Std` | the frame nearest the full hour | ≈ 7 h |
| `6 Std` | the frame nearest 03:00 / 09:00 / 15:00 / 21:00 | ≈ 42 h |
| `1 Tag` | **the brightest accepted frame of that day** | ≈ 7 d |

At day grain the strip becomes a growth flipbook: seven days of plant, lit, side by side, and a fling
crosses a month. That is the strongest single argument for the camera in the product, and it costs
one `max(luma)` per day.

### 3.5 Missing frames — the honest rules

1. **Gap tile.** A run of ≥ 2 missing capture slots renders as one hatched tile with the duration
   written on it: `4 Std 12 Min ohne Bild`. It is scrubbable — you can park inside it.
2. **The picture never lies about its age.** With `t` inside a gap, the pane shows the last frame
   *before* the gap at 40 % opacity with a caption bar: `Letztes Bild vor 4 Std 12 Min · 13.10. 23:00`.
   The header values are **not** dimmed, because they are real. Dimming the picture and not the
   numbers is precisely the statement "this photo is not from this moment".
3. **Night frames are kept in the strip and not shown as JPEGs.** A near-black frame reads as a
   broken image. Below `luma < 12` the tile renders as a flat `--tc-step-100`-dark block with the
   hour on it (`01:00`) and a small moon glyph. Time is not compressed — the night still occupies
   its real width, because the strip is an axis.
4. **No camera ever paired** → synthetic tiles for the whole strip: warm for `out_light > 0`, dark
   blue otherwise, hour label. Every gesture in §3.4 works identically. Honest, and it is why the
   navigation model does not collapse for a monitor-only user.
5. **Camera stopped and stayed stopped** → an alarm, §8.6. Not a quiet strip.

## 4. The no-modes mechanism

Three mechanisms, none of which is a toggle, plus one refusal.

### 4.1 One page, ordered by frequency of need, never by user sophistication

There is one route per device and one scroll. The blocks are ordered by how often the answer is
needed by *anyone*: what is running now, then the history, then what happened, then plants, then
feeding, then the goals, then the hardware. The stoned user reads the fixed frame and stops. The
techie scrolls to block 2, taps a panel open and gets 5-second raw samples, min/max envelope and a
CSV button. They started at the same pixel.

**Refusal that makes this real: scroll position is never persisted.** Every open starts at the top.
A remembered scroll position is an implicit mode — "the app I get is shaped by what I did last
time" — and it is the exact failure the brief warns about with adaptive ordering. Block order is
identical for every user of a given device forever.

Each block collapses to at most three rows at rest and expands on tap. The rule that keeps the stack
from silently becoming a mode-by-exhaustion is a hard cap: **no block exceeds 3 rows collapsed**, so
block 7 is never further than ~5 screens down.

### 4.2 Progressive precision inside one component

Every number in the product is rendered by one component, `<tc-wert>`, which always draws three
layers in a fixed 3-line box:

```
   ↑ 24,1 °C          layer A — glyph + colour: is it right?      (stoner)
     Ziel 22,0        layer B — the number and what it is judged against  (normal)
     ────────         tap → the block below expands: raw 5 s series,
                      min/max envelope, method picker, "Als Tabelle", CSV  (techie)
```

Nobody is denied the number; nobody has to read it. The same component is in the header, in the
`Steuerung` rows, in the event list and in place of the chart tooltip. There is one implementation
and therefore one behaviour to learn. `value-display` (the existing 266-line SVG gauge, which
already renders `—` for missing values) is the layer-A/B renderer; it is reused, not replaced.

### 4.3 Reveal by capability, computed per role — and reveal by data

`deviceControlCapability()` collapses `hardwareInfo.sockets` into three buckets and **fails open to
`'full'`** when the key is missing. C12 replaces it with `parseSocketRoles()` → a `Set<Role>` and
renders per role:

- a `heater` socket and no `dehumidifier` → a temperature target row, **no humidity target row**, no
  humidity deviation warning, no dehumidifier alarm preset, no `out_dehumidifier` series in the
  chart, no dehumidifier dot in the header;
- no `co2` socket and no SCD4x → no CO₂ cell, no CO₂ panel, no CO₂ target;
- missing `hardwareInfo.sockets` (old firmware) → **fail closed to monitor**, with one row in
  `Gerät`: `Angeschlossene Geräte unbekannt — Firmware zu alt. [ Manuell festlegen ]`.

And by data: zero `Plant` rows for a device → block 4 does not exist and the capture sheet has no
plant picker. Zero `Event{type:'duengen'}` → block 5 is one row: `Noch nichts gedüngt. [ Düngen ]`.
Features appear when there is something to show. Nobody is asked what kind of user they are, ever —
there is no onboarding question about experience and no `localStorage['app-settings-expert']`. That
key is deleted, and `docs/einfach-modus.md` is deleted with it.

## 5. The chart (block 2 — `Verlauf`)

Apache ECharts 6, tree-shaken, ~50-line hand-rolled Angular directive. Highcharts, `highcharts-angular`,
`chart.js`, `ng2-charts` and `chartjs-adapter-luxon` are all removed.

**The chart does not own a crosshair. It renders the app's cursor.** `axisPointer.link` +
`echarts.connect()` across the panels, and the cursor's x-value is bound one-way to `t`. Dragging
inside the plot area moves `t`, which moves the strip and the photo. This is the second Zeiger.

Panels, max four on a phone at ~110 px each, only for roles/sensors that exist:

```
Temperatur                                       ┌ 28
   ░░░░░░░ target band (alarm limits, if set)    │
   ────── actual, minmax-sampled                 │
   ╌╌╌╌╌╌ setpoint, step:'end'                   └ 18
   ▓ signed deviation fill between the two
   ▒ day/night markArea from measured out_light
                                    ▲ t
Feuchte / VPD          band walks with the stage
CO₂                    only if deviceHasCo2()
Ausgänge               state-timeline lanes, one per paired role
```

- **Setpoint** is a real series, not a `markLine`: built client-side from `device.configuration`
  `day.temperature` / `night.temperature` and `daynight.day` / `daynight.night` (seconds-of-day, UTC)
  resampled onto the query's x grid with `step: 'end'`, dashed. It steps at lights-on and lights-off.
  No new API — the dossier is right that goal-vs-actual is a presentation gap.
- **Band source is named on the panel**, one at a time: `Band: deine Alarmgrenzen` (hard, from
  `Alarm.upperThreshold`/`lowerThreshold`, which the chart ignores today) → `Band: Ziel ± 1,5 °C`
  (soft) → `Band: VPD Blüte 1,2–1,5` (advisory, from `GROW_STAGE_PRESETS.vpdRange`).
- **Deviation fill** between actual and setpoint, signed, two-colour. Never fill-to-axis — the
  current `type:'area', threshold:null, fillOpacity:0.1` encodes nothing and fails WCAG 1.4.11.
- **Day/night** `markArea` derived from measured `out_light > 0`, falling back to the `daynight`
  schedule, in an explicit named timezone. Where the schedule says light and `out_light` is 0 the
  band is **missing** — a failed contactor becomes a diagnostic instead of an invisible fact.
- **Outputs** are a state-timeline lane, region length = duration, null = gap. Not 0/1 lines on a
  value axis. `out_dehumidifier` is labelled by workmode: `Entfeuchter` in `small`/`full`,
  `Kühlung` in `temp`/`breed` — one socket, two meanings, made visible.
- **Sampling**: `sampling: 'minmax'` on the plot (LTTB smooths away exactly the bang-bang
  oscillation a grower is looking for), LTTB only for the strip's value track.
- **Axis labels are never hidden.** Below 320 px they shorten to three ticks and the unit moves into
  the panel title.
- Series names come from `translate`, not from the hardcoded English literals shipping today.
- Colours: Okabe-Ito. `darkMode: 'auto'` replaces the hand-maintained 30-property dark palette and
  the `MutationObserver` on `body.class`.

**Annotations are on the strip, not in the plot.** An event puts a 10 px coloured dot on the strip
tile nearest its time, with the actor's 2-letter chip when the device has actors. Stage changes draw
a full-height dashed line in the chart *and* a labelled divider in the strip. This deletes the
height-1 hover-only column series, which are unusable on touch.

Server: one `/data/series` change is required first, and it is a security fix regardless —
`measure`, `from`, `to` and `interval` are interpolated raw into Flux today. Allowlist `measure`
against `VALID_SENSORS`/`VALID_OUTPUTS`, clamp `interval` so `(to-from)/interval ≤ 5000`, and move
the `limit()` before `yield()`. Then add `{mean,min,max}` in one response instead of three round
trips.

## 6. The diary and multi-plant

New Mongo collections, all cloud-side (`device.configuration` cannot carry new keys), all fields
optional, read-time normalisers, no migration tooling required.

```ts
Event {                       // the first-class diary row; DeviceLog stays for device messages
  event_id: string;           // uuid v4 minted ON THE CLIENT — see §7.4
  device_id: string;
  run_id?: string;
  plant_ids: string[];        // [] === the whole tent. Present from day one.
  type: 'giessen'|'duengen'|'messung'|'training'|'notiz'|'foto'|'stage'|'ernte'|'co2'|'schaedling';
  at: number;                 // ms epoch — the moment the act happened
  actor_id?: string;
  taps?: number;              // number of watering-can presses
  canLiters?: number;         // the can size in force at capture
  amountL?: number;           // taps * canLiters, editable
  feed?: { product: string; mlPerL: number }[];   // brand names are plain text only
  ec?: number; ph?: number; runoffEc?: number; runoffPh?: number;
  heightCm?: number;
  text?: string;
  image_ids?: string[];
  regime_id?: string; regime_step?: number;
  deviation?: boolean;        // true when the user edited a prefilled value
  doubleAckOf?: string;       // event_id acknowledged past the soft block
  correctsEventId?: string;   // append-only: a correction, never an in-place edit
  createdAt: number;
}

Plant     { plant_id, device_id, label, strain?, source?, startedAt, stage,
            stageSince, run_id, potLiters?, medium_id?, archivedAt? }
Run       { run_id, device_id, name?, startedAt, endedAt?, flipAt? }
Condition { condition_id, device_id, text, plant_ids?, openedBy, openedAt, closedBy?, closedAt? }
Actor     { actor_id, device_id, name, colour }
GrowMedium / FeedRegime  // content collection: {source_url, retrieved_at, chart_version, steps[]}
```

`Run` replaces `convertEventsToGrowCycles()`, the 57-line browser-side heuristic that starts a new
cycle whenever a stage ordinal rolls back or a trimmed `lifecycleName` string differs — so renaming
a plant forks its history and a re-veg fabricates a plant. It becomes a one-time boot-time backfill,
not a runtime algorithm.

**The tax on someone who ignores plants is zero.** `plant_ids: []` means the tent. With no `Plant`
rows, the capture sheet has no plant picker, block 4 does not render, and every aggregate is
tent-scoped. Creating the first plant makes a plant chip row appear in the sheet with `Ganzes Zelt`
preselected. There is no question, no setting and no migration.

**"It does the diary keeping for you"** is delivered by three things, not by prose:

1. **Every event is born with its conditions.** `Event.at` is a timestamp on the same clock as the
   telemetry, so the event list renders the temperature, humidity, VPD and light state at `at` next
   to every row, computed at read time and stored nowhere. Tap the row → the cursor jumps to `at`
   and the whole app is at that moment, photo included.
2. **The machine writes its own entries.** The 18/6 → 12/12 flip is detected from `out_light`
   history and lands as an `Event{type:'stage'}` *proposal* — a badge on the strip tile with
   `Blüte gestartet? [ Ja ] [ Nein ]`. Alarms, camera gaps, firmware updates and config changes
   already write `DeviceLog` rows and are already on the strip. The timeline fills without typing.
3. **The weekly summary is generated, not typed.** `Run` week N shows day/night mean temp, mean RH,
   mean VPD, min/max, time-in-band %, DLI where lux exists, litres watered, products fed, photos.
   Every one of those is arithmetic over data already stored, and eleven of GrowDiaries' sixteen
   per-week fields are among them.

**Legacy the diary inherits and this concept fixes:** `deleted: true` is a visibility flag, not a
deletion, so `DELETE /device/logs/:device_id` ("Clear logs") removes nothing from the diary. C12
does not build on `DeviceLog` for user entries at all, so the trap does not propagate. The server's
own stage entries written with `categories: ['diary-plant-lifecycle']` (no `'diary'` prefix, hence
invisible in the Entries report) are read by the backfill and re-emitted as `Event{type:'stage'}`.

## 7. Watering & fertilising

No irrigation hardware exists. This is a capture-and-recall problem, and capture happens with one
wet hand in the dark.

### 7.1 The can counter

You do not type litres at 02:00. You count cans, because that is what you are physically doing.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Gießen                              │
│                                      │
│      ┌────────────────────────┐      │
│      │      ＋ 1 Kanne        │      │   72 px tall, full width
│      └────────────────────────┘      │
│         3 Kannen · 15 l              │   updates on every press
│         Kanne: 5 l  ▸                │   set once, in Gerät
│                                      │
│  [ Ganzes Zelt ]  A1  A2  A3         │   only when plants exist
│  Wer: [ Anna ] Ben  Chris            │   only when actors exist
│                                      │
│           [ Speichern ]              │
└──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Stored: `taps: 3, canLiters: 5, amountL: 15`. Keeping `taps` separate means a later correction to
the can size is exact rather than a guess. With plants selected, `amountL` is per plant and the
label says so: `3 Kannen · 15 l · je 5 l`.

### 7.2 Tap counts, stated

| Flow | Taps |
| --- | --- |
| Water the tent, one can | `＋` → `Gießen` → `＋1 Kanne` → `Speichern` = **4** |
| Water the tent, three cans | **6** |
| Feed with a regime selected (products, ml/L and target EC prefilled) | **4** |
| Feed when a feed is *due* (the sheet opens with `Düngen` preselected) | **3** |
| Feed exactly as scheduled | **long-press `＋` = 1** |
| Photo | `＋` → `Foto` → shutter = **3** |
| Note | `＋` → `Notiz` → dictate/type → `Speichern` = **3** + typing |
| Close an open condition | `Erledigt` on the pinned row = **1** |

The long-press path is the "it thinks for me" face. When a feed is due, the capture button's glyph
becomes a drop and the `Jetzt` tile in the strip carries a due badge. Long-press writes the event
exactly as the regime specifies, `deviation: false`, with a 4-second `Rückgängig` toast. Short-press
still opens the sheet. Same button, same place, no mode.

### 7.3 The double-feed soft block

On `Speichern`, if any selected plant — or the tent, when `plant_ids` is `[]` — carries a
`giessen`/`duengen` event inside the regime's `minIntervalHours` (default 12 water / 20 feed), the
save button is **replaced** by:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
│  ⚠  Anna hat A1–A3 vor 1 Std 30      │
│     gefüttert.                       │
│                                      │
│  [ Trotzdem speichern ]  [ Abbrechen ]│
└──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Not a dialog that dismisses on an outside tap. One deliberate extra tap. Proceeding stamps
`doubleAckOf` with the earlier `event_id`, so the club report can show intentional double feeds
distinctly from accidents. This reads across actors — that is the entire point.

### 7.4 Offline, without double-logging

`event_id` is a client-minted uuid v4, generated **before** the first network attempt, and the
server upserts on it. Retry is therefore idempotent by construction: the failure mode where a
retry queue writes a second feed — indistinguishable from a real double feed in a shared record —
cannot occur. The queue itself (IndexedDB + `@angular/service-worker`) is a v1.1 item; the id
discipline ships in v1 so adding the queue later is safe.

### 7.5 Following a schedule

A regime is `{ medium, feed_line, steps[] }` — modelled as the grower says it: *Biobizz All-Mix*
(substrate, heavily pre-fertilised → first feed around week 3-4), *Biobizz Light-Mix* (first feed
around week 2), *BioTabs* (re-tab every ~5 weeks, almost no liquid feeding). A regime that cannot
express "All-Mix, so don't feed yet" is not modelling the domain.

Steps advance by **feed event number** with a soft calendar mapping (`Schritt 6 von 13`), not by
wall-clock weeks — growers feed Tue/Fri and slip. When the cursor is parked on a feed event, the
header's date line reads `Di 14. Okt · 03:12 · Schritt 6 von 13`. EC is stored canonically in
mS/cm and never rendered as a bare ppm number. Where a chart is published as `EC +` over source
water, `ec_basis: 'delta_over_source'` is stored and the displayed target is `chart + your tap EC`.

### 7.6 The cumulative question

Block 5 with a plant chip selected:

```
A3 · dieser Lauf
  Wasser        41,0 l    ·  9 Gaben
  Bio-Grow     240 ml     ·  6 Gaben
  Bio-Bloom    118 ml     ·  4 Gaben
  zuletzt      Anna, vor 2 Tagen · EC 1,4
  [ Alle 19 Einträge ]
```

A straight aggregation over `Event`. Every data model in round one could have computed it; none

## 8. Camera, timelapse and image correlation

The headline. What exists today: the cloud pulls a still every 30 s over MQTT/RTSP/O-KAM-P2P, stores
it as `Image{format:'jpeg'}` in Mongo, thins it on four tiers (1 min after a day, 5 min after a
week, 15 min after a month, 60 min after 90 days), keeps it 3 years, and rebuilds three rolling
timelapses hourly with `libx265 -crf 30` at 25 fps. `timestampEnd` already marks an mp4 as spanning
a range. That machinery is good. What is missing is a film a person can hold and a reason to look.

### 8.1 Correlation, in four directions — all free, because time is the join key

1. **Cursor → picture + numbers.** §3. The default state of the product.
2. **Picture → numbers.** Tap the pane → fullscreen. Values are burned into the overlay. Swipe
   left/right = ±1 frame. Long-press = play at 8×, release = stop.
3. **Event → picture.** Every row in `Ereignisse` carries the frame nearest `Event.at` as a 56 px
   thumbnail. Tap → the cursor jumps there and the entire app renders that moment. "Show me the day
   I topped her" is one tap and stores no new field — `Image` needs no back-reference.
4. **Chart → picture.** Dragging in the plot area moves `t`. Same variable.

### 8.2 Feeding the strip without N requests per fling

`GET /image/:device_id/strip?from&to&grain&token`

```jsonc
{ "sprite": "/image/<id>/sprite/<hash>.jpg",   // ≤100 tiles, 160×90, q60, ≈120 KB
  "tileW": 160, "tileH": 90, "cols": 10,
  "frames": [{ "t": 1760000000000, "i": 0, "luma": 84, "night": false, "events": 2 }, …],
  "gaps":   [{ "from": …, "to": … }],
  "track":  { "t": [...], "temperature": [...], "humidity": [...], "vpd": [...], "co2": [...] } }
```

One request per screenful of strip serves **both** the pictures and the numbers, so scrubbing reads
from an in-memory array and never touches the network. That is the technical crux of the whole
concept. Sprites are cached in the existing `Image` collection as `format: 'sprite'` with
`timestamp`/`timestampEnd` as the range, reusing its unique index shape.

### 8.3 The film — what ships in October

`/device/:id/film`:

```
Film

[ Heute ]  [ 7 Tage ]  [ Ganzer Lauf ]  [ Zeitraum wählen ]

  ▣ Nachtbilder weglassen              (an)
  ▣ Werte einblenden                   (an)

  1 240 Bilder · ca. 52 s · ca. 14 MB

           [ Film speichern ]
```

`GET /image/:device_id/film?from&to&fps=24&width=1280&night=0&burn=1&token=<imageToken>`

- **Codec: H.264 (`libx264`), `yuv420p`, CRF 21, `-movflags +faststart`, 1280×720, 24 fps.** Not
  the current `libx265`. HEVC in an mp4 does not preview in Telegram, does not play in WhatsApp and
  needs a paid extension on stock Windows. "A film someone can hold" is decided by this line.
  `libx265` stays for the in-app rolling `1d`/`1w`/`1m` previews, where bandwidth matters and the
  player is ours.
- **Filename**: `Content-Disposition: attachment; filename="TerpControl_Zelt-1_Lauf-3_2026-08-01_2026-10-14.mp4"`.
  Device name slugified, run name, ISO dates. It is legible in a Downloads list six months later.
- **How it leaves**: an `<a download href="…">` to that URL. The browser downloads it to
  `Downloads`/Files. The copy says `Downloads`, not "camera roll" — a PWA cannot write to the
  camera roll and promising it produces support tickets.
- **How it is shared**: the URL carries `imageToken`, which is already a 30-day, deliberately
  URL-embeddable token in this codebase. Paste it into Telegram and **the recipient needs no
  login**. A `Link kopieren` button next to `Film speichern`, with the expiry stated:
  `Link gilt 30 Tage.` A share that produces a login page is not a share.
- **Long renders**: ranges over 7 days return `202 { job_id }`; the app polls and shows
  `Film wird gebaut … 2 400 von 5 100 Bildern` as a row on the photo pane. Hard cap
  `to − from ≤ 90 d` and one concurrent job per user.

### 8.4 What is actually in the film — culling

In order, all server-side, all with dependencies already installed (`sharp`, `ffmpeg`):

1. **Dark cull.** `Image.luma` (mean luma, one float, computed by `sharp().stats()` at capture time
   and stored on the row) < 12 → drop, unless `night=1`. Cross-checked against `out_light` from
   Influx where available, which is more reliable than luma alone and costs one query. Effect: the
   lights-off half of a 12-week grow disappears; the film halves and becomes watchable.
2. **Transition cull.** Frames within ±6 min of a light-state change are mid-ramp and strobe. Drop.
3. **Dedup.** `Image.diff` (mean absolute difference against the previously kept frame, also one
   float at capture time) < 0.002 → drop. The tent at 04:00 with nothing happening does not need
   4 000 frames.
4. **Frame budget.** After culling, if the count exceeds `fps × 300` (5 minutes), drop uniformly in
   time — never uniformly in index, which would over-represent the dense early days.

Two floats per `Image` row make all of this possible. That is the entire storage cost.

### 8.5 What does not ship in October, and why I am saying so

- **Human/door suppression.** The heuristic is a diff spike that reverts: drop frames where
  `diff(frame, lastKept) > 0.08` and `diff(frame+2, lastKept) < 0.03`. It is cheap and it works on
  paper. It has not been tuned against real tent footage, it will not catch a person standing still
  for twenty minutes, and a badly tuned threshold deletes the day the plant actually changed. Ship
  it in v1.1 after the first 150 units have produced footage. **The October film will contain the
  grower's arm.**
- **Exposure/white-balance normalisation.** The naive version — per-frame auto-levels — pumps
  visibly and is worse than doing nothing. The correct version is a **run-constant** per-channel
  gain learned from the first ~50 accepted lit frames, plus a slowly-smoothed drift correction, with
  a magenta-aware target grey (grey-world WB under magenta LEDs turns leaves grey-green). That is a
  week of tuning with real footage, not a day. v1.1.
- **Stabilisation.** `vidstabdetect` + `vidstabtransform` two-pass, `smoothing=30`. Doubles render
  time. Exports only, never the rolling previews. v1.1.

### 8.6 The camera gets an alarm

A camera that stopped three days into flower must say so. `image.service.ts` already runs an hourly
loop over every device with `cloudSettings.rtspStream`; add ~30 lines: if the newest
`format: 'jpeg'` row is older than `cloudSettings.cameraGapHours` (default 6), write

```ts
DeviceLog { severity: 1, title: 'message-camera-gap',
            message: 'message-camera-gap:6', categories: ['webcam','error'] }
```

which already flows into the existing notification path and onto the strip. Copy:
`Kamera liefert seit 6 Std kein Bild.` with `[ Testbild ]` (the existing `POST /image/test/:id`) and
`[ Kamera prüfen ]`. Re-fires on the existing cooldown semantics. Resolves silently on the next
frame.

### 8.7 Burn-in

Bottom-left, 14 px monospace, 60 % black plate: `Tag 41 · 14.10. 13:00 · 24,1 °C · 62 %`. Generated
per frame with ffmpeg `drawtext` from a frame list, toggle `Werte einblenden`, default on. **No
brand watermark, on or off by default.** § 6 KCanG bans cannabis advertising and sponsoring with
fines to €30 000; an auto-appended "Powered by Terp Control" on thousands of German growers' posts
is the single riskiest string available. If it ships at all it ships off, opt-in, after counsel.

## 9. Clubs

**Attribution in v1 without touching auth.** `Actor { actor_id, device_id, name, colour }`. The
owner types the names once in `Gerät` → `Wer ist am Zelt`. No accounts, no email lookup, no
`auth.middleware.ts:172` surgery. Every `Event` and `Condition` stamps `actor_id`. The last-used
actor is remembered per browser per device in `localStorage`, preselected in the capture sheet as a
chip row, one tap to change. The UI states what it is:

> `Namen sind eine Notiz, keine Anmeldung. Jeder mit dem Link kann sie setzen.`

That is honest — attribution is a label, authorisation is a gate, and this ships the label. Real
memberships (a `Membership` collection, rewriting `auth.middleware.ts:172` and `:207`, loosening
~10 owner-scoped queries, an account-lookup endpoint hardened against enumeration) is explicitly
**not v1**.

**The double feed** is §7.3 — the one thing in a shared room that is genuinely expensive, and the
only place where the design deliberately spends a tap.

**Conditions** are the standing facts that get taped to a tent door. Open conditions pin directly
under the readout header, max two, `+2 weitere`:

```
⚠  CO₂-Flasche fast leer · Anna, vor 2 Tg        [ Erledigt ]
⚠  A3 nicht gießen, übergossen · Ben, vor 5 Std  [ Erledigt ]
```

Created from the capture sheet (`Notiz` → `Als offene Notiz`). `Erledigt` stamps `closedBy` /
`closedAt`; closed conditions stay in the record as a closed pair, never deleted.

**Privacy posture — private by default, and the same in every jurisdiction unless the owner says
otherwise.**

- No harvest weight is ever prompted. `Ernte` exists in the capture sheet and its weight field is
  empty and optional. Plant counts are never surfaced in an export unless plant rows are included.
- Exports carry `Namen: [ Voll | Pseudonymisiert | Anonym ]`, **defaulting to Pseudonymisiert**
  (`Person A`, `Person B`).
- Per-member dispensing quantities and THC content — § 26 KCanG's core — have **no field anywhere**
  and will not get one. That is Art. 9 GDPR territory for data a grow controller has no reason to
  hold.
- No location, no GPS, no persisted IP-geolocation, no analytics SDK, no community feed.
- Events are append-only with compensating corrections (`correctsEventId`), never in-place edits —
  but the account owner can still delete their own data. Immutability protects a regulated club and
  victimises an unregulated grower.
- No `KCanG-konform` badge, ever, in any language.

## 10. Old devices (device_type `fan`, `light`, `plug`)

Exactly what changes for them, with nothing hand-waved.

**The shell is the same page.** They get the fixed frame and the depth stack. The picture pane is
absent (no `rtspStream`, no `webcam_did`), so the strip sits at the top of the scroll area rendering
synthetic light-state tiles, and the depth stack starts 38 vh higher.

**The readout header** renders one cell per sensor the device actually reports, computed once from
the last sample's keys. A `plug` reporting only temperature shows one 22 px number and one target
sub-line, centred. Not four cells with three dashes.

**The depth stack for a `plug`:**

| # | Block | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Steuerung | its relay state at `t`, and the on/off it is scheduled for |
| 2 | Verlauf | its series only — temperature, `out_relais` — same setpoint/band/day-night treatment |
| 3 | Ereignisse | its `DeviceLog` rows, plus any `Event` the owner captured |
| 6 | Ziele | **`<app-plug-settings>` embedded verbatim** |
| 7 | Gerät | firmware, alarms, shares, name |

Blocks 4 and 5 do not render (no plants, no feed events). Block 6 is the existing
`fan-settings` / `light-settings` / `plug-settings` component, moved from a route into a card by the
same `ngSwitch` on `device_type` that exists today. **They are not rewritten.** That is the "must
not be broken" guarantee, delivered by not touching the code.

**Their URLs survive.** `/device/:id/settings` 301s to `/device/:id#ziele`; `/device/:id/charts`
301s to `#verlauf` with every query parameter preserved; existing `ShareLink.query` strings parse
unchanged because `t` and `grain` are additive optionals.

**Three known bugs in their surfaces are fixed in the same pass** because they render raw i18n keys
today: `settings.limits.overtemperature.enabled`, `.undertemperature.enabled`, `.time.enabled` are
the plug settings checkboxes and they show the key string to a paying customer.

`dryer` is dead per `AGENTS.md` and is not touched. `fridge` (type 5's closed-loop box) gets the
identical page with `fridge-settings` in block 6 — plus the camera if paired, and a sealed box is
the one place a timelapse is unambiguously good.

## 11. Per-user-type walkthrough

**Type 1 — monitor only.** Opens the app, reads `Jetzt · alles im Ziel` and two numbers, closes it.
The strip is synthetic tiles; a flick to `1 Tag` grain and a drag back a week answers "was it ever
too warm while I was away" without opening a chart, because the header numbers move with the thumb.
Block 2's time-in-band figure gives the verdict he never had. The concept's centre — the camera —
is a coloured bar for him, and that is a real cost.

**Type 2 — monitor with a camera.** This is the type the concept is built for. The strip *is* his
grow; day grain is a flipbook of his plant; long-press plays it. Correlation costs zero gestures
because the picture and the numbers are already the same object. In October he gets an actual h264
film with the values burned in, a filename that means something, and a link his Telegram group can
open without an account. He does not get stabilisation or the arm-removal heuristic until v1.1 and
he will notice.

**Type 3 — controls one or two things.** Per-role reveal means his heater-only tent shows a
temperature target and no humidity target, no dehumidifier dot, no humidity-deviation nag and no
dehumidifier alarm preset. The chart draws the setpoint his heater is chasing and the day/night band
his lamp actually produced. Nothing on his screen refers to kit he does not own — and he never
declared that; the socket list did.

**Type 4 — full tent.** Best served by block 1 and block 2's output lane, where every paired role
gets a state-timeline row and `out_dehumidifier` is finally labelled `Kühlung` or `Entfeuchter`
depending on workmode. The hard truth is unchanged: five socket roles exist, there is no humidifier
and no exhaust role, so "ein Controller für dein ganzes Zelt" still overstates the firmware. C12
does not pretend otherwise — it shows him exactly the roles he has and nothing else.

**Type 5 — closed-loop DIY.** Gets the depth: every `<tc-wert>` opens into raw 5-second samples with
a min/max envelope, a method picker and a CSV button; `?t=` and `?grain=` make any moment a URL; the
strip endpoint returns clean JSON. He does not get a write API in October — that is honest and it is
the thing he actually wants. What he does get is the Flux injection fixed and a documented,
allowlisted `/data/series`, which is the precondition for one.

**Type 6 — club.** Attribution in v1 via typed names, the double-feed soft block reading across
people, the cumulative per-plant ledger, and conditions pinned where the tent-door note used to be.
The cut that hurts: `Plant` is v1.1, so October's events are tent-scoped and "how much has A3 had"
is answerable only for the whole tent. The `plant_ids` field exists from day one so no migration
follows.

**Type 7 — old device owner.** §10. Nothing he has today disappears, his settings screen is the same
component, his bookmarks redirect, and three raw i18n keys on his plug settings stop being visible.
He gains a strip and a chart with a real axis. He is not the audience and he is not broken.

## 13. Honest weaknesses

1. **Most opens are not scrubbing.** Four opens in five ask "is it fine right now?", and for that
   question the strip does nothing. The concept spends its best real estate on a gesture performed
   rarely. C02's one-sentence verdict answers the common case better and this design should be
   judged knowing that.
2. **Without a camera the centre of the concept is a coloured ruler.** Types 1, 3 and most of 4 have
   no camera. The synthetic-tile fallback is honest, works identically, and is still a consolation
   prize — which makes the design's value partly contingent on a €30–60 hardware upsell.
3. **The fixed frame eats the phone.** Picture 38 vh + header 88 px + strip 96 px + capture button
   leaves roughly 45 % of a 390 × 844 screen for the depth stack — about two chart panels. The whole
   design is a bet that the top half is worth more than the bottom half, and on a small phone it is
   a close call.
4. **Sprites are a new storage line item on a system with no storage discipline.** Mongo already
   holds every JPEG as a Buffer with no retention beyond a 3-year sweep and no Influx downsampling
   at all. Adding cached sprites per grain per device is fine for 150 units and is not fine for
   5 000. v1 has a cache but no eviction policy.
5. **The film render is unbounded work started by a tap.** There is no job queue in this codebase —
   no Redis, no BullMQ, only `pLimit(10)` around ffmpeg. Ten exports on launch weekend queue for an
   hour. The 90-day cap and one-job-per-user are limits, not a solution, and I would not defend them
   past a few hundred devices.
6. **The precision-by-gesture idea got disarmed.** Binding grain to scroll velocity was the elegant
   no-modes mechanism — the techie's careful thumb gets raw samples, the stoner's flick gets the
   calm answer, nobody chose anything. I dropped it because unpredictable behaviour hurts impaired
   users badly, and what remains (pinch, or tap a chip) is a good gesture and not a novel mechanism.
   §4's answer is therefore three solid conventional mechanisms rather than one new one.
7. **Depth-as-disclosure erodes.** Block 1 will grow a row per socket role, block 3 will grow filter
   chips, and the raw-data affordance ends up six screens down — a mode created by exhaustion rather
   than by a toggle. The three-rows-collapsed cap is the defence and caps like that are the first
   thing a sprint drops.
8. **`t` in the URL turns every share into a moment, and I have not designed the stale case.** A
   link shared in October and opened in January shows October with no signal that time has passed.
   `Jetzt teilen` vs `Diesen Moment teilen` is the obvious fix and it is not specified here.
9. **In a club, two people scrub independently and the cursor is per-browser.** A member who scrolled
   back three days and then taps `＋` would log a feed at the wrong instant. The mitigation — the
   capture sheet always defaults to `Jetzt` and offers `Zeit 14.10. 03:12 übernehmen` as a second,
   explicit choice — breaks the concept's own "one cursor drives everything" purity. I would rather
   break the purity than write a feed into last Tuesday.
10. **The controller still cannot do what the marketing says.** Five socket roles, no humidifier, no
    exhaust role, `out_dehumidifier` doubling as the cooler. C12 makes that visible instead of fixing
    it, because fixing it is firmware work gated by the same October date. A type 4 grower will read
    the honesty as a downgrade from today's UI, which cheerfully offered him controls that did
    nothing.
