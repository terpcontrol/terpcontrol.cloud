# C40 — "Reaktion"

**Family:** feeding · **Centre of gravity:** feeding is a closed loop of observation.

---

## 1. Thesis

**Every feed opens a window; the app watches the plants through it and tells you what the dose did.**

Terp Control already has the two halves nobody else has in one box: it knows what you poured in (because
you logged it) and it can see and measure what happened next (camera + climate + your runoff readings).
Today those two halves never meet. `Reaktion` welds them: pressing *Gießen* writes a `FeedEvent` **and**
opens a `ResponseWindow`. For the next few days the server samples the camera and the sensors against a
baseline taken from the 24 h *before* the feed. When the window closes — after N days, or the moment the
same plants get fed again — a **Reaktionskarte** lands in the diary: one German sentence with the numbers
in it, and every number expandable in place into its derivation.

The club's literal question is *"Wer hat was gemacht? Wie haben die Pflanzen reagiert?"* Ten concepts
answered the first half. This one answers the second, and it answers it with the camera, which is what
makes the camera a headline feature instead of a tile: **the camera is the response sensor.** Leaf colour,
canopy area and canopy height are measurable from stills the product already captures every 30 s, with a
library (`sharp`) already in `package.json`. The timelapse is not decoration on top of that — it is the
same pipeline's second output, and the per-window clip is the piece of evidence a grower actually shows
somebody.

It is deliberately arguable. A 7-day n=1 correlation is not science, and this document says so in §13. But
"my leaves went 5 % darker in the week after I doubled Bio-Grow, and here is the 4-second clip" is
enormously more useful than the flat event list the product ships today, and it is buildable by October.

One device = one grow space. Everything hangs off `/device/:device_id`.

**Navigation model — a bottom tab bar with the capture action in the middle.**

```
│                                               │
```

- `Gießen` is a raised 64 px circular button, not a route: it opens the capture sheet over whatever you

- Settings, Personen, Testmode, Wartung, Freigaben live behind the header gear on Heute — not in the bar.
- The `ion-menu` stays for account/devices/logout. No change to `AuthGuard`, share-mode or demo-mode
  handling; every new page calls `resolveDeviceAccessInfo` like the existing ones.

Desktop (`ion-split-pane` ≥ 992 px): the tab bar becomes a left rail, and **Verlauf and Tagebuch render
side by side** with a shared cursor — the big screen the dossier calls unexploited. Same components,
two columns.

## 4. NO-MODES MECHANISM

The mechanism is called **Satz → Zahl → Herleitung** and it is one component, `<tc-reaktionskarte>`,
rendered identically for everybody.

**Rule 1 — the numbers are the words.** A reaction card is a German sentence. The quantities inside it are
not adjacent to the sentence, they *are* nouns in it: `Blätter 4 % dunkler, Wuchs +11 %, Runoff-EC 1,9 bei
1,6 rein.` The stoner reads a sentence and stops. The techie reads the same sentence and has already
received four numbers. Nobody navigated anywhere. There is no "details" link doing the work.

**Rule 2 — every number is a tappable word that expands in place.** Tapping `+11 %` does not navigate. The
line splits open underneath the tapped token into a 90 px derivation strip:

```
Blätter 4 % dunkler, Wuchs [+11 %], Runoff-EC 1,9 bei 1,6 rein.
        ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │ Wuchs = Anteil grüner Pixel im Bild               │
        │ vorher 0,281  (14.09., 41 Bilder)                 │
        │ nachher 0,312 (21.09., 39 Bilder)                 │
        │ ▁▂▂▃▃▄▄▅▅▆▆▇  ← Kurve über 7 Tage                 │
        │ 80 von 96 Bildern verwendet · 16 verworfen        │
        │ [ Bilder ansehen ]   [ im Verlauf öffnen ]        │
        └──────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

That strip contains the raw before/after values, the sample counts, the culling count, a sparkline, and two
exits. It is the technical layer, and it is **inside the same card, at the same place on screen, opened by
touching the thing you were curious about.** There is no screen a techie goes to that a casual user does
not have.

**Rule 3 — expansion is per-token, non-sticky, and forgotten on navigation.** It is not persisted, not a
preference, not remembered per user. This is the explicit defence against the brief's warning about
adaptive UI: the card looks the same every single time you open the app. A 2 a.m. user never finds the
screen rearranged by something they did last week.

**Rule 4 — density comes from coverage, not from a setting.** A card renders exactly the signals it has
inputs for. Monitor-only, no camera, no meter → the card is one clause: `Klima blieb im Band (94 % der
Zeit).` Camera + EC meter + a human thumb → five clauses. Nobody was shown an empty field and nobody was
denied a filled one. The techie's card is denser because the techie owns more instruments, not because
they ticked a box.

**Rule 5 — the same applies to the capture sheet.** One sheet. The tap counter is the whole interaction
for the casual user (§7); the precision the techie wants (litres, ml/l, EC in, EC runoff, pH) is *derived
from the same taps plus equipment they defined once*, rendered as the subtitle of the counter and editable
by tapping it. There is no "erweitert" section. A field the device cannot inform (runoff EC without a
meter) simply has no prefill — it is still there, still one tap, still empty by default.

**Why this is not a mode with a new name:** a mode is a persisted branch that changes what is on the
screen for the same data. Here, two users looking at the *same* reaction card see the *same* pixels. The
only thing that differs is what they touched half a second ago, and that resets. And two users looking at
*different* data see different cards — which is not a mode, that is just data.

## 5. The chart — VERLAUF

Apache ECharts 6, tree-shaken, hand-rolled ~50-line Angular directive (owner decision). Chart.js /
ng2-charts / chartjs-adapter-luxon deleted in the same PR.

**Structure: stacked small multiples, one shared x-axis, one linked crosshair (`axisPointer.link`).**

```
 ├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ Ausgänge   Licht ▬▬▬▬▬▬  Heizung ▬ ▬ ▬   Entf. ▬▬             │  ← state timeline lane
 ├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ Gaben      ▼            ▼✅          ▼😐        ▼              │  ← annotation rail, row 1
 │ Ereignisse   ○ Stufe Blüte    ○ Alarm Temp                     │  ← row 2
 ├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ EC          ● rein 1,6   ○ Runoff 1,9   ● 1,6   ○ 2,1         │  ← paired dots, only if logged
 ├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ Bilder  [▪][▪][▪][▪][▪][▪][▪][▪][▪][▪][▪][▪][▪][▪][▪][▪]      │  ← film strip = an axis
 └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
              Pinned scrubbing header, no floating tooltip
```

- **Goal vs actual.** `markLine` renders a dashed **stepped** setpoint that steps at `daynight.day` /
  `daynight.night` between `day.temperature` and `night.temperature`, parsed from `device.configuration`
  server-side and returned with the series. Behind it a `markArea` target band; between the two, the
  signed deviation is drawn as a hatched fill (never fill-to-axis — the current `threshold:null` area
  encodes nothing). Band priority, one at a time, labelled in the panel header: Alarmgrenzen > Sollwert ±
  Toleranz > VPD-Band der Stufe.
- **Day/night** from measured `out_light > 0` where present, falling back to the configured `daynight`
  schedule, in an explicit named timezone. A failed contactor then reads as a *missing band* — a
  diagnostic. Sunrise/sunset ramps drawn as gradient edges from `lights.sunrise` / `lights.sunset`.
- **Feed markers are the concept's signature.** Every `FeedEvent` is a full-height dashed vertical rule
  plus a rail glyph carrying its window's verdict colour (green ✅ / grey 😐 / amber 👎 / hollow = still
  open). Tapping it opens the Reaktionskarte inline above the chart. Clustering: 8 px bins with a count
  badge, max two rows.
- **Min/max envelope** on the primary measure (`aggregateWindow` already supports it) and
  `sampling: 'minmax'` for the plot — LTTB smooths away exactly the bang-bang oscillation a grower is
  looking for. LTTB stays on the navigator.
- **Film strip.** Kept `Observation` frames positioned by real timestamp on the same x-scale, so gaps read
  as gaps. Delivered as **one sprite + JSON index per window** (256 × 160 tiles, ≤ 100 tiles), not N image
  requests. `Image.timestampEnd` mp4s render as a bracket, not a point.
- **Correlation, both directions.** Dragging the chart cursor moves the film strip and swaps the pinned
  image. Tapping a film-strip thumbnail moves the chart cursor and pins the scrub header to that instant:
  `21.09. 14:32 · 24,1 °C · 57 % · 1,15 kPa · Licht 100 % · 3 Tage nach Gabe #12`.
- **Mobile rules.** One finger = page scroll. Two fingers = pan/zoom (`dataZoom: inside`). Tap/long-press
  = crosshair. Axis labels never hidden — reduced to 3 ticks with the unit in the panel title. `≥ 44 px`
  range presets: `24 Std · 7 Tage · Lauf · Alles`.
- **URL contract preserved.** All new state is *added* query params (`panels`, `feeds`, `strip`,
  `plant`), never renamed; `applyViewParams` keeps parsing the legacy `measures/date/vpdMode/...` shape so
  existing presets and share links do not break.
- Prerequisite, non-negotiable: fix the raw Flux interpolation of `measure/from/to/interval` and move the
  `limit()` before `yield()` **before** any new measure vocabulary is added.

## 6. The diary and multi-plant

**The diary is the reaction record.** `Tagebuch` is a reverse-chronological stream of cards over one
merged source: `DeviceLog` rows (unchanged, translated as today) + `FeedEvent`s + closed `ResponseWindow`s
+ `Condition` open/close events. Filter chips reuse `collectLogCategories`.

### New collections (all cloud-side Mongo, `device_id`-keyed, all fields optional, read-time normalisers)

```ts
Plant {
  plant_id: string; device_id: string;
  code: string;                 // 'A1'  — short, thumb-typed, unique per device
  name?: string; strain?: string;
  startedAt: number; stage: DiaryLifecycleStage;
  medium_id?: string; regime_id?: string; potL?: number;
  archivedAt?: number;
}

PlantGroup { group_id; device_id; label; members: string[]; }   // 'Tisch links' = a saved selection

Actor { actor_id; device_id; name; colour; createdBy; createdAt; retiredAt?; }

Condition {                      // the Zettel an der Zelttür
  condition_id; device_id; text;
  targets?: string[];            // plant_ids, or absent = the whole tent
  openedBy: string;              // actor_id
  openedAt: number; closedBy?: string; closedAt?: number;
}

Equipment { device_id; canLabel: string; canVolumeL: number; potL?: number; }
```

`FeedEvent`, `ResponseWindow`, `Observation`, `Timelapse` and `Regime` are in §7 and §8.

### The untaxed path — how a user who ignores plants pays nothing

There is a **virtual plant** with `plant_id === 'tent'`. It is never listed, never shown, never named
"Pflanze". If the `plants` collection has zero rows for a device:

- every `FeedEvent.targets` is `['tent']`, written automatically;
- every card says `Zelt`, never `A1–A4`;
- the plant chip row in the capture sheet is **not rendered at all** — not greyed, not empty;
- `/pflanzen` is not linked from anywhere and the tab does not exist;
- the double-feed guard still works (it guards on `targets` overlap, and `['tent']` overlaps itself).

The first plant is created from exactly one place: a `+ Pflanze` row at the bottom of the capture sheet's
target area, which appears **only after the fifth feed has been logged** — i.e. reveal by data, once the
product has evidence you are actually using it. Creating one costs typing `A1` and pressing return.
Creating one plant retro-assigns nothing; older feeds keep `targets: ['tent']` and render as `Zelt`.

### Lifecycle, honestly

`DiaryLifecycleStage` gains no new value (that is a shared type both sides import and firmware-adjacent
code reads). Harvest is recorded as `Condition`-free `DeviceLog` category `diary-harvest` with
`data.harvestedAt`, and — **only if the user opts in per device in Einstellungen** — `data.dryWeightG`.
Default off, per dossier §6 and the privacy posture in §9. The runtime cycle-inference
(`convertEventsToGrowCycles`) becomes a **one-time backfill** into `Plant` rows: string-compare cycle
forking is a bug, not an algorithm, and it must stop running per page load.


**Tap counts are the primitive.** The counter records `cans`, an integer, produced by literal taps. It is
a 120 px target — usable one-handed, in the dark, impaired. Everything else on the sheet is derived:

- `canVolumeL` comes from `Equipment`, set once during setup (`Wie groß ist deine Kanne?` → default 5 l).
- `volumeL = cans × canVolumeL`, unless the user taps `(ändern)` and types a litre figure directly, which
  sets `volumeOverrideL` and leaves `cans` intact as the raw record.
- `perTarget` toggles between *3 Kannen insgesamt* and *3 Kannen pro Pflanze*; default is `insgesamt`,
  and the label under the counter says which: `15 l insgesamt` / `15 l pro Pflanze · 60 l gesamt`.
- Doses in ml are computed `mlPerL × volumeL` and shown to the right of every dose row. Editing the ml
  figure back-solves `mlPerL`. Both are stored.

**Schedule following — the regime prefills, it does not command.** `Regime` is a Mongo content collection
(never bundled), selected once in Einstellungen as **Medium + Düngelinie** the way growers say it:

```ts
Regime {
  regime_id; label;              // 'Biobizz All-Mix + Bio-Grow/Bio-Bloom'
  mediumLabel;                   // 'All-Mix'  (plain text, no logo, no brand colour)
  prefertilisedWeeks: number;    // All-Mix: 3 — the reason the sheet says "noch nicht düngen"
  steps: RegimeStep[];
  ec_basis: 'absolute' | 'delta_over_source';
  source_url: string; retrieved_at: number; chart_version: string;
}
RegimeStep {
  fromDay: number; toDay?: number; stage?: DiaryLifecycleStage;
  kind: 'water' | 'feed' | 'flush';
  everyNDays: number;            // 3 → the Durst card's due date
  doses: { productLabel: string; mlPerL?: number | [number, number] }[];
  ecTarget?: number | [number, number]; phTarget?: [number, number];
  note_key?: string;             // paraphrased in our own words, never brand prose
}
```

Which step applies is chosen by **feed count within the current stage**, not wall-clock weeks — growers
feed Tue/Fri and slip. The photoperiod flip (18/6 → 12/12) is detected from `out_light` history and
proposed, never applied silently: `Licht steht seit 4 Tagen auf 12/12. Blüte-Plan starten?`

All-Mix at day 6: `Mit Dünger` is **off** and the line under it reads
`All-Mix ist vorgedüngt — laut Plan erst ab Woche 3 düngen.` The toggle still works. Deviating is the
normal case.

**The double-feed guard (the club critic's #1 gap).** At the moment the sheet opens, the server is asked
for `FeedEvent`s in the last `guardHours` (default 12, from the regime's `everyNDays`) overlapping the
selected targets. If any exist:

```
⚠ Max hat A2 vor 1 Std 40 gegossen (2 Kannen, mit Dünger).
   [ A2 abwählen ]      [ Trotzdem gießen ]
```

`A2 abwählen` is the primary, green button. `Trotzdem gießen` is the deliberate extra tap, and when used
it stamps `overrideOf: <feed_id>` on the new event so the record shows a knowing double, not an accident.
The guard is per-plant: A1 and A4 stay selected and are unaffected.

### What is stored

```ts
FeedEvent {
  feed_id; device_id; time;              // ms epoch, editable afterwards
  actor_id?;                             // absent = device owner
  targets: string[];                     // plant_id[] or ['tent']
  kind: 'water' | 'feed' | 'flush';
  cans: number;                          // THE RAW TAP COUNT
  canVolumeL: number;                    // snapshot at capture, not a live join
  perTarget: boolean;
  volumeOverrideL?: number;
  volumeL: number;                       // resolved, denormalised for queries
  regime_id?; regimeStep?: number;
  doses?: { productLabel; mlPerL?; totalMl?; source: 'regime' | 'manual' }[];
  inputEc?; inputPh?; runoffEc?; runoffPh?;   // EC canonical in mS/cm. Never store bare "ppm".
  note?; images?: string[];
  window_id?: string;
  overrideOf?: string;                   // set when the double-feed guard was overridden
  supersededBy?: string;                 // corrections are new rows, not edits (storno pattern)
  createdAt: number; clientKey: string;  // idempotency key — see below
}
```

**The cumulative question, answered.** Because doses are stored per target with volumes, the plant page
renders, with no new machinery: `A3 · dieser Lauf: 61 l Wasser, 122 ml Bio-Grow, 40 ml Bio-Bloom · 14
Gaben · Anna 9, Max 5.` That is a `$unwind targets` + `$group` aggregation, ~15 lines.

**Cellar capture.** `@angular/service-worker` is added (a real new dependency; flagged, ~1 day). The
capture sheet writes to IndexedDB first and posts second. **Double-logging is prevented by `clientKey`** —
a uuid minted when the sheet opens, unique-indexed on the collection, so a retry is a no-op upsert rather
than a second feed. This matters more here than anywhere: in a shared record an accidental duplicate is
indistinguishable from a real double feed, which is the exact thing the guard exists to prevent.

## 8. Camera, timelapse and image correlation

**Headline. The camera is the instrument that answers "how did the plants react?".**

### 8.1 The observation pipeline (server, nightly + on new frame)

For every stored `format:'jpeg'` frame, one `Observation` row is written by a worker using `sharp`
(already a dependency, used in `image.controller.ts`):

```ts
Observation {
  obs_id; device_id; image_id; timestamp;
  kept: boolean; cullReason?: 'lightsOff'|'motion'|'duplicate'|'exposure'|'corrupt'|'dark';
  meanLuma: number;                // 0..255, whole frame
  leafHue: number; leafSat: number;// mean H,S over the green mask
  canopyFraction: number;          // green-mask pixels / total, 0..1
  canopyTopPx: number;             // topmost green-mask row, 0 = frame top
  offsetX: number; offsetY: number;// integer alignment vs the run's reference frame
  temperature?; humidity?; vpd?; co2?; lightPct?;  // snapshotted from Influx at build time
}
```

Cost per frame: one 256 px `sharp` resize + `raw()` pixel pass. ~8 ms. At 2 880 frames/day/device that is
~25 s of CPU per device per day. Acceptable; `pLimit` is already the house pattern here.

**Green mask** = HSV with `H ∈ [60°,160°]`, `S > 0.15`, `V > 0.10`. Under magenta LED the mask shrinks —
which is why colour is only ever compared **within the same lighting condition**: baseline and result
samples both use frames whose `lightPct` matches within ±10 and whose `meanLuma` is within ±15 %. If they
cannot be matched, `leafColour.available = false` and the clause is simply absent from the card. Honest
absence beats a confident wrong number.

### 8.2 Frame culling and normalisation — the content problem, specified

Applied in order; each writes `cullReason`:

1. **Lights off** — `lightPct < 5` at frame time (from Influx `out_light`), or `meanLuma < 25`. Dropped.
   This alone removes ~half the frames and the black flicker that makes tent timelapses unwatchable.
2. **Corrupt** — already handled upstream by `FFMPEG_CORRUPT_FRAME_PATTERN`. Kept as a reason for counting.
3. **Human / door** — frame-difference against the previous kept frame on a 128 px grayscale thumb. If
   `changedPixelFraction > 0.25` **or** `|Δ meanLuma| > 40` (door open, torch, phone flash), drop it *and*
   the next one. Two consecutive drops arm a 2-minute blackout so a whole tent visit disappears.
4. **Camera nudge** — phase-correlation of the 128 px thumb against the run's reference frame gives
   integer `offsetX/offsetY`. `|offset| > 12 px` for three consecutive frames ⇒ the reference is re-baselined
   and the run gets a diary line `Kamera hat sich bewegt — Zeitraffer ab hier neu ausgerichtet.` Otherwise
   the offset is applied as a translate at render time inside a **6 % safety crop**. This is de-jitter, not
   true stabilisation; rotation is not corrected (see §13).
5. **Near-duplicate** — if `changedPixelFraction < 0.005` and the previous kept frame is < 20 min old, drop.
6. **Exposure/WB normalisation** — per frame: `sharp().linear(a, b)` where `a,b` map the frame's
   5th/95th luma percentiles onto the run's reference percentiles, then a per-channel gain from the
   **reference patch**: the median colour of a fixed 64 × 64 region the user picks once (`Weißpunkt setzen`
   — point at the pot rim, a tent wall, anything not green). Without a chosen patch, fall back to
   grey-world on the non-green mask. This is what removes the "magenta LED vs daylight through the door"
   flicker across 12 weeks.

### 8.3 What ships in v1 — a timelapse someone can hold

Three artefacts, all real files:

| Artefact | Frames | Length | Trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Reaktions-Clip** | every kept frame in a `ResponseWindow`, ≤ 150, evenly sampled | 4–6 s @ 25 fps | built when the window closes |
| **Lauf-Zeitraffer** | 1 frame/day at the run's brightest matched hour, plus 4/day in the last 14 days | 20–60 s | rebuilt nightly |
| **Tag** | existing `1d` rolling build | ~30 s | unchanged, hourly |

**Codec correction — a real defect found in the repo.** `convertRtspStreamImagesToVideo` currently encodes
**`libx265` in mp4**. That does not play in Chrome, Firefox, or most Android video pickers, and it will not
import into Instagram or a phone gallery. **Exports must be H.264.** Concretely:

```
ffmpeg -framerate 25 -f image2 -i %d.jpeg
       -vf "scale=1080:-2:flags=lanczos,crop=..,format=yuv420p"
       -c:v libx264 -profile:v high -level 4.0 -pix_fmt yuv420p
       -crf 21 -movflags +faststart -an out.mp4
```

The internal rolling `1d/1w/1m` previews may stay x265 to save storage; **anything the user downloads is
x264 + `yuv420p` + `faststart`.**

### 8.4 How a video leaves the product

`GET /image/:device_id/timelapse/:timelapse_id.mp4?token=<imageToken>&dl=1`

- Auth: owner cookie, or the existing 30-day `imageToken`, or a `share_id` whose `webcam === true`.
- Response: `Content-Type: video/mp4`, `Accept-Ranges: bytes`,
  `Content-Disposition: attachment; filename="terpcontrol_zelt-nord_A1-A4_2026-09-14_2026-09-21.mp4"`
  (slugified device name, target codes, ISO dates — sortable, self-describing, safe on every filesystem).
- Storage: `Image {format:'mp4'}` as today. **The 16 MB BSON limit is a hard ceiling** — a 60 s 1080p CRF-21
  clip is ~6–10 MB, so it fits, but the builder must check `bytes` and step down to 720p rather than throw.
  Above 16 MB the build is refused with `Zeitraffer zu lang — bitte Zeitraum kürzen.` (Honest limit, not a
  silent failure. Moving blobs out of Mongo is post-October work.)
- On the page: a real `<a download href=…>` plus, on mobile, `navigator.share({files:[…]})` when available,
  so the clip lands in WhatsApp/Telegram/the camera roll in one tap. Copy: `Video speichern` /
  `Teilen…`. A URL to a login page is not a share, and this is not one.

### 8.5 Image correlation — both directions, everywhere

1. **Chart → picture.** Dragging the Verlauf cursor swaps the pinned still (sprite-backed, no request per
   frame) and updates the scrub header.
2. **Picture → data.** Tapping any thumbnail — in the film strip, in the Kamera tab, in a Reaktionskarte —
   opens it full-screen with its own numbers burned into the caption bar, from `Observation`:
   `21.09.2026 14:32 · 24,1 °C · 57 % rF · VPD 1,15 kPa · CO₂ 812 ppm · Licht 100 % · Tag 41 · 3 Tage nach Gabe #12`
3. **Reaction → picture.** Every Reaktionskarte carries the before/after frame pair side by side, aligned,
   normalised, with the two dates. This is the single most convincing thing in the product.
4. **Picture → reaction.** In the Kamera tab the frame browser's scrub bar shows feed markers, so you can
   drag to a feed and watch what came after.
5. **Correlation is not claimed as causation** anywhere in the copy. The card says
   `Nach dieser Gabe:` — *after*, not *because of*. §13 is honest about why.

## 9. Clubs

**Attribution without an auth rewrite.** `Actor` is device-scoped: the owner types first names once in
`Einstellungen → Personen` (`Anna`, `Max`, `Jo`). The capture sheet's header carries a name selector,
defaulting to the last actor used **on this browser** (localStorage, per device_id). Selecting stamps
`actor_id` on `FeedEvent`, `Condition` and diary entries. No accounts, no invites, no `auth.middleware`
surgery. Cost ~2 days. Everything renders `Anna` next to the event, forever.

The honest label on it, shown once in Personen: `Namen sind Etiketten, keine Zugänge. Jeder mit dem
Gerätezugang kann jeden Namen auswählen.` When real memberships land later, `actor_id` becomes a
foreign key to `Member` and every historical row keeps meaning.

**The double-feed problem** is §7's guard: soft block on target overlap inside the window, per-plant
deselect as the primary action, deliberate second tap to proceed, `overrideOf` recorded. Plus the passive
version: the Durst card on Heute always names who last watered and when.

**Conditions** are `Condition` rows rendered as `Zettel an der Zelttür` on Heute and pinned to the top of
Tagebuch until closed. Creating one: `+ Zettel` → text + optional plant chips → save. Closing one:
swipe → `Erledigt`, stamping `closedBy`/`closedAt`. They also appear inline in the capture sheet when they
target a selected plant: selecting A3 with an open `A3 nicht gießen` condition surfaces it in red above
the counter. That is the club's pinned note doing actual work.

**Cumulative reporting** — the club's "detailed logging and reporting": `/device/:id/pflanze/:plant_id`
shows total water, total ml per product, feed count, per-actor breakdown, the reaction strip, and
`Bericht exportieren` → CSV + a Markdown block. CSV columns:
`time,actor,targets,kind,cans,volume_l,product,ml_per_l,total_ml,input_ec,runoff_ec,input_ph,runoff_ph,note`.

**Privacy posture.** Default-private and deliberately thin:
- No real names required, no email, no phone. Actor names are free text and can be `A`, `B`, `C`.
- Dry weight and plant count are **off by default** and per-device opt-in.
- No per-member dispensing, no grams ledger, no membership numbers. We are not the club's § 26 system of
  record and the settings page says so in one sentence.
- Export carries a `Namen: voll / abgekürzt / anonym` selector defaulting to **abgekürzt** (`A.`, `M.`).
- No location, no analytics SDK, no community feed. A public diary link is opt-in, unlisted, revocable,
  and shows no actor names at all.

## 11. Per-user-type walkthrough

**1 — Lazy monitoring-only.** Opens Heute, reads three numbers with an age under them, sees the room is in
band, closes the app. No plants, no feeds, no camera ⇒ the screen is four cards tall and contains no
control they cannot use. Once a month they open Verlauf, drag two fingers, and see the dashed setpoint
against the measured line with night shaded — the first time the chart has ever told them anything. They
never meet a Reaktionskarte, and nothing on screen advertises one.

**2 — Same, but with a camera.** Their Heute has the camera card with `Lauf-Zeitraffer · 41 Tage · 38 s`
and a download button that produces a playable H.264 file named after their tent. They scrub the film
strip under the chart and watch the canopy fill the frame while the temperature line moves under it. Their
Reaktionskarten carry the leaf-colour and growth clauses because they own the instrument that measures
them — no setting, no upgrade prompt, they just have a camera. This is the type the concept is best for.

**3 — Controls certain aspects (CO₂ or heating).** Their Heute shows only the outputs their sockets
actually carry, from `hardwareInfo.sockets` parsed **per role**, not through the three-bucket
`deviceControlCapability()` collapse. A heater-only tent gets no humidity target row, no dehumidifier
alarm preset, no humidity-deviation nag. Verlauf's output lane has one region. Their reaction cards carry
the climate clause but not runoff EC, because they do not own a meter.

**4 — Full tent.** Every socket role appears as a lane in Verlauf's state timeline and a chip on Heute.
They are the heaviest user of the double-feed guard because they are also the type most likely to share
the tent. They will notice that only five socket roles exist — `dehumidifier` doubling as cooler is
visible in their own data — and the honest fix is firmware, out of scope for October; the UI at least
labels the socket by the role it is paired as rather than by what the workmode secretly means.

**5 — Closed-loop DIY.** They want the numbers out. Every `Observation`, `FeedEvent` and `ResponseWindow`
is exposed as JSON on the same authenticated endpoints the UI uses, documented in the existing Swagger
blocks, plus the CSV export. They can compute their own verdicts from `ResponseSample` — the derivation
strip in §4 exists partly for them, because it shows them exactly what the server did. They still get no
write API for outputs in this release, and the concept does not pretend otherwise.

**6 — Club.** Actor picker at the top of every capture, names on every event, the double-feed guard, the
Zettel, the per-plant cumulative report with per-actor breakdown, the anonymised CSV. They are the reason
`targets` is an array and `perTarget` exists. What they do not get and are told plainly they do not get: a
§ 26 ledger, per-member dispensing, or a compliance claim.

**7 — Old device owner.** §10. Nothing removed, one tab fewer, the age line gained.

**Capability lenses.** *Stoner:* one 64 px button, a 120 px tap counter, a sentence, three emoji. *Normal:*
a card that says whether the feed worked, and a clip they can send to a friend. *Techie:* every number in
the sentence opens its own derivation with sample counts and cull counts, plus JSON and CSV. Same screen.

## 13. Honest weaknesses

**1. The camera-derived signals may be noise wearing a lab coat.** Leaf hue under a magenta LED, autogain
on a cheap sensor, leaves that move, a canopy that grows out of frame — all of it degrades
`canopyFraction` and `leafHue`. The mitigations (light-matched sampling, reference patch, ±15 % luma gate)
reduce it; they do not eliminate it. **A 4 % hue shift may be the camera, not the plant.** The design's
defence is coverage honesty — the clause disappears when frames cannot be matched — but a user who sees
`+11 % Wuchs` will believe it, and sometimes it will be a lens smear.

**2. Correlation is presented next to a dose, and people will read causation.** The copy says *nach*, not
*wegen*. That is a fig leaf. In the same 7 days the user also raised the light, defoliated, and the
weather changed. This concept has no confound control and cannot get one at n=1.

**3. n is tiny.** A home grower logs ~12–20 feeds per run. There is no statistical population here, ever.
Anything the product says about "what worked" is one anecdote per card. The concept deliberately refuses
to aggregate across feeds in v1 for exactly this reason — which also means it never delivers the
satisfying "your best dose is X" payoff a critic will ask for.

**4. Without a camera the loop is thin.** Type 1 and type 3 users get a card with one or two clauses:
climate-in-band and their own thumb. That is barely more than the diary they have today. The concept is
honest about it (the clauses are simply absent) but the value proposition is genuinely camera-weighted,
and the camera is a €30–60 upsell. A critic will call this a feature that sells hardware.

**5. The verdict can be demoralising and can be wrong.** `Nach der Gabe vom 14.09. ging es bergab` on a
plant that is actually fine, because the camera got nudged, is a bad experience. The three-state scale and
the `insufficient` fallback exist for this, but `insufficient` will fire often — and a card that usually
says *zu wenig Daten* teaches users to stop reading cards.

**6. New server cost that nobody has budgeted.** An `Observation` per frame, per device, forever, plus
per-window Influx queries, plus two extra ffmpeg encodes per device per day. On top of an InfluxDB bucket
that already has **no retention policy and no downsampling**. This concept makes an unbounded storage
problem worse and does not solve it.

**7. The 16 MB BSON ceiling is a real product limit dressed as a message.** "Zeitraffer zu lang" is an
honest error, but it is still the product telling a customer it cannot make the thing it sold them. A
90-day 1080p timelapse does not fit. The fix is object storage, which is post-October.

**8. Tap counts assume every can is the same can.** They are not. A grower with a 5 l can and a 10 l
bucket will produce garbage volumes until they notice `(ändern)`. Storing `cans` raw at least means the
data is repairable later; it does not mean the numbers were right at the time.

**9. Actors are trust-on-tap.** Anyone at the tent can select any name. In a club with a dispute, the
attribution is worthless as evidence — and the product must never be described as if it were. The one-line
disclaimer is doing a lot of work.

**10. It adds a tab bar, five collections, a service worker and a chart-library migration to a codebase
with 38 `it()` blocks and no migration tooling.** Every new field is optional with a read-time normaliser,
which is the house style and also means the schema will drift quietly. The `convertEventsToGrowCycles`
backfill is a one-shot script against real customer data with no rollback.

**11. The reaction window's closing rule is arguable.** "Closes at the next feed of the same plants" means
a grower who waters every 2 days never gets a 7-day window — their windows are 2 days long and measure
almost nothing. The fallback (minimum 72 h, extend past intervening waterings when `kind === 'water'` and
the open window was `kind === 'feed'`) is a rule with an arbitrary constant in it, and constants like that
are where products lose credibility with the people who read them.
