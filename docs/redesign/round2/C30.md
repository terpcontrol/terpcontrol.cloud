# C30 — Zeitraffer des Textes

**Family:** diary · **Centre of gravity:** the diary is indexed by the plant's life, not the clock.

---

## 1. Thesis

**Every fact in this product has one address — `Blüte · Tag 12` — and the diary, the photo, the numbers
and the chart window are four faces of that one address, so scrolling the written record is literally
scrolling the timelapse.**

Today the app is organised by wall-clock time, which is the one axis a grower never uses when talking
about a grow. Nobody says "on the 22nd of August the humidity was high"; they say "day 10 of flower ran
wet". The clock is an implementation detail of the plant's life. C30 makes day-of-stage the primary key:
a `Run` has stage segments, every entry, photo and chart cursor carries a denormalised
`{stage, dayInStage, dayInRun}` alongside its timestamp, and the home screen is a vertical strip of
numbered days from the start of the run to today.

That single decision pays three ways. **Runs become comparable** — `Blüte T12` of this run sits next to
`Blüte T12` of the last one with no interpolation. **The timelapse becomes watchable** — one canonical
frame per day is the natural sampling rate of a plant, and 84 frames is a 7-second film a person can
actually hold, unlike 120 000 stills. And **the diary keeps itself** — the strip has a row for every day
whether or not a human wrote anything, filled by the machine with a photo and a one-line verdict, which
is exactly the owner's "it does the diary keeping for you".

The name: the text and the pictures scroll on the same axis at the same rate. The written record *is*
the timelapse, played by your thumb.

## 2. Information architecture

There are four surfaces and one object. The object is **the day**. Every surface is the day at a
different zoom.

```
        /list                       device list (unchanged; 1 device → straight through)
          │
          ▼
  /device/:id                       DER STREIFEN  — the strip of days. THE home screen.
          │                         · today's card, then every day back to run start
          │                         · row height set by what happened that day
          │
          ├─ tap a day ──────────►  /device/:id/day/:dayKey        DAS TAGESBLATT
          │                         · that one day, whole: photos, 24 h chart, entries, feed, verdict
          │
          ├─ tap the numbers ────►  /device/:id/charts?...          DER VERLAUF (existing route!)
          │                         · ECharts small multiples, dual x-labels (date + address)
          │                         · scrub moves the film strip; film strip moves the cursor
          │
          ├─ tap the photo ──────►  /device/:id/film?run=...        DER FILM
          │                         · the run as a video; build, preview, download, share
          │
          └─ tap the header ─────►  /device/:id/plan                DER PLAN
                                    · targets per stage, kit, alarms, device settings
```

**Routes** (Angular 15 NgModule, lazy `loadChildren`, one module per surface):

| Path | Status | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `/device/:device_id` | **new** | Streifen. Replaces the overview-inside-`/list` pattern. |
| `/device/:device_id/day/:dayKey` | **new** | `dayKey` = local `YYYY-MM-DD`. Deep-linkable, stable. Accepts `?addr=fl-12` as an alias that resolves to a dayKey. |
| `/device/:device_id/film` | **new** | Film build + download. |
| `/device/:device_id/charts` | **kept, re-skinned** | The query-string contract is untouched (constraint 9: presets + share links are persisted user data). New params `axis=stage` and `cmp=<run_id>` are additive and ignored by old parsers. |
| `/device/:device_id/plan` | **new** | `/settings` becomes a permanent redirect to it. Old device types render their existing settings components inside it verbatim. |
| `/device/:device_id/diary` | **redirect** | → `/device/:id`. Share links with `page:'diary'` land on a read-only Streifen. |
| `/device/:device_id/testmode`, `/shares`, `/account`, `/classes`, `/demo`, `/login`, `/diagnostics` | unchanged | |

**Navigation model.** The existing `ion-split-pane` + `ion-menu` stays (Geräte / Geteilte Links / Konto).
No tab bar is introduced. Inside a device there is exactly one back destination — the Streifen — and the
Ionic back button is wired for it. On a wide screen the split-pane's second pane holds the Streifen and
the third column holds the currently selected Tagesblatt, so desktop is master-detail for free; this is
the "big screen is an unexploited advantage" win and it costs a CSS grid, not a second design.

The three restricted contexts all work because they are attributes of the Streifen, not separate screens:
share/view-only hides the capture buttons and the Plan link (`canEdit === false`), demo disables saves,
public view swaps the menu button for the theme toggle.

The header address is the concept in one line and it is always on screen. Below it, days descend into

### 3.2 Real German copy — the pieces

- Header: `Blüte · Tag 12` / `Gesamttag 47`. Before a run exists: `Noch kein Durchgang` + `[ Grow starten ]`.
- Freshness chip (three states, never faked):
  `● live` (last sample < 2 min) · `● vor 14 Min` (2–10 min) · `● offline seit 13:20` (> 10 min).
- Verdicts, in priority order, one per day:
  `Gerät offline` › `Kamera ohne Bild` › `Alarm: {name}` › `Nachts zu feucht` ›
  `Zu warm am Tag` › `VPD zu hoch` › `VPD zu niedrig` › `Licht lief nicht` ›
  `Zu wenig Daten` › `Alles im Ziel`.
- Numbers line: `25,4 °C · 54 % · VPD 1,32 · CO₂ 890 ppm`. Comma decimal separator, `de-DE`.
  A missing sensor is simply absent — never `—`, never `n/a`, never a zero.
- Capture: `Notiz` · `Gießen` · `Düngen` · `Foto`. Conditions: `⚠ {text} · {actor}, seit {weekday}` + `[ Erledigt ]`.

### 3.3 Stale, offline, absent — specified

| Situation | Header | Today card | Days below |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Live** | `● live` | photo + verdict + numbers | normal |
| **Stale** (last sample 2–10 min) | `● vor 6 Min` | numbers rendered with the age chip attached to the numbers line: `25,4 °C · 54 % · vor 6 Min` | normal |
| **Offline** (> 10 min) | `● offline seit 13:20` | photo dimmed to 60 % with an overlay strip `Letztes Bild 13:04`; verdict replaced by `Gerät offline seit 13:20`; the numbers line shows the **last known values with their own age**, never blank: `zuletzt 25,4 °C · 54 % · 13:20`; capture buttons stay enabled (logging a watering does not need the device) | normal |
| **Camera silent** (no accepted frame in a lights-on window > 6 h) | unchanged | photo slot becomes a flat surface with `Kamera seit gestern 14:00 ohne Bild` + `[ Testbild ]` | thumbs missing for those days, row keeps its verdict |
| **Camera producing unusable frames** (frames arrive, all culled 3 days running) | unchanged | `Bilder unbrauchbar (zu dunkel) — Kamera prüfen` | |
| **No data at all today** (device never reported) | `● offline` | `Noch keine Messwerte heute` — no fabricated verdict | |
| **No run** | `Noch kein Durchgang` | card shows live numbers, verdict, photo and capture; instead of an address the date | rows addressed by date only |
| **No camera paired** | unchanged | card has no photo slot at all; verdict moves to the top | rows have no thumb column |
| **Brand-new device, no history** | date | `Willkommen. Erster Messwert in wenigen Minuten.` | `[ Grow starten ]` + the setup-wizard entry |

Two rules that hold everywhere: **an age is attached to any number older than two minutes**, and **the
absence of a capability removes its row rather than showing an empty one**.

## 4. NO-MODES MECHANISM — the tent sets the density, not the user

There is one screen. What changes between the stoned user at 2am and the techie on a laptop is not the
screen — it is *which line their eye stops on*, and every line is present for both.

### 4.1 The grammar: `Adresse · Urteil · Zahl`

Every row in this product, on every surface, is the same three parts in the same order:

```
Blüte T10   ·   Nachts zu feucht   ·   68 % (Ziel 50)
└─ where ──┘    └── what happened ─┘    └── how much ──┘
```

The stoner reads the middle and stops. The normal user reads the middle and glances right. The techie
taps the right-hand part, which opens the raw series for exactly that window. **The number is never
hidden and never required.** There is no "show details" — the detail is already the third word of the
sentence. This is progressive precision inside a single line of text, and it is the same line for
everyone, so nothing has to be predicted about who is holding the phone.

### 4.2 Ranked density: the day's own eventfulness sets its height

Row height is a pure function of the data, computed server-side and returned with the day:

```ts
dayScore =
    2 * (photoCount > 0 ? 1 : 0)
  + 3 * humanEntryCount
  + 4 * (openConditions > 0 ? 1 : 0)
  + 5 * (alarmMinutes > 0 ? 1 : 0)
  + 3 * (stageChanged ? 1 : 0)
  + Math.min(4, Math.floor(outOfBandMinutes / 60))
  + 2 * (dataCoverage < 0.8 ? 1 : 0)

score === 0        → Zeile   (44 px: thumb 32 px, address, verdict)
score 1–4          → Zeile+  (72 px: thumb 56 px, address, verdict, numbers)
score >= 5         → Karte   (photo 16:9, entries listed, deviation stated)
```

Today is always a Karte. The day you arrived at from a deep link is always a Karte.

**Why this is not a mode.** A mode asks who you are and then hides things. This asks what happened and
then *sizes* things — nothing is ever removed, and a tap on any collapsed row expands it in place with
no navigation. On a quiet week even the techie's strip is short, because there is genuinely nothing to
say. On a bad night even the stoner's strip is tall, because something is wrong and it should be in the
way. **The tent decides, and it decides the same way for both of them.** That is the property a
preference can never have: it is *correct*, not merely *chosen*.

### 4.3 Reveal by depth: four levels, one object, four URLs

`Zeile → Karte → Tagesblatt → Verlauf (raw series)`. Each level is the same day at more zoom, each is
addressable, each is reached by tapping the thing you were already looking at. The techie's raw
InfluxDB series is at the bottom of the stoner's own path — not behind a switch he would have to know
exists. Nobody is denied it; nobody trips over it.

### 4.4 Reveal by capability and by data

- CO₂ appears in the numbers line only if `deviceHasCo2()`. Fixed today's bug where a controller without
  the SCD4x upgrade is offered CO₂ targets.
- The Ausgänge lane on the chart draws exactly the socket roles in `hardwareInfo.sockets` — per role, not
  the current three-bucket collapse. A heater-only tent is never shown a dehumidifier line, a humidity
  target row, or the "Entfeuchter läuft dauerhaft" alarm preset. (Dossier P0 #3.)
- The photo column disappears when `hardwareInfo.webcam_did` is absent.
- The plant picker does not exist until a `Plant` exists. The actor chip does not exist until a second
  `Actor` exists. The feed prefill line does not exist until a scheme is chosen.

### 4.5 What is deliberately NOT adaptive

Order is strictly chronological, always. Nothing floats up because you touched it. A grower at 2am must
be able to find yesterday by muscle memory. The only thing that varies is height, and height varies
because the *tent* varied — which is exactly the information the user came for.

**The one thing that comes closest to a mode, named honestly:** a `Alle Tage ausklappen` control in the
strip's overflow menu, persisted per device in `localStorage`. It hides nothing — it only forces every
row to Karte. I keep it because a techie reviewing a whole run wants uniform rows, and I flag it in §13
as the seam a critic will push on.

## 5. The chart — Der Verlauf

Apache ECharts 6, tree-shaken, hand-rolled ~50-line Angular directive (owner decision). Chart.js /
ng2-charts / chartjs-adapter-luxon deleted. Existing query-string contract preserved.

### 5.1 Structure

```

│ 27,1 °C  ·  68 %  ·  VPD 0,91  ·  Licht 0 %           │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ [film strip: thumbs positioned by timestamp]          │  56 px
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Temperatur °C                                    32 ─ │
│    ░░░░ night ░░░░        ▒▒▒ day ▒▒▒      ░░░░       │  markArea day/night
│    ┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈ 20 ┈┈┈┈┈┈┈╱─────── 25 ─────╲┈┈┈┈┈┈       │  dashed stepped SETPOINT
│    ▓▓▓ tolerance band ±1,5 ▓▓▓                        │  markArea soft band
│    ───── actual (min/max envelope shaded) ─────       │  solid, minmax sampling
│                                                  18 ─ │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Luftfeuchte %                                         │  own panel, own axis
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤

├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Ausgänge   ▬▬▬Licht▬▬▬  ▪Heizung▪  ▬▬▬▬Entf.▬▬▬       │  state timeline lane
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ▲  ▲▲(3)        ▲              ▲                      │  annotation rail, row 1 human

│ Sa 22.8.   So 23.8.   Mo 24.8.   Di 25.8.             │  ← wall clock
│ Blüte T10  Blüte T11  Blüte T12  Blüte T13            │  ← THE ADDRESS
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**The dual x-axis label is C30's signature in the chart.** Two `xAxis` entries sharing one scale: the
lower one is a category axis of day addresses, the upper the date. `axis=stage` swaps them so day-of-stage
is primary and the date becomes the subtitle — the same data, re-addressed, no new query.

### 5.2 Goal vs actual

- **Setpoint**: a dashed stepped `line` series built client-side from `device.configuration`
  `day.temperature` / `night.temperature` and `daynight.day` / `daynight.night` (seconds-of-day, UTC →
  converted with an explicit device timezone). It steps at lights-on and lights-off, with a
  `lights.sunrise` / `sunset` ramp if configured. No new API — the data is already on the device payload.
- **Tolerance band**: `markArea` at setpoint ± the grow-assistant's existing tolerances (±1,5 °C, ±7 % RH).
- **Deviation**: signed fill between actual and setpoint only — never fill to the axis.
- **Band source priority**, one at a time, always labelled: user alarm threshold (hard) > setpoint ±
  tolerance (soft) > stage `vpdRange` (advisory).
- **Day/night** derived from measured `out_light > 0`, falling back to the schedule. A failed contactor
  then shows as a *missing* shaded band — a diagnostic, not decoration.
- **Time in range**, computed server-side on raw Influx samples (never on `aggregateWindow(mean)`), shown
  as one stacked bar under the panel title: `Tag 92 % im Ziel · Nacht 71 % · 4 h 10 zu feucht`, greyed out
  below 80 % data coverage with `Zu wenig Daten (63 %)`.

### 5.3 Annotations and outputs

- Stage changes: full-height dashed `markLine`, label `Blüte T1`.
- Everything else on a 2-row rail *below* the plot: row 1 human entries (Notiz, Gießen, Düngen, Foto),
  row 2 device events (Alarm, Firmware, Konfiguration). Pixel-proximity clustering at 10 px with a count
  badge. Tap → the Tagesblatt scrolled to that entry. No hover anywhere.
- Outputs in a state-timeline lane: region length = duration, `null` renders as a gap.

### 5.4 Comparison — free, because of the address

`cmp=<run_id>` overlays the previous run's series **aligned on day-of-stage, not on the calendar**, drawn
at 40 % opacity with a dotted stroke. `Blüte T12` sits on `Blüte T12`. This is the retention feature and
in C30 it costs a query parameter, because the alignment key already exists on every row.

### 5.5 Mobile rules

One finger scrolls, two fingers pan/zoom (`dataZoom:{type:'inside'}`), tap sets the crosshair. No floating
tooltip — the pinned header is the readout. Axis labels are never hidden; below 320 px they reduce to
three ticks and the unit moves into the panel title. Okabe-Ito palette, `darkMode:'auto'`,
`sampling:'minmax'` (LTTB smooths away the bang-bang oscillation a grower is looking for), `Als Tabelle`
disclosure per panel.

Indexes: `entries {device_id, occurredAt}`, `entries {run_id, stage, dayInStage}`,
`entries {entry_id} unique`, `dayframes {device_id, dayKey} unique`.

### 6.2 Why the address is denormalised

`dayInStage` could be derived from `StageSegment` at read time. It is stored anyway because it is the
sort key, the group key and the join key for run comparison — deriving it per row would put a segment
lookup inside every query. The cost is `POST /runs/:run_id/recompute`, an idempotent `bulkWrite` that
re-derives the address for a whole run, triggered whenever a stage boundary is edited.

### 6.3 Migration from `DeviceLog`

A boot-time, idempotent backfill (house style — no migration tooling exists):

1. For each device, run the existing `convertEventsToGrowCycles` logic **server-side, once** → `Run` +
   `StageSegment` rows. The runtime string-comparison algorithm becomes a one-time migration, exactly as
   the dossier recommends. Renaming a plant no longer forks history, because history is now a row.
2. Every `DeviceLog` with `categories[0] === 'diary'` or `'diary-plant-lifecycle'` (both slugs, plus the
   legacy `plant-lifecycle`) becomes an `Entry`, `kind` mapped from the category, `dayKey`/address
   computed from the run.
3. The server's own stage-transition rows — today written without the `'diary'` prefix and therefore
   invisible and uneditable — are included. That silently fixes dossier P0 #7.
4. `DeviceLog` keeps working untouched for device events; the strip reads both and the chart's
   annotation rail row 2 is `DeviceLog`, row 1 is `Entry`.
5. `deleted: true` is no longer written on new entries. "Logs löschen" now deletes `DeviceLog` rows only
   and says so: `Geräte-Meldungen löschen (Tagebuch bleibt)`. Dossier P0 #6, closed by a label.

### 6.4 Multi-plant, and how ignoring it costs nothing

The default is one implicit subject: the tent. `plant_ids` empty means "everything". A grower who never
opens `Pflanzen` never sees a plant control anywhere — not a picker, not a chip, not an empty state.

When a plant exists, the capture sheets grow one row: a horizontal chip strip `[Alle] [A1] [A2] [A3]`,
`Alle` preselected. That is the entire multi-plant UI in the capture path. On the Tagesblatt, entries
group under plant chips only when more than one plant was touched that day.

Creating plants is one screen reached from the run header: a text field and `+`, or `A1…A6` generated
from a count. `Pflanzen: 6` on the run header is the only prompt, and it is a link, not a nag.

### 6.5 The diary that keeps itself

Every day gets a row from the machine even if nobody writes: the Tagesbild, the verdict, the numbers, the
time-in-range, the stage address, and any device events. Human entries are additions to a record that
already exists. This is the difference between "a diary you must maintain" and the owner's
"it does the diary keeping for you", and it falls out of the day being the primary object rather than
the entry.

```
│  └─────────────────────────────────────┘ │
│                                          │
│  EC 1,8   pH 6,2          [ messen ]     │  ← collapsed until tapped
│                                          │
│  Wer?  ( Anna )  ( Chris )  [ + ]        │  ← only if >1 Actor exists
│  Wo?   [ Alle ] [A1] [A2] [A3]           │  ← only if plants exist
│                                          │
│  ⚠ Anna hat vor 1 Std 30 gegossen.       │
│     Trotzdem eintragen?                  │
│                                          │
│  [           Eintragen           ]       │  green, 56 px
└──────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**The tap pad is the whole point.** The grower is holding a watering can in one hand in a dark tent. He
taps the pad once per can. There is no keyboard, no slider, no number pad, no decimal. Each tap fires a
20 ms haptic pulse and adds a dot. `← eine weg` undoes. The can size is remembered per device
(`device_prefs.canLitres`, default 2 L) and setting it is four chips. Typing an exact volume is available
by tapping `= 6 L`, which turns it into a number field — progressive precision, not a second flow.

The same pad serves `Düngen` (the sheet is the same sheet with the Dünger block expanded and the litres
still counted by taps, because the dose is per litre) and a bare `Notiz` (the pad is replaced by a text
field). One component, three entry points.

### 7.2 Schemes — selected once, prefill forever

`MediumRegime` is a Mongo content collection, never bundled:

```ts
MediumRegime {
  regime_id: string;
  mediumLabel: string;        // "Biobizz All-Mix"      — plain text, no logo, no colour
  regimeLabel: string;        // "Biobizz Flüssigdünger"
  prefertilised: 'none'|'light'|'heavy'|'slow-release';
  feedStartsAtStage: DiaryLifecycleStage; feedStartsAtWeek: number;
  ecBasis: 'absolute' | 'delta_over_source';
  steps: Array<{ step_id; stage; week; products: Array<{ label; mlPerL; note? }>;
                 ecTarget?: [number, number]; phTarget?: [number, number] }>;
  source_url: string; retrieved_at: number; chart_version: string;
  kind: 'official_pdf' | 'official_calculator' | 'ownerless';
}
```

Chosen once, on the run header: `Medium & Dünger: Biobizz All-Mix + Biobizz` (or `keins`). Then it does
exactly one job: it fills the Dünger block of the capture sheet with the products and doses for the
current address, and every value stays editable. Deviating is the normal case.

The All-Mix rule is expressible and is why the model is medium+regime: with `prefertilised:'heavy'` and
`feedStartsAtStage:'vegetative', feedStartsAtWeek:3`, opening `Düngen` in week 1 shows
`All-Mix ist vorgedüngt — bis Woche 3 nur gießen.` with `[ trotzdem düngen ]` beneath it. A scheme that
cannot say "don't feed yet" is not modelling the domain.

`Zuletzt geprüft: 14.03.2026` sits under the scheme name — the legal source-tracking doubles as a user-
facing trust signal.

**The reminder** (the "thinks for me / reminds me" face): if the scheme's step for the current address
has not been logged and the last `water` entry is older than the run's median watering interval, today's
card grows one line: `Fällig: Bio-Bloom 2 ml/L` + `[ Erledigt ]`, which opens the sheet prefilled. It is
one line on a card that already exists, not a task system.

### 7.3 The double-feed guard

At capture, before the sheet renders its warning:

```
GET /entries/:device_id/recent?kinds=water,feed&plants=A1,A2,A3&withinMinutes=360
```

If anything comes back with a plant-set intersection (or either side is "whole tent"), the amber block
appears with the actor's name and the elapsed time, and `Eintragen` requires one more deliberate tap.
Windows: `water` 6 h, `feed` 24 h, per-device adjustable in Plan. Proceeding sets
`duplicateAcknowledged: true`, so the club report can show `2 Einträge, bewusst bestätigt` instead of a
silent double.

### 7.4 Offline capture

No service worker exists today. v1 adds `@angular/service-worker` for **one thing only**: an outbox that
holds `POST /entries` bodies made while offline and replays them. Full app offline is not claimed.
Double-logging on retry is structurally impossible because `entry_id` is generated on the client at the
moment of tapping and carries a unique index — a replayed POST is a no-op, not a second row. This is
flagged as a new dependency and a build change.

### 7.5 The cumulative answer

The run header answers it without a report: `46 L Wasser · Bio-Bloom 380 ml · Bio-Grow 190 ml`. Tapping
it opens a per-plant breakdown with actors:

```
A3          14 L      Bio-Bloom 128 ml     zuletzt: Anna, gestern 19:40
A2          16 L      Bio-Bloom 132 ml     zuletzt: Chris, Sa 09:10
```

One aggregation pipeline over `entries` grouped by `plant_ids` and `feed.productLabel`.

## 8. Camera, timelapse and image correlation

This is the headline. Everything below ships in v1 unless marked.

### 8.1 The Tagesbild — one canonical frame per day

The device already delivers a still every 30 s. That is ~2 000 frames per lights-on day and 170 000 per
run — unusable as a film and unusable as a diary index. Once per day, a job picks **one** frame per
`{device_id, dayKey}` and writes a `DayFrame`. That frame is the strip thumbnail, the Karte photo, the
film frame and the export's poster.

**Selection.** Target time = lights-on + photoperiod/2 (the tent's own noon, derived from measured
`out_light`, falling back to `daynight`). Candidates = the 60 frames nearest that time. Each is scored
and the best survivor wins. All of it uses `sharp`, already a server dependency.

**The cull, in order (each is a cheap operation on a 64×64 greyscale downscale unless noted):**

| # | Reject | Test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Lights off | `out_light == 0` at that timestamp (Influx point lookup), or mean luma < 25 |
| 2 | Blown out | mean luma > 220, or > 8 % of pixels at 255 (door open onto daylight) |
| 3 | Blurred / condensation | 3×3 Laplacian via `sharp.convolve()`, then `stats().channels[0].stdev` below the run's rolling 20th percentile |
| 4 | Human / door / hand in shot | 32×32 greyscale absolute difference against the **median 32×32 of that day's candidates**; reject if mean diff > 12/255 or if any 4×4 tile differs by > 60/255. Catches a person, an open door, a moved fan |
| 5 | Camera nudged | integer cross-correlation of the 64×64 against the run's reference frame over ±16 px. Best offset stored as `shiftX/shiftY`. Offset ≤ 24 px → compensate by cropping. > 24 px for three consecutive days → re-baseline the reference and log `Kamera wurde bewegt — Film ab Blüte T12 neu ausgerichtet` |
| 6 | Near-duplicate | 64-bit dHash; Hamming < 4 from the previous kept frame (only used for sub-day films, never for the day film) |

If every candidate is rejected the day has no `DayFrame`: the strip row shows no thumb and the reason
(`kein brauchbares Bild: zu dunkel`). Three such days in a row raises the camera-quality alarm. **Nothing
is fabricated.**

**Cost bound.** Only the 60 candidates around tent-noon are decoded, at 64×64, once per day, per device.
That is ~60 sharp calls per device per day — trivial next to the ffmpeg work already running hourly.

### 8.2 Normalisation across twelve weeks

Two corrections, both stored on the `DayFrame` and both **smoothed over a 7-day rolling median** so a
single odd day cannot flicker:

- **Exposure**: scale luma toward the run's median target with `sharp.linear(a, b)`.
- **White balance**: grey-world per-channel gains `gainR/gainG/gainB`, again rolling-median smoothed.

The intent is *deflicker*, not beautification. A magenta LED tent stays magenta; what disappears is the
jump between a magenta day and a daylight-contaminated one. Stated plainly in the UI:
`Bilder werden angeglichen (Helligkeit & Weißabgleich)` with an off switch on the Film screen.

Stabilisation is translation-only, applied as a crop to the intersection rectangle of all kept frames'
offsets in the run. Rotation is not corrected — honest limitation, stated in §13.

### 8.3 The film that leaves the product

```
GET /image/:device_id/film
      ?run=<run_id>
      &from=<dayKey|addr>&to=<dayKey|addr>       default: whole run
      &fps=12&hold=2&stamp=1&normalise=1
      &token=<imageToken>
```

- **Codec: `libx264`, High profile, `-pix_fmt yuv420p`, `-crf 20`, `-movflags +faststart`.**
  Today's timelapses are `libx265`, which does not play in Safari, in most Android gallery apps, or in
  Telegram's inline player. **Changing the codec is a one-line fix and it is the difference between a
  video that leaves the product and one that does not.** The hourly `1d/1w/1m` timelapses switch too.
- **Resolution**: source width capped to 1280, `scale=1280:-2` (even height required by yuv420p).
- **Length**: `hold=2` holds each day frame for 2 output frames, so a 12-week run of 84 days is 168
  frames = 7,0 s at 12 fps. Measured target ≈ 2–4 MB — under Telegram's limit, under the 16 MB BSON cap,
  and short enough that someone watches it twice.
- **Burn-in caption** (`stamp=1`, default on): ffmpeg `drawtext` reading a generated timeline file,
  bottom-left, Inter (already in `assets/`), 3 % of frame height, `box=1:boxcolor=black@0.45`:

  ```
  Blüte T12 · 22.8. · 25,4 °C · 54 % · VPD 1,32
  ```

  **This is image correlation shipped inside the artefact.** The numbers travel with the file, so a video
  pasted into Telegram is self-explaining and needs no login — which is precisely the failure the camera
  critic named. `stamp=0` produces a clean film.
- **Download**: `Content-Disposition: attachment; filename="terpcontrol_zelt-1_bluete-t01-t56_2026-10-14.mp4"`
  plus `filename*=UTF-8''…` for umlauts. ASCII-folded, lower-cased, non-alphanumerics collapsed to `-`.
  No Content-Disposition header exists anywhere in the codebase today; this is new and it is four lines.
- **Path to the phone**: a plain `<a href download>` with the token in the URL. iOS Safari → Files →
  share sheet → Fotos. Android Chrome → Downloads → gallery. No login wall, because `imageToken` is
  already designed to be URL-embeddable. Under Capacitor later: `Filesystem.writeFile` + `Share.share`.
- **Build**: queued through the existing `pLimit(10)`; a `FilmJob {job_id, state, progress, image_id}`
  row drives `Film wird gebaut … 40 %` on the Film screen and a push when it is done. Result cached as
  `Image { format:'mp4', duration:'run', timestamp, timestampEnd }` — one new value in an existing enum,
  and `timestampEnd` already marks ranged mp4s. Invalidated when a new `DayFrame` lands.

### 8.5 Image correlation — three bindings, both directions

1. **Row ⟷ day.** Every strip row carries its Tagesbild. Scrolling the diary flips through the timelapse.
   This is the concept's name and it costs nothing extra — the frame is already the row's thumbnail.
2. **Chart ⟷ frame.** ECharts `axisPointer` emits a timestamp; the film strip above the chart moves its
   marker and the pinned header shows that frame. Dragging the film strip moves the chart cursor.
   Delivery is one sprite per window, not N requests:
   `GET /image/:device_id/strip?from&to&n=100` → a JPEG sprite of 100 tiles at 256×144 plus a JSON index
   of `{timestamp, x, y}`. (v1.1 — v1 lazy-loads individual 200×112 thumbs and is honest about it.)
3. **Frame ⟷ numbers.** Tap any photo → fullscreen, address and numbers of that minute overlaid:
   `Blüte T12 · 13:04 · 25,4 °C · 54 % · VPD 1,32 · Licht 100 %`, with `[ Im Verlauf zeigen ]` opening
   the chart at that instant and `[ Als Tagebuchbild ]` pinning it to the day's entry.

### 8.6 The camera alarm

Two, both new, both on the existing alarm/notification path:

- **`Kamera stumm`** — no accepted frame for > 6 h inside a lights-on window. Device log severity 1,
  push/e-mail per the user's existing alarm targets, and the today card states it.
- **`Bilder unbrauchbar`** — frames arrive but every one is culled, three days running, with the dominant
  reason named. This is the case that silently destroys a 12-week film, and nobody looks until harvest.

## 9. Clubs

The posture: **attribution without accounts, safety at capture, no personal data.**

- **Actors are device-scoped names.** The owner types `Anna`, `Chris` once in Plan → Personen. No login,
  no invitation, no `auth.middleware` surgery. Stamped onto `Entry.actor_id` + `actorName` at capture. A
  free-text name works: `Chris`, `Rot`, `Zelt-Dienst Di` are all valid — the product never demands a real
  identity. Roughly two days of work, and it is the only realistic multi-user story for October.
- **Reveal by data, not by mode**: the `Wer?` row appears in the capture sheet only when a second Actor
  exists. A solo grower never sees attribution anywhere. There is no "club mode" switch.
- **The double-feed guard** (§7.3) is the club feature that actually matters. A double feed is the most
  expensive thing that happens in a shared room, and it is prevented at the moment of capture, by name,
  with one deliberate extra tap and a record that the operator knew.
- **Conditions** are the pinned note on the tent door, promoted to the top of the strip:
  `⚠ Steckdose 3 sitzt locker · Chris, seit Di` / `⚠ A3 nicht gießen — übergossen · Anna, seit gestern`.
  Open facts with an owner, no due date, closed by anyone with `[ Erledigt ]`. They appear above today's
  card, so they cannot be missed, and they vanish completely when none are open.
- **The cumulative answer** (§7.5) is the club's real reporting need: who gave what to which plants this
  cycle.
- **Corrections, not edits.** A wrong entry is superseded (`supersedesEntryId`), never overwritten; the
  strip greys the original. Append-only without cryptographic theatre.
- **Privacy posture, default private:** no real names required, no location collected or inferred, no
  harvest weight prompted (the field exists on `harvest` entries and is empty and optional), no community
  feed, and `Personen löschen` wipes every `actor_id` and `actorName` from the device's entries in one
  action. The account owner can always delete anything — immutability would victimise an unregulated
  grower, not protect him.
- **Sharing**: the existing `ShareLink` with `page:'diary'` renders a read-only Streifen. Export offers
  `Namen: vollständig / Initialen / anonym`, defaulting to **Initialen**. A public run URL
  (`/g/<slug>-<5char>`) is v1.1.
- **Refused, deliberately:** per-member dispensing records, grams, THC content, membership numbers, birth
  years, any § 26 KCanG filing. Terp Control is the device-attested environmental layer; it is not the
  club's system of record and must never claim compliance.

## 10. Old devices (type 7) — fan, light, plug

A fan/light/plug owner opens the app and gets **the same Streifen**, degraded honestly by capability. No
separate design, no legacy shell.

**What is different, concretely:**

- **No run, no address by default.** The header reads `Abluft Keller · Sa 24.8.` — a day number is never
  invented. `Grow starten` is offered once, quietly, on the run-footer; if he takes it, he gets the full
  address grammar. If he never does, the strip is a strip of dates and everything else works.
- **No photo column.** `hardwareInfo.webcam_did` is absent, so the thumb column and the Film entry point
  do not render at all.
- **The day row shows what the device actually measures.** Fan: `2 400 U/min · Ø 26,1 °C · 14 h an`.
  Plug: `An 14 h 20 · 7 Schaltungen`. Light: `An 06:00–18:00 · 100 %`. The verdict grammar still applies:
  `Alles im Ziel` / `Licht lief nicht` / `Gerät offline seit 13:20`.
- **Capture is reduced to what makes sense**: `Notiz` and `Foto` (a phone upload — `user/jpeg` already
  exists) always; `Gießen` / `Düngen` only when a Run exists.
- **Settings are untouched.** `/device/:id/plan` renders the existing `fan-settings` /
  `light-settings` / `plug-settings` components verbatim inside a `Geräteeinstellungen` card, behind the
  same `ngSwitch` on `device_type` that exists today. Zero rewrite, zero regression risk. The six
  raw-key i18n bugs in plug settings (`settings.limits.overtemperature.enabled` and friends) are fixed
  in the same pass, because they are visible on the screen we are now sending people to.
- **Every existing link keeps working.** `/device/:id/diary` redirects to the Streifen; `/charts` keeps
  its query-string contract, so saved presets and circulating share links resolve unchanged.
- **The chart degrades by measure**: panels are built from measures the device actually reported, so a
  plug gets one output lane, no setpoint band and no empty temperature panel.

What a type-7 owner gains: a diary that fills itself without a camera, and a chart with day/night shading
and a real axis.

## 11. Per-user-type walkthrough

**1 — Monitor-only, lazy.** He opens the app once a week. The strip is seven short rows, most of them
`Alles im Ziel`, and the two that are not are taller and say why. He never touches a control because the
capability probe removed all of them. The value he came for — "was it OK while I wasn't looking" — is the
verdict column, and the time-in-range bar on the Tagesblatt turns that from an impression into a number.

**2 — Monitor plus camera.** This is C30's best-served type by a wide margin. The strip is his timelapse;
he scrolls it as entertainment. At harvest he opens Film, picks `Keimung T1 → Heute`, waits for a
progress bar, and downloads a 7-second, 3 MB mp4 with the temperature burnt into the corner that plays in
his phone's gallery and in Telegram. He never learned a feature; he tapped a photo.

**3 — Partial control (CO₂ or heating only).** He sees setpoint bands and output lanes for exactly the
roles his sockets report and nothing else — no humidity target he cannot act on, no dehumidifier alarm
preset. His `Plan` screen has two target rows. The strip's verdicts are drawn only from the measures he
controls, so the app stops nagging him about a lever he does not own.

**4 — Full tent.** Five socket roles is what the firmware has, and C30 does not pretend otherwise: the
Ausgänge lane draws the five, `dehumidifier` is labelled by workmode (`Entfeuchter` / `Kühlung`) so the
double meaning is at least visible, and the setpoint/deviation drawing finally lets him see the
controller fighting itself at lights-on. He gets no new hardware capability from this concept, and the
Plan screen says which of his targets the firmware actually reads — the dead `daynight.floating` block is
removed rather than shipped as a working control.

**5 — Closed-loop DIY.** Worst served, and I will not pretend. He gets a stable `Entry` schema, a
documented `dayKey`/address model, and a JSON export that is the first clean grow-diary schema anyone has
published. He does not get a write API, custom measures, or MQTT in v1. The honest pitch is that the
address model is the thing worth designing an API *around*, and it now exists.

**6 — Club / shared tent.** Two or three people, one tent, one login. They add three actor names once.
Every water and feed carries a name and a colour chip. When Chris opens `Gießen` ninety minutes after
Anna did, the app says so before he taps, by name. Conditions live above today's card where the note
would have been taped. At the end of the cycle the run header reconstructs who gave what to which plants.
No accounts were created and no personal data was stored.

**7 — Diary keeper / publisher.** He gets the record without maintaining it: a row per day, filled by the
machine, that he decorates with notes and photos. `Woche exportieren` produces a Markdown/BBCode block
with day-of-stage headings and hot-linked image URLs he pastes into a forum or Telegram. The week
buckets are derived from `dayInRun` and stored, so a GrowDiaries-shaped export is not lossy. Publishing
is copy-and-paste by design; nothing auto-posts anywhere.

## 13. Honest weaknesses

1. **Autoflowers have no stage flip, and half the hobby market grows them.** There is no 18/6 → 12/12
   transition to detect and no meaningful "Blüte T1". The address degrades to `Tag 47` and C30's primary
   organising idea quietly stops paying. Partial mitigation: an autoflower run is marked as such and the
   address becomes `Tag 47` everywhere consistently — but the comparison feature loses most of its
   alignment value, and I have no better answer.
2. **The address is wrong until someone sets a stage.** Inference from `out_light` history is fallible: a
   grower who runs 20/4 then 12/12, or who flips while the device is offline, gets a wrong `Blüte T1`.
   Every entry then carries a wrong denormalised address until the recompute job runs. The job is
   idempotent, but a half-failed recompute leaves an internally inconsistent diary, and there is no
   migration tooling in this codebase to lean on.
3. **One address per run cannot express a perpetual grow.** Plants at different stages in one tent — the
   common club case — share the run's stage. A staggered room is served badly, and `Plant` carrying its
   own stage segments is real work I have not scoped.
4. **Ranked density changes under the user's thumb.** A row can grow from 44 px to a Karte when an alarm
   resolves or a photo lands, shifting scroll position. Anchoring by dayKey mitigates it; it will still
   occasionally jump, and the impaired 2am user is exactly who that hurts. A mode would not have this
   problem. I think the trade is right; a critic will not agree.
5. **`Alle Tage ausklappen` is the seam.** It is persisted, it changes the whole screen, and if it ever
   grows a second option it becomes the mode this round bans. It survives only because it hides nothing.
6. **The film is one frame per day, which is not what Instagram taught people to expect.** No swaying
   leaves, no time-of-day motion — 84 still portraits. It is the honest sampling rate of a plant and it
   is watchable, but the first reaction from some users will be "that's it?". The existing `1d` timelapse
   stays available beside it, and that is a partial answer, not a full one.
7. **Stabilisation is translation-only, and culling is heuristic.** A camera knocked and *rotated* on
   day 12 leaves a visible step nothing corrects; a legitimately dark late-flower frame can score as
   "too dark" and heavy defoliation looks like "change". The skipped-days list exists because I expect
   this, and a user who does not read it will wonder where day 4 went.
8. **Strip performance on a mid-range Android.** Ninety day-rows each with a thumbnail is an image budget
   problem, and the sprite that solves it is cut to v1.1. v1 will be janky on a €150 phone.
9. **Attribution without authorisation.** Anyone holding the single login can stamp "Anna". The label
    is useful between people who trust each other and worthless as evidence, and the UI must never imply
    otherwise.
10. **Type 5 gets almost nothing.** No write API, no custom measures, no MQTT. The most sceptical,
    highest-advocacy segment in the market is deferred, again.
11. **The strip is a lot of German text.** Ranked density keeps quiet days to one line, but a bad week
    produces a tall wall of prose, and a stoned user at 2am may still bounce off it. The verdict-first
    line ordering is the mitigation; it is not proof.
12. **`dayKey` depends on `Run.timezone`.** A user who travels, or a device whose timezone is wrong,
    gets days that start at the wrong hour, and a 12/12 flip near a DST boundary drifts. It is stored
    explicitly for exactly this reason, and it will still generate at least one bug report.
