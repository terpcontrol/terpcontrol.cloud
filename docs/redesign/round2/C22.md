# C22 — "Das Wochenblatt"

**Family:** diary · **Centre of gravity:** the week is a printed page, and that page is a stored row.

---

## 1. Thesis

**Terp Control's unit of product is one sheet per week — fourteen fixed rows of numbers, pictures,
feeds and events — computed by the cloud, stored as a row in a table, and printed identically for
every user; the app is a binder of those sheets.**

Every other concept in this bake-off is a *view over a query*. You choose a timespan, the app runs
Flux, something is drawn, and when you leave, nothing remains. That is why the diary is abandoned in
week three and why "export" has never existed: there is nothing to export, only a query to re-run.

Das Wochenblatt inverts it. When a week ends, a job computes a `GrowWeek` document — day and night
climate with time-in-band, photoperiod and DLI, actuator runtimes, litres watered, millilitres fed
per product, plant heights, the culled week film, the events, the grower's note, the open
conditions — and **seals it**. That document is simultaneously the screen, the export, the share, the
forum post and the club's record. GrowDiaries buckets by week. Grow with Jane indexes by day. The
CSV a club treasurer opens is one row per week. A grower says "letzte Woche war zu feucht". The week
is the shape the domain already has, and nobody has stored it.

Two consequences follow that no query-based concept gets. First, **absence becomes visible**: a week
with no contact is still a page, printed empty, and an empty page is an obviously wrong thing in a
binder. A missing chart is not. Second, **the diary keeps itself**: thirteen of the fourteen rows are
filled by the cloud from data the device already sends. The grower supplies row 7 (watered), row 8
(fed), row 9 (heights) and row 12 (the note). Everything else is already written when he opens the app.

---

## 2. Information architecture

Five screens. No tabs hiding features; the sheet is the destination and everything else is reached
from a row on it.

```
/list                                 device list (unchanged; one device → straight to its sheet)
/device/:id                           DAS BLATT — the current week's sheet          ← the home screen
/device/:id/w/:isoweek                one past sheet (deep link, shareable)
/device/:id/mappe                     DIE MAPPE — all weeks as one table + export   (the binder)
/device/:id/verlauf                   the chart, entered from a row, week pre-selected
/device/:id/regeln                    settings (today's fridge/controller settings, renamed + cleaned)
/device/:id/leute                     people at the tent, privacy posture, share links (club)
/device/:id/testmode  /diagnostics    unchanged, admin
```

**Navigation model.** The `ion-menu` stays for account-level things. Inside a device there is a
persistent **week rail** pinned under the header — the spine of the binder:

```
  ‹ 33  34  35  36 [37] ›            KW 37 · Grow-Woche 7 · Tag 45–51
```

Horizontal swipe = previous/next sheet. Tap a number = that sheet. The rail is the only navigation
inside a device that changes *what you are looking at*; the four other screens are reached by tapping
into a row (`Serie ansehen` → chart, `Alle Wochen` → binder, `Regeln` in the header, a person chip →
`/leute`). No bottom tab bar, no FAB. This keeps the existing single-`ion-split-pane` shell intact.

**Wide screen.** The sheet is a literal spread. Left page = rows 0–9 (the numbers). Right page =
rows 10–13 (pictures, events, note, open conditions). Same order, same rows, one scroll on a phone,
two columns at ≥ 900 px. This is the only place in the concept where the big screen changes anything,
and it costs one CSS grid rule.

One row, four taps deep, no preference written anywhere.

### States

**Fresh (< 10 min).** Row 0 prints `vor 40 Sek.` in normal weight.

**Stale (10 min – 3 h).** `vor 1 Std. 12 Min.` in `--tc-text-warning`, values printed normally.
Never blanked — the last known value is information.

**Offline (> 3 h, or `lastseen` beyond `ONLINE_TIMEOUT`).** Row 0's header becomes
`JETZT — Gerät offline seit Mi, 14:02`, the four values grey out but stay printed, and the strip in
row 10 stops at the last frame with the server's existing offline caption burned in. The aggregate
rows are unaffected — they describe a week that already happened.

**Partial coverage.** Any aggregate computed from < 80 % coverage prints
`nur 61 % Messabdeckung — Werte mit Vorsicht lesen.` under the number, and the in-band bar renders
hatched instead of solid. Never a silently averaged lie.

**No data at all.** The sheet still exists. Every cell prints `—`, and one line sits across the
numeric block: `Kein Kontakt zum Gerät vom 8. – 14. Sep.` The page is the point.

**No camera.** Row 10 prints `BILDER — keine Kamera gekoppelt` with `Kamera einrichten ›`. It is not
removed; a missing camera is a fact about the week.

**Camera silent.** Row 10 header turns red: `Kamera seit Mi, 14:02 still` with `Prüfen ›`.

**First week ever.** Rows 1–6 fill within an hour of the device coming online (the compute job runs
hourly on the open week). Rows 7–9 and 12 print their prompts. The sheet is honest about being new:
the header reads `KW 37 · Grow-Woche 1 · Tag 1–3` because the run started on a Friday.

## 4. NO-MODES MECHANISM — "Das Blatt ist ein Formular, kein Dashboard"

The mechanism is **fixed-slot printing with absence printed**. Three rules, and one falsifiable test.

**(a) Invariant geography.** Fourteen rows, always the same order, on every device, every week, for
every user, forever. Nothing is promoted, demoted, reordered, added or removed. Ever. The impaired
user at 2 a.m. does not search — he scrolls to where the number *always is*. This is a deliberate
refusal of the brief's "adaptive ordering by behaviour" option: unpredictability is precisely the tax
the stoned user cannot pay, and a printed form's whole value is that page 3 is always page 3.

**(b) Absence is content, not a hidden feature.** A row without data prints its own prompt
(`Noch nichts eingetragen · + Gießen`). A row the hardware cannot fill prints its own reason
(`CO₂ — dieses Gerät misst kein CO₂`). A row whose entity does not exist prints the offer
(`PFLANZEN — keine angelegt · +`). **Because nothing is ever hidden, there is nothing to unhide, and
therefore nothing to toggle.** The entire class of "Erweitert" accordions is structurally unavailable
to this design: you cannot hide a row without leaving a hole in a printed page, and a hole is worse
than a grey line.

**(c) Depth is per-cell, in place, and transient.** Every printed cell expands where it stands into
its own evidence — band, coverage, sample count, per-day breakdown, raw CSV, the exact frames. It
collapses when you leave the row. **No expansion state is ever persisted.** A drawer that closes
itself is not a mode, because a mode is a state you are *in*.

**The falsifiable test.** Hand two phones to two people looking at the same device in the same week:
**the pixels must be identical.** Under this design they are, with exactly one exception — which
person chip is preselected in the capture sheet (`localStorage['tc-actor-<device_id>']`). That is a
name, not a capability. If anyone can produce a second pixel difference between two users of the same
device, the concept has failed its own rule and the row causing it must be un-hidden.

**What is *not* an exception.** Capability differences (`CO₂` grey on a device without an SCD4x) are
device-level, not user-level: both people see the same grey row on the same device. Two devices
differ; two users of one device do not.

**Where the techie is served without a mode.** The last line of every expanded row is
`Rohdaten CSV` and, on climate rows, the literal Flux window and the sample count. The binder
(`/mappe`) is a dense table — one row per week, 34 columns — with a `JSON` button that returns the
`GrowWeek` documents verbatim. That is not an expert screen; it is the *same fourteen fields*,
transposed. A techie who ignores it loses nothing; a stoner who lands on it sees a table and swipes
back.

## 5. The chart

Reached only from a row (`Serie ansehen ›`), which means it always opens with a context: **this
week, this measure**. ECharts 6, hand-rolled ~50-line Angular directive, tree-shaken (owner
decision). Stacked small multiples, shared x-axis, one linked crosshair via `axisPointer.link` +
`echarts.connect()`.

- **Goal vs actual.** `markLine` with `type: 'line', step: 'end'` drawn from
  `GrowWeek.climate.setpointDay/setpointNight` — the *frozen snapshot of what the setpoints were
  during that week*, not today's config. This is the single reason the sheet must persist setpoints:
  a chart of last month drawn against this month's target is a lie, and every query-based concept
  ships that lie. Band = `markArea` at setpoint ± tolerance, or at the user's own alarm thresholds
  when they exist (labelled `dein Alarm` vs `Ziel ± 1,5`). Deviation is a signed fill between actual
  and setpoint — never a fill to the axis.
- **Actual** is `{min, max, mean}` from one combined Influx query: mean as the line, min/max as a
  faint envelope, `sampling: 'minmax'` in the plot (LTTB smooths away exactly the bang-bang
  oscillation the grower is looking for) and LTTB in the navigator.
- **Day/night** from measured `out_light > 0` where available, falling back to `daynight.day/night`
  in the device's explicit timezone. A failed contactor shows as a *missing band* — a diagnosis.
- **Annotations** live in a 16 px rail under the axis, two rows (row 1 human `CareEvent`s, row 2
  device logs), clustered at 10 px with count badges, tappable — not hoverable. Lifecycle changes are
  full-height dashed lines. Week boundaries are thin labelled lines, because the chart belongs to a
  binder.
- **Outputs** are a state-timeline lane, region length = duration, nulls render as gaps.
- **v1 honesty:** the chart ships as *one* configurable panel plus the outputs lane and the rail. The
  existing 1205-line Highcharts page stays reachable as `Alle Serien` until v1.1 finishes the port.
  A full 15-measure ECharts rewrite in six weeks alongside everything else is not credible.

## 6. The diary and multi-plant

The `GrowWeek` is the aggregate. Underneath it are four small new collections. All new fields are
optional, all reads pass through a normaliser, `schemaVersion` is stamped — no migration tooling
exists.

```ts
Run {                                     // "Durchgang" — the grow this week belongs to
  _id; device_id;
  label: string;                          // "Blaue Traube"
  startedAt: number; endedAt?: number;
  stage: DiaryLifecycleStage; stageChangedAt: number;
  plan?: 'photoperiod' | 'autoflower';
  mediumId?: string; regimeId?: string;   // → FeedRegime
  privacy: 'private' | 'club';            // storage posture, see §9
  schemaVersion: 1;
}

Plant {                                   // deliberately tiny
  _id; device_id; run_id;
  label: string;                          // "A1"
  strain?: string;                        // plain text, no catalogue
  source?: 'seed' | 'clone';
  potLiters?: number;
  startedAt?: number; endedAt?: number;
  note?: string;
}

CareEvent {                               // one row per human act
  _id; event_uuid: string;                // client-generated v4, UNIQUE INDEX → idempotent replay
  device_id; run_id; week_id;             // week_id denormalised at write, for the aggregate
  kind: 'water' | 'feed' | 'measure' | 'train' | 'note' | 'harvest';
  at: number; capturedAt: number;         // at = when it happened, capturedAt = when it was saved
  actor_id?: string; actor_name: string;  // denormalised: survives deleting the person
  plant_ids: string[];                    // [] === "das ganze Zelt"
  liters?: number;
  items?: { product: string; amountMl?: number; mlPerL?: number; source: 'regime' | 'manual' }[];
  ec?: number; ph?: number;
  ecBasis?: 'absolute' | 'delta_over_source';
  runoffEc?: number; runoffPh?: number;
  heightCm?: number;                       // kind === 'measure'
  regime_id?: string; regimeStep?: number; deviatedFromRegime?: boolean;
  note?: string; image_ids?: string[];
  supersededBy?: string;                   // corrections append, never edit in place
}

Condition {                               // an open fact with an owner — row 13
  _id; device_id; run_id?;
  text: string;
  openedBy: string; openedAt: number;
  closedBy?: string; closedAt?: number;
}
```

**The `GrowWeek` itself** (the thing this concept is named after):

```ts
GrowWeek {
  _id; device_id; run_id?;
  isoYear: number; isoWeek: number;       // the page's identity: 2026-W37
  startsAt: number; endsAt: number;       // Monday 00:00 in the device's timezone
  weekOfRun?: number;                     // GrowDiaries bucket
  dayOfRun?: [number, number];            // [45, 51] — Grow-with-Jane compatibility
  stage?: DiaryLifecycleStage; stageWeek?: number;
  climate: {
    dayTempMin, dayTempMax, dayTempMean, nightTempMin, nightTempMax, nightTempMean,
    dayRhMean, nightRhMean, vpdMean, vpdMin, vpdMax, co2Mean?, leafTempMean?,
    tempInBandPct, rhInBandPct, coveragePct,           // computed on RAW samples, never on means
    longestExcursionMs, longestExcursionAt,
    setpointDay: {temperature, humidity}, setpointNight: {temperature, humidity},
    bandSource: 'alarm' | 'setpoint' | 'stage'
  };
  light: { photoperiodH, limitPct, dliMol?, firstOnAt, lastOffAt, flipDetected?: boolean };
  outputs: { heaterOnMs, dehumidifierOnMs, co2OnMs, lightOnMs, secondaryLightOnMs };
  water: { events: number; totalLiters: number; byPlant: Record<string, number> };
  feed:  { events: number; byProduct: {product, totalMl}[]; ecMean?, phMean? };
  plants: { plant_id, label, heightCm?, heightDeltaCm? }[];
  media: { heroImageId?, stripImageIds: string[], filmImageId?, filmSeconds?,
           framesTotal, framesKept, culled: {night, door, duplicate, blurred} };
  events: { alarmCount, configChanges, logIds: string[] };
  note?: { text, updatedAt, actor_name };
  openConditions: string[];
  computedAt: number; sealed: boolean; dirty: boolean; schemaVersion: 1;
}
```

**How a user who ignores plants is untaxed.** If no `Plant` exists for the run, `plant_ids` is `[]`
on every event, meaning "the whole tent". The capture sheet does not draw a plant picker. Row 9
prints `PFLANZEN — keine angelegt · +` and nothing else. Every aggregate reports per tent. Nothing
anywhere requires a plant to be created, and creating one later does not retro-tax old events —
they stay tent-scoped and the cumulative view says so honestly (`vor A1: 8 l ans Zelt`).

**Legacy.** `DeviceLog` rows with `categories[0] === 'diary'` are read into row 11 unchanged, through
the existing `LogTranslateService` and its seven legacy regexes. `convertEventsToGrowCycles()` runs
**once**, as a boot-time backfill that creates `Run` documents from the inferred cycles, and is then
deleted from the runtime. Two existing bugs are fixed as part of this because the sheet exposes them:
the server's stage-transition entries get the missing `'diary'` category prefix, and `deleted` stops
meaning "visible in the diary" (a real `hiddenFromOverview` boolean replaces it, backfilled).

## 7. Watering & fertilising

Row 7's button is the entire capture flow. **The tap count is the spec.**

**The repeat case — 2 taps.**

```
Tap 1: [+ Gießen]  on the sheet
   ↓  bottom sheet, prefilled from the last event + the regime
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Wer?   (Anna) (Mo) (Jens)          ← sticky │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  GIESSEN                        Heute 19:42  │
│                                              │
│      −      4,0 l      +                     │
│                                              │
│  Alle Pflanzen  ·  A1 A2 A3 A4   ← alle an   │
│                                              │
│  ☑ Bio-Grow 2 ml/l  (Plan Woche 7)   8 ml    │
│    All-Mix · Biobizz Bio-Grow/Bio-Bloom      │
│                                              │
│  EC 1,4   pH 6,3        (letzte Werte)       │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│              [ Speichern ]                   │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
Tap 2: [Speichern]
```

Everything is prefilled: the volume from the last watering, the plants from all active plants, the
nutrient line from the `FeedRegime` step for `weekOfRun`, EC/pH from the last measured values, the
person from `localStorage`. **Two taps and the week's row 7 and row 8 both move.** Deviating costs
taps and only taps: `−`/`+` is 1 tap per 0,5 l; deselecting a plant is 1 tap; swapping a product is
2 taps (open, pick); adding a second product is 2 taps.

**The double-feed guard — the deliberate third tap.** On `Speichern`, if any selected plant has a
`CareEvent` of kind `feed` inside `regime.minGapHours` (default 20 h) *by anyone*, the button is
replaced in place:

```
│  ⚠ Anna hat A1–A3 vor 1 Std. 30 Min. gefüttert.│
│              [ Trotzdem eintragen ]            │
```

One extra tap, made deliberately, with the name and the elapsed time in it. A watering inside the
window does not warn — over-watering is recoverable, over-feeding is not. The warning is computed
client-side from the week's already-loaded events plus one `GET /device/:id/care?since=` so it works
while a second person is at the tent.

**Schedule following.** The regime is chosen once, at run start, as a **medium + feed line pair**
named the way growers say it:

```
│  Worin wächst es?                             │
│  ( Biobizz Light-Mix )  ( Biobizz All-Mix )   │
│  ( Floragard Light )    ( Coco )  ( Eigenes ) │
│  Wie düngst du?                               │
│  ( Biobizz Bio-Grow/Bio-Bloom )               │
│  ( Green House Powder Feeding )  ( BioTabs )  │
│  ( Lucas-Formel )  ( Kein Plan )              │
```

`FeedRegime { _id, device_id?, mediumLabel, regimeLabel, steps: [{ weekOfRun, items: [{product,
mlPerL}], everyNthWatering?, hint? }], minGapHours, source_url, retrieved_at, chart_version }` lives
in a **MongoDB content collection**, never in the bundle, so any brand comes out in one DB update.
Brand names are plain text labels; no logos, no colours, never inside a Terp Control feature name.

The regime's only job is to prefill. Its second job is to make the row say the right thing when the
answer is "don't feed":

```
│ ▸ GEDÜNGT                          nichts     │
│   All-Mix ist vorgedüngt — bis Woche 4 nur    │
│   Wasser.                        + Trotzdem   │
```

And when a feed is due and none is logged, the row 8 button reads `Düngen fällig ›` in green. That is
the whole reminder system: no task list, no inbox, no badge — **the sheet's own row tells you it is
empty when it should not be.** A push notification (existing alarm mail path) fires once on the
second overdue day, opt-in.

```
│   Bio-Bloom      0 ml           38 ml         │
│   A3 gesamt:     46 ml Bio-Grow               │
│   Zuletzt: Fr 18:20 · Anna · 2 ml/l · EC 1,4  │
│   [Alle Einträge ›]   [CSV]                   │
```

One `$match` + `$unwind items` + `$group` aggregation over `CareEvent`. It is answerable because
`plant_ids` and `run_id` are on every row.

**Offline.** `@angular/service-worker` ships in v1 (`ng add @angular/pwa`) for app-shell caching, so
the app loads in a cellar. Capture writes to IndexedDB immediately with the client-generated
`event_uuid` and the real `at` timestamp; the queue drains on reconnect via `POST /device/:id/care`
which **upserts on `event_uuid`**. A retried request is a no-op, so the retry queue cannot double-log
— which in a shared record is indistinguishable from a double feed. The sheet shows queued events in
italic with `wartet auf Verbindung`.

## 8. Camera, timelapse and image correlation

The camera is not a row that happens to be on the sheet; **the week film is the second half of the
product** and the reason a €349 bundle exists.

### 8.1 What ships in v1 — a film someone can hold

**The Wochenfilm.** For every `GrowWeek`, one mp4: 15–20 seconds, 1280×720, 25 fps, ~375–500 frames,
built when the week seals and rebuilt hourly while the week is open. Stored as an `Image` document
with `format:'mp4'`, `duration:'gw'` (the enum `['1d','1w','1m']` gains `'gw'` and `'run'` — a
one-line Mongoose change), `timestamp` = week start, `timestampEnd` = last frame, plus new optional
`week_id`, `run_id`, `framesKept`, `framesCulled`. At CRF 20 that is 3–6 MB, comfortably inside the
16 MB BSON limit.

**Three v1 bugs in the existing pipeline that this fixes, and must fix:**

1. `convertRtspStreamImagesToVideo()` encodes **`libx265` at CRF 30**. HEVC does not decode in Chrome
   or Firefox and many Android gallery apps refuse it. **Today's timelapses are, for most users, an
   unplayable file.** v1 switches to `libx264 -profile:v high -pix_fmt yuv420p -crf 20 -movflags
   +faststart`. This is the single highest-value line change in the whole concept.
2. No `+faststart` means the file cannot start playing before it is fully downloaded.
3. `-pix_fmt yuv420p` is required because JPEG input yields `yuvj420p`, which several players
   render with wrong levels or reject.

**How the video leaves the product.** Two buttons in row 10, both v1:

- `Film sichern` → `GET /image/film/:device_id/:week_id.mp4?token=<imageToken>` →
  `Content-Type: video/mp4`, `Accept-Ranges: bytes` (required for iOS Safari playback),
  `Content-Disposition: attachment; filename="terpcontrol_blaue-traube_2026-W37.mp4"`.
  Filename pattern: `terpcontrol_<run-label-slug>_<isoYear>-W<isoWeek>.mp4`, ASCII-folded, 64 chars
  max. The existing 30-day URL-embeddable `imageToken` carries the auth, so the URL can be pasted.
- `Film teilen` → `navigator.share({ files: [new File([blob], name, {type:'video/mp4'})] })` when
  `navigator.canShare({files})` is true (Android Chrome, iOS 15+ Safari). This is the **only honest
  route to a phone's camera roll without a native app**: the OS share sheet contains "In Fotos
  sichern". Where it is unavailable the download button is shown alone, with the plain-language hint
  `Auf dem iPhone: Video antippen, dann „In Fotos sichern".`

**The Durchgangsfilm** (whole run, ~90–120 s) is built **on demand and streamed, never stored** —
2 500 frames would exceed the BSON limit. It is offered on the run's last sheet as
`Ganzen Durchgang als Film ›` with a progress line; the job reuses the same culling and the same
encoder.

### 8.2 Culling and normalisation — making it watchable

A raw tent timelapse is mostly garbage. At capture time the server already holds the frame and the
device's last status, so per-frame metadata is free:

```ts
Image.meta = {
  meanLuma, stdLuma, rMean, gMean, bMean,   // sharp().stats(), already a dependency
  sig: Buffer,                              // 8×8 grayscale fingerprint, 64 bytes
  ctx: { t, rh, vpd, co2, lightPct, day: 0|1 }   // from the last MQTT status, zero extra queries
}
```

Six rules, applied in order when the film is built:

1. **Nacht.** Drop frames where `ctx.lightPct === 0` or `meanLuma < 12`. A dark tent is not content.
   Opt-in `Nachtbilder` keeps one per night.
2. **Zelt offen / Hand im Bild.** `hamming(sig, dayMedianSig) > 22` **and** `meanLuma` more than 35 %
   above its ±2 neighbours → a person opened the tent. Drop it and its two neighbours. This works
   because a tent is a static scene under constant light: a large, brief, bright delta has exactly
   one cause.
3. **Doppelt.** Consecutive frames with `hamming < 3` → keep the first. Kills the dead hours.
4. **Beschlagen.** A high-pass `stdLuma` below threshold → fogged lens. Drop; if > 30 % of a day is
   fogged, open a `Condition` automatically: `Kameralinse beschlagen (automatisch erkannt)`.
5. **Takt.** From what survives, pick evenly to hit the target frame count (≈ 60/day for a week film)
   so an outage reads as a *shorter* film rather than a lurch.
6. **Angleichen.** Per-frame `sharp().linear(gain, offset)` so each frame's `meanLuma` matches a
   rolling 24-frame median (deflicker), plus per-channel gain toward the week's median `r/g/b` ratio,
   **clamped to ±20 %** so a genuine change (lamp dimmed, door daylight) is softened, not erased.
   This is what removes the magenta-LED-versus-daylight strobe that makes tent timelapses unwatchable.

**Stabilisation is explicitly NOT in v1, and the honest substitute ships instead.** A persistent step
in the day-median fingerprint (> 30 hamming, holding for > 6 h) means *the camera moved*. The film is
cut into segments at that point, and row 10 prints
`Kamera am 12. Sep. verschoben — Film ab hier neu ausgerichtet.` Pretending to stabilise badly is
worse than saying the camera moved. `vidstab` two-pass is v1.1.

**Culling is visible and reversible.** Row 10 prints `1 344 Bilder · 402 im Film · 942 aussortiert`,
and tapping it gives `Nacht 780 · Zelt offen 41 · doppelt 98 · beschlagen 23` with
`Wieder aufnehmen` per reason per day. **Culled frames are never deleted** — only excluded from the
film. The existing thinning tiers continue to govern storage.

**The camera alarm.** A watchdog in the hourly job: if the newest `jpeg` is older than 3 h while
`lastseen` is fresh, write a `DeviceLog` `message-camera-silent` at severity 2 and send it down the
existing alarm-notification path. Row 10 turns red. A camera that stopped on day 3 of flower tells
you on day 3, not on day 60 when you open the film.

### 8.3 Image correlation

Because `Image.meta.ctx` is stamped at capture, correlation is a lookup, not an analysis.

- **Frame → numbers.** Tap any thumbnail in row 10 → full screen, the picture with
  `Mi, 10. Sep. 14:02 · 26,3 °C · 51 % · VPD 1,44 kPa · Licht 100 %` printed beneath, and left/right
  scrubbing through the week's kept frames.
- **Numbers → frame.** Scrubbing the chart moves a thumbnail pinned in the chart's scrubbing header.
  In v1 that is one `GET /image/:device_id?timestamp=` per settle (debounced 250 ms, and the endpoint
  already resizes server-side via `?width=`). In v1.1 it becomes a sprite: `GET /device/:id/sprite?
  from&to&n=50` → one 50-tile JPEG at 256×160 plus a JSON index, so a week costs one request.
- **Frame → week.** Any full-screen frame has `Diese Woche ansehen ›` back to its sheet. A picture is
  always attached to a page.
- **Event → frame.** A `CareEvent` with no photo still gets one: the sheet prints the nearest kept
  frame beside `Fr 18:20 Entlaubt (Anna)`. The camera photographs the diary whether or not anyone
  remembers to.

## 9. Clubs

**Attribution without accounts.** `Person { _id, device_id, name, color, createdBy, active }` — the
owner types the names once in `/leute`. There are no logins, no invitations, no `auth.middleware`
surgery. The capture sheet's top row is a chip row; the last-used chip is preselected per browser and
stamped onto `CareEvent.actor_id` + `actor_name` (denormalised, so deleting a person does not orphan
history). Cost: about two days.

**The row does not exist until it does.** A single grower never adds a name, so the chip row is not
drawn and `actor_name` is `''`. Reveal by data, not by a club switch.

**Authorisation, honestly labelled.** `/leute` prints, permanently:
`Namen sind Zuordnung, keine Zugangskontrolle.` Attribution is a label. The one real grant this
concept adds is `ShareLink.canLog: boolean` — a share link that may `POST /device/:id/care` and
upload images, and **nothing else**: it cannot read or write `device.configuration`, alarms, cloud
settings or firmware. That is one new field plus one branch in `isUserDeviceOrShareMiddelware`, and
it gives a club real multi-person capture in v1 without touching `auth.middleware.ts:172`. It is a
genuine relaxation and §13 names it as such.

**The double-feed problem** is solved at the moment of capture (§7), across people, because the guard
queries `CareEvent` by plant and window, not by author.

**Conditions** are row 13 — the thing that gets taped to the tent door.
`Condition {text, openedBy, openedAt, closedBy?, closedAt?}` prints on **every** sheet until closed,
so a new person at the tent reads "CO₂-Flasche fast leer · seit Di (Anna)" before they touch
anything. Closing is one tap (`✓`), and the close is stamped with a name.

**Privacy posture — a storage policy, not a UI mode.** `Run.privacy`:

- `private` (default): no harvest weight is ever prompted, no plant count is required, `Person` is
  empty, `GrowWeek` retention 24 months, and `/leute` offers `Alles löschen` (device data wiped in
  one action).
- `club`: attribution required on capture, retention 5 years, the CSV/JSON export gains an
  `Anonymisieren` toggle **defaulted on** (names → `Person A`, `Person B`).

This changes what is *stored and exported*. It changes no screen, hides no control, and reveals none:
row 7's capture sheet has the same fields either way; in `club` the person chip is simply required
before `Speichern` enables. Every pixel of the sheet is identical. That is the line between a data
policy and the banned mode.

**Refused, permanently:** per-member dispensing records, THC content, membership numbers, dates of
birth, addresses, any § 26 KCanG filing, any `KCanG-konform` badge. The sheet is a *cultivation*
record. The club's own compliance SaaS is a better home for the rest, and if integration is ever
pursued, the `GrowWeek` JSON is already shaped like `Cannanas.CreateDiaryRequest.measurements`.

## 10. Old devices (device_type ∈ {fan, light, plug})

Nothing about the sheet is controller-specific — it is fourteen rows over whatever a device emits.

**A `light` owner** opens `/device/:id` and sees: header, week rail, `JETZT` (its temperature if it
has one, its dim level, its on/off state, with an age), `KLIMA TAG` / `KLIMA NACHT` / `FEUCHTE`
filled from whatever sensors it reports, `CO₂ — dieses Gerät misst kein CO₂` in grey,
`LICHT 12 Std. 00 · 60 % · An 06:00 Aus 18:00 · gemessen`,
`TECHNIK — Licht 84 Std. 00 · 0 Alarme`, `EREIGNISSE`, `NOTIZ`, `OFFEN`. Rows 7, 8, 9 and 10 are
governed by data, not by type: **water, feed and plants appear once a `Run` exists**, and `BILDER`
appears once a camera is paired. A light over a windowsill plant, with a run started, gets the whole
sheet. A light in a cupboard, with no run, gets nine rows and no nagging.

**A `plug` owner** gets the same nine rows, with `LICHT` replaced by
`SCHALTER — An 6 Std. 20 · 14 Schaltungen`. **A `fan` owner** gets `TECHNIK — Lüfter ⌀ 1 840 rpm ·
Laufzeit 168 Std. 00` and its rpm series charted like any other measure.

**Settings are untouched.** `/device/:id/regeln` keeps today's `ngSwitch` to `fan-settings`,
`light-settings`, `plug-settings` verbatim — the only change is the route name and the removal of the
"FRIDGE GROW" label from the overview card (which today literally tells a controller owner he owns a
fridge). No old device loses a control, gets a new required field, or is asked to migrate anything.

**What an old device *gains*:** a weekly record, a CSV, an offline-capable app shell, and a page that
says plainly when it was not reachable. What it loses: nothing. The `dryer` type stays dead per
`AGENTS.md`.

## 11. Per-user-type walkthrough

**1 — Lazy monitor-only.** He opens the app after three weeks and gets three sheets he did not write.
Rows 1–6 are complete, rows 7–13 mostly empty, and that is a fine outcome: he wanted values recorded
and looked at later, and later has arrived with the numbers already summarised into
`91 % im Band`. He never taps `+ Gießen` and is never asked to. His `CO₂` row is grey with a reason.

**2 — Monitor + camera.** The bundle buyer. Row 10 is why he bought it: seven day-frames and an
18-second film per week, culled and deflickered, with a `Film sichern` button that produces
`terpcontrol_blaue-traube_2026-W37.mp4` in his phone. Tapping any frame shows the temperature at that
second. He is the one user type this concept is unambiguously built for, and the one all ten
round-one concepts failed.

**3 — Partial control (CO₂ or heating only).** He controls one thing, so exactly one line of row 6
carries a number and the rest print `nicht angeschlossen` — capability read from
`hardwareInfo.sockets` per role, not collapsed into three buckets. His `KLIMA NACHT` row shows
`Ziel 20,0` and `78 % im Band`, which is the only feedback loop he actually has, and the chart he
reaches from that row draws his setpoint against his actual.

**4 — Full tent.** Row 6 is dense: four runtimes and an alarm count, expandable into the outputs
state-timeline. He is also the type most likely to notice the hardware's real limit — there is no
humidifier role and `out_dehumidifier` doubles as the cooler — and the sheet does not paper over it:
row 6 prints `Entfeuchter/Kühler` with both meanings, because one socket is being asked to do two
jobs and the data cannot distinguish them.

**5 — Closed-loop DIY.** The binder is his API. `/device/:id/mappe` is a 34-column table with `JSON`
and `CSV` buttons, and `GET /device/:id/weeks.json?from=&to=` returns the `GrowWeek` documents
verbatim with a documented, versioned schema — the first published grow-diary schema anywhere. He
gets no write API in v1 and the doc says so. What he does get is a stable weekly aggregate he can
poll, which is more than Pulse's read-only API gives him.

**6 — Club.** Three people, one tent, no accounts. Chips at capture, `actor_name` on every row, a
soft block on the double feed with the other person's name in it, cumulative "how much has A3 had
this cycle and from whom" in row 8, standing conditions in row 13, and a `canLog` share link so the
person without the login can still record a feed. Export defaults to anonymised. No member ledger,
no grams, no THC.

**7 — Old device owner.** §10. Nine rows, no new required fields, settings untouched, and for the
first time a record of what his cupboard did last week.

**Capability lenses.** *Stoner:* fourteen rows in a fixed order that never move, one green button that
says `Düngen fällig`, two taps to log, and a film to watch. *Normal:* a page that looks like a grow
journal, with a photo strip and a shareable weekly post — the thing he was going to type into
GrowDiaries anyway. *Techie:* every cell opens onto its band, coverage, sample count and raw CSV, and
the whole binder is one JSON endpoint.

## 12. v1 scope for October 2026

Six to eight weeks. This is a large concept and the cut list matters more than the ship list.

**Ships in v1:**

1. `GrowWeek` collection, the hourly/nightly compute job, and a one-time backfill from existing Influx
   and `DeviceLog` history. Time-in-band computed on **raw** samples, never on `aggregateWindow(mean)`.
2. The sheet page for all device types, phone + spread layout. Fourteen rows, all states in §3.
3. The week rail and `/mappe` (the binder table) with CSV + JSON export.
4. `Run`, `Plant`, `CareEvent`, `Condition` collections; the 2-tap capture sheet; the double-feed
   guard; `event_uuid` idempotency; IndexedDB queue; `ng add @angular/pwa` for app-shell caching.
5. Camera: **x264/yuv420p/faststart encoder fix**, per-week film with the six culling rules and
   deflicker, `Image.meta` stamping at capture, hero frame + day strip, the download endpoint with a
   real filename, `navigator.share` file sharing, the camera-silence watchdog.
6. `Person` chips + `ShareLink.canLog`.
7. `FeedRegime` content collection with six seeded medium+regime pairs (Biobizz Light-Mix, Biobizz
   All-Mix, Floragard Light, Green House Powder Feeding, BioTabs, Lucas-Formel), prefill only.
8. Chart: ECharts 6 single-measure panel with stepped setpoint, band, day/night, annotation rail and
   outputs lane, reached from a row. `Alle Serien` keeps the existing Highcharts page.
9. Free correctness fixes the sheet forces: controller naming pass (kill "FRIDGE GROW" + the missing
   `devices.controller` i18n block), the `deleted`/`hiddenFromOverview` fix, the missing `'diary'`
   category on server stage transitions, server-side log pagination, the six raw-key i18n bugs, the
   duplicate `noData()` call, and clamping `interval` against `timespan` in `/data/series`.
10. de + en for every new string, in the same commit, using the `message-*` convention.

**Explicitly v1.1 or later:** stabilisation (`vidstab`); the sprite film-strip as the chart's x-axis;
the full 15-measure ECharts port; the run film as a stored artefact; per-plant camera crops; the
public diary URL; Telegram/Discord/webhook publishing; the Markdown/BBCode post generator (a strong
candidate to pull forward — it is one template and one clipboard call); harvest weights; a real
`Membership` collection; a write API; VPD as an expressible goal.

**The realistic risk in this plan** is item 1 plus item 5 landing in the same six weeks. If the
schedule slips, the cut is item 8 (keep Highcharts entirely for v1) and item 3's JSON endpoint — not
the camera, and not the sheet.

## 13. Honest weaknesses

1. **A week is the wrong resolution for a crisis.** A tent at 34 °C right now is served by one sticky
   row at the top of a long retrospective page. Row 0 turns red and alarms still mail, but the
   concept's centre of gravity is backward-looking, and against a verdict-first dashboard it will
   lose the "is my tent OK *now*" comparison. I accept this: alarms are a notification problem, not a
   layout problem, and the app is not where you find out about an emergency.
2. **Week 1 is a nearly empty page**, and week 1 is exactly when the first 150 buyers form their
   opinion. Rows 1–6 fill within an hour, but rows 7–13 are prompts. The unboxing experience of Das
   Wochenblatt is structurally weaker than any live-tile concept's.
3. **ISO weeks and grow weeks are not the same week.** GrowDiaries buckets by day-of-run; I page by
   calendar week because that is how a grower speaks. A run starting Thursday makes week 1 four days
   long. Both indices are stored on the same row and the header prints both
   (`KW 37 · Grow-Woche 7 · Tag 45–51`), but this seam will confuse someone, and comparing "week 3"
   across two runs that started on different weekdays is subtly unfair.
4. **Persisted aggregates go stale, and stale aggregates are a support burden.** A backdated feed, an
   edited timestamp, or a changed setpoint marks the week `dirty` and triggers recompute — so a
   sealed number can change after someone has already exported it. Corrections append rather than
   edit, which helps the audit but not the confusion.
5. **Compute cost is real and the infrastructure is not ready for it.** Raw time-in-band over a week
   is ~120 000 samples per measure per device, and InfluxDB today has **no retention policy and no
   downsampling tasks** — raw 5-second samples are kept forever. The nightly job is fine for 150
   devices and is not obviously fine for 5 000.
6. **The culling can hide your best moment.** A grower who defoliates on Friday evening produces
   exactly the signature rule 2 drops as "Zelt offen". The counter is printed and every frame is
   recoverable, but an automatic editor that removes the week's most interesting event is a trust
   problem, and no amount of receipts fully fixes it.
7. **Row 0 duplicates the chart.** The live values appear on the sheet and again in the chart's
   scrubbing header. That is a redundancy I chose deliberately (the sheet must be self-sufficient)
   and it is still redundancy.
8. **The fixed row order is the anti-mode mechanism and also a rigidity.** The DIY closed-loop builder
   wants his own measures at the top and cannot have them; the club wants row 8 first and cannot have
   it. I refuse reordering because predictability is the whole reason the stoned user can use this
   page, and I pay for it in exactly the segments that are loudest.
9. **`ShareLink.canLog` is a write grant on a bearer URL.** Anyone who gets the link — including
   anyone the link is forwarded to in a Telegram group — can log a feed. It is revocable, expiring,
   scoped to `CareEvent` and images, and cannot touch the device, but it is a real relaxation of a
   read-only model, and it will produce at least one "who logged that?" support ticket.
10. **No hardware means no prevention.** The double-feed guard is a dialogue box. With no dosing
    hardware, a club member who taps `Trotzdem eintragen` — or who simply does not open the app —
    still double-feeds the plants. This concept improves the *record*, not the *outcome*.
11. **Autoflowers make a thin binder.** Eleven pages, ever. And a dryer or a curing jar produces four
    sheets of nearly identical numbers. The weekly cadence flatters a 16-week photoperiod run and
    flatters nothing else.
12. **Two collections that must not drift.** `GrowWeek.water.totalLiters` is a cached sum of
    `CareEvent.liters`. If the recompute job ever falls behind, the sheet and the CSV disagree with
    the event list on the same screen. A nightly consistency check is in scope; the class of bug is
    not eliminable, only monitored.
