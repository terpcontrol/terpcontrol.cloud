# C15 — Pflanzenporträt

**Family:** camera · **Centre of gravity:** a portrait series per plant, from one fixed camera.

---

## 1. Thesis

**One tent camera already photographs every plant every 30 seconds; the only thing missing is a rectangle
per plant, so give each plant a crop region with a validity window and every frame becomes a frame of
that plant's own growth film.**

Terp Control already stores a JPEG per device every 30 s and already builds three rolling mp4s an hour
(`server/src/services/image.service.ts`). It throws away the fact that a tent frame is not one picture —
it is four pictures stacked in one file. A `PlantView` is a normalised rectangle `{x,y,w,h}` over the
source frame with a `validFrom`/`validTo`; the render path calls `sharp().extract()` on frames that are
already in Mongo. Zero extra capture, zero extra stored bytes for stills, no second camera, no firmware
change. The rectangle is the whole trick, and the validity window is what makes it survive reality: when
you nudge the camera on day 12 or move the pot, you close the old rect and open a new one, and eight
weeks of A1's history stays A1's history.

That gives the product something no competitor has: **a film per plant, not per room**, and a caption
band burned into every frame that carries the temperature, humidity and VPD of that exact instant. The
data leaves with the picture. That is what "image correlation" means when it is a file on a phone and
not a tooltip in a web app.

## 2. Information architecture

Every existing route survives with its query-string contract intact, because share links and chart
presets are persisted user data (`ChartPreset.query`, `ShareLink.query`).

| Route | State | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `/list` | kept | Device list; one device → redirect to `/device/:id` |
| `/device/:id` | **new** | **Zelt** — the home screen. Picture, strip, plants, chart, capture bar |
| `/device/:id/plant/:plant_id` | **new** | **Pflanzenporträt** — same page, cropped to one plant |
| `/device/:id/film` | **new** | **Filmwerkstatt** — pick a range, build, download, share |
| `/device/:id/charts` | kept | Unchanged component and query params. Reachable from `Alles zeigen` |
| `/device/:id/diary` | kept | `entries` / `growreport` / `co2report` + a new `plant=` filter param |
| `/device/:id/settings` | kept | `ngSwitch` on `device_type`, unchanged for fan/light/plug |
| `/device/:id/testmode`, `/shares`, `/account`, `/classes` | kept | untouched |

`/device/:id` and `/device/:id/plant/:pid` are **the same Angular component and the same NgModule**
(`DeviceHomeModule`), differing only in whether a `plant_id` is present in the route. Navigating between
tent and plant is a route param change, not a page change: the picture cross-fades from full frame to
crop, the strip re-fetches, the chart keeps its cursor and its window. There is no "plant mode".

**Navigation model.** Keep the single `ion-split-pane` + `ion-menu` (Geräte / Geteilte Links / Konto).
Add no tab bar. Inside the device shell there is exactly one navigation affordance — the **Pflanzenzeile**,
a horizontal row of crop thumbnails: `Zelt · A1 · A2 · B1 · ⊕`. Tapping one changes the route param.
On a wide screen (≥ 992 px) the row becomes a left rail and the picture, strip and chart all get bigger —
the big screen is spent on resolution, which is the one thing this concept actually needs.

### 3.3 Stale, offline, absent — precisely

**Picture is stale (last frame 10–120 min old).** Show it anyway, at full size, unmodified. The burned
caption already says `Di 14:02`, so the picture cannot lie about its age. Above the strip, one line:
`Neuestes Bild ist 47 Min alt.` No greying, no spinner, no skeleton — a real old photo beats a placeholder.

**Camera silent > 2 h.** A `Zustand`-style amber row appears above the picture:
`Kamera meldet sich seit 3 Std nicht. Letztes Bild: Di 14:02.  [Testbild holen]`
`[Testbild holen]` calls the existing `POST /image/test/:device_id` with the stored `rtspStream` and shows
the result or the ffmpeg error verbatim. This is also a **push notification** (§8.6) — round one gave the
camera no alarm at all.

**Device offline.** The frame keeps rendering; the server already composites an overlay
(`imageService.addOfflineOverlay`). That overlay's caption is currently English
(`buildOfflineCaption` → `Offline since 12 Sep 2026, 14:02 CEST`) — v1 localises it to
`Offline seit 12.09.2026, 14:02 MESZ`. The chart draws a grey `markArea` over the gap with the label
`keine Daten`, not a straight interpolated line.

**No camera paired at all** (`hardwareInfo.webcam_did` absent and `cloudSettings.rtspStream` empty).
The picture block does not render as an empty frame. It is replaced by the caption band promoted to a
full card — the same two lines, same typography, at card size — plus one muted line beneath:
`Keine Kamera gekoppelt.  [Kamera einrichten]`. The Pflanzenzeile does not render (a crop of nothing is
nothing). The strip does not render. Everything else on the page is identical. This is reveal-by-capability
in its plainest form: the picture is missing because there is no picture, and the numbers that would have
been drawn on it are drawn instead.

**No plants yet.** The Pflanzenzeile renders exactly one card: `⊕ Ausschnitt ziehen`, subtitle
`Mach aus deinem Zeltbild eine Pflanze`. That single 96 px card is the entire tax on a user who never
wants plants. It never grows, never nags, never opens a tour.

**No data at all** (brand-new device, no samples). Picture block shows the placeholder PNG the server
already ships. Chart shows `Noch keine Messwerte. Das Gerät meldet sich alle 5 Sekunden.` Capture bar
stays live — a user can log a watering before the first sample arrives.

## 4. NO-MODES MECHANISM

**The picture is the index, and the numbers are inside the picture.**

Every entry point in this product is a photograph with its own measurements burned into it. There is no
"simple view" that shows fewer things and no "expert view" that shows more. There is one screen whose
top layer happens to be a photograph and whose lower layers are the series that produced it, and the
photograph is not a *summary* of that data — it is a datum with the same timestamp, taken by the same
device, subject to the same freshness rules.

Three mechanisms make that work, none of them a mode:

**(a) The caption band is a chart with n = 1.** The stoner at 2 a.m. reads `24,6 °C · 58 % · VPD 1,12`
off the picture without touching anything, in the dark, one-handed, and the number is exact — not a
verdict, not a colour, not a smiley. The techie reads the identical string. Nobody was given a
simplification and nobody was denied a number. Progressive precision here does not mean "verdict then
number" — it means **the number is the top layer, and the series behind it is one gesture away.**

**(b) The split is proportional, never inventory.** The picture, the strip and the chart are all on
screen at every scroll position. Dragging the strip's grab-handle up or down reallocates height between
picture and chart — continuously, from 70/30 to 30/70 — and at no position does anything disappear.
The chart never goes below 140 px and the picture never below 120 px. The split persists per device in
`localStorage['tc-split-<device_id>']`, which is safe **because it changes no feature's existence**: a
second user on the same account, or the same user on a new phone, sees every control they saw before,
just at a different proportion. Compare this with the banned `app-settings-expert`, where the persisted
value determined whether the recipe editor existed at all. A remembered proportion is a scroll position.
A remembered mode is a different app.

**(c) Everything on the page is reached by pointing at a picture.** Plants are crops you tap. Time is a
strip you drag. Events are dots under the chart that scrub the picture. There is no menu of features to
be intimidated by and no submenu of features to be hidden in. The techie is not served by extra chrome;
they are served by the fact that **every picture is addressable by timestamp and every timestamp is
addressable by picture**, in both directions, with the raw series and a `Als CSV` link one tap under the
chart. The techie's density comes from the coupling, not from a second interface.

**What this deliberately does not do:** it does not adapt ordering by behaviour. The Pflanzenzeile is in
creation order, forever. The capture bar is Gießen / Füttern / Foto / Notiz, in that order, forever. An
impaired user's muscle memory is worth more than any personalisation gain, and the brief is right to
flag adaptive ordering as dangerous.

## 5. The chart

Apache ECharts 6, tree-shaken, hand-rolled ~50-line Angular directive (owner decision). Chart.js /
ng2-charts / chartjs-adapter-luxon deleted in the same commit.

**Panels.** Small multiples with one shared x-axis and one linked crosshair (`grid[]` +
`axisPointer.link`). On the device home the default stack is two panels at 90–110 px each:

```
Panel 1 — Temperatur                                   °C
  ▬▬▬  gemessen (solid, Okabe-Ito vermillion #D55E00)
  ┄┄┄  Ziel, gestuft (dashed, same hue at 55 % alpha)
  ▒▒▒  Abweichungsfläche zwischen beiden, signed fill
  ░░░  markArea Nacht (grey 6 %) / Tag (transparent)

Panel 2 — Luftfeuchte + VPD                            % / kPa
  ▬▬▬  rF gemessen (#0072B2)
  ┄┄┄  rF Ziel, gestuft
  ▬▬▬  VPD (#009E73), with the stage band as markArea
```

`Alles zeigen` opens `/device/:id/charts` — the full measure catalogue, unchanged URL contract.

**Goal vs actual.** The setpoint is a **stepped dashed line** built client-side from
`device.configuration`: it steps at `daynight.day` and `daynight.night` (seconds-of-day, UTC → rendered
in the device's timezone) between `day.temperature` and `night.temperature`. The target *band* is drawn
behind it, priority order, one at a time, labelled: the user's own alarm thresholds
(`Alarm.upperThreshold`/`lowerThreshold` — today unused by the chart) beat setpoint ± tolerance, which
beats the stage `vpdRange` advisory from `GROW_STAGE_PRESETS`. A caption under the panel names the
source: `Band: dein Alarm 18–28 °C` / `Band: Ziel ±1,5 °C`. The deviation fill goes **between actual and
setpoint**, signed — never `threshold: null` to the axis, which encodes nothing.

**Day/night.** `markArea` from measured `out_light > 0` where the series exists; falls back to the
configured `daynight` schedule when it does not, with a differently-weighted edge so the two are
distinguishable. Ramps from `lights.sunrise`/`lights.sunset` render as gradient edges. A failed contactor
therefore shows as a **missing band** — a diagnostic. Timezone is explicit and named on the axis
(`MESZ`), because a 12/12 flip near a DST boundary otherwise drifts an hour.

**Outputs.** A state-timeline lane, 22 px, under the panels: one row per paired socket role, region
length = duration, null = gap. Roles come from `hardwareInfo.sockets`, so a heater-only tent has one row
and no phantom dehumidifier row. Legend labels come from `socket-info.ts`.

**Annotations.** A rail *under* the x-axis, 20 px, max two rows: row 1 human events (`Event`), row 2
device events (`DeviceLog` severity ≥ 1). Pixel-proximity clustering at 10 px bins with count badges.
Stage changes draw a full-height dashed vertical with a top label (`Blüte`). Tapping a dot does two
things at once: it scrubs the picture to that moment **and** opens a one-line summary above the chart.
This is the mobile fix for round one's hover-only height-1 columns.

**Interaction.** One finger scrolls the page. Two fingers pan/zoom (`dataZoom: inside`). Tap or long-press
sets the crosshair. There is no floating tooltip — the crosshair writes into a **pinned scrubbing header**
directly above the chart, which is the same DOM element as the caption band under the picture. Scrubbing
the chart therefore visibly rewrites the caption on the photo. That is the correlation, felt.

**Sampling.** `sampling: 'minmax'` on the plot (LTTB smooths away exactly the bang-bang oscillation a
grower is looking for), LTTB on the navigator. Server-side, the existing `/data/series` Flux injection
and the unbounded `limit`-after-`yield` must be fixed before the measure vocabulary widens by one term.

### 6.2 How a user who ignores plants is untaxed

`plant_ids: []` means "the tent". Every capture defaults to `[]` when the device has zero plants and to
"all non-archived plants" when it has some. The plant chip row inside a capture sheet **does not render
at all** below one plant. The diary reports, the chart annotation rail, the film builder and the export
all treat `[]` as a first-class value, not a missing foreign key. Nothing in the app requires a plant to
exist, and nothing prompts for one after the single `⊕ Ausschnitt` card.

The migration is honest about its own noise. `convertEventsToGrowCycles()` (today a runtime browser
algorithm that forks history on rename) becomes a **one-time server backfill**: each inferred cycle
becomes a `Plant` with `origin: 'backfill'` and `label` from `lifecycleName`. For most legacy users that
produces plants literally called `My Strain`. So backfilled plants are held behind one dismissible card
on first open: `3 frühere Durchgänge gefunden. [Übernehmen] [Verwerfen]` — Verwerfen deletes them.
After that, the runtime string-comparison algorithm is deleted, and renaming a plant no longer forks
eight weeks of history.

dose list. A scheme that cannot express "All-Mix, so don't feed yet" is not modelling the domain.

### 7.3 Double-feed guard

At sheet-open time, `GET /event?device_id=&type=feed&since=<now-20h>` (water: 6 h). If any selected plant
has a hit, the sheet renders, **above the primary button**:

```
│ ⚠ Anna hat A1–A3 vor 1 Std 30 gefüttert.  │
│   [ Nur B1 füttern ]   [ Trotzdem ]       │
```

Already-fed plants get an amber chip. `Nur B1 füttern` deselects them and the warning disappears — the
safe path is **fewer** taps than the unsafe path, which is the only way a guard survives contact with a
person in a hurry. `Trotzdem` is one deliberate extra tap and stamps `feed.note = 'doppelt bestätigt'` so
the history says what happened. This is device-scoped and needs no accounts.

### 7.4 What is stored, and the cellar problem

One `Event` per capture. `event_id` is **generated on the client** as a uuid before the POST, and the
server upserts on `event_id`. That single decision makes the offline retry queue idempotent by
construction — a double POST cannot become a double feed, which in a shared record is indistinguishable
from an actual double feed. Captures go to IndexedDB first and flush on reconnect; rows queued but not
yet confirmed render with a `↑` glyph and the text `wird gesendet`.

`@angular/service-worker` is a new dependency and a build change. In v1 the IndexedDB queue and the
idempotent id ship (they are pure app code); the service worker that makes the *app shell* load in a
cellar with no signal is v1.1. Saying otherwise would be a lie about what "works offline" means.

### 7.5 The cumulative question

On `/device/:id/plant/:pid`, directly under the picture:

```
Bilanz — Tag 34, seit 04.07.
34,5 L Wasser · 12 Gaben · Bio-Bloom 210 ml · Bio-Grow 96 ml
zuletzt Di 19:40 von Anna
```

One aggregation over `Event` filtered by `plant_ids`. `Alle Gaben zeigen` opens the diary entries report

## 8. Camera, timelapse and image correlation

This is the concept, not a tile.

### 8.1 The crop — the whole mechanism, in one query parameter

`sendImage()` in `image.controller.ts` already pipes through `sharp()` for `width`/`height`. Add:

```
GET /image/:device_id?format=jpeg&timestamp=<ms>&crop=0.12,0.31,0.34,0.52&width=1080
GET /image/:device_id?format=jpeg&timestamp=<ms>&plant=<plant_id>&width=1080
```

`crop` is four normalised floats, clamped to `[0,1]` and to the frame's real dimensions, then
`sharp(image).extract({left,top,width,height}).resize(...)`. `plant` resolves server-side to the
`PlantView` whose `[validFrom, validTo)` contains the frame's timestamp — so the client never has to know
that rectangles have a history. Roughly 25 lines in the controller plus one indexed lookup.

**Cost accounting, honestly:** zero extra stored bytes for stills, one extra sharp `extract` per request
(~4 ms at 1080p), and `Cache-Control: max-age=3600` is already set so the strip's thumbnails are cached.

**Drawing a rect.** `⊕ Ausschnitt` opens the newest frame full-screen with a draggable/resizable box and
a live crop preview beside it. `Speichern` writes a `Plant` (label prefilled `A1`, `A2`, `B1` by position
in the frame — left-to-right, top-to-bottom) plus a `PlantView` with `validFrom = Plant.startedAt` (so
the crop applies retroactively to every frame since the plant began). Two fields, both skippable:
`Sorte` and `Start`. Three taps to a plant that has eight weeks of history the moment it exists.

**When the camera moves.** `Ausschnitt neu ziehen` closes the current `PlantView` with
`validTo = now` and opens a new one. Nothing is re-encoded, nothing is lost, and a film spanning the
change renders the correct rectangle on each side of it. The app also *offers* this unprompted: the
nightly green-fraction job (§8.5) flags a plant whose green mass touches its rect edge and shows
`A1 wächst aus dem Ausschnitt. [Neu ziehen]` once, on the plant page.

### 8.2 Der Streifen — the film strip that is an axis

Thumbnails positioned by real timestamp on the same x-scale as the chart, so uneven capture reads
honestly as gaps and a dead camera night is a visible hole, not a compressed sequence.

Delivery is **one sprite per window**, not N requests (N is in the thousands):

```
GET /image/:device_id/strip.jpg?from=&to=&cols=24&h=96[&plant=<id>]  → one JPEG sprite
GET /image/:device_id/strip.json?from=&to=&cols=24[&plant=<id>]      → [{i, timestamp, image_id}, …]
```

The server picks `cols` frames spread evenly over `[from,to]` (nearest actual frame to each target
timestamp, so gaps stay gaps), extracts the plant rect if asked, resizes to `h`, and composites them
side by side with one `sharp().composite()` call. Cached one hour; a closed past window is stable
forever.

Cursor sync is a tiny shared service, not `echarts.connect()` (the strip is plain DOM):
`CursorService.set(timestamp, source)` → the chart moves its `axisPointer`, the strip scrolls its
active frame under the marker, the picture block loads that frame, and the caption band rewrites. One
subject, three subscribers, no page transition. Scrubbing the chart moves the picture; dragging the
strip moves the chart cursor. **That is the correlation feature, and it is about 120 lines.**

### 8.3 Der Film — what ships in October that someone can hold

`/device/:id/film`, reached from a `Film` button on the picture block.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Film bauen                               │
│                                          │
│ Motiv     [ Zelt ][ A1 ][ A2 ][ B1 ]     │  crop selector
│ Von       04.07.2026   (Tag 1)           │
│ Bis       12.09.2026   (Tag 70)          │
│                                          │
│ ☑ Nächte weglassen                       │
│ ☑ Störbilder weglassen  (Hand, Tür)      │
│ ☑ Zahlen einblenden                      │
│ ☐ Namen einblenden      (A1 · Gelato)    │
│                                          │
│ ≈ 600 Bilder · 24 Sek · ca. 6 MB         │
│  ┌────────────────────────────────────┐  │
│  │          Film bauen                │  │
│  └────────────────────────────────────┘  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

`POST /film` → `202 { film_id }`. The card becomes `Film wird gebaut … 40 %` and survives navigation
(it is a `PlantFilm` doc, polled). On completion a `DeviceLog` row `message-film-ready` fires the
existing notification path.

**Frame selection.** Target ≈ 600 frames (24 s at 25 fps), not a fixed interval. Candidates are drawn
from the thinning tiers that already exist, so a 10-week film reads from 60-minute-spaced frames and a
3-day film from 60-second ones, with no special casing.

**Culling — the content problem all ten round-one concepts skipped.** Per candidate frame, one 64×64
greyscale thumbnail via `sharp().resize(64,64).greyscale().raw()` (~3 ms):

1. **Lights off.** Primary signal is `out_light` from Influx at that timestamp — one range query for the
   whole film, not per frame. Fallback where no light output exists: mean luma < 25. Dropped unless
   `Nächte weglassen` is off.
2. **Human / door / knock.** Mean absolute difference to the previous *kept* frame. If MAD > 3× the
   rolling median MAD of the last 20 frames, the frame is `disturbed` and dropped. A hand in shot, an
   open door and a nudged camera all read as spikes. **But** if the spike persists for more than 10
   minutes, it is treated as a new baseline, not a disturbance — that is the camera actually having been
   moved, and dropping every subsequent frame would be catastrophic.
3. **Daylight flood.** `mean(B)/mean(R)` outside the run's median ±35 % → dropped. Magenta LED and
   daylight through an open door are the two ends of that ratio.
4. **Near-duplicates.** MAD < 0,5 % of range → dropped. Nothing happened.
5. **Fog.** Luma standard deviation < 12 → dropped. Partial proxy for condensation, and **it will also
   drop a genuinely hazy tent.** Said plainly in §13.

Force-keep overrides: any frame within ±15 min of an `Event` of type `train` or `harvest` is never
culled. Defoliation day is the frame you most want, and it is exactly what the disturbance heuristic
would eat.

**Normalisation across 12 weeks.** Per-frame `sharp().normalise()` is wrong — it re-stretches every frame
independently and produces visible flicker. Instead: compute per-frame mean luma and per-channel means
(already free from the thumbnail pass), take a 7-frame rolling median as the target, and apply
`sharp().linear(a, b)` per frame toward it, plus a per-channel gain holding grey-world white balance at
the run's median. Two extra sharp ops per frame, and it kills both the flicker and the magenta-to-daylight
swing in one pass.

**Stabilisation.** Not full stabilisation. Render at 92 % of the crop rect and shift each frame by the
integer `(dx,dy)` minimising MAD against the previous kept frame, searched ±4 % over a 3-level pyramid on
the 64×64 thumbnail. Handles nudges and slow drift. **Does not handle rotation.** v1.1 if the frame
budget slips; the film is watchable without it.

**Caption burn-in.** A bottom band composited by `sharp` + SVG — the exact pattern `addOfflineOverlay`
already uses in this codebase:

```
A1 · Gelato · Tag 34 · 12.09. 14:02 · 24,6 °C · 58 % · VPD 1,12
```

Values come from **one** Influx query over the whole range, nearest sample per frame. With
`Zahlen einblenden` off, the band is just `Tag 34 · 12.09.` With `Namen einblenden` off it reads
`Pflanze 1` — the privacy default (§9). **This is image correlation as a physical artefact: the file
that lands in someone's camera roll carries the climate that produced it.**

**Encoding.**

```
ffmpeg -framerate 25 -f image2 -i %d.jpeg \
       -vcodec libx264 -pix_fmt yuv420p -crf 23 -preset veryfast \
       -movflags +faststart -f mp4 out.mp4
```

Plant portrait: 1080×1080 (square travels everywhere and a tent crop is portrait-ish anyway).
Tent film: 1920×1080. **Explicitly not libx265.** The existing rolling timelapses encode HEVC at
`crf 30`, which will not play in Chrome on many desktops, in Telegram's web client, or in older Android
galleries. Switching `convertRtspStreamImagesToVideo` to libx264 + yuv420p + faststart is a one-line
change with a real compatibility payoff and roughly 2× the bytes at equal quality. Take the bytes.

Render budget: ~600 frames × ~25 ms of sharp work = ~15 s, plus ~5 s of x264. Under 30 s on one core,
behind the existing `pLimit(10)`.

### 8.4 How the video LEAVES the product

```
GET /film/:film_id.mp4?token=<imageToken>
Content-Type: video/mp4
Content-Disposition: attachment; filename="terpcontrol_a1-gelato_2026-07-04_2026-09-12_tag1-70.mp4"
Accept-Ranges: bytes
```

Two buttons on the finished card, and the difference between them matters:

- **`Speichern`** — `<a [href]="url" download>`. Desktop and Android save to disk. iOS Safari opens the
  player with the system share sheet, from which `In "Fotos" sichern` is one tap. Two taps to the camera
  roll on every platform.
- **`Teilen`** — `navigator.share({ files: [new File([blob], name, {type:'video/mp4'})] })` where
  supported (Chrome Android, Safari iOS 15+). This puts the actual mp4 into WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal or
  Instagram **with no URL, no login and no account of ours in the middle**. Where `canShare({files})` is
  false, the button falls back to `Speichern` and says so. A share that produces a URL to a page requiring
  a login is not a share, and this concept does not ship one.

Filename rule: `terpcontrol_<slug(label[-strain])>_<from>_<to>_tag<a>-<b>.mp4`, umlauts transliterated
(`ä→ae`), lowercased, non-alphanumerics collapsed to `-`, capped at 120 chars.

**Storage.** Films go to **GridFS** (`mongoose.mongo.GridFSBucket` — the driver is already there, no new
dependency), not to `Image.data`. Two reasons, both hard: the 16 MB BSON limit would silently cap a long
film, and the unique index `{device_id, format, timestamp, duration}` on `images` cannot express "two
different plants' films for the same window". `PlantFilm` carries `expiresAt = createdAt + 30 d` with a
TTL index; re-rendering is deterministic and cheap. The user is told: `Film wird in 30 Tagen gelöscht —
lade ihn dir runter.`

### 8.5 Correlation, in four places

1. **Chart ↔ strip ↔ caption**, one shared cursor, both directions (§8.2).
2. **Frame → what happened.** Tapping a frame writes that instant's numbers into the caption and shows
   `Was war da los?` listing `Event`s and `DeviceLog`s within ±30 min.
3. **Alarm → frame.** Every alarm row in the diary gets `Bild dazu` opening the frame from the moment the
   alarm fired. `Zeig mir das Bild vom Ausschlag` is the single most useful thing a camera can do for a
   controller and it is four lines of cursor plumbing.
4. **Wuchskurve** (v1.1, but cheap and worth naming): a nightly job computing the green-pixel fraction
   inside each plant's rect from one frame per day (`sharp().extract().stats()` on a hue mask), stored as
   `Plant.growthSeries: [{day, greenFraction}]` and drawn as a third chart panel on the plant page. It is
   **not height** and the label says so — `Grünanteil im Ausschnitt` — but over ten weeks it is a real
   growth curve computed from data already stored, with no new hardware. It also drives the
   "grown out of its rectangle" nudge.

### 8.6 The camera alarm

Round one gave the camera none. Here: `imageService` already tracks `deviceIdToLastRtspState` with a
failure count and an exponential backoff. When `failureCount` implies silence sustained for 60 minutes,
write one `DeviceLog` `{ severity: 1, title: 'message-camera-silent', categories: ['webcam','error'] }`
— that category already exists in the codebase — and route it through the notification path the alarms
already use. De-bounced: one entry per silence episode, one on recovery
(`message-camera-back:<duration>`). A camera that stopped three days into flower now tells you.
A second, quieter signal: if a day yields fewer than 20 usable frames after culling, the daily job logs
`message-film-thin:<n>` at severity 0, which shows up on the strip as a hatched region rather than a push.

## 9. Clubs

**Attribution without an auth rewrite.** A device-scoped `Actor` list — names the owner types once in
`Einstellungen → Wer ist am Zelt` (`Anna`, `Ben`, `Chris`). No accounts, no e-mail, no
`auth.middleware.ts:172` surgery. Every capture sheet carries a compact actor row at the top; the last
used actor is preselected per browser (`localStorage['tc-actor-<device_id>']`), so the common case is
zero extra taps. `actor_id` is stamped on `Event`, `Condition` and `PlantView`. ~2 days of work, and it
is honest about what it is: **attribution is a label; authorisation is still one owner and one login.**
The app never claims otherwise — the settings page says `Alle am Zelt teilen sich einen Zugang. Die
Namen sagen, wer was gemacht hat.`

**Reveal by data.** With one actor (the default), no actor row renders anywhere, no initials appear on
any event, and a solo grower never learns the feature exists. It appears the moment a second name is
typed.

**The double-feed problem** is §7.3 and it is the club feature that matters most: it is the only place
in the product where one person's action can cost another person a crop, and it is solved at the moment
of capture, device-scoped, without accounts.

**Conditions** are the club's tent door. `Zustände` pin to the top of the device home, one line each,
amber left border, `+ Zustand` at the end of the list: `CO₂-Flasche fast leer`, `Steckdose 3 wackelt`,
`A3 nicht gießen — übergossen`. `openedBy`/`closedBy` are actor labels. Anyone can close one; closing
writes an `Event` of type `note` so the history keeps it. Max three shown, then `2 weitere`.

**Privacy posture — privatsphäre-maximal by default.**

- No harvest weight is ever prompted. `harvest` events exist as a type and the field is
  `harvest.note` only; a weight field appears **only** if the user types a number into it once, and it
  is never suggested, never charted, never exported by default.
- Plant count lives on one device and is visible only to that device's login. There is no aggregate
  anywhere, no "growers near you", no location, no analytics SDK.
- Film captions default to `Namen einblenden = aus`: `Pflanze 1`, not `A1 · Gelato`. The number band
  can also be turned off entirely. A film shared to Telegram carries a picture, a day counter and a date
  unless the user deliberately adds more.
- Actor names are device-scoped strings, not identities. They are not accounts, not e-mails, not
  exportable to any third party, and `Namen löschen` wipes them and blanks every `actor_id` in one action.
- **Refused outright:** per-member dispensing records, grams handed over, THC content, member DOB or
  address, and any § 26 KCanG reporting. Terp Control is not the club's system of record and must never
  advertise itself as one. No `KCanG-konform` badge, ever.

## 11. Per-user-type walkthrough

**Type 1 — monitor-only, lazy.** Opens the app, sees a photograph of the tent with `24,6 °C · 58 % ·
VPD 1,12` written on it, closes the app. That is the whole session and it took two seconds. The chart is
under the picture with the target band drawn, so on the day he does care whether the tent was OK, the
answer is a shape, not fifteen translucent areas. He never creates a plant, never opens a capture sheet,
and pays a one-card tax for it.

**Type 2 — the same, with a camera.** This is the type the concept was designed for. He draws one
rectangle in week one and from then on the app builds him a per-plant film he can save to his camera roll
in two taps, with the climate burned into every frame. He scrubs the chart at the point where humidity
spiked and the picture jumps to it. He is the reason `Film` is on the picture block and not three levels
down a menu.

**Type 3 — controls certain aspects (CO₂, heating).** His device reports `heater` and `co2` sockets and
nothing else, so the output lane has two rows, the chart shows a temperature target band and a CO₂
series, and there is no dehumidifier row, no humidity target he cannot act on and no
"Entfeuchter läuft dauerhaft" alarm preset. Capability comes from `hardwareInfo.sockets` per role, not
from the three-bucket `deviceControlCapability()` that today fails open to `'full'`.

**Type 4 — full tent.** Gets the most out of the output lane and the deviation fill, because with five
roles running there is actually something to see: the heater firing against a cold night while the
dehumidifier holds RH is one glance in the state timeline. This concept does not invent the humidifier
or exhaust role the firmware lacks, and it does not pretend to — his gap is firmware, and no camera
concept closes it. What he gains here is the picture-plus-chart correlation and the diary.

**Type 5 — closed-loop DIY.** Served by the coupling, not by chrome: every frame is addressable as
`/image/:id?timestamp=…&crop=…`, every film as `/film/:id.mp4`, both with a bearer token, and `Als CSV`
sits under the chart. The strip's `strip.json` is a plain index he can script against. He is not given a
write API in this release and the doc says so rather than implying it. He is the type this concept serves
least, and honestly so.

**Type 6 — club.** Three names typed once become initials on every feeding row, a `Bilanz` line that
answers "how much Bio-Bloom has A3 had this cycle, and from whom", a soft block that stops Ben feeding
what Anna fed 90 minutes ago, and a pinboard of open conditions. All device-scoped, no accounts, no
`auth.middleware` rewrite, no grams, no member data. What he does *not* get is separate logins, and the
UI says that in plain German instead of implying it.

**Type 7 — old device owner.** Nothing he has is removed and every share link still resolves. He gets a
device home that no longer calls his fan a fridge, a chart with a target band, and — if he wants it — a
captioned growth film built from phone photos. §10 has the exact screen.

## 13. Honest weaknesses

1. **One camera, four plants, and the arithmetic is brutal.** A 1080p tent frame cropped to one of four
   plants yields roughly 500×500 real pixels. A 1080² portrait film is upscaled and soft. Films render at
   the crop's native resolution capped at 1080, so a four-plant tent produces visibly softer films than a
   one-plant tent. The fix is a better camera, not better software, and this concept's headline gets worse
   as the tent gets fuller.

2. **Occlusion destroys it exactly when the grow gets interesting.** By week eight the front plant covers
   the back one and B1's rectangle is mostly A2's leaves. There is no software answer. The per-plant
   promise degrades over precisely the period a grower most wants to see per-plant progress.

3. **The rectangle goes stale and re-drawing it is a chore.** A plant outgrows its crop in about three
   weeks. Validity windows make that *recoverable*, not *automatic*, and the nudge relies on the green-
   fraction job that is v1.1. A stoned user will not re-draw rectangles, and their films will slowly
   become close-ups of one branch.

4. **Culling heuristics throw away real content.** MAD-based disturbance detection will drop defoliation
   day, a plant falling over, and the moment someone puts up a trellis — the frames people most want.
   Force-keeping frames near `train`/`harvest` events only helps users who log those events, which is
   exactly the users who need the help least. Culled frames are recoverable via
   `Verworfene Bilder zeigen`, but a heuristic that silently removes content is a trust risk, and the fog
   filter will also discard genuinely humid tents.

5. **The strip is the performance risk.** Sprites make it survivable, but a twelve-week strip is many
   sprites, and a `KeyedCache`-less `ngFor` over thumbnails will rebuild the DOM every change detection.
   Virtualisation is required, not optional, and it is the item most likely to feel bad on a mid-range
   Android.

6. **The no-modes mechanism leans on one gesture.** If the drag-split is undiscoverable, the techie may
   never realise the chart goes bigger. Mitigations — the chart is never below 140 px and there is a plain
   `Zahlen ▾` button — cost the picture vertical space it wants. This is a real tension between the two
   audiences and it is resolved by compromise, not by cleverness.

7. **Film storage has an uncosted curve.** 150 units × 3 films/month × 6 MB ≈ 2,7 GB/month is nothing.
   5 000 units is ≈ 90 GB/month of GridFS churn plus the render CPU. The 30-day TTL is a guess, not a
   plan, and nobody has priced re-render-on-demand against keep-forever.

8. **Multi-plant is served visually, which does not help the club's hardest problem.** Crops make plants
   *visible*; they do not make "which plants did Anna feed" *automatic*. That still gets typed by hand
   into chip rows, and a hurried person will tap `alle`. The concept's headline and the club's headline
   are different features that happen to share a screen.

9. **No firmware change means no local frame buffer.** Frames are pulled from the cloud every 30 seconds;
   a six-hour cloud outage is six hours permanently missing from every film. For a product whose headline
   is the camera, that fragility is structural and unfixable in this release.

10. **The `images` unique index is a wall.** `{device_id, format, timestamp, duration}` forbids two rows
    at the same millisecond, so any future optimisation that wants to *store* crops rather than derive
    them needs an index migration — and there is no migration tooling in this repo.

11. **It bets the product on a €30 accessory.** Everything distinctive here — the strip, the portraits,
    the films, the burned captions, half the correlation story — is worth nothing to a customer who
    bought the €289 controller without the camera bundle. The fallback (§3.3) is honest and complete, but
    it is a different, plainer product, and this concept is deliberately not optimised for it.
