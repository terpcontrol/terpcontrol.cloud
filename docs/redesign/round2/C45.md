# C45 — "Ein langer Lauf"

*Family: structure. Centre of gravity: one continuous scroll, no navigation at all.*

## 1. Thesis

**Terp Control is one document that starts at *now* and runs backwards to the seed; scrolling is the only
way to move, and everything the product can do — settings, plants, charts, films, club history — is a block
in that one document at the moment it belongs to.**

The app has no tabs, no menu, no back button, no device switcher, no modal, no page transition. There is a
single `<ion-content>` whose scroll height is the life of the grow. The top of it is this second. The bottom
of it is the day the device first spoke. In between, the product's entire surface is laid out in the one
order every human already understands: newest first.

Two things make that a product rather than a stunt.

**First, settings are facts with a start time.** "Tagestemperatur 25 °C" is not a preference living in a
settings screen; it is a fact that has been true since 4 August. Facts that are still true sit at the head of
the document, above the *Jetzt* line, where their validity reaches the present — so they are one flick from
the top, always, in a fixed published order. Change one and the change is written into the stream directly
below the *Jetzt* line as the newest history block. **The settings screen and the diary are literally the same
object.** That is also, for free, the owner's "it does the diary keeping for you": every setpoint change,
every socket switch, every stage flip is already a diary block that the grower did not write.

**Second, scrolling is scrubbing.** A sticky 16:9 head sits at the top of the viewport and always shows the
tent as it looked at the moment currently under the centre of the screen, with the numbers that were true
then. Flick from now to week 3 and you watch twelve weeks of tent run backwards under your thumb. The
camera is not a tile in this concept — the camera is the surface you are dragging. Image correlation is not
a feature you enable; it is what the scrollbar *is*.

## 2. Information architecture

### Every screen

There is one. `/device/:device_id` renders `LaufPage`. That is the whole route table for a logged-in user:

| Path | Kept |
| --- | --- |
| `/device/:device_id` | **the document** |
| `/login`, `/demo`, `/link-expired`, `/connection-error` | unchanged |
| `/s/:share_id` | a read-only render of the same document, clamped to a range |
| `/classes`, `/diagnostics` | admin only, unchanged, not part of this design |

`/list`, `/charts`, `/diary`, `/settings`, `/testmode`, `/account`, `/shares` all redirect into the document.
Old share links (`/device/:id/charts?measures=…&date=…`) redirect to `/device/:id#t=<date>` and land the
scroll at that timestamp, which is the back-compat path for `applyViewParams`.

### The navigation model: one document, two verbs, zero destinations

Scroll moves you. Nothing else moves you. Blocks respond to taps, but a tap **never replaces the page** — it
changes the block you touched, in place, and the scroll is anchored so that the block's top edge stays where
your thumb left it.

- **Aufklappen** — a block gets taller. Three heights, always the same three, for every block kind in the
  product: **Zeile** (one German sentence + the number it is about) → **Karte** (the numbers, the controls,
  the picture) → **Werkbank** (raw series, full table, every field, CSV, the block's API URL). Tap the block
  to go one level down; tap its header to collapse.
- **Aufbrechen** — a block becomes many blocks. A *Woche* block breaks into seven *Tag* blocks inserted in
  place; a *Tag* breaks into its hours; an hour breaks into raw samples. Depth in **time** is the same
  gesture as depth in **detail**.

Those two verbs are the entire interaction vocabulary. There is nothing else to learn, and nothing that can
be got wrong by a person who has had two joints.

### The vertical order (top to bottom)

```
┌─ KOPF (sticky, 168 px) ──────────────────────────────┐
│  frame from the moment under the cursor              │
│  Do, 3. Sep · 14:05   24,1 °C · 58 % · VPD 1,12      │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
  ① Zustand          verdict + live values + freshness
  ② Erfassen         Gegossen · Gefüttert · Notiz · Foto
  ③ Offene Punkte    conditions + triggered alarms      (absent if none)
  ④ Ziele            setpoints, editable in place
  ⑤ Pflanzen & Plan  plants, medium+regime, next due    (absent if no plants AND no regime)
  ⑥ Gerät            sockets, camera, firmware, shares, account, language, logout
─────────────────── J E T Z T ─────────────────────────
  heute      ~1 block per event, plus one Stunde block per hour with anything in it
  gestern    ~1 block per half-day + every user event
  d-2…d-7    1 Tag block each + every user event + the day's Film block
  d-8…       1 Tag block each, compressed
  older runs 1 Durchgang block each (breaks open into weeks)
─────────────────── Anfang ────────────────────────────
```

The head is **6 blocks and never more than 6**, so the standing facts are at a constant, learnable distance
from the top. A user who wants "settings" flicks once. There is no other place they could be.

### Reaching an old moment

Three mechanisms, all of them scroll:

1. **The document is compressed by age, not linear in time.** Recent hours are one block each; days older
   than a week are one block each; previous runs are one block each. A 90-day grow is ~140 blocks ≈ 19,000 px
   of scroll — about eight seconds of continuous flicking with momentum on a phone, and roughly one turn of a
   trackpad wheel per week on a desktop.
2. **Der Zeitgriff.** A permanent 44 px-wide grip on the right edge (the scrollbar, made thumb-sized). Drag
   it and a label follows your thumb: `Blüte · Tag 12 · Do 3. Sep`. This is fast-scroll, not navigation:
   it changes your position in one ordered document, it has no destinations, and releasing it leaves you in
   exactly the same document you were already in.
3. **Aufbrechen** for going *finer*, once you are there.

Honest: there is no search, and no jump-to-date field, in v1. See §13.

## 3. The home screen

There is no home screen; there is the head of the document. Precise build spec.

### Der Kopf (sticky)

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ [ tent frame, 16:9, object-fit: cover, 100% width ]      │
│                                                          │
│  Do, 3. Sep · 14:05                       ● live         │
│  24,1 °C   58 %   VPD 1,12 kPa                           │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- Height 168 px on a phone, `position: sticky; top: 0`.
- The frame is the camera still nearest the timestamp at the **vertical centre of the viewport**. While
  scrolling it is served from a sprite (see §8); when the scroll settles for 250 ms the full-resolution
  still for that exact timestamp replaces it.
- The three numbers are the values at that timestamp, not the live values, whenever the cursor is in the
  past. At the top of the document they are live and the dot reads `● live`.
- **No camera paired:** the frame area collapses to 0 and the head is a 64 px strip with the date and the
  three numbers. Nothing says "add a camera".
- **Camera silent > 6 h:** the last frame is shown at 55 % opacity with a caption bar drawn *into the
  picture* by the existing `addOfflineOverlay` path: `Kamera still seit 3 Tagen`.
- Tapping the head does nothing. It is a display, not a control. (Deliberate: it is under the thumb during
  every scroll.)

### ① Zustand

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Alles im Rahmen.                                        │
│  24,1 °C · 58 % · VPD 1,12 kPa · 1000 ppm                │
│  vor 40 Sekunden                                         │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Zeile → Karte adds one `value-display` gauge per measure the device actually reports plus the target next to
each. Karte → Werkbank adds the last-6-h ECharts panel with the target band and the outputs lane, plus
`Als Tabelle` / `CSV` / `GET /v1/blocks/zustand-<device_id>`.

German copy, exhaustive for the states that exist:

| State | Zeile | Second line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| in band | `Alles im Rahmen.` | `vor 40 Sekunden` |
| out of band, actionable | `Zu warm seit 1 Std 10.` | `25,4 °C · Ziel 22,0 °C` |
| out of band, not actionable (no kit) | `Zu feucht seit 2 Std. Kein Entfeuchter angelernt.` | `71 % · Ziel 58 %` |
| stale (last sample 2–10 min) | `Letzter Wert vor 4 Minuten.` | values in `--tc-text-warning` |
| offline (> 10 min) | `Gerät seit 3 Std 20 offline.` | `Letzte Werte von 06:12.` |
| never reported | `Noch keine Messwerte. Das Gerät hat sich noch nicht gemeldet.` | `Seriennummer 4711` |
| maintenance | `Wartung bis 15:40. Regelung aus.` | current values, greyed |
| demo/share | identical; edit affordances absent, no explanatory banner |

Freshness is per value, not per device: each number carries its own age when the ages differ (CO₂ from an
SCD4x can lag). Nothing is ever rendered as if current when it is not — the #1 complaint in the competitor
review corpus.

### ② Erfassen

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  [ Gegossen ]  [ Gefüttert ]  [ Notiz ]  [ 📷 ]          │
│  Hand: ( Chris ) ( Anna ) ( + )                          │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Four 56 px targets in a row; thumb-reachable, high contrast, no icons-only. The `Hand:` row exists only if
more than one Actor has been created — reveal by data, and a solo grower never sees it. §7 has the flow.

### ③ Offene Punkte

Absent when empty. One line per open `Condition` and per currently-triggered `Alarm`:

```
  ⚠ CO₂-Flasche fast leer — Anna, vor 2 Tagen        [ Erledigt ]
  ⚠ Kamera seit 3 Tagen still                        [ Erledigt ]
  ● Temperatur über 28 °C — seit 1 Std 10
```

`[ Erledigt ]` closes the condition and writes the closure into the stream below *Jetzt*. Alarms cannot be
"erledigt" from here — they clear themselves — but they can be silenced for 8 h with a long press
(`Alarm.cooldownSeconds` already exists).

### ④ Ziele

Zeile: `Tag 24 °C · Nacht 20 °C · 62 % · Licht 18 Std · Blüte`.
Karte: the existing `value-edit-row` components, one per target, each with the *since* date on the right:

```
  Tagestemperatur      24,0 °C     seit 4. Aug   ›
  Nachttemperatur      20,0 °C     seit 4. Aug   ›
  Luftfeuchte Tag        62 %      seit 4. Aug   ›
  Licht an             06:00       seit 12. Aug  ›
  Licht aus            18:00       seit 12. Aug  ›
  Dimmung               100 %      seit 12. Aug  ›
  Phase                Blüte       seit 12. Aug  ›   [ Blüte ▸ ]
```

Only rows the device can act on are present. A tent with a heater socket and no dehumidifier has no humidity
row at all — instead one grey line: `Luftfeuchte 58 % — wird nur gemessen.` This replaces
`deviceControlCapability()`'s three failing-open buckets with per-role truth from `hardwareInfo.sockets`,
and fixes the P0 bug where a heater-only tent is nagged about humidity.

Editing is immediate: the range disarms after 8 s via the existing `rangeGuard`, the value is pushed on
release, and a `Ziel geändert` block appears below *Jetzt* within a second. **There is no Save button and no
navigate-away-to-/list.** Dead controls (`daynight.floating`, `lights.maintenanceOn`, `co2.sunsetOff`,
`daynight.linearChange`) are deleted, not hidden.

Werkbank on Ziele shows the raw `device.configuration` JSON, read-only, with a copy button. The techie who
wants to know exactly what is on the wire gets it without a Testmode page.

### ⑤ Pflanzen & Plan

Absent when there are no plants **and** no feed regime. With them:

```
  4 Pflanzen · Blüte Tag 12 · All-Mix + Bio-Bloom
  Nächste Gabe: heute — Bio-Bloom 2 ml/l, Bio-Grow 1 ml/l
  A1  A2  A3  A4
```

Karte lists each plant with its last feed and last water. Werkbank gives per-plant cumulative product totals
for the run and who gave them (§9).

### ⑥ Gerät

Zeile: `Zelt 1 · online · Firmware 2.14 · 6 Steckdosen · Kamera`.
Karte: socket list with roles and live states, camera pairing, firmware update, share links, language,
dark mode, account, logout, `Gerät entfernen`. It is the last block before *Jetzt* because it is the least
frequently touched standing fact, and because everyone's finger already knows "the bottom of the head".

### The Jetzt line

A 1 px rule with the centred label `— Jetzt —`, `position: sticky` from the bottom until it is passed. Above
it: what is true. Below it: what happened. That single rule is the entire mental model, and it never moves.

## 4. NO-MODES MECHANISM

**The mechanism is that height replaces navigation, and height is per block, not per person.**

In an app with pages, "simple" and "expert" are different *places*, so somebody has to be sorted into one.
This concept has one place. The only variable left is how tall a given block is right now — and that is a
property of *that block*, chosen by whoever last touched *that block*, visible on screen, reversible with the
same tap that set it.

Concretely:

1. **Three heights, identical for every block kind in the product.** Zeile is one German sentence with the
   number in it. Karte is the numbers and the controls. Werkbank is the raw series, the full table, the
   export and the block's own API URL. Learning `Zustand` teaches `Tag 12`, `Gefüttert`, `Ziele` and
   `Durchgang`. There is one lesson.
2. **Nothing is ever hidden behind a level — the level changes resolution, not membership.** The number is
   in the Zeile. The chart is in the Karte. Werkbank adds precision, never a new capability. A grower who
   never taps a block still sees every value, every target and every event, because the Zeile *contains* the
   value. Progressive precision, not progressive disclosure.
3. **Arrival height is a published function of the block's content, not of the user.** The rules, in the
   help text verbatim:
   - a block whose values were outside the target band, or which carries an alarm, arrives as **Karte**;
   - a block the grower created in the last 10 minutes arrives as **Karte** (so it can be corrected);
   - everything else arrives as **Zeile**.
   Deterministic, identical on two phones looking at the same tent. This is the brief's "ranked density"
   applied to height instead of to order — the drama sets the size, attention does the rest.
4. **Expansion persists per block instance, never per block kind.** The techie who opens
   `Di 1. Sep` to Werkbank finds it that way tomorrow. `Mi 2. Sep` still arrives as a Zeile. Nothing
   accumulates into a preference, so no state ever silently becomes an expert account. Cost: the techie taps
   twice per block. That is the price of not having a mode, and it is stated in §13 rather than hidden.
5. **Aufbrechen is the same idea in the time axis.** Nobody is denied 5-second resolution on a day from six
   weeks ago; they break the day open. Nobody is forced to see 17,280 points; the day stays one line until
   they do.

Why this is not a mode with a new name: a mode is a persistent global state that changes what the interface
*offers*. Here there is no global state, no preference key, no toggle, no "Advanced" label, no onboarding
question, and the interface offers exactly the same things at every height — a taller block is the same block
with more of itself showing. Two users on the same account see the same document with the same blocks in the
same order; only the heights of blocks they personally touched differ, and one tap makes them agree again.

The second, quieter no-modes mechanism is **composition by capability**. The document is assembled from what
exists: no camera → no Kopf frame and no Film blocks; no dehumidifier socket → no humidity target row; no
plants → no plant chips anywhere in the product, and `Gefüttert` writes against the tent; no CO₂ sensor → no
ppm anywhere. A monitor-only Type 1 and a full-tent Type 4 run the same code path and get documents of very
different lengths. Nobody chose that; the hardware did.

## 5. The chart

Apache ECharts 6, tree-shaken, behind a ~50-line hand-rolled Angular directive (`[tcChart]`). Highcharts,
`chart.js`, `ng2-charts` and `chartjs-adapter-luxon` are deleted.

**The chart has no timespan control, because the block is the timespan.** A Stunde block charts its hour, a
Tag block charts its day, a Woche block charts its week, a Durchgang block charts the run. This is the
single largest simplification the architecture buys: the `timespan` × `interval` dropdown pair disappears,
and with it the `3y × 5s` ≈ 18.9 M-window self-DoS, by construction. Interval is derived: `range / 240`,
clamped to the Influx aggregation set.

Anatomy of a Tag block at Werkbank (three stacked panels, ~110 px each, one linked crosshair via
`axisPointer.link`, never a shared y-axis):

```
 °C  ╭───────────────────────────────────────────────╮
     │▓▓▓▓▓▓░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓│  ← night = markArea, grey
 26  │      ┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈┈            │  ← setpoint, dashed, STEPPED at 06:00/18:00
     │▁▂▃▄▅▆▇█▇▆▅▄▃▂▁  ▁▂▃▄▅▆▇█▇▆▅▄▃▂▁               │  ← actual, solid, min/max envelope
 18  ╰───────────────────────────────────────────────╯
 %   ╭───────────────────────────────────────────────╮
     ╰───────────────────────────────────────────────╯
 out ▐███ Licht ███████████▌     ▐██ Heizung ▌  ▐█ Heizung ▌   ← state-timeline lane
     ├──────┬──────┬──────┬──────┬──────┬──────┤
     00     04     08     12     16     20     24
     ▲          ▲▲                 ▲                            ← annotation rail, 2 rows max
```

- **Goal vs actual.** The dashed line is the setpoint, stepped at `daynight.day`/`daynight.night` between
  `day.temperature` and `night.temperature`. Behind it, one soft band, chosen in this priority and labelled
  with which one it is: the user's own alarm thresholds (hard) > setpoint ± tolerance (soft) > the stage's
  `vpdRange` (advisory, VPD panel only). The fill between actual and setpoint is signed and tinted — never
  filled to the axis, which is what today's `type:'area', threshold:null` does and which encodes nothing.
- **Day/night** is `markArea` derived from measured `out_light > 0`, falling back to the configured
  `daynight` schedule when the output is unknown, drawn in an explicit timezone. A failed contactor shows
  as a *missing* band — a diagnostic, not decoration. `lights.sunrise`/`sunset` render as gradient edges.
- **Annotations** live on a 14 px rail under the x-axis, not as height-1 columns inside the plot. Two rows:
  row 1 human events (feed, water, note, photo), row 2 device events (alarm, config change, reboot).
  Pixel-proximity clustering in 10 px bins with a count badge. Lifecycle stage changes are full-height dashed
  verticals. **Tapping a tick does not open a tooltip — it scrolls the document to that event's block.** The
  chart is a map of the scroll it lives in.
- **Outputs** are a state-timeline lane; region length is duration; null renders as a gap.
- **Sampling** `minmax`, never LTTB, for the plot — a bang-bang controller's oscillation is the signal.
- **Verdict.** Every Tag/Woche/Durchgang block's Karte carries one line above the chart:
  `Im Band: 91 % · Tag 94 % · Nacht 86 % · längste Abweichung 1 Std 40`. Computed **server-side on raw
  Influx samples**, never on `aggregateWindow(mean)` output, and greyed out below 80 % data coverage with
  `Datenlücke — 62 % abgedeckt`.
- Colours: Okabe-Ito. Axis labels are never hidden; below 320 px the tick count drops to three and the unit
  moves into the panel title.
- Touch: one finger scrolls the page (the chart must never eat the only gesture the product has), long-press
  places the crosshair, and the values appear in the pinned row above the panel, not in a floating tooltip.
  **The chart is not pannable or zoomable.** To go finer, break the block open. To go wider, scroll down.

## 6. The diary and multi-plant

The diary is not a feature of this concept; the document below *Jetzt* **is** the diary. It contains
machine-written blocks and human-written blocks interleaved, because both are things that happened.

New Mongo collections, all cloud-side, all keyed by `device_id`, all fields optional with read-time
normalisers (there is no migration tooling):

```ts
Plant     { plant_id, device_id, label,            // "A1"
            strain?, startedAt, medium?, feedRegimeId?,
            endedAt?, archivedAt?, colour? }

Event     { event_id,                              // uuid v4 generated IN THE BROWSER
            device_id, at, createdAt,
            kind: 'water'|'feed'|'note'|'photo'|'measure'|'stage'|'train'|'pest'|'harvest',
            plant_ids: string[],                   // [] means "the whole tent"
            actor_id?, source: 'user'|'device'|'cloud',
            body: {                                // per-kind, validated server-side
              volume_l?, products?: [{name, ml_per_l, note?}],
              ec?, ph?, ec_basis?: 'absolute'|'delta_over_source',
              runoff_ec?, runoff_ph?, height_cm?, text?, ...
            },
            images: string[],                      // image_id
            supersedes?: string, deletedAt? }

Condition { condition_id, device_id, text, plant_ids: string[],
            openedBy, openedAt, closedBy?, closedAt? }

Actor     { actor_id, device_id, name, colour }    // a hand at the tent. Not an account.

Run       { run_id, device_id, startedAt, endedAt?, label? }   // replaces convertEventsToGrowCycles()
```

`convertEventsToGrowCycles()` — which today forks a grow's history when someone renames a plant — becomes a
one-time boot backfill that writes `Run` rows, then is deleted.

`DeviceLog` stays exactly as it is and keeps producing machine blocks. Nothing about the legacy diary is
migrated in v1 except the lifecycle rows; old `diary-*` rows render as blocks through the existing
`LogTranslateService`. The `deleted`-means-hidden trap is defused by never calling "Clear logs" from this UI
and by the document requesting `deleted=1` always, as the diary already does.

**How a user who ignores plants is untaxed:** if `Plant` count for the device is 0, no plant chip, no plant
column, no plant picker and no plant filter is rendered anywhere in the product. `Gefüttert` writes
`plant_ids: []` and the block reads `Gefüttert · das ganze Zelt`. Nothing invites them to create a plant
except one grey line inside the Pflanzen block's Karte, which is itself absent unless a feed regime is
chosen. Plants appear the first time somebody types a label; the day they do, chips appear on every capture
block from that day forward and on none before it. Reveal by data, with no announcement.

Multi-plant, when used: plants are chips `A1 A2 A3 A4` on the capture row and on every event block. Selection
is sticky within a session only. A plant's own history is not a page — it is the document with a plant chip
active in the Kopf, which dims non-matching blocks in place. Filtering, not navigating.

## 7. Watering & fertilising

No irrigation hardware exists. This is a capture-and-recall problem, and the metric is taps.

### The flow

Everything happens in block ②, at the top of the document, always visible, never behind a FAB.

**Tap 1 — `Gefüttert`.** The event is written immediately (optimistically, with a browser-generated
`event_id`) and a new block appears directly beneath the *Jetzt* line, already at Karte height, prefilled:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Gefüttert · alle Pflanzen · vor wenigen Sekunden        │
│                                                          │
│  Bio-Grow      1,0 ml/l        ›                         │
│  Bio-Bloom     2,0 ml/l        ›                         │
│  Menge         5,0 l           ›                         │
│  EC / pH       — / —           ›                         │
│  Hand          Chris                                     │
│                                                          │
│  [ Rückgängig ]                                          │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Prefill comes from the selected medium+regime at the current run-day, and every value stays editable
forever. `[ Rückgängig ]` is present for 10 minutes.

**Tap counts, measured against the block above:**

| Action | Taps |
| --- | --- |
| Fed everything, exactly as the plan says | **1** |
| Watered everything, same amount as last time | **1** |
| Fed, but 7 l instead of 5 | 1 + tap `Menge` + two `+` = **4** |
| Fed only A1 and A3 | 1 + two chips = **3** |
| Fed with an EC reading | 1 + tap `EC` + type = **3** + keyboard |
| Note with a photo | `📷` → capture → **2** |
| Fed when someone already fed 90 min ago | **2** (the extra tap is the point) |

### Following a schedule

The regime is chosen once, in the Pflanzen block, as **medium + feed line the way growers say it**:
`Biobizz All-Mix + Bio-Grow/Bio-Bloom`, `Biobizz Light-Mix + Bio-Grow/Bio-Bloom`, `Floragard Light + …`,
`BioTabs`, `Green House Powder Feeding`, plus ownerless entries (Lucas 0-5-10, Jack's 321, generic EC ladder).

```ts
FeedRegime { regime_id, medium_label, feed_label,      // plain text, no logos, no brand colours
             source_url, retrieved_at, chart_version,
             steps: [{ from_day, to_day, phase,
                       products: [{name, ml_per_l | ml_per_l_range}],
                       ec_target?, ec_basis, ph_target?, note? }],
             no_feed_until_day? }                       // All-Mix: 21. BioTabs: null + re_tab_days: 42
```

Stored in a MongoDB content collection, never in the webapp bundle, so a brand is removable in one DB
update. Each regime's Karte shows `Zuletzt geprüft: 12. Juni 2026` — the legal posture doubling as a trust
feature.

The plan does not nag on a calendar. A due step surfaces as one line in the Pflanzen block:
`Nächste Gabe: heute — Bio-Bloom 2 ml/l`. All-Mix says `Noch nicht düngen — All-Mix ist vorgedüngt (bis Tag
21).` and the `Gefüttert` button prefills nothing but still works, because the grower who deviates is normal.
Steps advance by **feed events**, not wall-clock weeks: after tap 1, the regime pointer moves to the next
step. Autoflowers get a ×0.25–0.5 strength multiplier and no 12/12 flip event.

The run-day used for prefill comes from the `Run` start plus the detected 18/6 → 12/12 flip in `out_light`
history — something no bottle-brand calculator can do, because they cannot see the tent.

### What is stored

One `Event` per act. EC canonical in mS/cm with `ec_basis` recorded (a CANNA-style chart publishes EC+, and
alarming against the raw number is actively wrong); ppm is rendered only with its scale named
(`1260 ppm (700)`), never bare.

### Capture in a cellar

A hand-rolled ~60-line service worker (new `@angular/service-worker` dependency, build change, flagged) with
an outbox in IndexedDB. Because `event_id` is generated **in the browser** and the server upserts on it, a
retry after a dropped connection is idempotent — the offline queue physically cannot double-log, which in a
shared record is indistinguishable from a double feed. The block shows `⌛ wartet auf Verbindung` and turns
solid when the server acknowledges.

## 8. Camera, timelapse and image correlation

This is the concept's headline, because in a scroll-only product the camera is the only thing that can carry
the scroll.

### Correlation: the scroll IS the scrub

The sticky Kopf shows the frame at the cursor. No separate control, no toggle, no chart mode.

- **Downwards (chart → picture):** the cursor is the vertical centre of the viewport. Every block declares
  its `at` timestamp; the Kopf renders the nearest frame to whatever is at the centre.
- **Upwards (picture → numbers):** the same head shows that moment's temperature, humidity and VPD, sampled
  from the same series the charts draw.
- **Sideways (crosshair → picture):** long-pressing inside any chart panel moves the *cursor* — so the Kopf
  frame changes as you drag along the chart, inside the same block, without leaving the document.

**Delivery, so this does not become 600 image requests.** A sprite endpoint, built by the same hourly job
that builds the timelapses, using `sharp` (already a dependency):

```
GET /image/:device_id/strip?from=<ms>&to=<ms>
→ { sprite: "/image/<id>/strip.jpg?k=…", cols: 10, rows: 10,
    tile: [256,144], frames: [{t: 1756900800000, i: 0}, …] }
```

One 10×10 sprite of 256×144 tiles ≈ 140 KB covers a day at ~15 min steps, or a week at ~100 min steps. The
webapp holds the sprites for the currently rendered window ±1, draws the tile with a CSS
`background-position`, and swaps in the full still 250 ms after the scroll settles. This is the Mux
storyboard pattern; it is the difference between a scrub that feels like film and one that feels like a
gallery.

### The timelapse that ships in October

**A Film block is a real object in the document, and there are four of them per week by default.** Every
completed day gets `Film · Mi 2. Sep · 0:12`; every completed week gets `Film · Blüte Woche 3 · 0:24`; every
phase change and every harvest closes a film. The blocks are produced by the cloud without being asked —
consistent with "it does the diary keeping for you".

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Film · Blüte Woche 3 · 0:24                             │
│  [ ▶ ]  1. – 7. Sep · 604 Bilder · 148 verworfen         │
│                                                          │
│  [ Herunterladen  12,4 MB ]   [ Link kopieren ]          │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**How it leaves the product** — the part all ten round-one concepts skipped:

- `GET /image/:device_id/film/:film_id?token=<imageToken>`
- `Content-Type: video/mp4`, `Content-Disposition: attachment; filename="terpcontrol_zelt-1_20260901-20260907_bluete-w3.mp4"`
- Filename shape: `terpcontrol_<devicename-slug>_<YYYYMMDD>-<YYYYMMDD>_<phase-slug>.mp4`
- **H.264 High, `yuv420p`, 1280×720, 25 fps, CRF 20, `-movflags +faststart`, no audio.** Not HEVC.
  Today's pipeline encodes **`-vcodec libx265 -crf 30`** into an mp4 with no `-pix_fmt` and no
  `-movflags +faststart` (`server/src/services/image.service.ts:564-590`, verified). HEVC in mp4 does not
  decode in Chrome or Firefox on desktop Linux/Windows — **the existing timelapses do not play for most of
  the userbase, and nobody has noticed because nothing in the UI makes them prominent.** This is a bug found
  while writing this document and it sits on the critical path of the owner's biggest selling point.
  `TIMELAPSE_FRAME_RATE` is already 25 (`:75`), so only the codec, pixel format and faststart flag change.
- On iOS Safari the attachment opens in the player with the share sheet → *Video sichern* → camera roll. On
  Android Chrome it lands in Downloads and appears in the gallery. Both are the platform's own save path;
  neither requires a login. `[ Link kopieren ]` copies the same URL with a 30-day `imageToken` so it can be
  pasted into Telegram — with an explicit warning line, because that URL is public to anyone holding it.
- Storage: an mp4 in a Mongo `Buffer` is capped by the 16 MB BSON limit. v1 clamps the render (720p, CRF 20,
  ≤ 30 s) to stay under ~14 MB and refuses longer ranges with `Zeitraum zu lang für einen Film — brich die
  Woche auf.` GridFS is the correct fix and is named as v1.1 work, not pretended away.

### Frame culling and normalisation — the content problem

Applied when a film is rendered, all server-side, `sharp` + `ffmpeg`, both already present:

1. **Lichtschleuse.** Drop every frame whose `out_light` was 0 at that timestamp (from Influx; falling back
   to the `daynight` schedule). One lighting regime per film. Skipped when the run's workmode is `dry`.
2. **Helligkeit.** `sharp().stats()` mean luminance; drop below 12 % or above 96 % — door open, torch, blown
   sensor.
3. **Hand im Bild.** Downscale to 32×18 grey; compare to the median of the 5 neighbouring kept frames. If
   MAD > 18/255 **and** the following frame returns to baseline, it is a transient — a person, an open door,
   a watering can — and it is dropped. A defoliation persists across frames and survives.
4. **Dubletten.** MAD to the previous kept frame < 1,5/255 → drop. A frozen camera stops producing frames
   instead of producing a static film.
5. **Weißabgleich.** Per kept frame, mean RGB of a fixed central 20 % crop; per-channel gain toward the
   7-frame rolling median, clamped to ±25 % so it cannot invent colours. `sharp().linear([r,g,b],[0,0,0])`.
   This is what stops the magenta-LED / daylight-through-the-door flicker.
6. **Ausrichtung.** Phase correlation on a 64×64 downscale against the previous kept frame; integer
   translation only, applied as a crop offset inside a 3 % safety margin. Handles the day-12 nudge. Rotation
   and `vidstab` are deferred.
7. **Takt.** Kept frames resampled to even time steps so a night gap reads as a cut, not a slow patch.

The counts are shown, because honesty is cheap: `604 Bilder · 148 verworfen`. Werkbank on the Film block
lists the reasons: `Licht aus 96 · Hand im Bild 31 · Dublette 18 · zu dunkel 3`.

### The camera alarm

A cloud-side check in `image.service` every hour: if `max(Image.timestamp)` for a device with a paired
camera is older than 6 h, open a `Condition` (`Kamera seit 3 Tagen still`) and fire the existing alarm
notification path. It closes itself when a frame arrives. A camera that quit on day 3 of flower says so.

## 9. Clubs

### Attribution without authorisation

**Hände.** The owner types names once in the Gerät block: `Chris`, `Anna`, `Jonas`. Each becomes an `Actor`
with a colour. The capture row shows the hands as chips; the last used one is preselected and persists for
the session. Every `Event` carries `actor_id`. Every block below *Jetzt* shows the hand:
`Gefüttert · A1–A3 · Anna · gestern 19:40`.

This is **attribution, not authorisation**, and the UI says so once, in the Gerät block:
`Hände sind Namen, keine Konten. Wer die Zugangsdaten hat, kann alles ändern.` No `auth.middleware` surgery,
no membership collection, ~2 days of work, and it delivers the club's actual question — *who did what* — in
v1. Real per-member logins remain a rewrite of `auth.middleware.ts:172`/`:207` and are v2.

### The double feed

The most expensive event in a shared room. At the moment of capture, if any selected plant has a `feed`
event inside 12 h (or a `water` event inside 6 h) from any hand, the optimistic write is **held** and the new
block arrives in a warning state instead of a saved state:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  ⚠ Anna hat A1–A3 vor 1 Std 30 gefüttert.                │
│    Bio-Grow 1,0 ml/l · Bio-Bloom 2,0 ml/l · 5,0 l        │
│                                                          │
│  [ Trotzdem eintragen ]      [ Verwerfen ]               │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

One deliberate extra tap to proceed. A soft block, never a hard one — the second feed is sometimes correct,
and the app does not know which. Cost: one extra tap only when it is dangerous. The window is per-kind and
configurable in the Gerät block, defaulting to 12 h / 6 h.

### The cumulative question

Werkbank on the Pflanzen block, per run:

```
  A3 · Blüte Tag 12 · seit 4. Aug
  Bio-Grow    14 Gaben   28,0 ml   Chris 9 · Anna 5
  Bio-Bloom    9 Gaben   36,0 ml   Chris 4 · Anna 5
  Wasser      31 Gaben    93,5 l
  Ø EC 1,4 · Ø pH 6,2 · Rücklauf-EC zuletzt 1,9 (12. Sep)
```

Computable from `Event` with one aggregation; nobody in the market surfaces it.

### Conditions

`Condition` is the thing that gets taped to a tent door: an open fact with an owner, not a task and not an
event. It is block ③, at the top of everyone's document, until someone closes it. `CO₂-Flasche fast leer`,
`Steckdose 3 wackelt`, `A3 nicht gießen — übergossen`.

### Privacy posture

- No real names required; a hand is a nickname the club chooses.
- No plant count and no harvest weight is ever *prompted*. `harvest` exists as an Event kind with an optional
  `weight_g`; the field is present only if the user types into it once, and it never appears in an export
  unless it exists.
- Export carries `Hände anonymisieren` (→ `Hand A`, `Hand B`), defaulting to **on**.
- No location, no analytics SDK, no community feed, no real-identity anything.
- The document is deletable by the account owner, block by block and wholesale. Tamper-evidence is not a
  default; an unregulated grower is victimised by an undeletable ledger.
- Terp Control does not become the club's § 26 KCanG system of record, and ships no compliance badge.

## 10. Old devices (type 7)

A `fan`, `light` or `plug` owner opens the app and gets the same one document. Concretely, for a `plug`:

```
  ① Zustand        Steckdose an. 21,4 °C.            vor 25 Sekunden
  ② Erfassen       [ Notiz ]  [ 📷 ]                 (no Gegossen/Gefüttert — see below)
  ③ Offene Punkte  (absent)
  ④ Ziele          Übertemperatur  28,0 °C  ›
                   Untertemperatur 15,0 °C  ›
                   Zeitschaltung   06:00–18:00  ›
  ⑥ Gerät          Terp Control Plug · online · Firmware 1.9
  ── Jetzt ──
  14:02  Steckdose aus — Übertemperatur 28,3 °C
  06:00  Steckdose an — Zeitschaltung
  gestern …
```

- Block ④ **renders the existing `plug-settings` / `fan-settings` / `light-settings` component unchanged,
  inside the block.** No rewrite, no risk of breaking a shipped device, and the three raw i18n keys
  (`settings.limits.overtemperature.enabled`, `.undertemperature.enabled`, `.time.enabled`) get fixed as
  part of the pass.
- `Gegossen` and `Gefüttert` are present only when the device has a temperature *and* humidity sensor or at
  least one plant exists — i.e. they never appear on a plug or a light. `Notiz` and `📷` always do, because
  a note is never wrong.
- No Kopf frame (no camera), so the head is the 64 px strip.
- No plants, no regime, no film, no VPD, no CO₂, no target band beyond the limits the device actually has.
- A `fan` gets `Drehzahl 1240 U/min` in Zustand and its speed row in Ziele; a `light` gets on/off + dim +
  its schedule and a day/night band on its charts.
- The history below *Jetzt* is their existing `DeviceLog` stream, rendered as blocks by
  `LogTranslateService` including its seven legacy English-prose regexes. Nothing they had is removed.
- The `fridge` type keeps working identically (it is what `controller` borrows today); `dryer` stays dead.

The cost to them is real and should be said: their app has changed shape, their `/settings` bookmark
redirects, and they gain a document with very few blocks in it. What they get back is freshness honesty, a
readable chart with their own limits drawn on it, and notes with photos.

## 11. Per-user-type walkthrough

**1 — Monitor only.** Their document is short: Zustand, Erfassen, Ziele (reduced to
`Temperatur 24,1 °C — wird nur gemessen`), Gerät, then a day block per day. There is no dehumidifier tile
they cannot act on and no humidity target they cannot reach, because nothing composes them. The Tag block's
`Im Band: 91 %` line turns their chart into a verdict, which is the thing they actually wanted after "some
time".

**2 — Monitor + camera.** The best-served type in this concept, by design. Their scroll is a film with
numbers under it; the Kopf makes every flick a scrub; a Film block lands every day and every week, already
culled and colour-normalised, with a Herunterladen button that puts an mp4 in their camera roll. Image
correlation is not a feature they find — it is the only way the app moves.

**3 — Controls certain aspects.** Block ④ contains exactly the rows their sockets can act on and one grey
measured-only line for everything else. The heater-only tent is never nagged about humidity again. Their
chart draws the setpoint they set and the band it kept, and the outputs lane shows the heater actually
switching — which is the answer to "did my kit do what I told it".

**4 — Full tent.** The longest document: six socket roles in Gerät, every target row live in Ziele, the
outputs lane dense with regions, the day blocks arriving pre-expanded whenever something went out of band.
The honest limit remains the firmware's five roles — there is no humidifier and no exhaust role, and this
concept does not pretend otherwise; it draws `out_dehumidifier` under the name the workmode gives it and
says so in the Gerät block.

**5 — Closed-loop DIY.** Every block at Werkbank prints its own API URL (`GET /v1/blocks/<block_id>`),
its raw series as a table and a CSV of exactly that range. The document endpoint
(`GET /v1/lauf/:device_id?before=&limit=`) is public, documented and the same one the webapp uses. They get
an API by reading the UI, and they get it without a mode. They still get no write API for outputs in v1, and
that should be stated to them plainly rather than implied.

**6 — Club.** Hands on every block, conditions at the top of everyone's scroll, the soft double-feed block,
cumulative per-plant totals at Werkbank, and an anonymised export. One shared login, honestly labelled. The
scroll is the shift handover: the person arriving at the tent reads down from *Jetzt* until they recognise
where they left off.

**7 — Old device owner.** §10. Same document, few blocks, nothing removed, three i18n bugs fixed.

**Capability lenses.** *Stoner:* one gesture, one direction, the answer in a German sentence at the top, and
a 56 px `Gefüttert` button that costs one tap and can be undone for ten minutes. Nothing can be navigated
into by accident because there is nowhere to go. *Normal:* it looks like a feed, which is the interaction
they use most in a day, and it produces a film they can show someone. *Techie:* Werkbank on anything, raw
samples via Aufbrechen, no data withheld, an API URL on every block — at the cost of two taps per block and
no chart zoom.

## 12. v1 scope for October 2026

Six to eight weeks, one small team, hardware ships with the software.

### In

1. **`LaufPage`** — the document shell: sticky Kopf, six head blocks, the *Jetzt* rule, the Zeitgriff,
   Aufklappen (3 heights), Aufbrechen (Woche→Tag→Stunde→Rohwerte). Windowed rendering with
   `IntersectionObserver` + measured spacers + `overflow-anchor: none` and manual scroll-position anchoring.
   *This is the single biggest engineering item and it should be built first, empty, and scrolled with 1,000
   fake blocks before anything else is written.*
2. **`GET /v1/lauf/:device_id?before=&limit=`** — server-composed blocks, cursor-paginated. This also closes
   the P0 "no server-side pagination for logs" gap.

   three failing-open buckets. Delete the four dead controls.
4. **Freshness honesty** everywhere, per value.

6. **Time-in-band verdict** computed server-side on raw samples, with coverage gating.
7. **`Event` / `Plant` / `Condition` / `Actor` / `Run` collections**, the capture row, tap counts as in §7,

12. **de + en in the same commit**, `message-*` convention, and the six live i18n bugs fixed.
13. `controller` gets its own i18n block and stops calling itself a fridge.

### Out — named, not fudged

- Real multi-user accounts, roles, invitations. (Hands only.)
- Search, jump-to-date, saved views, chart presets beyond redirecting the old ones.
- Public diary URL, Markdown/BBCode generator, Discord/Telegram destinations. (v1.1 — they are one webhook

- Run-over-run comparison.
- GridFS for films; v1 clamps size instead.
- Rotation-corrected stabilisation; translation only.
- VPD as a steerable goal.
- Per-plant crop regions in the camera frame.
- Harvest reporting beyond an optional weight field.

Windowed variable-height scrolling → film render cost per device-week → sprite build cost → ECharts in

## 13. Honest weaknesses

1. **No search, no jump-to-date.** "What did I do on 12 August?" costs a Zeitgriff drag with a date label
   under the thumb. That is genuinely worse than typing a date, and it gets worse the longer the account
   lives. Mitigation is compression, not a fix. This is the concept's largest usability debt and the first
   thing I would add in v1.1 — as a filter in the Kopf, not as a destination.
2. **Multiple devices are painful.** Two or three tents interleave into one document acceptably with device
   chips in the Kopf that dim non-matching blocks. Six tents in a club do not: the document becomes noise and
   the chips become a de-facto navigation bar. A club with many tents is served worse by this concept than by
   almost any other in the round.
3. **The techie pays a tax on every block.** Two taps to Werkbank, every time, forever, because persisting
   the level per block *kind* would be a mode. Some of them will find this insulting. The counter-argument —
   that a mode is worse — is a design conviction, not a proof.
4. **The chart cannot be zoomed or panned.** Range comes from position in the document. Aufbrechen covers
   going finer and scrolling covers going wider, but "show me exactly 03:10 to 03:40 across two days" is not
   expressible in v1. Comparison across non-adjacent times is impossible by construction.
5. **Variable-height virtual scrolling with in-place expansion is the hardest thing in this document.** Get
   the anchoring wrong and the page jumps under an impaired user's thumb, which is the exact failure the
   whole concept was designed to avoid. iOS Safari's scroll-anchoring behaviour is not fully controllable.
   If this cannot be made smooth in the first two weeks, the concept should be abandoned rather than shipped
   janky.
6. **Deep linking is weak.** A share link into the middle of a 19,000 px document has to restore a scroll
   offset in a lazily-rendered list. It works (`#t=<timestamp>` + progressive load until the timestamp is
   materialised), but it is slower and more fragile than opening a page.
7. **The Kopf costs 168 px of a 667 px phone permanently** — a quarter of the screen — and it is useless to
   the third of users with no camera, where it shrinks to 64 px and looks like a design accident.
8. **Film rendering is real CPU.** 600 frames × sharp normalisation + an x264 encode, per device, per day and
   per week. At 150 units that is manageable; the pipeline is `pLimit(10)` and single-threaded ffmpeg today.
   At 1,500 units it needs a queue and probably a worker, and the concept does not budget one.
9. **The Zeitgriff is the most attackable idea here.** A critic will call it a navigation control wearing a
   scrollbar costume. The defence — it moves position within one ordered document and has no destinations —
   is real but it is a definition, and definitions lose arguments.
10. **Everything is one page, so everything is one bundle and one component tree.** The route-level code
    splitting that `loadChildren` gives today is lost; block components must be lazily instantiated by hand.
    The initial payload gets worse before it gets better.
11. **Old-device owners lose their bookmarks and their muscle memory** for a redesign aimed at a product they
    did not buy.
12. **The double-feed guard depends on people using their own hand chip.** In a tired club at 23:00 they will
    not, and then the guard fires on the wrong person's behalf or not at all. Attribution without
    authorisation is honest, but it is also weak, and no amount of UI fixes that.
