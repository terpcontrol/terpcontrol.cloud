# C20 — "Der Rückblick"

**Family:** camera · **Centre of gravity:** the product's unit of output is a generated weekly episode.

---

## 1. Thesis

**Terp Control's deliverable is not a screen — it is a weekly episode: every Monday morning the cloud
finishes a short film of the past seven days, with the numbers burned into it, the events captioned on it
and the pictures culled down to the ones worth seeing, and pushes it to the grower. The app is where you
watch this week's episode being made and last week's episode on the shelf.**

The diary problem is not that capture is slow. Five concepts already optimised capture. The problem is that
nobody reviews. A grow diary is a chore whose payoff arrives eleven weeks later, so it is abandoned in
week three and the grower ends up with 20,000 unwatched JPEGs and an Influx bucket.

Terp Control already owns ~90 % of what a diary entry would contain and it owns it *without asking*:
climate, VPD, photoperiod, actuator states, alarms, stage, and a photograph every 30 seconds. The missing
10 % is the human acts — watering, feeding, training — and those cannot be sensed. So: **generate the
diary from what the machine knows, and let the hole in the generated diary be the thing that asks for the
rest.** An episode that says *"Woche 5: 3 148 Bilder, 0 × gegossen"* is a stronger capture prompt than any
button, because it is visibly, embarrassingly wrong.

This is "it does the diary keeping for you" delivered as an object you can hold: a 21-second MP4 with a
filename, that leaves the product, that plays in a Telegram group, that you still have in three years.

## 2. Information architecture

Five routes. Nothing is a tab; the product is one scroll per device plus permalinks into it.

| Path | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `/list` | unchanged (device list; single device → straight through). Each card becomes an **episode poster** with the live strip above it. |
| `/device/:device_id` | **Der Rückblick.** One infinite vertical scroll: live strip → episode in progress → last finished episode, fully expanded inline → older episodes, collapsed to poster + one sentence. This is the app. |
| `/device/:device_id/e/:recap_id` | One episode, permalink. Byte-identical layout to the expanded episode in the scroll. Shareable inside the account. |
| `/device/:device_id/e/:recap_id/k/:chapter` | **The chapter, unabridged.** Same title, same first sentence, all rows, full chart, full event table, filters. `:chapter ∈ klima · wasser · pflanzen · geraete · ereignisse · kamera · rohdaten`. |
| `/device/:device_id/settings` | unchanged existing settings page (`ngSwitch` on `device_type`). Reached from the Geräte chapter and from the menu. Not touched by this concept. |
| `/r/:slug` | public no-login episode page. **v1.1**, off by default. |

Existing `/device/:id/charts`, `/diary`, `/testmode`, `/shares`, `/account` all stay routable and are not
deleted in v1 — they become the "unabridged" targets for two chapters (`klima` → charts,
`ereignisse` → diary entries) until the chapter pages replace them. **Nothing that works today is removed
in October.**

Navigation model: the existing `ion-split-pane` + `ion-menu` is kept. The six outline buttons on the
device overview collapse to one primary (`Rückblick`) plus `Einstellungen`. There is no tab bar and no
device switcher (unchanged). A **capture bar is pinned to the bottom of every device route** — it is not
navigation, it is a writing surface, and it never changes what is on screen behind it.

On a wide screen (the unexploited advantage): the scroll becomes two columns — episodes left at a
fixed 560 px, the currently focused chapter's unabridged view right, so a techie on a laptop reads the
episode and its full chart simultaneously without leaving the page. Same components, `@media` only.

### 3.2 Real German copy — the strings that carry the design

Live strip verdicts (generated, one of a fixed set, always with the number visible above it):
- `Alles im Zielbereich.`
- `Zu warm: 27,4 °C, Ziel 25,0 °C. Seit 40 Minuten.`
- `Zu feucht: 71 %, Ziel 55 %. Der Entfeuchter läuft seit 2 Std durch.`
- `Licht aus. Nachtwerte gelten.`

Freshness (the #1 competitor complaint, and nearly free):
- `vor 22 Sek` with a green dot — live.
- `vor 14 Min` amber dot — `Letzter Messwert vor 14 Minuten.`
- `Offline seit 2 Std 40 Min` red dot, camera image is dimmed and carries the existing
  `addOfflineOverlay` caption (already implemented server-side, German string added).
- No device ever seen: `Noch kein Messwert. Das Gerät hat sich noch nicht gemeldet.`

Empty / early states — this is where a weekly product is weakest and must be explicit:
- Day 0–2, no episode possible: the "DIESE WOCHE" block reads
  `Folge 1 entsteht. Fertig am Montag, 06:00 Uhr — noch 4 Tage.` and beneath it, in place of the
  episode: `Solange läuft der Film schon mit.` plus the live rolling 24 h timelapse that **already
  exists today** (`duration:'1d'`). So there is a film on day one; it is just not yet an episode.
- Day 2+, camera missing: `Ohne Kamera hat die Folge kein Video. Alles andere entsteht trotzdem.`
  `[ Kamera einrichten ]`
- Device offline for a whole week: Folge N is still generated, and its first sentence is
  `Das Gerät war die ganze Woche offline. Diese Folge ist leer.` — a generated episode never silently
  skips, because a missing episode is indistinguishable from a broken product.
- Data gap inside a week: the chapter strips draw gaps as gaps (null → no line, no interpolation) and
  the sentence names it: `Zwischen Tag 33 und Tag 34 fehlen 19 Stunden.`

### 3.3 Behaviour rules

- The scroll **always opens at the top**. No restored scroll position, no "continue where you left off".
  The impaired user gets the same first screen every time.
- The video **never autoplays**. Tap to play, muted, inline (`playsinline`), with the poster as the
  placeholder. Data cost of an autoplaying 4 MB file on mobile is not acceptable and neither is a tent
  video starting itself in public.
- Older episodes load 5 at a time on scroll; the poster is a 320 px JPEG, not a video element.
- Pull-to-refresh refreshes the live strip only. The episode does not change under the reader.

## 4. NO-MODES MECHANISM — **Satz · Zahl · Beleg**

The rule, applied to every assertion in the product without exception:

> **Every statement Terp Control makes is rendered as one block containing a sentence, the number inside
> that sentence, and its evidence drawn directly underneath. All three are always present. None of the
> three is ever behind a toggle, an accordion, a "mehr anzeigen", or a preference.**

```
Nacht 19,4 °C statt 20,0 — jede Nacht ab 3 Uhr zu kalt.   ← SATZ, with the ZAHL set in it
▁▂▃▅▆▅▃▂▁▂▃▅▆▅▃▂▁▂▃▅▆▅▃▂▁▂▃▅▆▅▃▂▁▂▃▅▆▅▃▂▁▂▃▅            ← BELEG, 56 px, always drawn
94 % im Zielbereich · 4 Std 20 Min darunter               ← the second number, same block
                                    Kapitel öffnen  ›     ← the same chapter, unabridged
```

Why this is a mechanism and not a mode:

1. **Nobody chooses.** There is no per-user state anywhere that changes what is rendered. Two users
   opening the same episode on the same phone see identical pixels. Nothing is remembered about how you
   read.
2. **The number is never denied and never forced.** It is inside the sentence, in tabular figures, at the
   same size as the prose. The impaired reader parses `zu kalt` and stops; the techie reads `19,4 statt
   20,0` from the same three words. Neither had to ask.
3. **The evidence is not a disclosure.** The 56 px strip is drawn whether or not you look at it. It is
   not collapsed, so there is no affordance to discover, no state to be in, and no "expert" who has
   turned something on.
4. **"Kapitel öffnen" is not "Advanced".** The chapter page opens with *the same title and the same first
   sentence* at the top, then continues. It is the same content at full length — a longer page, not a
   different one. A user who lands there by accident is not lost, because the first thing they read is
   what they already read. That is what makes it depth and not a second app.
5. **Ranked density does the filtering, not a setting.** The middle chapters are ordered each week by how
   far the week deviated: the chapter that went wrong is at the top. The stoner reads the top and stops;
   they read the *right* top. The techie scrolls all of it. Attention is the filter, and it is the reader's
   attention, not a flag.
6. **The techie's floor is inside the same scroll.** `ROHDATEN` is the last chapter of every episode, for
   every user, always: the count of frames and samples, and `[ JSON ] [ CSV ] [ Alle Bilder ]`. The
   episode is a persisted `WeekRecap` document, so the JSON the techie downloads is *literally the object
   the film was rendered from* — not an export path, not a second implementation. One generated artifact,
   two readings.

**Defence of the ranked ordering** (the brief flags unpredictability as harmful): only the middle
chapters reorder. The film, the summary sentence and `ROHDATEN` are pinned first, second and last, always.
Every chapter keeps a stable icon, a stable accent colour and a stable German title, so the landmark set
is constant even when the sequence is not. If this still proves too unstable in testing, fixed order is a
one-line change and costs nothing — but ranked ordering is what lets one screen serve a user who has 15
seconds and a user who has 15 minutes.

**Where an old mode dies:** the `localStorage['app-settings-expert']` segment on the settings page is
deleted. Settings become the `GERÄTE` chapter's unabridged page, one list of every socket and every
target the device actually reports, each row a `value-edit-row`. There is no simple half and no expert
half because the list contains exactly the rows your hardware has (reveal by capability, from
`hardwareInfo.sockets` per-role, not the three collapsed buckets of `deviceControlCapability()`).

## 5. The chart

One chart engine, ECharts 6 (owner decision), hand-rolled ~50-line directive. Two sizes of the same
chart: the 56 px chapter strip, and the full chapter chart. Same option builder, `compact: boolean`.

**Goal vs actual.** The setpoint is drawn, not implied:

- A **stepped dashed line** for the setpoint: `type:'line', step:'end', lineStyle:{type:'dashed', width:1}`,
  stepping at `daynight.day` / `daynight.night` (seconds-of-day, UTC → rendered in the device's
  `cloudSettings.recapTimezone`) between `day.temperature` and `night.temperature`, parsed from
  `device.configuration`.
- A **soft target band** behind it with one declared source, labelled in the corner of the panel:
  `Alarmgrenzen` (the user's own alarm thresholds — hard edge) > `Ziel ± 1,5 °C` (soft) >
  `VPD-Fenster der Phase` (advisory, from `GROW_STAGE_PRESETS.vpdRange`). Never two at once.
- A **signed deviation fill** between actual and setpoint — `markArea` is not used for this; two
  `line` series with `areaStyle` and opposite `stack` signs give the "above is warm, below is cold"
  read. Never fill to the axis: `threshold:null` area (today's encoding) means nothing.
- **Min/max envelope** on the primary measure — `aggregateWindow(min)` and `(max)` alongside `mean`,
  drawn as a light band. A single mean line is a lie about a bang-bang controller.
- `sampling: 'minmax'` on the plot (LTTB smooths away exactly the oscillation the grower is looking for);
  LTTB only on the navigator.

**Day/night.** `markArea` bands derived from the *measured* `out_light > 0` series at 1-minute
resolution, falling back to the configured `daynight` schedule when the output series is missing, with
the fallback named in the panel corner (`nach Zeitplan` vs `nach Messung`). A failed contactor then reads
as a **missing band** — a diagnosis, not decoration. `lights.sunrise`/`sunset` ramps draw as gradient
edges. Timezone is explicit and stored, so a 12/12 flip near a DST boundary does not drift.

**Annotations.** A 16 px rail *under* the x-axis, never columns inside the plot. Two rows maximum: row 1
human events (water, feed, note, photo), row 2 device events (alarms, config changes, reboots).
Pixel-proximity clustering at 10 px with a count badge. Lifecycle stage changes are full-height dashed
verticals. **Tap, not hover** — the rail is the touch target and it is 44 px tall including padding, with
the visible mark 16 px.

**Outputs.** A state-timeline lane below the plot, one row per paired socket role that actually exists,
region length = duration, null renders as a gap. Not 0/1 lines on a value axis.

**Scrubbing header, not a tooltip.** A pinned two-line header above the chart shows the values at the
cursor. A fingertip occludes a tooltip and there is no hover on touch. One finger scrolls the page, two
fingers pan/zoom (`dataZoom: inside`), long-press sets the cursor.

**X axis is day-of-run.** Major ticks `Tag 29 … Tag 35`, minor label the date. A grower thinks in days
since flip, not in calendar weeks — and it makes the run-over-run comparison possible later without a
second axis model.

**Time-in-range is computed server-side on raw Influx samples**, at generation time, and stored in the
recap document. Never on `aggregateWindow(mean)` output — averaging destroys the excursions the metric
exists to count, and the error is invisible by eye. This is also why the chapter strip is cheap: the
verdict is already a number in a Mongo document, not a query.

## 6. The diary and multi-plant

New Mongo collections, all cloud-side (`device.configuration` cannot carry new keys), all with optional
fields and read-time normalisers (no migration tooling exists).

```ts
GrowRun {
  run_id: string; device_id: string;
  name: string;                       // "Lauf 3" default, editable
  startedAt: number; endedAt?: number;
  dayZeroAt: number;                  // day-of-run origin; defaults to startedAt
  mediumId?: string; feedRegimeId?: string;   // "Biobizz All-Mix" + "Bio-Grow/Bio-Bloom"
  createdAt: number;
}

Plant {
  plant_id: string; run_id: string; device_id: string;
  label: string;                      // "A1"
  strain?: string;                    // plain text
  startedAt: number; endedAt?: number; archived?: boolean;
}

Event {                               // the diary. One collection, one discriminator.
  event_id: string;                   // CLIENT-generated uuid at tap time → idempotent
  device_id: string; run_id?: string;
  plant_ids: string[];                // [] === "das ganze Zelt"
  type: 'water'|'feed'|'note'|'photo'|'train'|'measure'|'stage'|'harvest';
  at: number; createdAt: number;
  actor_id?: string; actorName?: string;      // denormalised, survives actor deletion
  source: 'app'|'schedule'|'device';
  water?: { litres: number; potCount?: number; runoff?: boolean };
  feed?:  { regimeId?: string; stepId?: string; litres: number;
            ec?: number; ecBasis?: 'absolute'|'delta'; ph?: number;
            items: { productLabel: string; mlPerLitre: number; ml: number }[] };
  measure?: Partial<DiaryEntryData>;   // reuses today's six fields verbatim
  note?: string; images?: string[];
  confirmedFrom?: string;              // the schedule step this confirmed
  supersedes?: string;                 // correction, never an edit-in-place
}

Condition {                           // the thing pinned to the tent door
  condition_id: string; device_id: string;
  text: string; openedBy?: string; openedAt: number;
  closedBy?: string; closedAt?: number;
}

DeviceActor { actor_id, device_id, name, colour, createdAt, disabledAt? }

WeekRecap {
  recap_id: string; device_id: string; run_id?: string;
  weekIndex: number; dayFrom: number; dayTo: number;
  from: number; to: number; timezone: string;
  generatedAt: number; generatorVersion: number;
  headline: string;                   // the generated sentence, already localised? NO — see below
  stats: { tempMean, tempMin, tempMax, timeInRangePct, timeBelowMs, timeAboveMs,
           rhMean, vpdMean, lightHoursPerDay, co2Mean?,
           framesCaptured, framesKept, framesRejected, rejectReasons: {...},
           coveragePct, waterLitres, feedCount, eventCount, alarmCount };
  chapters: { key, rank, sentenceKey, params }[];   // i18n keys + params, NOT prose
  videoImageId?: string; posterImageId?: string; spriteImageId?: string;
  publicSlug?: string; deliveredAt?: number;
}

RecapFrame {                          // the correlation index
  recap_id, image_id, t, dayOfRun,
  temperature?, humidity?, vpd?, co2?, lightPct, outputs: string[],
  sharpness, kept: boolean, rejectReason?: 'night'|'ramp'|'motion'|'dup'|'dark'
}
```

**The generated sentence is stored as an i18n key plus params, never as prose.** `chapters[].sentenceKey
= 'recap.klima.nightTooCold'`, `params = {actual: 19.4, target: 20.0, sinceHour: 3}`. Both `de.json` and
`en.json` get the key in the same commit. A German user and an English user open the same episode and
each reads their own language, and the video's burned-in captions are rendered at request time in the
requesting user's language into a **second video variant**, cached — or, in v1, in the device owner's
language only, which is the honest v1 answer.

**A user who ignores plants pays nothing.** `Plant` rows are created only by an explicit
`[ Pflanze anlegen ]` in the `PFLANZEN` chapter — a chapter that **does not exist while there are zero
plants**. Every event with `plant_ids: []` reads as "das ganze Zelt"; the capture sheet shows no plant
selector; the episode says `Das Zelt`, not `Pflanze 1`. The moment a second plant exists, plant chips
appear in the capture sheet with **all selected by default**, so the tap count does not change.

**The five existing diary categories keep working.** `DeviceLog` rows with `categories[0]==='diary'` are
read at generation time and folded into the episode's `EREIGNISSE` chapter alongside `Event` rows. A
one-time idempotent backfill turns each existing `diary-plant-lifecycle` row into a `stage` Event and
each `diary-measurement` into a `measure` Event, and runs `convertEventsToGrowCycles()` once to create
`GrowRun` rows — the browser-side cycle inference becomes a migration, not a runtime algorithm.

**Two existing bugs that must be fixed for this to be honest:** the server writes its own stage
transitions with `categories:['diary-plant-lifecycle']` (missing the `'diary'` prefix) so they are
invisible in the Entries report — the generator reads both spellings; and `deleted:true` means "hidden",
not deleted, so `DELETE /device/logs/:device_id` removes nothing from the diary. Once the diary is the
product, that reads as data loss. Fix both.

Buttons are 112 × 48 px — thumb-sized, reachable one-handed, high contrast, and they do not move.

**Multi-plant, all plants — still 2 taps** (chips default to all-selected). Selecting a subset costs one
tap per deselected plant.

### 7.2 Schedule following

The medium+regime pair is chosen **once**, in the `WASSER & DÜNGER` chapter, as the grower says it out
loud: `Biobizz All-Mix` · `Biobizz Bio-Grow / Bio-Bloom`. All-Mix is heavily pre-fertilised, so the
regime's own first step is `Woche 1–3: nicht düngen` and the app does not nag before then. BioTabs is a
re-tab reminder every ~5 weeks and almost no liquid feeding. The regime advances **by feed events**
("Schritt 5 von 12"), not by wall-clock weeks, with a soft calendar mapping — growers feed Tuesday and
Friday and they slip.

The bloom-week index is anchored by the **measured 18/6 → 12/12 flip** detected from `out_light` history.
No bottle brand's calculator can do that, because none of them can see the tent. When the flip is
detected the episode says so: `Am Tag 28 auf 12/12 umgestellt. Blütewoche 1 beginnt.`

Reminders are **inside the episode**, not push spam: the in-progress block shows
`Fällig seit gestern: Bio-Grow 2 ml/L  [ Erledigt ]` — one tap logs it with the schedule's values and
`confirmedFrom = stepId`.

Brand names are plain text labels from a Mongo content collection carrying `source_url` + `retrieved_at`,
shown as `zuletzt geprüft 12.06.2026`. No logos, no brand colours, no brand name inside a Terp Control
feature name. EC is stored canonical with `ecBasis` (`delta` for CANNA-style `EC+` charts) and never
rendered as a bare ppm number.

### 7.3 Offline capture (the cellar)

No service worker in v1. The capture sheet writes to **IndexedDB** with the client-generated `event_id`
before it does anything else, shows the toast immediately, and drains the queue on the next successful
request. The server route is `PUT /event/:event_id` — **idempotent by construction**, so a retry storm
cannot double-log. A queued-but-unsent event renders in the episode with a small `⇡` and the text
`noch nicht gesendet`. This is the one place where a retry queue would otherwise be indistinguishable
from a double feed, which is why the id is minted at tap time and not at send time.

### 7.4 What is stored

Every water/feed event stores: litres, the products and their ml/L **and** the computed ml, EC/pH with
basis if given, the actor, the plant ids, the source (`app` vs `schedule`), the schedule step it
confirmed, and the timestamp. That is enough to answer the cumulative question — see §9.

## 8. CAMERA, TIMELAPSE AND IMAGE CORRELATION

This is the concept's headline, not a tile. What follows is what ships in October.

### 8.1 What exists today (verified in `server/src/services/image.service.ts`)

- A still every 30 s per camera device (`IMAGE_LOAD_INTERVAL_MS = 30_000`), stored as Mongo `Buffer`.
- Thinning tiers: >1 d → 1/min, >7 d → 1/5 min, >30 d → 1/15 min, >90 d → 1/h. Retention 3 years.
- Three rolling timelapses rebuilt hourly (`1d`/`1w`/`1m`), frame interval 2/14/60 min, 25 fps.
- **They are encoded `libx265 -crf 30`.** HEVC in MP4 does not play in Firefox at all and only plays in
  Chrome where a hardware decoder is present. **Today's timelapses are unplayable for a large share of
  users and unshareable everywhere.** Fixing this is a prerequisite, not a nice-to-have.
- `sharp` is already a dependency and `addOfflineOverlay()` already composites an SVG onto a JPEG. The
  machinery for burning a lower third exists in the repo today.
- `ffmpeg` is available via `execFile` with `pLimit(10)` and a 1 s throttle.

### 8.2 Die Folge — the episode video, v1

A **21-second H.264 MP4** per device per week. Generated Monday 06:00 in the device's
`cloudSettings.recapTimezone` (a cloud-side field — legal to add, unlike device config).

**Culling pipeline**, in order, all cheap:

1. **Photoperiod gate.** Join the week's frames against the `out_light` series at 1-minute resolution
   (one Influx query, ~10 k points). Drop every frame taken while the light was off. Night frames are
   black or IR-green and they are half of the roll. This single step is the biggest watchability win in
   the pipeline. Exception: workmode `dry`, or `out_light` never above 0 (monitor-only tents with a
   wall-timer lamp) → keep all frames, and say so in the chapter.
2. **Ramp gate.** Drop frames within 4 minutes of lights-on and lights-off — sunrise/sunset ramps plus
   the camera's own exposure hunting produce the ugliest frames in the roll.
3. **One frame per 10-minute slot.** ~60 slots per lit day at 12/12 → **~420 frames per week**. Within a
   slot, keep the frame with the highest **sharpness proxy = JPEG byte length** (a blurred or
   motion-smeared frame compresses smaller). This costs zero decoding. The real Laplacian variance is a
   v1.1 upgrade; the byte proxy is right ~80 % of the time and wrong harmlessly.
4. **Human / door rejection.** Decode each candidate to a 16×16 greyscale thumbnail
   (`sharp().resize(16,16).greyscale().raw()`, ~1–2 ms). Reject a frame when *both*: its mean absolute
   difference to the slot's median thumbnail exceeds 3× the week's median MAD, **and** either its mean
   brightness jumped >25 % (door open, room light on) or its lower third changed far more than its upper
   third (a person, a hand, a watering can). The next-best frame in the slot is promoted. **Rejections
   are counted and reported**: `41 Bilder verworfen: Tür offen, Hand im Bild.` Honesty about culling is
   the only defence against a grower who notices their favourite frame is missing.
5. **Deduplication / frozen-camera detection.** Consecutive kept thumbnails with MAD < 1.5 mean nothing
   moved. Keep the first, drop the rest, and if that lasts >6 h it is a *frozen camera*, not a calm tent:
   the chapter says `Die Kamera lieferte ab Tag 33 immer dasselbe Bild.`
6. **Exposure and white-balance normalisation.** For each kept frame, take mean R/G/B from its 16×16
   thumbnail; the target is a 25-frame rolling median of those means; apply per-channel linear gain via
   `sharp().linear([gr,gg,gb],[0,0,0])`, **clamped to ±30 %** so a genuinely dark frame stays dark. This
   is the magenta-LED-versus-daylight fix and it costs one extra sharp pass over ~420 frames.
7. **Stabilisation: detected, not corrected, in v1.** Brute-force the best integer shift of each
   thumbnail against the previous day's median over ±2 thumbnail pixels (= ±64 full-res pixels, 25
   comparisons). If the median daily shift exceeds 1 thumbnail pixel, the episode reports
   `Die Kamera wurde am Tag 32 verschoben.` and does **not** blend across the jump. Real stabilisation
   (`vidstabdetect` + `vidstabtransform`, two passes, ~3× encode cost) is v1.1. Naming the nudge is more
   useful than half-fixing it.

**Lower third, burned in** — reusing the exact `sharp` + SVG composite already in the repo:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                            │
│              [ tent frame ]                │
│                                            │
├────────────────────────────────────────────┤  bottom 12%, rgba(13,14,18,.62)
│ Tag 31 · Do 28.08.  25,4 °C  57 %  VPD 1,18│  DejaVu Sans Bold, width/34
│ 4 L gegossen · Anna                        │  event line, green, holds 25 frames
└────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Burned in, not overlaid in the player, because the video must carry its own meaning when it is playing
inside a Telegram group where Terp Control has no code running. **That is the whole point of image
correlation for a shared artifact.**

**Encode:**
```
ffmpeg -framerate 25 -f image2 -i %d.jpeg \
       -vf scale='min(1280,iw)':-2 \
       -c:v libx264 -preset veryfast -crf 24 -pix_fmt yuv420p \
       -movflags +faststart -f mp4 out.mp4
```
`yuv420p` and `+faststart` are not optional — without them Safari refuses and progressive playback
breaks. ~420 frames + a 100-frame endcard hold at 25 fps ≈ **21 s, 2–4 MB**.

**The endcard** is the Wochenblatt, held 4 s, rendered by the same sharp+SVG path:
```
┌──────────────────────────────┐
│  ZELT 1                      │
│  Folge 5 · Tag 29–35         │
│  25.–31. August 2026         │
│                              │
│  25,1 °C     58 %            │
│  VPD 1,19    12,0 Std Licht  │
│                              │
│  94 % im Zielbereich         │
│  2 × gegossen · 1 × gedüngt  │
└──────────────────────────────┘
```
No nutrient brand logo ever. The Terp Control wordmark on the endcard is a **setting, defaulting OFF in
DE** — § 6 KCanG bans cannabis advertising and sponsoring with fines to €30,000, and a branded footer on
thousands of German growers' cannabis posts is the single riskiest string in the product.

### 8.3 How the video LEAVES the product

Three paths, in preference order. The first that the browser supports is the button's action.

1. **Web Share, files.** `navigator.canShare({files:[…]})` → fetch the blob → `navigator.share({files:
   [new File([blob], name, {type:'video/mp4'})], title, text})`. Supported on iOS Safari 15+ and Android
   Chrome. This opens the OS share sheet, which contains **Video sichern** (camera roll), Telegram,
   WhatsApp, Signal, AirDrop. This is the path that actually reaches a phone's gallery.
2. **Download.**
   `GET /image/:device_id?format=mp4&duration=recap&recap_id=<id>&download=1&token=<imageToken>`
   → `Content-Type: video/mp4`, `Accept-Ranges: bytes` with **real range support** (required for
   `<video>` seeking and for iOS to accept the save), and
   `Content-Disposition: attachment; filename="terpcontrol-zelt-1-folge-05-tag29-35.mp4"`.
   Filename rule: `terpcontrol-<device-slug>-folge-<NN>-tag<from>-<to>.mp4`, ASCII only, lowercase,
   umlauts transliterated (`ä→ae`), spaces → `-`, max 80 chars. A `filename*=UTF-8''…` is sent alongside.
3. **The honest iOS sentence.** On iOS without Web Share, a download lands in **Dateien → Downloads**,
   *not* in Fotos. So the button on that platform reads `[ Video sichern (Dateien) ]` and carries a
   second line: `Danach in Fotos: Datei antippen › Teilen › Video sichern.` Round one shipped no download
   path at all; shipping one that silently puts the file where the user cannot find it is not better.

Also: `[ Bild sichern ]` on the poster → a 1080 × 1350 JPEG of the Wochenblatt. Long-press on iOS saves a
JPEG to the camera roll directly, which is the one guaranteed route to a phone's gallery on every
platform. **The poster exists in every episode, camera or not.**

And `[ ⇪ Teilen ]` → a copy-to-clipboard block for Telegram/Discord (the community already lives on
Telegram):
```
Zelt 1 · Woche 5 (Tag 29–35)
25,1 °C · 58 % rF · VPD 1,19 kPa · 94 % im Zielbereich
2× gegossen (8 L) · 1× gedüngt
```
plus the file. A public `/r/<slug>` URL is v1.1 and off by default — it is a privacy decision, not a
feature toggle.

### 8.4 Image correlation

**The correlation is an index built at cull time, not a query at read time.** For every kept frame the
generator writes a `RecapFrame` row carrying that instant's temperature, humidity, VPD, CO₂, light
percentage, output states and day-of-run — from the one Influx query the pipeline already ran. 420 rows
per episode. That index is what makes every correlation interaction O(1).

Three interactions, all on the same index:

1. **Der Filmstreifen.** Under every chapter chart, a 56 px strip of thumbnails **positioned by
   timestamp on the chart's own x-scale** — so an uneven capture interval reads honestly as a gap, not as
   an evenly spaced lie. Delivered as **one sprite sheet per episode**: 96 tiles at 160 × 90 in a 12 × 8
   grid = 1920 × 720 JPEG, ~120 KB, one request, stored as `Image {format:'jpeg', duration:'recap-sprite'}`
   with a JSON index of `[t, dayOfRun, temp, rh, vpd]` per tile. Not 96 image requests.
2. **Dragging the chart moves the picture.** One cursor drives both: the scrubbing header shows the
   numbers, the strip highlights the nearest tile, and the full frame loads (debounced 250 ms) into the
   chapter's picture slot.
3. **Picking a picture shows its numbers.** Tap a tile → the cursor jumps there, the header fills, and
   the frame opens with its stamped values in the corner:
   `Tag 33 · 14:02 · 26,1 °C · 61 % · VPD 1,09 · Licht 100 % · Entfeuchter an`. Below it,
   `[ In der Kurve zeigen ]` and `[ Als Notiz speichern ]` — the second creates an `Event {type:'photo'}`
   pointing at that `image_id`, which is how a webcam frame becomes a diary entry with one tap.

### 8.5 The camera alarm

Round one gave the camera no alarm. This one does, using the existing alarm pipeline unchanged:
a device-scoped `Alarm { sensorType: 'webcam_age', upperThreshold: 10800 }` (3 h), evaluated
server-side against the newest `format:'jpeg'` row **only during a lit period** (a dark tent is not a
dead camera). It fires `actionType:'email'|'webhook'` like every other alarm, with the German string
`Die Kamera meldet sich seit 3 Stunden nicht.` It is created automatically the first time a camera pairs
and is deletable like any other alarm. A camera that dies three days into flower now tells you.

### 8.6 Cost, storage, retention — the numbers

**Generation, per device-week, measured against the pipeline above:**

| Step | Work | CPU |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Influx join (`out_light`, temp, RH, CO₂ @1 min) | 1 query, ~10 k points | ~1 s |
| Thumbnails for ~1 000 candidates | `sharp` resize 16×16, 1–2 ms each | ~2–4 s |
| Normalise + composite lower third on ~420 kept | `sharp linear` + SVG composite, ~25 ms each | ~11 s |
| x264 encode 520 frames @1280×720 veryfast | | ~4–8 s |
| Poster + sprite sheet | | ~2 s |
| **Total** | | **≈ 25–30 s of one core** |

At the October first series of **150 units**: ~75 CPU-minutes per week. Irrelevant. At 5 000 devices:
~42 CPU-hours per week — which is why generation runs on a rolling per-device schedule anchored to each
device's own delivery time, under a **separate `pLimit(2)`** from the existing capture limiter (which is
`pLimit(10)` and must not be starved), skipping any device whose previous job still runs.

**Storage, per device-week:** video 2–4 MB + poster 300 KB + sprite 120 KB ≈ **4 MB**, i.e. ~210 MB per
device-year. The source stills already cost far more and are unchanged.

**The 16 MB BSON cliff is real and must be guarded:** if the encode exceeds 12 MB, re-encode once at
`crf 28`; if still over, at 960 px; if still over, drop to one frame per 15-minute slot. A buffer over
16 MB must never reach Mongo. (Video in a document database is the wrong architecture; GridFS or object
storage is the right answer and is explicitly out of scope for October — see §13.)

**Retention:**
- Episodes are the product, so they outlive their inputs: **kept until the run is deleted**, while the
  source stills follow today's unchanged 3-year + thinning policy. An episode from 2027 survives the
  deletion of the frames that made it.
- `RecapFrame` rows live and die with their episode.
- `DELETE /recap/:recap_id` deletes the video, poster, sprite and frame index **and calls
  `DELETE /image/:image_id`** — a route that exists today and that the webapp never calls, which is why
  `user/jpeg` diary photos currently leak forever. Wiring it here fixes that leak.
- Per-device `Rückblicke: aus` stops generation entirely; deleting a run deletes its episodes.

### 8.7 Degradation with no camera

**The episode still generates. It always generates.** What changes is exactly one thing: there is no
video, so the poster becomes the centrepiece.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ FOLGE 5 · Tag 29–35 · 25.–31. August           │
│  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐  │
│  │  ZELT 1 · Folge 5 · Tag 29–35            │  │   the Wochenblatt PNG
│  │  25,1 °C   58 %   VPD 1,19   12,0 Std    │  │   1080×1350, sharp+SVG
│  │  94 % im Zielbereich                     │  │   downloadable, long-press

│  └──────────────────────────────────────────┘  │
```

## 9. Clubs

**Attribution without authorisation.** A device-scoped actor picker: the owner types names once in the
`GERÄTE` chapter (`DeviceActor {name, colour}`). The capture bar shows `👤 Anna ▾`; the choice sticks in
`localStorage['tc-actor-<device_id>']` for 12 hours, then asks again. Names are stamped onto
`Event.actor_id` **and** denormalised into `Event.actorName` so an event survives an actor being removed.
**No accounts, no invitations, no `auth.middleware.ts` surgery, no membership collection.** Roughly two
days of work, and it is the difference between "someone fed A3" and "Anna fed A3 at 18:40".

Real multi-user (separate logins, separate notifications) needs the membership collection and the rewrite
of `auth.middleware.ts:172` and `:207`. It is not in October. The actor picker is what ships, and it is
honestly labelled: `Wer ist am Zelt?` — not `Mitglied`.

**The double-feed problem, solved at the moment of capture.** When `[ Gedüngt ]` or `[ Gegossen ]` is
tapped, the sheet queries `Event` for the last 12 h with overlapping `plant_ids`. If it finds one:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
│  ⚠  Schon gefüttert                  │
│                                      │
│  Anna hat A1–A3 vor 1 Std 30 Min     │
│  gedüngt: 4 L, Bio-Grow 2 ml/L.      │
│                                      │
│  [ Abbrechen ]   [ Trotzdem ]        │   deliberate extra tap
└──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

A soft block, never a hard one — a split feed is legitimate. `Trotzdem` is one deliberate extra tap and
the resulting event is tagged `overlapWith: <event_id>` so the episode can report it:
`Am Tag 31 zweimal gedüngt, 90 Minuten auseinander (Anna, Ben).` A double feed is the most expensive
thing that happens in a shared room and this is the only place to catch it.

**The cumulative question**, which nobody answered: the `WASSER & DÜNGER` chapter's unabridged page is a
per-plant table for the whole run, not the week:

```
Lauf 3 · Tag 1–39                       Wasser    Bio-Grow   Bio-Bloom
A1                                       23,0 L      46 ml       0 ml
A2                                       23,0 L      46 ml       0 ml
A3                                       11,5 L      12 ml       0 ml     ⚠ zurück
das ganze Zelt                           47,0 L      94 ml       0 ml
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Anna  18 ×        Ben  6 ×        Plan   4 × bestätigt, 2 × versäumt
```

**Standing conditions.** `Condition` rows are the thing pinned to the tent door: an open fact with an
owner, not an event and not a task. They render at the top of the in-progress block and are **reprinted
in every episode until closed** — `Offen seit 9 Tagen: CO2-Flasche fast leer — Anna`. Closing one takes
one tap and stamps `closedBy`.

**Privacy posture.** Actor names are free text a club chooses; a club that wants pseudonyms types `A`,
`B`, `C` and nothing in the product objects. Episodes contain no membership numbers, no dates of birth,
no addresses, no dispensing records — Terp Control does not become the club's § 26 system of record, and
holding per-member consumption data would drag a grow controller into Art. 9 GDPR territory for data it
has no need for. Harvest weight is off by default and never appears in an episode unless typed. The
public `/r/` page (v1.1) strips actor names unless explicitly enabled. No location, ever. No
`KCanG-konform` badge, ever.

## 11. Per-user-type walkthrough

**1 — Lazy monitoring-only.** He never opens the app. On Monday morning an email arrives with the
Wochenblatt poster and one sentence: *"Ruhige Woche. 94 % im Zielbereich."* That is the entire product
for him, and it is more than he gets today, where the app is a chart he stopped opening in week two. When
something goes wrong the sentence changes and he clicks through. Served best of all seven.

**2 — Monitoring with a camera.** The owner's named type, and the one this concept is built for. He gets
a 21-second film every Monday with the numbers burned in, culled so it does not contain forty black
frames and his own arm, downloadable to his phone, playable in the Telegram group. Over twelve weeks he
accumulates twelve episodes that add up to the record of the grow, without ever having written a diary
entry. This is the concept's reason to exist.

**3 — Controls certain aspects (CO₂, heating).** The `GERÄTE` chapter shows exactly the sockets his
device reports and nothing else — no dehumidifier row on a heater-only tent, because capability comes
from `hardwareInfo.sockets` per role, not from the three collapsed buckets that currently fail open. His
weekly episode tells him whether the heater actually held the night setpoint, with the setpoint drawn.

**4 — Controls the entire tent.** Best served on the chapter pages: five socket roles, the state
timeline, the deviation fill, time-in-range split day/night. He will still hit the wall the firmware
imposes — no humidifier role, no exhaust role, `dehumidifier` doubling as the cooler — and this concept
does not fix that, it just stops lying about it: a role the hardware does not have does not get a row.
His episode is the weekly proof that the tent held.

**5 — Closed-loop DIY.** Served worst, and the concept should say so. He wants a write API and a live
stream; a weekly episode is the least real-time object in the product. What he does get is the
`ROHDATEN` chapter: `WeekRecap` as JSON, `RecapFrame` as CSV, and a stable `GET /recap/:id` — a
documented, versioned, machine-readable weekly summary that nobody else in the market publishes. That is
a consolation, not an answer.

**6 — Grow club.** The episode is the shared object, and it is what gets posted in the club's group chat.
Attribution rides on every capture, the double-feed guard runs at the moment two people can collide,
conditions stay pinned until someone closes them, and the cumulative per-plant table answers "how much
Bio-Bloom has A3 had, and from whom". No accounts, no auth rewrite, no compliance claims.

**7 — Old device owner.** Sees §10. Two-chapter episodes, a poster, a JSON, and nothing broken. A plug
owner is not the audience for a weekly film and is not sold one.

**Capability lenses.** *Stoner, 2 am, one hand, dark tent:* the top of the scroll is a photograph, three
big numbers and one sentence, and the capture bar is three fixed thumb-sized buttons that never move.
Two taps to log a watering. *Normal:* a weekly film they can show a friend, which is the thing AC
Infinity does not have and Instagram does. *Techie:* every sentence carries its number, every chapter has
an unabridged page one tap away, and the last chapter of every episode is the raw object.

**Does not ship in October:** real stabilisation; per-plant crop regions; the public `/r/` page; Telegram
and Discord *delivery* (the clipboard block ships, the integration does not); harvest and yield;
run-over-run episode comparison; cross-device episodes; a whole-grow "best of" film; real multi-user
logins; a service worker; the API for type 5 beyond the raw recap document.

**The honest schedule.** Generator + culling ≈ 2 weeks of one server developer. Scroll + chapter pages ≈
2.5 weeks of one front-end developer. Capture bar + collections + pagination ≈ 1.5 weeks. ECharts chapter
chart ≈ 1.5 weeks. That is ~7.5 developer-weeks against a 6–8 week calendar, so **it needs two people**.
If it is one: cut the ECharts chapter chart and render the `KLIMA` chapter's evidence strip as a **static
PNG from the same sharp pipeline that already draws the poster** — the strip is 56 px of sparkline and a
band, the server can draw it, and the interactive chart lands in v1.1. That fallback is real, cheap and
loses only the scrubbing.

## 13. Honest weaknesses

1. **A weekly heartbeat is the wrong tempo for an emergency.** The concept's centre of gravity is seven
   days old by construction. Everything urgent has been pushed into the live strip at the top of the
   scroll and into the existing alarm pipeline, and if that strip is even slightly weak, a stoner at 2 am
   is worse served here than by a screen of honest tiles. This is the single biggest structural risk and
   no amount of good episode design fixes it.

2. **Week one is empty.** A customer unboxing in October gets the product's centrepiece on the following
   Monday. The mitigation — a partial "Folge 1 entsteht" block plus the existing rolling 24 h timelapse —
   is a promise, not a payoff, and first impressions are decided in the first hour, not the first week.
   Generating a "Folge 0" after 48 hours produces a bad film, and a bad first film is worse than none.

3. **Generated artifacts go stale and cannot be recalled.** If someone corrects Tuesday's feed on Friday,
   last week's episode is now wrong. Episodes can be regenerated (`generatorVersion` is stored, and
   `Event.supersedes` gives corrections rather than edits) — but a video already downloaded and posted in
   a Telegram group is permanent. Every product that generates artifacts has this problem and none of
   them have solved it.

4. **The culling heuristics are crude and their failures are invisible.** 16 × 16 thumbnails, a byte-length
   sharpness proxy and a brightness rule will reject legitimate frames — a grower who moves plants every
   day will be told, in effect, that they are a door. Reporting the reject count and reasons is a
   mitigation, not a fix, and the first support ticket about "my best frame is missing" is a matter of
   when.

5. **Video in MongoDB.** 4 MB per device-week of Buffer in a document database, against a 16 MB BSON
   ceiling, with a guard chain of re-encodes to stay under it. This is architecturally wrong and it is
   shipped knowingly because GridFS or object storage is not October work. It will need doing in 2027 and
   it will be a migration.

6. **Type 5 is served worst, and type 5 is the loudest.** The DIY closed-loop grower wants an API, a
   stream and control; he is offered a weekly JSON. He will say so publicly.

7. **"It does the diary keeping for you" is only three-quarters true.** No hardware observes watering or
   feeding. The episode's hole-shaped prompt is the whole mechanism for getting those, and if a user
   ignores it, the episode is climate plus pictures — a weather report with a plant in it. The concept is
   betting that visible emptiness motivates capture better than a button does. That bet is untested.

8. **Ranked chapter ordering is mild unpredictability**, which the brief explicitly flags as harmful to
   the impaired user. Pinning the first two and last chapters and keeping icons and colours stable
   reduces the cost but does not remove it. Fixed order was considered and rejected, and if user testing
   disagrees, reverting is one line — which is also an admission that the mechanism is not load-bearing.

9. **The unabridged chapter page will be called an expert mode by a critic.** The defence — same title,
   same first sentence, no state, no preference, one tap from where everyone already is — is good, but it
   is an argument, and arguments lose to first impressions. If the chapter pages drift into looking
   different from the chapters they expand, the concept has quietly failed the round's hard rule.

10. **Localised burned-in captions do not scale.** v1 burns the device owner's language into the video. A
    German club that shares an episode with an English-speaking friend shares a German video. Rendering a
    second variant per language doubles encode cost and storage, and doing it lazily on request means a
    30-second wait behind a share button.
