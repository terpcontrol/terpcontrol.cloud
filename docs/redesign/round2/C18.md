# C18 — Der Kontaktbogen

*Family: camera. Round two. Centre of gravity: the contact sheet is the navigation.*

---

## 1. Thesis

**The grow is a roll of film, the app is the contact sheet, and every frame carries the numbers that
were true when it was taken — so browsing time is browsing pictures, and there is nothing else to learn.**

A photographic contact sheet is a whole roll printed at thumbnail size on one page. You do not read it;
you *scan* it, and your eye lands on the frame that looks different. That is exactly the interaction a
grower needs and never gets: 84 days of a tent, laid out, with the bad Tuesday visibly darker than its
neighbours. Terp Control already takes a still every 30 seconds and already stores it with a timestamp.
Today those stills are a tile on a dashboard and three rolling mp4s nobody can download.

This concept makes the sheet the product's spine. The home screen is a sheet. The chart's x-axis has a
sheet glued under it. The diary is the sheet with events stamped into the frames. A timelapse is not a
feature you find in a menu — it is "make a film out of the part of the sheet I am looking at".

The two things that make it real rather than pretty are both engineering, and both are in scope:
**one sprite per window instead of N image requests**, so a 90-day sheet costs two HTTP requests; and
**frame culling, normalisation and a downloadable H.264 file**, so the film is watchable and leaves the
product onto a phone.

## 2. Information architecture

There is one navigational object: **the cell**. A cell is a span of time, drawn. Tapping a cell shows a
shorter span at a bigger size. That is the entire navigation model, and it is also the no-modes mechanism
(§4).

```
/list                                  device list (unchanged; 1 device → straight through)
/device/:id                            DER BOGEN — home. Kopf + Zettel + sheet + capture bar
   ↳ tap a day cell     →              day expands IN PLACE into its 48-frame strip (no route change)
   ↳ tap a frame        →  /device/:id/bild?t=<ms>      BILD — one frame, full-bleed, with its numbers
        ↳ pull the drawer →            raw series, per-measure, CSV
   ↳ tap the Kopf       →  /device/:id/charts?t=<ms>    the chart, cursor at that moment
   ↳ "Film"             →  /device/:id/film             film builder + the device's films
/device/:id/charts                     small multiples + film strip axis (shares the sheet's sprite)
/device/:id/pflanzen                   plants — ONLY EXISTS IF A PLANT EXISTS
/device/:id/bilanz?run=<id>            cumulative water/feed per plant per run, per actor
/device/:id/settings                   unchanged routes, capability-filtered content
/device/:id/testmode, /shares, /account, /classes   unchanged
```

Deleted as destinations: the diary page's three-report switcher. `entries`, `growreport` and `co2report`
become **renderings of the sheet's current window**, reachable from the sheet's overflow as
`Als Liste` / `Als Bericht`. Same rows, same date range, same query params — a table view of a picture
view, which is also the accessibility fallback the dossier asks for. Not a mode: it does not persist, it
does not change any other screen, and it is one tap back.

Query params (extend, never redefine — presets and share links are on-disk data):
`from`, `to` (existing `date`/`dateEnd` kept as aliases), `t` (cursor ms, new), `level` (`run|day|frame`,
new, purely positional), `plant`, `sort` (`zeit|uhrzeit|abweichung`), `nacht` (0/1).

### 3.2 The cell

```
┌────────────┐
│            │   background-image: the month sprite
│            │   background-position: -(col*96)px -(row*54)px
│         26°│   only at M (160×90) and larger

  21  💧🧪       day, then up to 3 event glyphs
```

The Tagesband is the whole data argument of this concept and it is 3 pixels tall. It is a stacked
horizontal bar over the day's 24 hours, computed **server-side on raw Influx samples** (never on
`aggregateWindow(mean)` — averaging erases the excursions the metric exists to count). At cell size it is
a texture: a wall of green with one amber Tuesday. At frame size it is the same object with an axis and
labels: `91,4 % im Ziel · 4 Std 12 Min unter Ziel · längste Abweichung 1 Std 40`.

Missing frames are not skipped and not faked. A day with no picture renders as
`--tc-surface-subtle` with the Tagesband still drawn — the data is there, the picture is not, and the
sheet says so by looking empty.

## 4. NO-MODES MECHANISM — the cell is the only object, and its size is a position, not a preference

The whole product is one component at four sizes. **The size determines the content by a fixed table.
The user never sets the size; they arrive at it by tapping.**

| Size | one cell = | picture | burned data | Tagesband | next tap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **XS** 44×25 | 1 week (year sheet) | week's representative frame | — | 2 px, week in band | → S |
| **S** 96×54 | 1 day (run sheet, the home screen) | day's representative frame | — | 3 px, 24 h | → M |
| **M** 160×90 | 30 min (day strip, expands in place) | that frame | corner: leading measure `26°` | 3 px, 30 min | → L |
| **L** full width | 1 frame | full-bleed | pinned header: all measures, actor, events | full labelled scorecard | drawer → raw series + CSV |

**Why this is not a mode.** A mode is state that persists and changes screens you have not visited yet.
Size here is a *position in the navigation*, exactly like a folder depth. There is no toggle, no
preference, no `localStorage` key, no separate route tree, no onboarding question, no "Erweitert"
disclosure. Going back is one gesture. Two people on the same device see the same thing when they open
the app. Nothing is withheld at any level: at every size, the affordance that reaches the next level is
the same visible tap on the same object, so the techie never has to *discover* depth — they just keep
going in the direction they were already going.

**Why it actually serves the stoner.** They tap a picture and get a bigger picture with three numbers on
it. They can stop at any point and the screen they stopped on is complete. There is no dead end, no
empty state that says "configure this first", and no vocabulary — the entire top level is photographs
and one German sentence.

**Why it actually serves the techie.** The distance from the home screen to a raw 1-second series is
three taps and a drawer pull, all in one direction, with a URL at every step. The pinned header on the
frame is a table of every measure at that instant, not a curated three. The drawer's CSV button exports
the window. Nothing they want is behind a "simple mode" wall, because there is no wall — only more taps,
which is what depth is.

**The second lever: the Tagesband is the same pixels for both.** One object encodes time-in-band. The
impaired user reads it as texture from across a dark room ("mostly green, one red day"). The precise user
reads `91,4 % im Ziel` off the same object one level down. Nobody was handed a different chart, and
nobody was denied the number — the number is *next to* the verdict, not instead of it.

**The third lever: reveal by capability and by data, all four derived, never chosen.**
- No camera in `hardwareInfo.webcam_did` → no photo cells; the Streifen renders duty cycles instead.
- No `Plant` document → no plant chips anywhere; `Gießen` logs the tent and never asks which plant.
- No `FeedRegime` selected → the `Düngen` button costs 2 taps instead of 1 and shows no week label.
- Exactly one `Actor` → capture never asks "Wer?". A second actor appears → it does, from then on.
- `parseSocketRoles()` says no `co2` socket and `hardwareInfo` says no SCD4x → no CO₂ panel, no CO₂
  alarm preset, no CO₂ row. This also finally kills the fail-open bug where a missing `sockets` key
  showed a heater-only tent a dehumidifier it does not own.

**Adaptive ordering is explicitly rejected.** The sheet is in chronological order, always. A grid whose
cells move is unusable at 2 a.m. The one reordering control (`Sortierung: Abweichung`) is a deliberate,
labelled, non-persisted action that says what it did — not an algorithm operating on you.

## 5. The chart

ECharts 6, tree-shaken, hand-rolled ~50-line directive (owner decision). Chart.js/ng2-charts deleted.

**Panels** — `grid[]` small multiples, shared x, one linked crosshair via `axisPointer.link`, ~110 px
each on a phone. Panels are chosen by capability, not by a picker:

```
┌─ FILMSTREIFEN ────────────────────────────┐  the sheet's M sprite, positioned by
│ ▪▪▪ ▪▪▪▪  ▪▪▪▪▪▪▪ ▪▪   ▪▪▪▪▪▪▪▪▪▪▪▪▪     │  timestamp on the SAME x-scale.
└───────────────────────────────────────────┘  Uneven capture reads honestly as gaps.
┌─ Temperatur °C ───────────────────────────┐  mp4 rows (timestampEnd) render as a
│  ░░░░░ target band ░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░  │  bracket, not a point.
│  ──── Ist (solid, min/max envelope)       │
│  ┅┅┅┅ Soll (dashed, STEPPED at day/night) │
└───────────────────────────────────────────┘
┌─ Feuchte % / VPD kPa ─────────────────────┐  two units allowed here only: physically
└───────────────────────────────────────────┘  coupled. Leaf offset stated on the panel:
┌─ CO₂ ppm ────────── (only if SCD4x) ──────┐  „Blatt = Luft − 2,0 °C (angenommen)".
└───────────────────────────────────────────┘
┌─ Ausgänge ────────────────────────────────┐  state-timeline lanes, one per PAIRED role.
│ Licht  ██████████░░░░░░░░░░██████████     │  Region length = duration. null = gap,
│ Heizer ░░██░░░░██░░░░██░░░░░░██░░░░░░     │  so a failed contactor is a missing band.
└───────────────────────────────────────────┘
  ▏ ▎  ▌ ▏   ▎▌▏      ▎                       Ereignis-Schiene: 2 rows under the axis,
  └ user events (row 1) · device events (row 2), clustered at 10 px with count badges
```

**Goal vs actual.** The setpoint is drawn as a **dashed stepped line** that steps at `daynight.day` /
`daynight.night` between `day.temperature` and `night.temperature`, with a soft band behind it and a
signed deviation fill *between actual and setpoint* — never to the axis. Band source, one at a time and
always labelled in the panel corner:
1. `Alarmgrenzen` — the user's own declared thresholds (hard edge),
2. `Soll ± Toleranz` — ±1,5 °C / ±7 % (the tolerances `grow-assistant-card` already uses),
3. `VPD-Fenster (Blüte)` — the stage's `vpdRange` from `GROW_STAGE_PRESETS`, advisory, and the band
   **changes along the x-axis** as the run passes stage events. That last one is the chart nobody
   verifiably ships.

**Day/night** from measured `out_light > 0` where available, falling back to the `daynight` schedule,
with `lights.sunrise`/`sunset` as gradient edges, in an explicit named timezone (a 12/12 flip near a DST
boundary otherwise drifts an hour).

**Scrubbing is the correlation.** One cursor drives the film strip, all panels, and a pinned header
above the chart (a compact table, one row per panel) — no floating tooltip, because a fingertip occludes
it and touch has no hover. One finger scrolls the page; two fingers pan/zoom (`dataZoom: inside`).
The strip is fed by the **same sprite** as the sheet, so dragging across 24 hours of chart issues **zero**
image requests.

`sampling: 'minmax'` on the plot (LTTB smooths away the bang-bang oscillation the grower is looking
for), LTTB only in the navigator. Okabe-Ito palette. `darkMode: 'auto'`, deleting the hand-maintained
30-property dark theme and its `MutationObserver`.

Prerequisite, not optional: `data.service.ts` interpolates `measure`, `from`, `to` and `interval` raw
into Flux and the `limit(n:50000)` guard is piped after `yield()`. Fix both before widening the measure
vocabulary.

## 6. The diary and multi-plant

**A diary entry is a frame.** Every event is placed into the sheet at its timestamp and adopts the
nearest camera frame within ±15 min as its picture, or its own attached photo if there is one. The sheet
*is* the diary — "it does the diary keeping for you" becomes literally true, because the camera writes
the boring 95 % and the human writes the 5 % that has words in it.

New MongoDB collections, all cloud-side (`device.configuration` cannot carry new keys), all fields
optional with read-time normalisers, no migration tooling required:

```ts
Plant {
  plant_id: string; device_id: string; label: string;          // "A3"
  strain?: string; source?: 'seed' | 'clone' | 'auto';
  medium_id?: string; regime_id?: string;                       // §7
  started_at: number; ended_at?: number;
  position?: string; archived?: boolean; created_by?: string;
}

GrowRun {
  run_id: string; device_id: string; label: string;
  started_at: number; ended_at?: number;
  plant_ids: string[];
  stage_events: { stage: DiaryLifecycleStage; at: number }[];
  cover_image_id?: string;                                      // the run sheet's poster frame
}

Event {
  event_id: string; device_id: string;
  client_event_id: string;          // minted on the phone; UNIQUE with device_id → idempotent
  run_id?: string; plant_ids: string[];   // [] === "the whole tent"
  type: 'water' | 'feed' | 'note' | 'train' | 'pest' | 'measure' | 'harvest' | 'stage';
  at: number; actor_id?: string; actor_name?: string;
  note?: string; images: string[];
  water?: { litres?: number; cans?: number; ph?: number; ec?: number;
            runoff_ph?: number; runoff_ec?: number };
  feed?:  { regime_id?: string; step_index?: number; litres?: number; cans?: number;
            products: { name: string; ml_per_l: number }[]; ph?: number; ec?: number };
  source: 'manual' | 'schedule' | 'device';
}

Condition {                          // gap 4: the thing pinned to the tent door
  condition_id: string; device_id: string; plant_ids?: string[];
  text: string; openedBy: string; openedAt: number;
  closedBy?: string; closedAt?: number;
}

Actor { actor_id: string; device_id: string; name: string; colour: string;
        created_by: string; archived?: boolean; }
```

`DeviceLog` is left alone. Existing diary rows are read into the sheet through an adapter
(`diary-plant-lifecycle` → `stage`, `diary-measurement` → `measure`, etc.) and a one-time idempotent
backfill turns `convertEventsToGrowCycles()` from a runtime string-comparison algorithm into a set of
`GrowRun` documents. That kills the bug where renaming a plant forks the history and a re-veg fabricates
a new one. `deleted: true` stays as the visibility flag it actually is; the sheet requests both.

**A user who ignores plants is untaxed.** `plant_ids: []` is the default and means the tent. With zero
`Plant` documents there are no chips, no selector, no "which plant?" step, no plant column in the list
view, and no `/pflanzen` route in the menu. The first plant is created by *typing a label into the
capture sheet's optional field*, not by visiting a setup screen. Reveal by data.

**Server-side pagination is a prerequisite, not a nice-to-have.** `getDeviceLogs` has no `.limit()` and
the Entries report fetches a device's entire history on open. Multiplying rows per plant without fixing
that ships a regression. Budgeted in §12.

## 7. Watering & fertilising — counted in taps

The capture bar is present on the sheet at all times. Tap counts are the specification.

**Gießen (water)**
- **1 tap.** `💧 Gießen` → logged: now, whole tent, `litres` prefilled from the last watering, actor =
  the only actor. A frame appears in today's cell with `💧 8 L`. A 3-second `Rückgängig` toast.
- **2 taps.** `💧` then a plant chip (`A1` `A2` `A3` `Alle`) → logged for that plant. Chips only exist
  if plants exist.
- **3 taps.** `💧` → `⋯` → the sheet opens with `Menge`, `Kannen`, `pH`, `EC`, `Ablauf-EC`, `Notiz`,
  `Foto`. Every field prefilled from last time, every field editable, `Speichern`.

`Kannen` is not decoration: a hand-waterer counts watering-can fills, not litres.
`Device.can_litres` (default 10) converts, and the number the grower said out loud is the number stored.

**Düngen (feed)**
- **1 tap** when a regime is selected. The button reads `🧪 Düngen · KW5` because the schedule knows
  which step is due. Confirming logs the step's products and ml/L, the litres from last time, at now.
- Editing any prefilled number is a tap **on the number itself** — no edit mode, no pencil.
- **2 taps** with no regime: `🧪` → free-text product + ml/L.

**The scheme is medium + feed regime, said the way growers say it.** One selection, once:

```
FeedRegime {
  regime_id: string; label: string;              // "Biobizz All-Mix + Bio-Grow/Bio-Bloom"
  medium: string;                                 // "Biobizz All-Mix"
  prefertilised: 'none' | 'light' | 'heavy';      // All-Mix = heavy → no feed for 3–4 weeks
  start_week: number;                             // All-Mix: 4. Light-Mix: 2. BioTabs: n/a
  cadence: 'per_watering' | 'weekly' | 'every_n_days' | 'retab';
  min_gap_hours: number;                          // double-feed window, default 48
  steps: { index: number; label: string;
           products: { name: string; ml_per_l: number | [number, number] }[];
           ec_basis?: 'absolute' | 'delta_over_source'; ec?: [number, number];
           ph?: [number, number] }[];
  source_url: string; retrieved_at: number; chart_version: string;
}
```

Stored in a MongoDB content collection, never in the bundle, so any brand is removable in one DB update.
Brand names are plain text labels only. `ec_basis: 'delta_over_source'` exists because CANNA publishes
`EC +`, not EC — alarming against the raw chart number is actively wrong. EC is canonical; a bare "ppm"
number is never rendered without its scale.

**Preventing the double feed — the soft block, at the moment of capture.** Before writing, the server
checks for an `Event` of the same type touching any of the same `plant_ids` within `min_gap_hours`. If
one exists, the confirm button turns amber and the sheet says, verbatim:

> **Anna hat A1–A3 vor 1 Std 30 gefüttert.**
> Ein zweiter Dünger am selben Tag verbrennt die Wurzeln.
> [ Abbrechen ]   [ Trotzdem eintragen ]

One deliberate extra tap to proceed, and the override is stored (`Event.note` gets
`überschrieben (Doppelgabe)`), because in a shared room a knowing double feed and a mistaken one need to
be told apart later.

**Capture in a cellar.** No service worker in v1 — adding `@angular/service-worker` is a build change and
a dependency, and it is not where the six weeks go. What ships instead is the part that makes offline
*safe* rather than *possible*: `client_event_id` is minted on the phone at the moment of the tap, the
write is queued in `localStorage` if the POST fails, and the server upserts on
`{device_id, client_event_id}`. A retry can therefore never double-log — which in a shared record is
indistinguishable from a double feed. The queue's contents are visible in the capture bar
(`2 Einträge warten auf Verbindung`), because a silent queue is worse than no queue.

## 8. Camera, timelapse and image correlation

This is the concept, not a section of it.

### 8.1 The sprite and the index — one request per window

**Today there is no endpoint that lists image timestamps at all.** The chart's webcam scrubber guesses by
timestamp and fires one request per hover. That is the thing to replace.

New collection (separate from `Image`, so the unique index
`{device_id, format, timestamp, duration}` is untouched):

```ts
ContactSheet {
  sheet_id: string; device_id: string;
  tier: 'run' | 'day';                 // run: 1 frame/day. day: 1 frame/30 min.
  size: 's' | 'm';                     // s = 96×54, m = 160×90
  bucket_start: number; bucket_end: number;   // run: calendar month. day: one day.
  cols: number; rows: number; tile_w: number; tile_h: number;
  hash: string;                        // content hash → immutable URL
  data: Buffer;                        // the WebP sprite, 55–210 KB
  built_at: number;
  frames: { i: number; t: number; image_id: string;
            flags: ('dark'|'dup'|'transient'|'break'|'manual')[];
            luma: number; sig: string;          // 144-cell grey signature, base64
            band: [number, number, number, number] }[];   // in/out/alarm/missing minutes
}
```

Two endpoints:

```
GET /sheet/:device_id?from=&to=&tier=run&size=s
    → { tile:{w,h}, buckets:[ { sprite:"/sheet/img/<hash>.webp", cols, rows,
                                frames:[{i,t,image_id,flags,band}] } ] }
GET /sheet/img/:hash.webp
    → Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable
```

**One index request + one sprite request per window.** The sprite URL is content-hashed, so it is cached
forever and a rebuild of the open day changes the hash and nothing else. Rendering a cell is a `<div>`
with `background-image` + `background-position` — no `<img>` per frame, no lazy-load observer per frame,
no request per frame.

**What the grid does with 90 days × 48 frames = 4320 frames: it never mounts them.**

- The **run sheet** is 90 cells, one per day, chunked by calendar month. Three months = three sprites,
  fetched lazily as you scroll. A month sprite at `s`: 16 cols × 2 rows = 1536×108 WebP q62 ≈ **48 KB**.
- Tapping a day **expands it in place** into that day's strip — one more sprite: 48 tiles at `m`,
  8 cols × 6 rows = 1280×540 ≈ **112 KB**. You do not leave the sheet, so you do not lose your place.
- Maximum ever mounted: ~90 + 48 = **138 cells from at most 4 sprites, ≈ 260 KB**, versus 4320 requests.
- The **year sheet** (XS, one cell per week) is a fourth tier built by picking each week's representative
  day frame. 52 cells, one sprite, ~30 KB.

Build cost, measured against what the code already does: sharp decode + resize + normalise ≈ 12 ms per
frame. A month's run sprite = 30 × 12 ms ≈ 0,4 s, rebuilt when a day closes. A day's sprite = 48 × 12 ms
≈ 0,6 s, rebuilt hourly for the open day only (the same throttle the existing timelapse rebuild uses).
Per device per day: ~1 s of CPU plus a handful of open-day rebuilds. At 150 units, negligible.

### 8.2 Culling and normalisation — the content problem

At ingest, one extra sharp pass per frame stores three cheap numbers on the `Image` row (new optional
fields, read-time normalised): `luma` (mean), `lumaSd`, and `sig` — a 16×9 = 144-cell grayscale
downsample, 144 bytes. Everything below is derived from those, with no ML and no per-pixel work at
render time.

| Problem | Rule | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lights off** | `out_light == 0` at that minute **and** `luma < 25` → `flags:['dark']` | excluded from sheet and film. `Nachtbilder zeigen` is a checkbox on the sheet — a filter on the same grid, like a date range, not a mode. |
| **A hand / the door** | `L1(sig, sig_prev) > θ` **and** `L1(sig, sig_next) > θ` **and** `L1(sig_prev, sig_next) < θ/3` → `transient` | the transient frame is culled; a real change (a plant grew) persists across neighbours and survives |
| **Door-open blowout** | `luma > p90(day) × 1.8` or top-third mean > 240 → `transient` | the white flash that ruins a film |
| **Near-identical frames** | `L1(sig, last_kept) < δ` → `dup` | 12 hours of an unchanging night-time-off tent collapse; the film does not stall |
| **Condensation / defocus** | `lumaSd` below the 7-day 10th percentile for > 3 consecutive frames → `soft`, kept but deprioritised as a day's representative frame | the day's poster frame is not the foggy one |
| **Camera nudged** | `L1(sig, median(sig, last 24 h))` crosses θ **and stays** for > 20 frames → `break` | **no stabilisation.** Instead the sheet draws a `Schnitt`: a 2 px vertical rule between the frames, labelled `Kamera verschoben · 12. Aug`, and a device-log entry `message-camera-moved` is written. A contact sheet from a real roll shows the splice. This is honest, buildable in a week, and more useful than bad stabilisation. |
| **Exposure / white balance** | at sprite/film build only, never on the archived jpeg: per-channel grey-world gain toward a **7-day rolling median of channel means over lights-on frames**, clamped to [0.75, 1.40]; luma gain clamped to [0.70, 1.50] | kills the magenta-LED ↔ daylight flip and the slow seasonal drift without inventing colours |

The **day's representative frame** (what the run sheet shows) is: among that day's non-`dark`,
non-`transient`, non-`soft` frames, the one closest to `lights_on + 6 h`, so the poster frame is at the
same point in the photoperiod every day — which is what makes the run sheet read as growth rather than
as flicker.

**The camera gets an alarm.** A tick in `image.service` compares the newest `jpeg` timestamp to now. If
the device is online, `webcam_did` is set, and the gap exceeds 6 h, it writes a severity-2 device log
(`message-camera-silent`, categories `['webcam','error']`) and sends one mail through the existing
`alarm.service` transport, with a 24 h cooldown. The Kopf shows the red band. ~40 lines. A camera that
died three days into flower now tells you instead of waiting to be noticed.

### 8.3 The film — v1 ships something you can hold

Today: `-vcodec libx265 -crf 30`, no `-pix_fmt`, no `-movflags +faststart`, output stored as a Mongo
`Buffer`, served only through a webcam tile. **HEVC in mp4 does not play in Firefox and fails on a large
share of Android Chrome.** Three concrete changes make the existing pipeline produce a shareable file.

```
ffmpeg -framerate 25 -f image2 -i %05d.jpg
       -c:v libx264 -profile:v high -pix_fmt yuv420p -crf 21
       -vf scale=1280:720:force_original_aspect_ratio=decrease,pad=1280:720:-1:-1
       -movflags +faststart -an out.mp4
```

**The sheet's current selection is the film's cut.** Under the sheet: `Film`. The dialog is three rows:

```
Zeitraum      2. Juni – 24. August  (84 Tage)          ← from the sheet's window
Länge         [ kurz ~15 s ]  [ mittel ~40 s ]  [ lang ~2 Min ]
              → 1 004 Bilder verfügbar · ergibt 40 Sek.  ← the REAL number, before rendering
Bilder        ☑ Nachtbilder weglassen   ☑ Helligkeit angleichen   ☑ Doppelte weglassen
                                                    [ Film erstellen ]
```

The server picks the frame stride from the culled, available frames to hit the target length, and it
**states the length it will actually get** before you commit. Over an 84-day run the thinning tiers mean
older days offer fewer frames (1/h beyond 90 days, 1/15 min beyond 30, 1/5 min beyond 7); "lang" degrades
gracefully and the dialog says so rather than pretending.

```ts
Film { film_id, device_id, run_id?, from, to, preset: 'kurz'|'mittel'|'lang',
       frames: number, duration_s: number, bytes: number, width: 1280, height: 720,
       status: 'queued'|'building'|'ready'|'failed', gridfs_id: ObjectId,
       built_at: number, expires_at: number }   // file expires after 14 days; frames do not
```

Stored in **GridFS**, not a Mongo `Buffer` — a 2-minute 720p CRF-21 film is 25–60 MB and the BSON limit
is 16 MB. GridFS needs no new dependency (Mongoose 8 exposes the native `GridFSBucket`). One build per
device per 10 minutes.

**How it leaves the product.** Three named paths, all real:

```
GET /film/:device_id/:film_id.mp4?token=<imageToken>
    Content-Type: video/mp4
    Content-Disposition: attachment; filename="terpcontrol_zelt-1_2026-06-02_2026-08-24_84d.mp4"
    Accept-Ranges: bytes
```

1. **Android Chrome** — the link downloads to `Downloads/`, and the system share sheet reaches Telegram
   and Discord (both are already where this community lives).
2. **iOS Safari** — the mp4 opens in the player; the share sheet's `Video sichern` puts it in the camera
   roll. The `faststart` flag is what makes it start playing before it has finished downloading.
3. **Web Share API** — where `navigator.canShare({files})` is true, the `Teilen` button hands the actual
   `File` to WhatsApp/Telegram/Signal directly. Feature-detected, with the download link as the fallback,
   never as the only option.

The `imageToken` is the existing deliberately-URL-embeddable 30-day token, so the URL survives being
pasted into a chat by its owner. It is scoped to the device and expires. A share link with
`webcam: true` grants the same route.

**Filename convention:** `terpcontrol_<gerätename-slug>_<von>_<bis>_<tage>d.mp4`. It sorts, it says what
it is in a Downloads folder six months later, and it contains no strain name, no plant count and no
account identifier.

### 8.4 Image correlation — both directions, one sprite

**Frame → numbers.** Opening a frame fetches `/data/series` for ±2 h around `t` at 1-minute resolution
and pins:

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────────┐
│              [frame, full-bleed]          │
│                                           │
├───────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  24. Aug 2026, 14:32          Tag 41 · Blüte
│  26,4 °C   58 % rF   VPD 1,21 kPa   CO₂ 1180
│  Licht 100 %   Heizer aus   Entfeuchter an
│  💧 Anna, 8 L, vor 2 Std                  │
│  ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓░░▒▒▓▓▓▓  91,4 % im Ziel       │  ← the Tagesband, now labelled
│                            [ Zahlen ▲ ]   │  ← drawer → raw series + CSV
└───────────────────────────────────────────┘
   ← swipe: previous frame · swipe →: next
```

**Numbers → frame.** The chart's film strip is the sheet's `m` sprite at the same x-scale. Dragging the
chart cursor moves the picture with **zero network traffic** — that is the concrete payoff of the sprite
design, and it is the answer to "scrubbing a chart should move the picture".

**Frames arranged by data.** One labelled control on the sheet, `Sortierung`:
- `Zeit` (default) — chronological.
- `Gleiche Uhrzeit` — align every frame to the same lights-on-relative minute rather than wall-clock,
  which removes the jump the 18/6 → 12/12 flip otherwise puts in the middle of every film.
- `Abweichung` — reorder the run sheet by each day's time-out-of-band, worst first. A grid of pictures
  that is also a ranked anomaly list. Non-persisted, explicitly labelled while active
  (`Sortiert nach Abweichung · zurück zu Zeit`).

## 9. Clubs

**Attribution is a label; authorisation is a gate.** v1 ships the label and does not pretend it is the
gate. `auth.middleware.ts:172` is untouched: one writing account per device, share links read-only.

**Who is at the tent.** `Actor` documents are names the owner types once (`Anna`, `Tom`, `Kai`), each
with a colour. No accounts, no invitations, no e-mail lookup, no `auth.middleware` surgery. At capture,
the "Wer?" row appears **only when more than one actor exists** — reveal by data. The chosen actor is
stamped onto `Event.actor_id`/`actor_name`, and the actor's colour is the dot on the frame's cell. Two
days of work, and it is honest about what it is: the app says who *said* they did it. The banner in
`/settings` says exactly that: `Namen sind Zuordnung, keine Zugangskontrolle — alle teilen einen Login.`

**The double feed** is §7's soft block. It is the single most valuable thing in this section, because a
double feed is the most expensive thing that happens in a shared room, and every round-one concept
optimised capture speed instead of capture safety.

**The cumulative question**, `/device/:id/bilanz?run=<id>`:

```
A3 · Zyklus 2 · Tag 41
  Wasser        21,0 L in 9 Gaben        letzte: vor 2 Std (Anna)
  Dünger         8 Gaben                 letzte: KW5, vor 3 Tagen (Tom)
    Bio-Grow    240 ml gesamt
    Bio-Bloom   640 ml gesamt
  Nach Person   Anna 5 · Tom 3 · Kai 1
  Ø EC          1,42 (Zulauf) · 1,71 (Ablauf)      Ø pH 6,3
                                             [ Als CSV ]
```

Pure aggregation over `Event`. Every data model in round one could compute this; none surfaced it.

**Conditions** are the Zettel strip at the top of the sheet — an open fact with an owner, not an event
and not a task. `📌 CO₂-Flasche fast leer · Tom, 21. Aug ✕`. Closing it stamps `closedBy`/`closedAt` and
it drops into the sheet as a frame. Max three shown, `+2 weitere`.

**Privacy posture.** Default is privacy-maximal, and the club settings are opt-in per device:
- No harvest weight and no plant count is ever prompted. `Event.type: 'harvest'` accepts a weight only
  if the owner has switched on `Ernte-Gewichte erfassen` in settings, off by default.
- Per-member dispensing records are **not stored, ever** — that is § 26 KCanG territory, arguably Art. 9
  GDPR health data, and a grow controller has no need for it. We are not the club's system of record.
- No `KCanG-konform` badge, no compliance warranty, no location, no analytics SDK.
- A public sheet link (`ShareLink.page: 'sheet'`, reusing the existing share machinery) defaults to
  `Namen anonymisieren` ON (actors become `A.`, `T.`), strips weights and plant counts, and excludes any
  frame flagged `manual` (a human-uploaded photo may contain a face; a webcam still of a tent does not).
- Retention is a device setting with a real default, because today raw 5-second Influx samples are kept
  forever and that is both a cost curve and, in a prohibition jurisdiction, evidence.

## 11. Per-user-type walkthrough

**1 — Lazy monitoring-only.** Opens the app, sees the Kopf's two gauges and a wall of Streifen cells
whose Tagesband is green. That is the whole interaction: a texture that says "fine". Once a month a cell
is amber and they tap it. They never configure anything, never see a plant, never see a feed button, and
never see a chart unless they tap the verdict line. What they gain over today is the honest age on every
value and a verdict instead of fifteen translucent areas with no axis.

**2 — Same, but with a camera.** The concept is built for this person. Their home screen is 84 days of
their own tent, in order, and they can hand a 40-second mp4 to their friend on Telegram without an
account, a login page or a URL that asks the recipient to sign in. The image-correlation payoff — tap a
frame, see what the room was doing — is the thing they bought the camera for and could not previously do.

**3 — Controls certain aspects (CO₂ or heating).** The Ausgänge lane on the chart has exactly the rows
they own, the goal band appears only for measures they can act on, and the humidity target row and the
dehumidifier alarm preset stop appearing for a heater-only tent. The fail-open capability bug is fixed by
reading per-role truth out of `hardwareInfo.sockets` instead of collapsing it to three buckets.

**4 — Full tent.** The Ausgänge state-timeline is their diagnostic surface: five roles as lanes, region
length as duration, `null` as a gap, so a failed contactor reads as a missing band rather than as
nothing. The concept does **not** invent socket roles — there is no humidifier, no exhaust fan and no
chiller role in the firmware, and pretending otherwise would need firmware work gated by the October
hardware date. Where a target cannot be acted on with the kit paired, the band is drawn as advisory and
labelled as such, which is more honest than today.

**5 — Closed-loop DIY.** The sheet index (`GET /sheet/:device_id`) is a documented JSON endpoint with
frame timestamps, image ids, flags and per-frame time-in-band — the first machine-readable thing this
product has ever offered, and it happens to be exactly what a Home Assistant camera card or a personal
Grafana wants. The frame drawer's CSV export and the raw `/data/series` are one tap from any picture.
They will still complain there is no write API for outputs. They will be right; it is not in this
release.

**6 — Grow club.** Actors, the double-feed soft block, the Bilanz, and the Zettel strip. Their real
question — "who fed A3 what, and how did it react?" — is answered by opening A3's Bilanz and then
scrubbing the sheet from the feed frame forward, which is the one thing a controller can do that no club
SaaS can. They do not get memberships, per-member ledgers or authority exports, and the settings page
says so in one sentence rather than implying otherwise.

**7 — Old device owner.** Gets the Streifen (§10), keeps their existing settings page byte-for-byte, and
loses nothing. Their app is smaller than it was, because a plug no longer renders a fridge dashboard.

## 13. Honest weaknesses

**1. The concept bets the product on the camera attach rate, and the camera is a €30–60 upsell.**
If most of the 150 first-series units ship without one, most users open the app to a Streifen of duty
bands — competent, honest, and *not the concept*. The Streifen is a real fallback, not a fig leaf, but
this is the single largest risk and it is commercial, not technical. If attach rate is under ~40 %, C18
is the wrong centre of gravity for this release.

**2. The sheet is a bad index for anomalies, which is exactly what a techie wants first.**
Finding a 3 a.m. temperature spike by scanning photographs is slow. `Sortierung: Abweichung` and the
Tagesband mitigate it; they do not fix it. A power user will want the chart as their home screen and will
experience the sheet as a layer in the way. The honest defence is that the verdict line reaches the chart
in one tap — but a critic who says "you made the analytical user pay for the visual user's home screen"
is not wrong.

**3. Weeks 6–12 of a grow look identical.** A contact sheet of a dense bud wall under magenta LEDs stops
being visually informative exactly when the grow gets interesting, and the normalisation pass makes the
frames *more* similar, not less. The Tagesband is the answer and it is 3 pixels tall. This is the
weakness a photographer would spot immediately.

**4. Twelve hours of every day are culled, so the sheet has a hole in it.**
`Gleiche Uhrzeit` alignment keeps the poster frames consistent, but a 12/12 flip moves lights-on by six
hours mid-run and the alignment jumps once. There is no elegant fix; the film gets a visible seam at the
flip and the sheet draws a stage line there, which at least explains it.

**5. GridFS, an ffmpeg build queue and a sprite builder are three new moving server parts.**
None need a new dependency, and all of them reuse the existing throttled-rebuild pattern — but the
current ffmpeg calls are fire-and-forget with a `pLimit(10)` and no queue, no retry and no backpressure.
A film build is 30–90 seconds of CPU; ten users pressing the button at once on one box is a real
degradation, and the 10-minute-per-device rate limit is a blunt instrument.

**6. The culling heuristics will be wrong sometimes, and being wrong is invisible.**
A frame silently dropped as `transient` is a frame the user cannot find. Mitigation shipped: the frame
detail always reports `3 Bilder in dieser halben Stunde ausgelassen · anzeigen`, and nothing is ever
deleted — only excluded from the sheet and the film. But the default is still that the software decided
what you see.

**7. Attribution without authorisation is a half-answer to clubs and will be read as a full one.**
Everyone shares one login. Anna's name on a frame means someone tapped "Anna". The settings banner says
so; nobody reads banners. A club that assumes otherwise will be disappointed at exactly the wrong moment.

**8. A timestamped photographic record of a cannabis grow, held in a cloud, is the most sensitive
artefact this product could possibly create** — more so than any number. The privacy defaults in §9 are
the mitigation, and they are real, but the concept's core mechanic increases the value of a subpoena.
That deserves to be an explicit owner decision, not an implementation detail.

**9. It does not solve type 4 or type 5.** The full-tent user still cannot express "cool AND dehumidify"
because the firmware has five socket roles and one of them means two things. The DIY user still has no
write API. C18 makes both of them *better informed* and neither of them *better served*. Any concept in
this round that claims to fix those without firmware work is lying; this one does not claim it.
